The bet in one paragraph

South Africa builds too slowly and too expensively. The national housing backlog is 2.6 million units, and private residential building plans are worth about R44bn a year. A wall system that goes up three times faster, needs no mortar and no added insulation, and can be installed by a small crew is a premium product with a real market. This plan starts as the Southern African distributor of Poland’s SYSTEM 3E, proves it on paid pilot builds, then localises production to cut the landed cost by roughly 30% and scale into garden cottages, farm buildings and eco-estates. Seed ask: R2.5m.

The gap: the market is not short of bricks. It is short of speed. A 120 m² house takes weeks of bricklaying, then plaster, then insulation. A dry-stack system collapses those steps into days — and that is what a backyard landlord, a farm build or an eco-estate will pay a premium for.
Part 2 — Product

What SYSTEM 3E actually is

SYSTEM 3E is a Polish-made wall system built from expanded perlite — a volcanic glass that is heated until it puffs up, then pressed with mineral binders into interlocking blocks. The blocks click together with a Morse-taper joint. There is no mortar, no glue and no water on site.

The result is a single-leaf wall that is load-bearing, self-insulating and fire-resistant. The manufacturer publishes these figures:

Part 3 — Customers

Who buys first, and why

1 · Backyard rental landlords

A 40 m² garden cottage that can be built in days instead of weeks. The customer is not buying a house; they are buying rentable floor area. Speed and a clean, dry build matter more than the last 5% of material cost.

Fastest cash Gauteng, Cape Town, Durban

2 · Eco-estate and premium self-builders

Buyers who want a high-performance envelope and can afford a premium. They respond to U-values, fire ratings and a build that does not depend on a large bricklaying crew.

Highest margin Western Cape, KZN

3 · Farm and agri buildings

Storerooms, workshops, staff accommodation and packhouses. Remote sites, tight programmes, and a preference for fire-resistant walls. A small crew can build without a mixer or water supply.

Longer sales cycle Free State, Limpopo, North West

4 · Small commercial fit-outs

Offices, clinics, classrooms and light-industrial partitions. Fire separation and acoustic performance are the buying criteria; the premium is easier to justify than in a house.

Specifier-led National

A finished home. The wall is the structure, the insulation and the fire barrier in one. Photo: SYSTEM 3E.
Who we do not chase first: state-subsidised housing. The procurement cycle is slow, the price ceiling is hard, and the system is not yet certified for bond-financed homes. We enter that market only after local production and an Agrément certificate are in place.

Our edge is not “cheaper than brick”. It is “faster than brick, warmer than brick, and installable without a bricklaying crew”. We win the jobs where time, skills and thermal performance are the constraint — not the jobs where the client is shopping on material price alone.
Could China do it cheaper? No. SYSTEM 3E is Polish, and its patents cover China as well as the EU and the USA. The elements are heavy: a 40ft container carries only about 213 m² of wall, so a longer sea route adds freight and time without cutting the factory price. Chinese AAC blocks are a different product, and Aertec already makes them in Cape Town and Durban. The cheaper path is local production under licence — not a Chinese copy.
Part 5 — Business model

Three revenue streams, one system

60%
Kit supply

We import, warehouse and deliver the elements to a builder or homeowner. Gross margin ~33%.

30%
Turnkey install

We supply and install with a certified crew, or project-manage a partner crew. Gross margin ~28% after labour.

10%
Training & certification

We train and certify independent crews, charge a per-crew fee and take a referral royalty on installed jobs.

The model in one line

Import the elements → certify the crews → sell the kit → localise the plant. Each stage funds the next, and each stage reduces the cost per square metre.

1 · LicenceDistributor agreement with SYSTEM 3E for South Africa and SADC.
2 · ProveTwo demo units and eight paid pilots. Start Agrément.
3 · CertifyTrain ten independent crews; publish a cost calculator.
4 · LocaliseLicense or build a small block plant in Gauteng.
5 · ExpandSupply neighbouring SADC markets from the local plant.

Part 8 — Go-to-market

How the first ten projects happen

1 · Two demo units

One in Gauteng, one in the Western Cape. A finished cottage is the salesperson. Every visitor can touch the wall and see the joint.

2 · Architect and builder lunches

Twelve practices and twenty builders in the first two months. The pitch is a costed wall system, not a slide deck.

3 · Pilot pricing

Eight paid pilots at a 10% launch discount, in exchange for a case study, photos and a testimonial. No free builds.

4 · Content engine

A time-lapse of every build, a live cost calculator, and a one-page comparison against brick, AAC and ICF. The website is the second salesperson.

5 · Finance partner

Introduce clients to a building-loan originator. The system needs an Agrément certificate before a bank will lend against it, so this starts after certification.

6 · Crew network

Certify independent crews in each metro. We sell the kit; they sell the labour. The network scales without adding payroll.

Part 10 — Compliance

The gate that decides the business

Agrément South Africa

Evaluates non-standard construction systems for fitness for purpose. Without it, insurers, banks and many municipalities will not accept the wall.

Certification process

SANS 10400

The national building regulations. The relevant parts are K (walls), T (fire) and XA (energy). The system’s published U-value and fire rating are the evidence base.

NHBRC

Every new home must be enrolled with the NHBRC at least 15 days before construction, and the builder must be registered.

NHBRC

Sequence matters. Start the Agrément application in Stage 1, in parallel with the pilot builds. Do not wait for the certificate to start selling cash-build cottages and farm buildings — but do not promise a bond-financed house until the certificate is in hand.
Would it work in South Africa? Technically, yes. The wall is fire-rated, vapour-permeable and self-insulating, which suits both the hot coastal summers and the cold Highveld winters. The hard parts are certification, insurance and builder acceptance — not physics. That is why the plan puts compliance in Stage 1, not at the end.

Walk-away test. If Agrément rejects the system, or the live landed cost cannot be held under R2,100/m², the venture stops at the end of Stage 1 and returns the unspent capital. That is what the R2.5m seed is sized for.
