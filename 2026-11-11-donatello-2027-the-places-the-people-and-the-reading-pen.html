The places with people in them — and the pen that reads back the words.

Every one of these is a real practice with a phone number, an address and a person who writes reports. Plus the reading pen, and the one assessment booking that unlocks all of it.

The short version

Go here first: Neurodiversity Centre, Durbanville — a multidisciplinary clinic in Bellville on the Durbanville border with educational psychologists, an occupational therapist and a collaborating speech therapist, doing dyslexia assessments and the follow-on therapy. 021 975 0376. If you want the report faster or closer, Charlene Wessels in Durbanville does psycho-educational and accommodation assessments — the exact report the school and the exam board ask for.

Then, weekly: Kip McGrath Durbanville — a free assessment by a qualified teacher, face-to-face twice-weekly remediation in reading, spelling and comprehension, and progress reports you can hand the school.

The pen: the C-Pen Exam Reader 2 — the current generation, AI-supported OCR, British/Irish/Scottish accents, no storage and no dictionary so it is exam-legal, 12-month warranty, quote from the SA distributor. The older original Exam Reader at R5,000 is the budget fallback, not the pick. Gate: 0.913. The catch: in a test it is an accommodation, and an accommodation needs the psychologist's report. So the report and the pen are one purchase, not two.

Part 3 — The pen that reads back the words

The pick — revised 11 November
C-Pen Exam Reader 2

Gate 0.913 (five-way revised shortlist) vs 0.079 for the original model · quote from Edit Microsystems (086 111 3973), the South African distributor

You run the pen across a line of print and it speaks the words back through the earphones, offline — no phone, no wifi, no app. It is a closed system: no storage, no dictionary, nothing can be loaded onto it, which is precisely why exam boards accept it.

Ask for the "2" by name. It is the current generation: AI-supported OCR, faster scanning and faster speech, 10+ scan languages, and — the part that matters for a South African child — accent options including British, Irish and Scottish English, plus an audio-aided menu he can navigate by ear. 8+ hour battery, 12-month warranty.

Why I changed it. The first version of this page led with the original C-Pen Exam Reader at R5,000 from The Dyslexia Shop SA. That price is real and the device works, but its spec sheet (600 MHz CPU, English-only menu, older scan engine) is the first generation, not the current one. The Exam Reader 2 is a materially better reader for the same job and is available through the local distributor on quote. The R5,000 pen is now the budget option, not the pick. If money is the binding constraint, it is still a legitimate purchase — it reads the page and it is legal in an exam — but it is the older tool.

What it is not

A pen reads the words to him. It does not teach him to read them. It removes the barrier in the moment — homework tonight, a test in March — and it will quietly mask progress if it becomes the only tool. Pair it with the structured literacy work; use the pen for access, and treat the reading sessions as the cure. The other honest limit: it has no dictionary and no translation, by design. If you want vocabulary support at home as well, that is the Reader 2 — a second device, not a substitute.

Buy the earphones-in, closed-system one first. It is the one that is legal in an exam, and it is the cheaper of the two.

Part 4 — The one booking that unlocks three things

A single psycho-educational assessment, done properly, is the document behind all three of the things you are currently being asked for. This is the piece to book this week, before the pen and before the facilitator's start date.

What the report unlocksWhy it needs the report
The school's written support planMrs Prins has confirmed Grade 4 on the understanding that "the support plan discussed will be in place from the very first day". A written plan with a named diagnosis, a named programme and named accommodations is what converts a conversation into an obligation.
The exam concessions — including the penThe IEB accommodation route requires "a full assessment by a psychologist (using very specific tests)". Reader, electronic reader, specific equipment, extra time, spelling and rest breaks are all on the list. Nothing gets granted on a parent's letter.
The facilitator's briefIt tells the facilitator, in writing, exactly what to scaffold and where to step back — which is the difference between support and dependence.

South Africa's assessment bodies now treat the pen as a named accommodation: the SACAI 2026 policy on accommodations and concessions, in force from 1 January 2026, devotes a section to "Computer use – Text-to-voice (C-Pen)". So the pen is not a gadget you smuggle into a test. It is a listed accommodation with a procedure — and the procedure starts with the psychologist.

Ask when you book: "Can you write this as an accommodation assessment to IEB/assessment-board format, and do you do the school observation?" Also ask for the fee in writing, whether they will submit the concession application or whether the school does, and whether they will attend a school meeting. Those four answers decide which practice you use.
