01The verdict

The cheapest property per square metre is not the best Airbnb. On Rhodes the gap between a town flat near a school and a flat with outdoor space in a resort corridor is bigger than the gap between any two purchase prices.

Buy route C: a 90 m² two-bedroom in the Ialysos–Kremasti strip, asking €155,000–€191,000, budget €170,000. All-in with purchase costs, a light refresh, furnishing and a hot tub: €229,243. It books about €29,500 a year, keeps €14,291 after every cost and the Greek tax, and that is 6.2% on your money.

Two things decide the ranking, and neither is the purchase price.

  • Who runs it. Fully managed from South Africa costs you 3.2% of yield — the single largest number in this brief. Same building, same guests, 6.2% light-touch against 3.1% fully managed.
  • Whether guests want the address. The auction lots are the best value in the market per square metre, and still lose on revenue, because both sit in a residential town 2.5 km inland whose demand is a fraction of the coast's.

The runner-up is not close on cash but is close on risk. The Archangelos shell at lot 2550292 starts at €810/m² — a third of the island's asking average — and finishes to €1,843/m². It clears €10,252 a year (6.4%), but only after you build it, and only if its 2006 permit is still good. Bid only with a lawyer's opinion in hand.

02What Rhodes actually earns

Two data providers disagree so badly about this island that one of them has to be wrong. The one whose own numbers add up is the one used here.

Rhodes runs about 4,000–4,300 active short-let listings. The market-wide median revenue is €33,000 a year at roughly 71–72 % occupancy, and inside Rhodes Town specifically it is €32,000 at a €120 nightly rate across 1,161 listings. Both of those figures reconcile: €120 × 365 × 71 % = €31,100, which is the €32,000. That internal consistency is why Airbtics is the basis here.

The competing source (AirROI) reports a €263 nightly rate for the same municipality — roughly double — on a sample of only 420 listings, and its own monthly table sums to about €43,000 against an annual figure it states as €26,149. When a source contradicts itself, it does not get to set the model.

Airbnb's own destination pages give the shape that matters more than the average: what a night costs month by month in Kremasti, one of the two corridors this brief recommends. In euro, at today's rate:

That average is across every unit type, so a genuine two-bedroom with a terrace prices roughly a quarter above it — which lands on €120–€125, almost exactly where Airbtics puts the Rhodes Town rate. Two independent sources converging on the same number is the best evidence in this document.

Two more facts set the frame. Rhodes asking prices averaged €2,612/m² in September 2026, up 13.7 % year on year, and the Dodecanese index ran from €1,960 to €2,222/m² in the same twelve months. The island is not cheap any more, and it is still rising — which is why buying below the market matters more than optimising the operating margin.

04The money

A short-let in Greece is taxed on the gross. Not on the profit — on the money that arrives in the bank, before the manager, the electricity and the cleaner are paid. That single rule decides the structure.

The tax line of €6,209 is computed on gross bookings less the 5 % statutory repairs allowance, with no relief for management, utilities or furnishing. It is charged at 15 % to €12,000, 25 % to €24,000, 35 % to €36,000, then 45 %.

This is why the operating model outweighs the building. Fully managed, the manager takes 20 % and Airbnb 15.5 % — 35.5 % of turnover before a single utility bill — and the tax still falls on the full gross. Net drops to 3.1%. Every percentage point of cost you remove lands almost entirely in your pocket, because the tax does not move.

Against doing nothing clever

The same €170,000 flat let long-term in Rhodes fetches around €750 a month. Gross yield about 5.0 %; after the 15 % band, an agent's fee and maintenance, roughly €5,418 a year, or 3.0%. The short-let earns more than that — but only if you keep the management cost down. Long-let wins the day you stop wanting to run a hospitality business.

05The seasonality trap

Rhodes is a six-month business wearing a twelve-month calendar. Underwriting it on an annual average hides the two decisions that actually break owners.

Cash flow. Roughly 80 % of the year's bookings arrive between May and September. The bills — ENFIA, insurance, the accountant, compliance renewals — arrive all year. Any owner who spends the summer's takings as income is short of money by February, every February.

Comparison shopping. A 71 % annual occupancy sounds like a busy property. It is 259 nights, which on a Rhodes season means near-full in July and August and near-empty in January. What guests see is the peak price — which is why the €137 August average in Kremasti is the number a new listing gets judged against, and why discounting in the shoulder months is where the money is actually won or lost.

The amenity that beats the season. Pools and hot tubs are the strongest revenue levers in coastal short-lets — a hot tub lifts daily rate by roughly a quarter in coastal markets. A €6,000 hot tub on a terrace is the cheapest way to move a two-bedroom from the market average into the top quartile, and it works in the shoulder months when a pool is too cold to matter.

06How each one fails

Every route has one failure that kills it. This is the section that decides the recommendation, because three of the four can be underwritten and one cannot.

Route A · Auction shell

The permit is twenty years old

A building permit runs four years. A shell left 65 % complete since 2006 has almost certainly lapsed, and without a valid permit and a legalised building identity you cannot obtain an AMA — and without an AMA you cannot list on Airbnb at all. That is a legal question, not a construction one, and it must be answered before the €63,000 is committed.

Route B · Renovated flat

You cannot see inside

Greek judicial auctions are sold as-is, and internal inspection usually depends on the occupant's goodwill. The revenue model is also the most fragile in this brief: a ground-floor 1974 flat near a school in a residential town, with no view and no outdoor space, is the product guests skip.

Route C · Resale

Your neighbours can close it

In July 2026 an Athens court stopped a flat short-letting because the building's regulations reserved apartments for residential use, and the Justice Ministry is drafting a bill that would let 51 % of ownership interests decide. Buy a flat in a purely residential block and the business can end by a vote you do not control. It also costs the most: you pay market price and create no value.

Route D · New build

The worst capital on the list

At €291,654 all-in it earns the same €29,500 as route C and returns 4.9%. It buys certainty and a warranty, and pays for both. There is also a date to watch: the suspension of 24 % VAT on new builds currently runs only to 31 December 2026.

Three that apply to all of them

  1. The non-EU transfer tax is about to quadruple. From 1 July 2027, a non-EU individual buying in their own name is expected to pay 15 % transfer tax instead of 3 % — 15.45 % with the municipal surcharge, against 3.09 % today. On a €170,000 purchase that is €21,012 more. It was announced in September 2026 and no bill has been voted, so it is not law; but a deed signed before that date pays the current rate. A purchase made through a company sits outside the measure.
  2. Compliance is now a licensing regime, not a formality. Every short-let needs an AMA number before it can be listed, plus an electrical certificate, fire extinguishers and detectors, emergency lighting, pest certification, an energy certificate and civil liability insurance from a Greek-licensed insurer. Budget €800–€1,500 up front and €300–€600 a year, with fines of €5,000–€20,000 for non-compliance.
  3. An AMA does not transfer on sale. Buy a property with an existing licence and you must register again in your own name. The guest tax — €8 a night April to October, €2 from November to March — is collected on top of the rate, not out of it.

None of this applies to central Athens or Thessaloniki, where new licences are frozen. All the islands, Rhodes included, remain open.

The recommendation, as a specification

  • Type — two bedrooms, 85–100 m², in the Ialysos–Kremasti corridor, 5–10 minutes from the airport and the beach strip.
  • Form — a maisonette, a ground-floor with its own entrance, or a small house. If it is a flat in a block, the building regulations must permit short-letting, and you want that in writing before you sign. This is now the cheapest risk you will ever buy out.
  • What to insist on — a private terrace or garden, the option to install a hot tub, and parking.
  • Budget — €155,000–€180,000 purchase; €229,243 all-in including €12,000 of furnishing and €6,000 of hot tub.
  • Operating model — you set prices (a revenue-management tool costs about €30 a month); a local co-host handles turnarounds at roughly 8 %; you keep the 20 % a full manager would take.
  • Structure — buy before 1 July 2027, or buy through a company if you intend to hold and the 15 % non-EU rate is enacted.

An independent entrance is not free. Two-bedroom maisonettes in Rhodes ask around €2,170/m² against €1,900 for a flat — roughly €30,000 more on this size — and at €200,000 the same revenue returns about 5.5 % rather than 6.2 %. That 0.7 of a point is what you pay to own something no assembly can vote into a house again.

If you would rather make the money on the purchase than on the guests: bid on lot 2550292, capped. At a €70,000 hammer the all-in lands at €159,320, against a finished value of roughly €2,000/m² — about €173,000, or €13,600 of value created, plus a flat you specified yourself. Above €80,000 the discount is gone and the build risk stops being paid for.

08Next four weeks

Two clocks are running. The auction closes on 24 November and the transfer-tax window closes on 30 June 2027. Everything else can wait.

  1. Get the AFM and the account — days, not weeks. A Greek tax number is the prerequisite for bidding, buying, and the AMA. A Greek bank account and a power of attorney to a local lawyer follow. None of it can be done from a distance without a notarised, apostilled mandate.
  2. Put a lawyer on lot 2550292 before 10 November. Three questions only: is the 2006 permit still valid, does the property have a legal building identity, and what exactly is being auctioned — full ownership, or a share of a plot that must be partitioned. If the answer to the first two is no, the flat cannot be licensed and the route is dead regardless of price.
  3. Register for the auction platform before 24 November. Bidding is electronic, registration needs the AFM and a deposit, and the deadline is not negotiable. Missing it costs nothing; discovering on 23 November that the account is not live costs the opportunity.
  4. Inspect five resales in Ialysos and Kremasti in one trip. You are looking for the same three things in each: a terrace, a building with no short-let restriction in its rules, and a price under €2,000/m². Anything above that is paying for someone else's view.
  5. Do not sign a deed after 30 June 2027 if the purchase is in your own name and you are a non-EU national. The date is the deed, not the deposit — a reservation protects nothing.

Three decisions, and only three

  1. Are you running this or buying a manager? That answer is worth 3.2% of yield — more than any choice of property in this document.
  2. Resale in the corridor, or the auction in the town? 6.2% with certainty and a sellable asset, against 6.4% with a build, a permit question and a discount you have to earn.
  3. Before 1 July 2027, or after? The deed date is worth €21,012 on a €170,000 purchase if the non-EU rate is enacted.
