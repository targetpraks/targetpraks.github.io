Paperwork review · Thursday 26 November 2026

17 Frances Street, Dunvegan
Offer to Purchase

Two files arrived. They are not the same document. One is the full eight-page offer at 8% commission; the other is a two-page fragment at 3.5% that also hands half the commission to a third agency. The money balances, the price is sound, and the offer expires at 22h00 tonight — but the paper you would be relying on is split across two different Zoho Sign documents and neither one is complete.

Reviewed: Francis house dunvegan.pdf (9 pages, 2,696,029 bytes) and francis second.pdf (2 pages, 608,515 bytes)  |  Property: Erf 211, Dunvegan  |  Seller: RM Nova  |  Agency: Pompa Realty Group (Pty) Ltd

Nothing here is unsafe to sign — but this is not one document yet

The arithmetic works: R5,650,000 − R300,000 deposit = R5,350,000 balance. The price was raised by hand from R5,400,000 to R5,650,000 and initialled. The bond, the deposit window and the occupation clause all read cleanly.

What does not hold together is the commission. Francis house dunvegan.pdf prints 8% + VAT. francis second.pdf strikes that out and writes 3.5%, then adds a handwritten clause 25 splitting it 50/50 with Jack Russel Estate. That is R292,387.50 of difference — and it exists only on a two-page extract of pages 4 and 7, while pages 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8 still exist only at 8%.

So the single most important question is: which of these two did everybody actually sign? The full pack has the only signature page, and on it the seller's line is signed but undated and unwitnessed, and the agent has not signed at all.

That is the same suspensive condition, so it is one risk, not two: if their house does not sell, there is no deal and no R950,000.

What is wrong

Seven of these need fixing before the document is worth signing. Two are commercial decisions that are yours alone.

Material There are two documents, not one

Each page of a Zoho Sign output carries a Document ID. These two files carry different IDs: 2A9C0716-4AJJ…_DYWROQ5YWTHIODJGKVWH2LKW4 on the fragment, 2A9C0716-BO_IC…-VJ0EMZHNXDQJ6TVDCDAM on the full pack. So the 2-page file is not an extract of the 9-page file — it is a separate Zoho Sign document, modified 26 November at 08:40, a day after the full pack was last touched (25 November 10:19).

Clause 13.1 of the offer itself says no alteration is of any force unless signed by both seller and purchaser. A two-page initialled extract is not a signed variation of an eight-page contract.

Material The commission you initialled is not on the document that has signatures

On the fragment, the printed 8% in clause 8 is struck through and 3,5%. is written above it, with initials in the margin. On the full pack, that same 8% is untouched — the pen marks there sit in the right margin only. Side by side, the fragment carries one extra initial that the full pack does not: a circled cursive mark on the seller's side.

That reads as you rejecting 8% and adopting 3.5%. It does not read as the agency agreeing to it, and it does not appear anywhere in the eight-page instrument.

Material Clause 25 exists in only one file

Page 7 of 8 of the fragment carries two handwritten clauses. Page 7 of 8 of the full pack carries one. The extra one reads:

“25. IT IS AGREED TO SHARE THE COMMISSION 50/50 BETWEEN POMA REALTY GROUP & JACK RUSSEL ESTATE”

A third agency appears nowhere else in the eight-page pack — not in the agency header, not in the commission clause, not on the signature page. Two things follow: whoever signs the full pack has agreed to pay 8% to Pompa Realty Group alone, and a 50/50 split written on a fragment is not something a conveyancer can act on.

Fix before signature Handwritten clause 24 does not parse

Both files carry the same three lines, and the third line defeats every reading:

“24. THE OFFER IS SUBJECT TO THE PURCHASER SIGNING THE CONDITION REPORT REFERRED TO IN CLAUSE 23 AND         FROM 5 DAYS OF SIGNING OF THE OFFER”

Read at 1200 dpi across several passes, the missing word comes back as ACCRUING, ACCOMPANYING, ACCEPTING or ACCORDING — none of which produces a sentence. It is probably “within”. As written, a clause that decides whether your offer is conditional at all is unenforceable in practice.

Fix before signature Annexure A is referenced twice and is not in the pack

Clause 23 makes the Immovable Property Condition Report part of the agreement. Clause 24 makes the whole offer conditional on the purchaser signing it. There is no Annexure A in either file. The document is therefore conditional on a piece of paper that does not exist yet, with a five-day clock running from signature — and nobody can tell whether the clock has started, because the seller's signature is undated.

Fix before signature Page 3 of 8 appears twice

The full pack is nine PDF pages for eight numbered pages: page 3 of 8 is scanned in twice. The two copies carry identical wording, including the handwritten solar-system line in clause 6 — but only one of them carries a footer initial. It is a bundle assembled from two scans of the same sheet, not a clean instrument, and a conveyancer will query it.

Fix before signature The seller is named in abbreviated form with no registration number

The parties block reads “RM NOVA PROP (PTY) LTD”. That is not the registered name and there is no company registration number anywhere on the form. The seller is a juristic person, so the registration number and the capacity of the signatory are exactly what a conveyancer checks first. It is a one-line fix now and an expensive one after lodgement.

Fix before signature The signature page is half-filled

Page 8 of 8: the purchasers signed at Edenvale on 22 November 2026 and their witness signed. The seller's line carries a signature but no place, no date, and the seller's witness line is blank. The agent's block is entirely empty — no signature, no date, and the Fidelity Fund Certificate number is blank, next to a warranty that the certificate is valid.

Under the Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 a practitioner may not earn commission without a valid certificate, and the warranty the agent is meant to sign exists precisely so that a seller can rely on it. An unsigned warranty protects nobody.

Consider Ninety days before you know anything

The purchasers have 10 days for a bond (plus 7 automatic), 60 days to sell their own home, and 90 days for that sale's conditions to be met. Balance is payable in 120 days. So for three months your property is off the market and conditionally sold, with the seller's remedy limited to the 48-hour clause in clause 4 — which only helps if you have another buyer waiting.

Worth knowing Two smaller points

Clause 1.3(b) — the VAT-registered seller option — is struck through and initialled, which leaves 1.3(a): you are not VAT-registered and the purchaser pays transfer duty. Confirm that is your position, because it changes who owes what at the deeds office.

Clause 4 records the purchasers' own property at “R3,700,000 gross”. Gross means before their bond is settled, before the agent's fee and before costs. Check what actually nets to them: if their equity in 68 Ruby Road is under R950,000 plus costs, the bond does not bridge the gap and the deal fails at the finance stage rather than the sale stage.

What I would do before you sign

Not a renegotiation — a re-issue. Every point below is a correction to the paperwork, not a change to the deal you have already negotiated.

  1. Ask for one 8-page document, issued fresh. Same offer, one Zoho Sign envelope, eight pages, page 3 once. Nothing gets signed off a fragment, and nothing gets signed off a file that carries a second version of itself.
  2. Have 3.5% typed into clause 8 and clause 25 typed into the body. Handwritten amendments over a struck 8% are the first thing an agency disputes. If the split with Jack Russel Estate is real, it belongs in clause 8 with the other agency named in full and its Fidelity Fund Certificate attached.
  3. Rewrite clause 24 in words that parse. One sentence: the purchaser has five days from signature to deliver the signed Annexure A, failing which either party may cancel. Then attach Annexure A, signed.
  4. Correct the parties block. Full registered name, company registration number, and the capacity of whoever signs for RM Nova. Confirm the surname of both purchasers against their identity documents at the same time.
  5. Get the agent's block completed before your ink goes on. Signature, date, Fidelity Fund Certificate number. And date and witness your own signature while you are at it — an undated seller signature is what makes every deadline in this deal arguable.
  6. Decide the 90-day question on commercial grounds, not legal ones. The paperwork will be fixable by this afternoon. Whether you want your property committed for three months to a buyer whose own house has not sold is not a paperwork question, and it is the only one here I cannot answer for you.

How this was checked

Both files are image scans with no text layer, so every page was rasterised at 200 dpi and again at 600–1200 dpi for each filled-in figure, then read twice: once by Apple's Vision text recogniser with language correction switched off, and once by eye on the high-resolution crops. Every money figure, date and handwritten amendment was verified visually — OCR alone mangles filled amounts, and did: it read the price as 5,809,000 and the balance as 5,700,000 on the first pass. Page-level image differencing was used to find what actually changed between the two versions, and it isolated the two amendments on pages 4 and 7 and nothing else of substance.

The legal points referenced above:

  • Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019 — the Fidelity Fund Certificate requirement and the agent's warranty.
  • Alienation of Land Act 68 of 1981 — section 2(1): a sale of land must be in writing and signed by the parties, which is why an initialled fragment is not a variation.
  • National Credit Act 34 of 2005 — the bond condition in clause 2.2 is drafted against section 92.
