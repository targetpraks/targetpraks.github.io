Replacing Zoho Projects and Filestage: Asana, ClickUp or Hive

Three tools, forty people, and one awkward question underneath the price lists — which of them lets a manager see what staff are actually doing, review the work itself, and finish that review with a signature instead of a comment thread. All prices are in rand, include VAT, and were taken from each vendor's own page this month.

The verdict

Hive Teams with Proofing, paid monthly, then annualised.

R17,417 a month for all forty people on monthly billing — R209,000 a year if we never convert, R154,478 if we move to annual once the first signed approval has proved the point. It is the only one of the three that ends a creative review in an approval rather than a comment, so it is the only one that lets Filestage go — R62,284 a year off the books.

ClickUp Business is the cheaper seat at R14,388 a month and the more honest contract, but its review stops at comments. With Filestage kept, the stack costs R234,936 a year — R25,936 more than Hive, for a better inbox and worse sign-off.

Asana Advanced is out: R23,088 a month, no video proofing, and a timer that only records what someone types in after the fact.

01What we are actually buying

Cheaper is irrelevant if the tool does not do the job. These are the tests, taken from the brief, and they are pass/fail before price gets a vote.

The five tests, and why each one exists
TestWhy it decides the purchase
A manager can see what staff are doingNot a wall of cards — a view by person, with hours, load and slippage. Zoho Projects fails here because the answer is a report, not a screen.
A manager can review the work itselfOpen the actual PDF, deck, image or video, mark it up, and finish. Reviewing a task description is not reviewing the work.
The review ends in a sign-offAn approval with a name and a date, not a thumbs-up in a thread. This is the requirement that removes Filestage — and the one that eliminates two of the three tools.
One task can reference anotherFive brands, shared suppliers, one launch feeding another. If relations are a side tab nobody opens, the manager has lost the thread.
It is not visually hostileThe honest reason the current tool is being replaced. If people avoid opening it, every other feature is theoretical.

Three secondary requirements sit on top: time tracking that staff will actually use, a client space where an outside brand sees only their own work, and an inbox where comments and review requests land in one place. Add a working API or MCP if we want agents reading and writing the same data.

02The three contenders

Contender 01

Hive Teams

$12 annual · $18 monthly, per user
+ $5 for Proofing & approvals

The generalist that bought a proofing company's problem. Projects, portfolios, workloads, dashboards, notes, chat and email in one workspace, plus a reviewer that opens PDFs, Office files, images and video in the same window and ends in an approval round.

  • Wins: sign-off on the actual asset, and an external approver reached by email who needs no account
  • Loses: there is no true inbox — a bell with tabs and a Home widget instead
  • Watch: seats never shrink, and cancellation runs through a finance email

Contender 02

ClickUp Business

$12 annual · $19 monthly, per user

The most complete work operating system of the three, and the best daily driver. Typed Relationships sit on every plan, a task can live in several Lists, the Inbox is a real triage queue with inline reply and snooze, and the whole thing behaves the same on the phone.

  • Wins: linking work, the inbox, guest seats, seat flexibility
  • Loses: its own proofing is annotation and assigned comments — no approval state on creative work

Asana Advanced

04Hive's real bill — the add-ons nobody quotes

The $12 seat does not include the review

Hive's own pricing calculator lists Proofing & approvals as a flexible add-on at +$5 per user, per month — and its help centre tells an admin to "confirm Proofing & Approvals" before the feature will work. At forty people that is R45,434 a year on top of the seat price. Anyone quoting R109,044 for Hive at 40 seats has quoted a project tool with no review in it.

What monthly billing actually does to the decision

It narrows the field to two, and it reverses the ranking. ClickUp is cheaper as a seat by R3,029 a month. ClickUp is more expensive as a solution by R25,936 a year, because it cannot retire Filestage. On annual billing the same gap is R16,850 — so monthly buying does not punish Hive, it mildly rewards it, since ClickUp's monthly premium (58%) is larger than Hive's (35%).

The one-way ratchet

If Infinity Brands hires five people for a season and adds them to Hive, those five seats stay on the invoice for the rest of the term even after they leave. At $23 a month each that is R5,300 a year per departed seat. It is not a reason to reject Hive, but it is a reason to add staff in one planned move rather than trickle them in, and to confirm the rule in writing before signing.

08Moving the whole company, in one move

No staggered pilot. Forty seats from day one, monthly billing, one migration weekend. The point of monthly is not to test on ten people — it is to keep the exit open while everyone uses the real thing.

  1. Use the trial for the whole company, not a sample. Hive gives fourteen days with full features; Asana gives thirty; ClickUp runs a free tier you can trial a paid plan on. Put all forty accounts in during the trial, migrate one live brand, and make the review test below the pass/fail gate.
  2. Run the sign-off test on day one of the trial. One 20-page PDF, one 60-second video, one image set, and one external approver reached by email with no account. The test passes only if it ends with an Approved stamp carrying a name and a date and the card moves. If it fails, Hive is out and ClickUp takes the seat price — with Filestage retained.
  3. Migrate in one weekend, in this order: five brand spaces, then the live projects on each, then the client-facing folders. Keep Zoho Projects read-only for ninety days so nothing is lost, then close it.
  4. Bill monthly for the first quarter, at forty seats. That is R17,417 a month. The monthly premium over annual is roughly R4,544 a month — the price of being able to walk away, and worth paying while the company is still learning the tool.
  5. Cancel Filestage only after the first approval signed inside Hive by a real external reviewer. Not after a demo, not after an internal test — after a live client round. Then the R62,284 comes off.
  6. Convert to annual before month four. That single step saves R54,522 in the first year and R54,522 every year after. From that point the platform costs R12,873 a month instead of R17,417.

09What would change the answer

The sign-off test fails on video.

If Hive cannot hold forty timestamped comments on a 60-second brand film, stack version 3 against 4 and lock the record, the primary requirement is not met. Then it is ClickUp Business plus PageProof — R18,330 a month, R913 more than Hive, and the only stack here that beats Filestage at the approval itself and keeps ClickUp's inbox.

Full working, with the six approval tests and the ClickUp integration in detail: artwork and video approval inside ClickUp.

The inbox gap turns out to matter more than expected.

Hive has no reply-in-place and no snooze. If a week of real use shows managers living in the bell and losing track, the honest answer is that a good inbox is worth real money — and ClickUp wins it outright. Configure Hive first: Approvals widget pinned to Home, bell filtered to Approvals plus mentions plus assignments, push set to "away or offline". Then judge.

The client-access quote comes back high.

External users is +$5 a user at forty — R45,434 a year. Most client review does not need a login because the email approver needs no account, so this should stay un-bought until a client asks. If several do, add it and the stack lands at R254,435 a year, still under Asana plus Filestage.

Hive's API is gated at Enterprise.

Its price page lists the Enterprise API under Enterprise while the help centre documents MCP over GraphQL. Get the Teams-level limit in writing. If agents cannot read and write the workspace at Teams, that is a real loss — and the only one of the three where the API is an upgrade rather than a plan feature.

Everyone hates it in week three.

This is the scenario the monthly billing exists for. One month at forty seats is R17,417 — a cheap mistake, and the reason not to sign an annual contract on day one.

10What to put to each vendor, in writing

  • Hive: Confirm Proofing & approvals is $5/user/month and is required for review; give the price at 40 users monthly and annual. Confirm whether licence counts can decrease, and what notice is needed to cancel a monthly plan. Confirm the API/MCP limits available on Teams rather than Enterprise. Confirm the external approver by email needs no paid seat.
  • ClickUp: Confirm Business at $19/user/month on monthly billing at 40 users, with no minimum term. Confirm unassigned seats drop at the next cycle. Confirm the approval capability on creative files — the help centre reserves formal approvals for timesheets, and the marketing page does not say that.
  • Asana: Confirm Advanced at $30.49/user/month monthly and that seats can be reduced at the next cycle. Ask specifically whether video can be reviewed and approved inside Asana, because the answer decides whether Filestage stays.
  • All three: Ask for the VAT invoice format and the ZAR billing currency, and whether a South African entity can be billed locally. This is a five-figure annual invoice; it should not arrive as a dollar charge on a personal card.
