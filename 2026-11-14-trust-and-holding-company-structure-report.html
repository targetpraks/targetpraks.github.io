A trust above a Cyprus company, once the family home moves

Where to put a trust and the company it owns, for a Portuguese passport holder with a South African business, who wants no personal tax residence. Ranked on the law, debated by a council of five AI models, then costed from published 2026 fee schedules.

14 November 2026 Ranking v2.1 · costs v1 Research for advisers, not legal or tax advice

Company
Cyprus. 5% SA dividends tax instead of 20%, 0% on royalties, no withholding when it pays the trust. It needs a real board in Cyprus.
Trust
Jersey. The trust's job is succession and protection, not tax, so legal strength decides it. A Cyprus trust is the budget option.
First decision
Where the family lives in 2028. While home is Cape Town you stay South African resident, and the structure is estate planning, not a tax saving.

The short answer

  • The saving comes from the holding company and from leaving SA, not from the trust. The company cuts SA dividends tax to 5%. Against holding the shares yourself, that saves 15 points of tax if you're resident nowhere, or 5 points if you live in Cyprus, where the treaty already gives you 10%.
  • Estate duty is the bigger prize. Once you're non-resident, shares in a foreign holding company are outside SA estate duty: about R10.1m saved on a R50m group.
  • Being resident nowhere is weaker than being resident somewhere light. With no treaty country, SA still takes the full 20%, banks ask for a tax residence, and you have no tie-breaker to stop SARS claiming you. Cyprus's 60-day rule fixes all three for about two months a year.
  • Leaving costs one exit tax, paid in cash. SA treats you as selling everything the day before you leave: about 18% of a founder's value, so R9m on a R50m group. Values are lowest early.
  • Move nothing while you're SA-resident. Prepare first, leave, then incorporate the company and settle the trust, in that order.
  • What it costs: about R360k a year for the Cyprus company alone. A Jersey trust adds about R255k a year, a Cyprus trust about R90k. Most of the company cost is local directors and an office, which keep the 5% rate safe.

Where you live decides the tax, not the passport

South Africa taxes by residence. The first test is ordinarily resident: where your home, family, business and belongings are. The day count comes second: more than 91 days this year and in each of the previous five, and more than 915 days over those five years. While your wife and children live in Cape Town, the first test keeps you resident wherever you travel. If you keep the house available to you, it counts against you in any treaty tie-break.

The decision that comes first: where does the family live in 2028?

Everything else follows from this, so get it in writing before anything is signed.

Family home in 2028

Stays in Cape Town

You stay SA-resident. SA's trust and foreign-company rules bring offshore income back to you, so an offshore structure saves little tax.

  • Keep the SA group as it is
  • Do the preparation work now
  • Use a local trust and will for estate planning
The structure in this report

Moves to Cyprus

You use the 60-day non-dom rule, cease SA residence and pay the exit tax once. Then the holding company starts saving 15 points of dividends tax, and SA estate duty falls away.

  • Incorporate the Cyprus company only after you leave SA
  • Sit on its board yourself
  • Add the Jersey trust when the group passes about R50m

Moves to Greece

You become Greek-resident however few days you spend there. The €100,000 flat tax on foreign income is then the route, not Cyprus.

  • Own Greek property personally or through an EU company with disclosed owners
  • Check Greek rules on foreign companies and trusts before relying on Cyprus

How to keep it down

  • Use one provider for the trust and the company: one compliance fee, bundled minimums.
  • Be your own second Cyprus director once you live there. It saves R40k–R110k a year and is the substance SARS looks for.
  • Choose Nicosia or Larnaca over Limassol: desks are 20–30% cheaper and rent about 45% cheaper.
  • Keep the holding company passive (dividends only) until royalties justify the transfer-pricing paperwork.
  • Agree fixed fees with an agreed list of in-scope events (dividends, board meetings, reporting), so time charges don't creep.
  • Stage it: start with the Cyprus company and a will, and add the Jersey trust when the group passes about R50m.

Order of moves

While you're SA-resident now to late 2027 · prepare, don't move

  1. Get an independent valuation of each company, then plan how to fund the exit tax.
  2. Decide where the family's home will be in 2028, in writing.
  3. Model your B-BBEE scorecard with foreign ownership before committing.
  4. Agree a shareholder agreement with any minority shareholders that allows a transfer to a holding company.
  5. Write wills that choose SA law for your estate under the EU succession rules.
  6. Keep new international or digital IP clean: record where it's developed, and by whom.
  7. Use the R2m-a-year allowance for personal offshore savings, outside the structure.

The move 2028 at the earliest, and only once the family home moves

  1. Cease SA residence and pay the exit tax.
  2. Then incorporate the Cyprus company. Run from Cape Town before you leave, it would also be SA-resident, and SA would win.
  3. Settle the trust, if you're using one now.
  4. Transfer the shares, with exchange-control sign-off through your bank and SARS tax compliance status.
  5. Hold real board meetings in Cyprus.

Later when the group passes about R50m

Add a Jersey trust, or a private trust company, above the same Cyprus company. Any child who stays SA-resident is taxed in SA on what vests in them, so plan distributions with that in mind.

Questions for your advisers

  1. Will the Cyprus company pass SA's treaty anti-abuse test with the board and office you'd fund? Get a written opinion.
  2. How do Cyprus's 2026 incorporation-residence rule and the SA–Cyprus tie-break work, so nothing is incorporated before you leave?
  3. Which trust deed clauses keep the trust outside estate duty if you die while still SA-resident?
  4. Is any company in the group land-rich at any level? What is the exit tax, and how will you fund it?
  5. What is the B-BBEE scorecard before and after?
  6. How does exchange control treat an emigrant's share transfer, including under the April 2026 draft regulations?
  7. If the family moves to Greece: what's the Greek residence position, and do you meet the €100k flat-tax conditions?
  8. How are distributions to beneficiaries living in SA or Greece taxed?

Ask each provider for a fixed annual fee with a written scope, director fees per meeting attended, set-up and exit costs, asset-based fees and caps, VAT treatment, and their experience with SA treaty structures.
