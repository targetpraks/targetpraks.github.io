The short answer

  • New in version 6: you're SA-resident for now, so build in SA and move nothing offshore yet. While you live here, the Cyprus company and the trust leave you less (R55.48 of every R100 of profit at best, against R58.40 owning the shares yourself) and cost R359k–R614k a year, so none of them breaks even. Three things pay now: an SA holding company for your shares (R73 of every R100 stays working until you take it out), a will that leaves the business to your wife (nothing due at your death, against about R16.8m on a R50m group otherwise), and life cover for that bill. The detail.
  • Two legs decide what reaches you, once you've left. SA takes 27% company tax and 5% dividends tax on the way to the Cyprus company; that's the same wherever you live. Then your country of residence taxes the trust's distributions to you: nothing in Cyprus as a non-dom (only a health levy capped at €4,770 a year), nothing in the UAE, nothing if you're resident nowhere.
  • Through the trust, about R69 of every R100 of SA profit reaches you in those three cases. Owning the SA shares yourself instead: R58 if you're resident nowhere, R66 in Cyprus or the UAE. The structure's running costs (about R360k–R615k a year) come off the trust route.
  • Two places make the trust route worse. In Portugal, distributions are taxed at 28% (35% from a Jersey trust), so R45–R50 reaches you against R53 owning the shares yourself. While you're SA-resident, SA taxes the distributions again: R55 at best, against R58.
  • Greece is a flat fee. Its non-dom regime charges €100,000 a year on all foreign income, plus €20,000 per family member, so it only pays at large distributions. Whether it covers trust distributions needs a Greek opinion.
  • Estate duty is the bigger prize. Once you're non-resident, shares in a foreign holding company are outside SA estate duty: about R10.1m saved on a R50m group.
  • Leaving costs one exit tax, paid in cash. SA treats you as selling everything the day before you leave: about 18% of a founder's value, so R9m on a R50m group. Values are lowest early.
  • Move nothing offshore while you're SA-resident. If you leave: leave first, then incorporate the company and settle the trust, in that order.
  • New in version 4. Hong Kong and Malta deliver the same cash as Cyprus at a higher cost. A gift to your wife has to happen before she leaves SA and saves no income tax on the business. An IP company pays only for brands created outside SA from day one. The detail.
  • New in version 5: your friend in Cyprus. He can be a director of the Cyprus holding company and the trust's protector without a licence, and he can rent you your Cyprus home. He can't be the trustee or hold shares for you. As a director he protects the 5% treaty rate, which is worth far more than the fee he saves, but only if the board really decides in Cyprus. That starts after you leave SA. The detail.
  • New in version 5.1: diagrams. While you're SA-resident: build in SA, move nothing offshore

You'll stay South African resident for now. SA then taxes your income and gains wherever they arise, and it reaches through anything offshore: its foreign-company rules (s9D) and its trust rules (s7(8), s25B) bring the income back to you. So the Cyprus company and the trust can't save tax while you live here. Through them, R55.48 of every R100 of profit reaches you at best, against R58.40 owning the shares yourself, and the running costs come on top. None of the set-ups in this report breaks even while you're SA-resident, at any level of dividends; the break-evens further down apply only once you've left.

Three things do pay now, all of them in SA: a holding company for your shares, a will that leaves the business to your wife, and life cover for the tax due at death.

The holding company: tax only on what you take out

Move your shares into a new SA company you own, in exchange for its shares. Section 42 rolls your base cost over, so the move triggers no gains tax. It's also free of securities transfer tax (section 8(1)(a)(i) of the Securities Transfer Tax Act), which SARS confirmed for a move to a holding company in ruling BPR 408. Dividends from your companies to it are free of dividends tax (s64F(1)(a)), so profit you don't spend stays R73 of every R100, not R58.40. You pay the 20% only on what it pays you. Two conditions: keep at least 10% of it for 18 months (s42(6)), and agree the move with any minority shareholder first. One thing it can't do is lend you money cheaply instead of paying a dividend: on a loan below the official interest rate, the interest you don't pay counts as a dividend (s64E(4)).

It also makes a later move simpler, if one comes: one company to value for the exit tax, and one set of shares for a Cyprus company to hold.

Your will and life cover: the tax if you die while resident

Dying while SA-resident triggers two taxes. SA treats you as selling everything at market value, so gains tax of up to 18% applies, after an exclusion of R440,000 in the year of death (2026 Budget). That tax is a debt of the estate, and estate duty is worked out on what's left: 20% up to R30m and 25% above, after a R3.5m abatement. On a R50m group with a founder's base cost near zero, that comes to about R16.8m, a third of the group. Holding through the SA holding company, or through a Cyprus company, changes nothing while you're resident: both are still your assets.

Leaving the business to your wife defers the whole bill while she's SA-resident: the spouse roll-over removes the gains tax at your death, and section 4(q) removes the duty. It defers the tax rather than removing it: the bill, about R15.9m, lands on the second death instead, so size life cover to it. A policy on your own life is usually counted in your estate too, so have it set up for this. While you live here, the main way to cut the bill rather than defer it is to let future growth build up outside your name, for example new ventures owned from day one by an SA family trust. SARS is watching loans to trusts (s7C), so have that designed, not improvised.

Where you live decides the tax, not the passport

South Africa taxes by residence, and there are two tests. The first is ordinarily resident: where your home, family, business and belongings are, the place you naturally return to. The day count comes second: more than 91 days this year and in each of the previous five, and more than 915 days over those five years. While your wife and children live in Cape Town, the first test keeps you resident wherever you travel. SARS reviewed the years in which people stopped being resident closely through 2026. If you keep the house available to you, it counts against you in any treaty tie-break: let it at arm's length, or accept that risk.

Being resident nowhere has a cost of its own. SA's definition of a resident leaves out only someone a treaty treats as resident somewhere else. With no residence anywhere, there's no tie-breaker, and the ordinarily-resident test stands alone.

Timing

You'll stay SA-resident for now. If the family's home moves, the earliest realistic exit is the 2028 tax year. Until then, the SA moves under While you're SA-resident are the ones that pay.

Leaving costs one exit tax, paid in cash

The day before you stop being resident, SA treats you as selling everything at market value, except SA property and retirement funds. Gains are taxed at up to 18%, and a founder's base cost is near zero, so this is roughly 18% of the whole value: R9m on a R50m group. Funding it with dividends while you're still resident costs 20% more: R11.25m of dividends to net R9m. Exit early, while values are low, and plan the cash.

What 2026 changed

  • Allowances: the single discretionary allowance doubled to R2m a year; the foreign investment allowance stays at R10m a year with SARS clearance. Draft Capital Flow Management Regulations (April 2026) would replace exchange control, but aren't in force.
  • Spouses: Treasury is closing the "move assets to the non-resident spouse first" route, backdated to 25 February 2026 if the bill passes. See version 4.
  • Trusts: SARS's High Wealth Individuals unit announced a focus on donations and interest-free loans to trusts (March 2026).
  • Banks and reporting: every bank asks for your tax residences and tax numbers. "None" usually means refusal, or reporting by address or passport. Build the structure to be reported, not hidden.

How the money reaches you

Every rand you live on passes four points. SA company tax (27%) and SA dividends tax on the way to the Cyprus company (5% under the treaty) are the same wherever you live. Cyprus charges nothing on dividends to the trust, and the trust itself pays nothing in Jersey or Cyprus. The last leg, the trust's distributions to you, is taxed by your country of residence, and that is what moves the answer.

Your friend in Cyprus: director and protector, not trustee

You asked whether your friend in Cyprus could be "the trust or something" the structure needs. Cyprus licenses most of these roles, so its licensing law decides which seats he can take. The short version:

  • He can't be the trustee himself. Without a licence, a person can only be trustee of a trust he set up himself, or one whose beneficiaries are all his own family. The exceptions: he's a Cyprus lawyer or a member of the Cyprus accountants' institute, or he sits on the board of a private trust company your family owns.
  • He can be a director of the Cyprus holding company without a licence, as long as he holds fewer than ten directorships and doesn't control the board. This is the role that matters: a Cyprus-resident director who really decides is what SARS and the treaty look for, and he replaces a paid one.
  • He can be the trust's protector. He oversees the professional trustee, consents to big decisions and can replace the trustee. Free, against R29k–R49k a year for a professional.
  • He can rent you your Cyprus home for the 60-day rule, with an arm's-length lease in your name.
  • He can't hold shares for you, be the company secretary or provide the registered office. Those are licensed services. Holding shares for you would hide nothing anyway: the trust and company registers record who really owns them.

The condition: the decisions really are made in Cyprus

SARS decides where a company is managed by where its key decisions are made "in substance", not where they're signed (Interpretation Note 6, Issue 3, June 2023). It checks whether the directors have the information, the experience and the time to decide, or only "rubber-stamp" someone else's decision. It names your risk directly: a shareholder who "usurps" the board "is of particular concern in connection with passive holding companies located in low-tax jurisdictions". If both countries claim the company, the SA–Cyprus treaty gives it to the country where its place of effective management is (article 4(3)). The 2015 protocol didn't change that paragraph, and Cyprus opted out of the multilateral instrument's replacement.

Also corrected in version 5

  • Cyprus's 60-day rule lost a condition on 1 January 2026. You no longer have to be tax-resident nowhere else. Four conditions remain: at least 60 days in Cyprus; no more than 183 days in any other single country; a Cyprus business, job or directorship, held through the year; and a Cyprus home you own or rent. If SA still claims you, the treaty tie-break decides: permanent home first, then centre of vital interests.
  • A Cyprus trust has a deadline. The settlor mustn't have been Cyprus-resident in the calendar year before the trust is set up. So settle it after you leave SA and no later than your first calendar year as a Cyprus resident.

Hong Kong, Malta, a gift to your wife, an IP company

Version 4 answers three follow-up questions. The short version:

  • Hong Kong and Malta deliver the same cash as Cyprus, R69.35 of every R100, but cost more and fit worse. Malta is the fallback if Cyprus ever fails. Hong Kong only makes sense for a push into Asia.
  • A gift to your wife works only before she leaves SA, and it doesn't reduce the tax on getting profit out of the companies. It's an estate and exchange-control move, not an income-tax one.
  • An IP company pays only for new brands created outside SA from day one. Moving existing brands out loses SA's tax deduction, costs 21.6% of their value in gains tax, and needs Reserve Bank approval.

Malta

  • The famous 5% is a refund. Malta charges 35%, then refunds shareholders 6/7 of the tax on trading profits (about 5% net), 5/7 on passive interest and royalties (about 10%), and 2/3 where the company claimed double-tax relief. A holding company that only receives dividends pays 0% under the participation exemption anyway, so the refund system adds nothing for you.
  • The optional 15% final tax (Legal Notice 188 of 2025, published 2 September 2025) replaces the refunds with a flat 15%. It locks you in for five years and can't come out lower than the refund system would. It doesn't matter for dividends either.
  • Royalties lose 10% in SA on the way to Malta, against 0% to Cyprus.
  • A Malta trust (7.05, #12 of 16) is EU and off Portugal's list, but no better than a Cyprus trust. Its one use is to avoid putting both layers in Cyprus: a Malta trust above a Cyprus company is the all-EU pair. It runs at about US$8k–US$15k a year.
  • As a home: resident non-doms pay Malta tax on foreign income only when they bring it into Malta, and foreign capital gains are exempt even if brought in. There's a €5,000 minimum a year once foreign income reaches €35,000. That works only if you live on money kept outside Malta, and it needs real residence. Cyprus exempts dividends wherever you spend them, under a 60-day rule.

Hong Kong

  • Same 5% treaty rate as Cyprus. Dividends are exempt under the participation exemption for foreign income, there's no withholding when it pays out, and it has been off Portugal's list since 1 January 2026.
  • Against it: audited accounts every year, bank accounts that are hard to open for a company owned by a South African family, a six-hour time difference, and political and sanctions risk since 2020.
  • Profits tax: 8.25% on the first HK$2m and 16.5% above, and only one company in a connected group gets the lower band. The 5% patent box excludes trademarks.
  • A Hong Kong trust (6.73, #14 of 16) has had modern law since 1 December 2013: no time limit, and the settlor can keep investment powers. But its firewall covers only foreign forced heirship, not divorce claims, and it costs about as much as Singapore. Its scores: tax 8.5, legal 7.5, reputation and banking 7, cost 4, fit 4.

A gift to your wife, taken to Greece

  1. Give before she leaves SA, never after. Gifts between spouses are free of SA donations tax (s56(1)(b)). The draft 2026 tax bill limits that to a spouse who is SA tax-resident, backdated to 25 February 2026. A gift once she has left would cost 20% (25% above R30m). Treasury's memo describes exactly this sequence: "one spouse first ceases to be a South African tax resident. The remaining resident spouse subsequently transfers substantial assets". The bill isn't law yet, so plan as if it will be. SA's capital gains roll-over between spouses (s9HB) is already limited where the receiving spouse isn't resident.
  2. It doesn't cut the tax on the business profits. The money reaches you as a dividend first: R58.40 of every R100 of profit while you're SA-resident, against R69.35 through the Cyprus company and trust once you've left. A gift moves money that has already been taxed.
  3. While you're SA-resident, SA taxes you on what the gift earns. Once she's non-resident, s7(8) puts the income from it back in your hands and paragraph 72 does the same for gains. There's no purpose test and no exception for spouses. While you're both resident, s7(2) and paragraph 68 do the same if the gift's main purpose is to save tax. So the gift saves no income tax until you've left too.
  4. Give cash, not shares. Cash carries no gain, so her exit tax on it is nil. Shares would be taxed when she leaves.
  5. The real gain is exchange control. She has her own R2m discretionary allowance and R10m investment allowance each year, so together you can move R24m a year. After she stops being resident, up to R10m a year can follow with a SARS AIT PIN. Above that she needs a SARS manual letter of compliance; sources differ on whether the Reserve Bank must also approve.
  6. Greece: give before she becomes Greek-resident. Greek gift tax applies when a Greek resident receives assets held abroad. A gift made while she's SA-resident, from a donor who isn't Greek, of money outside Greece falls outside it. Once she's resident, gifts between spouses are tax-free up to €800,000 and taxed at 10% above that.
  7. Living in Greece: she's taxed on worldwide income (dividends 5%, interest and capital gains 15%), unless the non-dom regime pays: €100,000 a year flat on foreign income, for up to 15 years, after a €500,000 investment in Greece. If she's a Greek national, her assets abroad stay inside Greek inheritance tax unless she has lived abroad for 10 years in a row. The top rate for a spouse or children is 10%, against SA's 20–25% estate duty.

The catch: Greece treats as resident anyone whose permanent home, habitual abode or centre of vital interests is there, or who spends more than 183 days there in 12 months. If your wife and children live in Greece, your centre of vital interests is very likely there too. That ends "resident nowhere" and makes Greece's €100,000 flat tax (plus €20,000 per family member) your realistic regime.

The money also becomes hers. Greece's default is separation of property. On divorce, a spouse can claim part of the other's increase in wealth, presumed to be a third, but gifts and inheritances are left out of that increase. Under SA's accrual system, check your antenuptial contract. First confirm which matrimonial property regime applies to your marriage: one concluded abroad can follow SA or foreign rules, and under SA community of property a "gift" between you works differently.

Verdict: worth doing as part of the move, in this order:

  1. Dividends to you while you're resident.
  2. A cash gift to your wife while she's still resident.
  3. She uses her own allowances.
  4. She stops being resident when the family moves.

An IP company that charges the SA company

SA's section 23I decides this. "Tainted" IP is IP that the SA company paying the royalty, or a connected SA resident, owned, or developed while holding at least 20% of the IP owner. For royalties on tainted IP, SARS allows no deduction if a treaty cuts SA withholding below 10% (Cyprus 0%, Hong Kong 5%), one third at 10% (UAE, Malta) and one half at 15% (a country with no treaty, such as Jersey). Non-residents aren't "taxable persons" under the section, so IP created outside SA, by people who aren't SA-resident, keeps the full deduction.

Order of moves

While you're SA-resident for now, with no leaving date set · build in SA, move nothing offshore

  1. Move your shares into an SA holding company under section 42: no gains tax or transfer tax on the move, and dividends into it are tax-free. Agree it with any minority shareholder first, and keep at least 10% of it for 18 months.
  2. Write wills that leave the business to your wife while she's SA-resident, and that choose SA law for your estate under the EU succession rules. Take out life cover sized to the tax due at death (about R16.8m on a R50m group), set up so its payout isn't itself counted in your estate.
  3. Get an independent valuation of each company, then plan how to fund the exit tax, in case you leave.
  4. Decide, in writing, whether and when the family's home moves.
  5. Model your B-BBEE scorecard with foreign ownership before committing to a move.
  6. Agree a shareholder agreement with any minority shareholders that allows the holding-company move now, and a transfer to a Cyprus company later.
  7. Keep new international or digital IP clean: record where it's developed, and by whom.
  8. Use the R2m-a-year allowance for personal offshore savings, outside the structure.
  9. If you'll give money to your wife, give cash while she's still SA-resident, and use both sets of allowances. Never give after she has left.
  10. Agree your friend's roles in writing: director and protector, not trustee. Until you've left, he lines up a bank, a provider and an office, and takes no role.

The move if the family home moves: 2028 at the earliest

  1. Cease SA residence and pay the exit tax.
  2. Then incorporate the Cyprus company, with you and your friend as its Cyprus directors. Run from Cape Town before you leave, it would also be SA-resident, and SA would win.
  3. Settle the trust, if you're using one now, with your friend as protector. A Cyprus trust must be settled no later than your first calendar year as a Cyprus resident.
  4. Transfer the shares (with the SA holding company in place, its shares), with exchange-control sign-off through your bank and SARS tax compliance status. Securities transfer tax of 0.25% applies.
  5. Hold real board meetings in Cyprus.
  6. Put new brands, and the foreign trademark rights of existing ones, into an IP company under the Cyprus company. Leave the SA rights of existing brands in SA.

Later when the group passes about R50m

Add a Jersey trust, or a private trust company, above the same Cyprus company. Until then, "no trust yet" is legitimate: own the Cyprus company personally, with a will that chooses SA law.

Beneficiaries: any child who stays SA-resident is taxed in SA on amounts that vest in them (s25B), and a minor's income can be attributed to a parent (s7(3)). CRS reports the trust to SARS while any controlling person is SA-resident. Plan distributions with that in mind.

Questions for your advisers

  1. Now, while you're SA-resident: does moving your shares into an SA holding company qualify under section 42 and the securities transfer tax exemption, given your shareholder agreement and the 18-month rule? What will it cost to run?
  2. Your will: does leaving the business to your wife, outright or to a trust where she's the only beneficiary, get the spouse roll-over and the section 4(q) deduction? How much life cover, and held how, so its payout isn't itself counted in your estate?
  3. Could new ventures sit in an SA family trust from day one, so their growth stays outside your estate, and how would it be funded without a section 7C cost?
  4. Will the Cyprus company pass SA's treaty anti-abuse test with the board and office you'd fund? Get a written opinion.
  5. How do Cyprus's 2026 incorporation-residence rule and the SA–Cyprus tie-break work, so nothing is incorporated before you leave?
