Writing practice · Grade 3 · 10–15 minutes a prompt

12 writing prompts for Donatello

Built around what he already loves — LEGO, football, robots, nature, drums — and aimed at the one thing at a time that moves his writing: a complete sentence, then a joined sentence, then a story with a shape. One prompt a day. Ideas first, spelling second.

How to run it

  • Say it before he writes it. He talks the sentence through out loud, then writes what he said. Talking is his strong side; let it lead.
  • You hold the pen for the word bank. Write 4–6 useful words on a scrap of paper (e.g. crashed, wobbled, scattered) so his ideas aren’t slowed down by spelling.
  • Correct one thing per piece. Pick the skill on the card and leave the rest alone that day. Praise the idea first, in private, specifically.
  • If he stalls, scribe for him. He says it, you write it, he reads it back and changes one word. Handwriting and ideas don’t have to move at the same speed.
  • Finish with the check strip at the bottom — out loud, pointing at the page, not as a test.
01

Warm-up — one strong sentence

Three sentences, no more. This is where the grammar he drops out loud gets put back in: was / were, is / are, he / she / they, and the past tense.

1

The LEGO crash

Write 3 sentences about a LEGO tower that fell down. Use was, were, and crashed.

was / were + past tenseWord bank: wobbled, scattered, rebuilt, too tall.
2

The robot that helps at home

You built a robot that does one job in your house. Write 3 sentences: what it is called, what it does, who it helps. Start them It is…, It can…, It helps…

is / can + full sentencesThe starters do the grammar work — he only has to fill them.
3

The goal

Write 4 sentences about the best goal you ever scored or watched. Use he, she or they at least once, plus the words suddenly and shouted.

pronouns + detailAsk: who exactly? If he says “they”, ask which ones — then he writes it.
02

Build a paragraph — five sentences that hold together

Order, joining words, and describing for a reason. This is the step between a sentence and a story.

4

Nature detective

You find a strange creature in the garden. Write 5 sentences: where it was, what it looked like, what it did, how you felt, what you did next. Use first, then, after that.

sequencingFive fingers, five sentences — he can count them on his hand as he plans.
5

The missing drumstick

Your drumstick disappears before practice. Write 5 sentences telling the story in order. One sentence must start with Because…

cause & reasonBecause is where his “why?” brain pays for itself. Let him explain the reason out loud first.
6

My perfect treehouse

Describe your dream treehouse in 5 sentences so a builder could make it. Use above, beside, under, and two colour words.

prepositions + describingPretend you are the builder and ask “where exactly?” — that question produces the detail.
03

Story lab — six to eight sentences

His strongest ground: story memory and imagination score above his writing mechanics. Keep the shape simple — someone, a problem, a fix, an end.

7

The robot who wanted to play football

Write a short story (6–8 sentences) about a robot that tries to join your football team. Give it a name and one problem it must solve.

character · problem · solutionAsk the three questions before he writes: who, what goes wrong, how does it end?
8

Trapped in the LEGO set

You wake up inside your LEGO set. Write 6–8 sentences about the first thing you build to escape. Use I, my and me correctly.

first-person pronounsQuick check at the end: point at each I / my / me and ask “does that sound right?”
9

The night the moon went out

The moon switches off and you have one night to fix it. Write 6–8 sentences. End with a sentence that starts with In the end…

story shape + endingThe ending starter stops the story from just stopping. He can plan the last line first if he likes.
04

Real-world writing — writing that does a job

Short, purposeful, and instantly worth reading. Best saved for a day he’s reluctant — these have an audience.

10

A letter to your teacher

Write 4 sentences asking your teacher for one extra playtime. Give two reasons, and say thank you.

persuasion with reasonsHe can actually send it. A real reply is worth ten exercises.
11

Instructions: the best sandwich

Write 5 steps for making your favourite sandwich. Start each step with a bossy verb: Take…, Spread…, Cut…

imperative verbs + orderThen make it, following only his words. Every missing step is funny, not a failure.
12

My week in five sentences

Write 5 sentences about your week: one thing you loved, one thing that was hard, one thing you learned, one person who helped you, one thing you want next week.

reflection + varied startsDon’t let every sentence start with I. Five different openings is the whole win here.

Before he puts the pencil down — four checks, out loud

1. Does it make sense if I read it back? 2. Capitals at the start, full stop at the end. 3. Finger spaces between words. 4. One word I could swap for a better one. That’s it — don’t fix everything in one go.
