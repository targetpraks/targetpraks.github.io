the verdict on that name

Own it — never print it on court paper

The product sells dignity, and this name sells relief. They cannot share an address. The app produces court-ready documents, and whatever domain sits on that paperwork gets read by a judge, a mediator and the other side’s attorney — where a profane address becomes their first exhibit. App review and paid ads treat it as a risk flag too, so you would fight the platforms before you fight the case. Kept as the venting channel, the podcast, the merch line — linked from the app, never printed on a document — the name has real energy and costs R110 to hold.

07 Which one actually ranks

Short answer: divorced-dads.co.za. It is the only name in the set that carries a South African signal — and the hyphen is not a compromise, it is what Google’s own URL guidance asks for.

NameSA signalVerdict
divorced-dads.co.za Country TLD, and the two words are separated the way Google reads concepts. YesBest
divorceddads.app No country signal. Its strength is installs and trust, not search. NoNeutral
divorceddads.net No country signal, one joined word. NoNeutral
divorceddads.club No country signal, and .club carries a discount-club smell in results. NoWeak
divorceddads.dad A novelty extension. No signal at all, in either direction. NoNone
  • The country signal is documented, not folklore. — Google’s geotargeting guidance lists a country-specific domain as “clear geotargeting”, and says targeting one country “can improve your page rankings in the target country”. None of the gTLDs here can do that for South Africa.
  • The hyphen is the recommendation. — Google’s URL structure guidance says to use hyphens “to separate words in your URLs” because it “helps users and search engines better identify concepts”, and lists “joining words together in the URL” as not recommended. So divorced-dads beats divorceddads for exactly the query you want.

What it does not mean: no extension ranks a site by itself. Content, links and local citations do that work; the domain only decides which country you are competing in. So pick one canonical — the .co.za — and 301 everything else to it. Six names serving six copies of the same page is the one way to lose.

What I did not do: I registered nothing, contacted no owner and spent nothing. The .com, the .co.za, the .org and the .dad belong to other people; whether to make an approach on any of them is your call, not mine.
