The reel, stripped to mechanics

The reel is the American accounting-firm roll-up thesis: buy fragmented, low-multiple service firms one at a time, merge them under one back office, then sell the consolidated group — because the market pays a size premium, not a performance premium.

Three claims carry the whole thing, and all three are checkable:

  • The multiple gap is real and is a function of size. A solo practice trades near the bottom of the range; a platform with €1.5M+ EBITDA trades near the top. Nothing about the underlying work changes — only the size of the buyer pool.
  • It is financed with other people's money. Bank debt plus a seller note that the vendor leaves in the deal.
  • AI is the accelerant, not the thesis. It makes one back office run ten former back offices, which is what makes the arbitrage survive contact with reality.

The question worth answering is not whether it works — it is whether it is still open in the three markets you named, or already crowded.

The verdict, three lines

Crowded at the bottom
Spain

Deepest market in Europe after the UK

Multiple PE-backed consolidators, dozens of deals a year, and a size ladder that pays 3.0–4.5× for a small firm and 6.0–8.5× for a platform. The arbitrage is proven and priced in.

Mid-cycle
Portugal

One full cycle already closed

Moneris ran the exact play — 24 acquisitions in four years, PE-backed, then bought its investor out. The next generation is only now forming.

Open field in accounting
Greece

No accounting roll-up. Clinics are on fire.

Domestic accounting consolidation is essentially absent. But Greek healthcare is the hottest buy-and-build market in the region, with €800M+ tickets.

Spain — where the ladder is widest and most crowded

The Spanish market is the clearest proof the reel's numbers are real: the pricing is published, by size band, exactly as the thesis predicts.

Why Spain is structurally ripe

  • ~65,000 professional firms, 78% of them below €500k revenue, median three employees. No player holds meaningful share (Capittal).
  • Succession is the seller. 24.3% of firm owners are over 60 and only ~25% of firms say they have a succession plan — a queue of motivated sellers (CopilotGestoria, citing Wolters Kluwer's 2026 barometer).
  • Regulation is the forcing function. VeriFactu and B2B e-invoicing raise the fixed cost of staying independent, and 70% of firms still run on spreadsheets (same source, citing Cegid).

The bit that matters for a single operator

Spain is the largest search-fund market in the world outside the US and Canada: IESE's 2026 study counts 98 Spanish search funds and 56 completed acquisitions (IESE International Search Fund Center). That means the financing rails for an individual buying one boring firm already exist — bank debt plus a vendor loan of 10–20% of price, which Spanish banks treat as quasi-equity and therefore expect to see (bank financing guide; six financing routes compared).

Portugal — the play already ran once

Portugal is the more interesting market precisely because it is later in the cycle than people assume. The textbook case is Moneris: IR Capital took 42.63% in 2007 with an explicit BPO consolidation thesis — fragmented, sub-digitalised, unprofessionalised management — and Moneris went on to acquire 24 accounting firms in roughly four years. It is now the country's largest national accounting group with 18 offices and ~4,000 clients. IR Capital exited in 2017 by selling back to the existing shareholders — a buyback, not a trade sale.

Read that exit twice. The consolidation created a real business, but the liquidity event was a buyback rather than a sale at a fat multiple. Scale in Portuguese accounting buys you a bigger firm; it does not automatically buy you a bigger exit. That is the single most useful fact about Portugal.

Where the Portuguese activity is now

  • Veterinary. OneVet went from 19 to 43 units in 18 months and ~€20M revenue, partnering with UNAVETS — Oaktree-backed — which then took a majority position, making the combined group one of Iberia's leading veterinary providers (OneVet deal history).
  • Accounting, round two. Grupo Your has launched a network model that integrates accounting offices without buying them — revenue-sharing instead of M&A — which is the tell of a market where purchase prices and integration costs have stopped making the arithmetic easy.
  • The buyer class exists but is thin. Portugal has roughly ten active search funds, including Viriato Capital, Matter Capital, SLY Capital, Sobreiro Capital and Zinc Capital.

Portuguese pricing: professional B2B services trade at 4.0–7.0× EBITDA (0.6–1.2× revenue) and clinics/labs at 5.0–8.0×, with micro-firms below €800k turnover dropping to 2.5–3.5× regardless of sector (2026 sector multiple matrix).

Greece — wrong sector for the reel, right country for the same logic

Search hard for a Greek accounting roll-up and you find nothing domestic. Greek financial press covers the wave as a global story — Thrive Holdings, Current, CBIZ, Blackstone and Citrin Cooperman — and concludes that Greek firm owners will eventually face the same choice, without naming a single Greek transaction (Startupper; OT.gr). Accountancy Europe's transaction census backs that up: Greece does not appear among the eleven countries with tracked PE deals in accounting, while Spain registers nine.

But Greece is running the identical play one sector over, at a scale Iberia has not seen:

DealShapeWhy it matters
PureHealth → Hellenic Healthcare Group ~€800M, closed October 2025 16 private hospitals and 50+ multi-specialty facilities, handed over from CVC to a Gulf strategic. The end-state of a decade of Greek clinic consolidation.
Dental Holding → Memodent Abris Capital, CEE platform, 2025 Cross-border dental roll-up swallowing the Greek market leader. Exactly the reel's logic — a platform buying a national champion as an add-on.
Affidea GBL majority; 300+ centres, 15 countries The most instructive one: founded in Greece in 2005, it became Europe's largest diagnostic-imaging roll-up, then entered Spain in 2017 and Portugal since. A Greek company eating the Iberian market from the other direction.

So the honest Greek answer is: the accounting version of the reel has not been done yet, and the healthcare version is already at €800M exits. If you want the reel's economics with Greek risk, clinics and diagnostics are the market that exists; accounting is a frontier nobody has opened.

What actually decides whether it works

  • The ladder is the whole return. In Spain the size premium alone takes you from ~3.5× to ~7×. Buy a €250k-EBITDA firm and flip it unchanged, and you have bought nothing.
  • The platform, not the practice, is the product. One back office, one tech stack, one sales function. Every deal that fails to integrate is a small firm bought at 3.5× that can only be sold at 3.5× — minus fees.
  • Vendor debt is structural, not clever. In Spain a seller note of 10–20% is treated as quasi-equity and is close to mandatory for bank comfort. The reel's "no money of their own" is really "the seller and the bank carry it, and you carry the integration".
  • Retention is priced in. Spanish deals rarely pay the full price at close; part is deferred against the partner staying and the client book holding. A roll-up of owners who leave in year one is a roll-up of churn.
  • Partner dependency is the discount. The multiple falls furthest where the founder is the client relationship. That is also the cheapest thing to fix before you sell.

Where the open field is

  • Spain, upper rungs. The bottom of the ladder is a bidding war between a dozen consolidators. The €1.5M+ EBITDA platform tier is where the 6–8.5× exit lives and where fewer buyers can actually write the cheque.
  • Portugal, the consolidation of the consolidators. Moneris proved the model and exited via buyback. The next move is either a second-generation platform, or buying the small groups that the current wave is assembling.
  • Greece, accounting. Fragmented, unconsolidated, no PE precedent. Highest risk, emptiest field — and the Greek clinic market has already proved that Greek buy-and-build exits do happen at scale when a real platform is built.
