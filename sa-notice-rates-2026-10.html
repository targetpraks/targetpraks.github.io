Who pays the most on a 32-day (or longer) notice account in South Africa

Every rate below is a nominal annual rate compounded monthly (NACM) — the number the banks advertise. The effective column is what you actually earn over 12 months once interest compounds. Context: SARB repo 7.25%, prime 10.75% (effective 25 September 2026).

Short answer

7.90% NACM · ≈ 8.19% effective

Investec SmartRate Notice Deposit, 90-day notice, R100 000 minimum. That is the highest 32-day-or-longer rate on the market right now. Same account: 7.80% at 60 days and 7.75% at 32 days.

If you want exactly 32 days: Nedbank 32Day Notice pays 7.80% — but only on R500 000+, opened and managed digitally. Under R500k Nedbank drops to 7.75%, which Investec matches at R100k.

Small balances? Discovery Bank pays 7.45% at 32 days, 7.50% at 60 and 7.55% at 90 — from R1. No other bank is close at that deposit size.

What this actually means

Locking up a year pays less

A 12-month fixed deposit at Discovery 7.50% or African Bank 7.49% (R1m) earns below Investec's 90-day notice. You give up access for a year and get paid less for it.

Instant access is close behind

Standard Bank MoneyMarket Select pays 7.30% from R250k with no notice. The 32-day lock is worth roughly 0.15–0.45 points — real, but not dramatic.

R100k is the insurance line

CODI covers up to R100 000 per depositor per bank. Above that, splitting across two or three banks buys both insurance and, often, better tiered rates.

Tax eats the difference

Interest is taxable above the annual exemption (R23 800 under 65, R34 500 65+). At a 41% marginal rate, 7.90% becomes about 4.66% net — which is why the wrapper, not the headline rate, decides the outcome.
