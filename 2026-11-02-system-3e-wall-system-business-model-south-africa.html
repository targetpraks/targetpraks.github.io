SYSTEM 3E is a wall made almost entirely of expanded perlite. The blocks click together dry. No mortar bed, no glue, no water on site. A small crew can close a shell in days, not weeks. The manufacturer claims about 100 m² of wall in 24 hours; treat that as a factory claim until a South African crew repeats it.

The commercial gap is certification and supply, not demand. No South African project is listed. A builder cannot get a bond on a system the banks and insurers have not seen. The first business is therefore not a factory. It is a certification and demonstration business with a product attached.

01

Market

The market is the same R44.4bn residential build that every model in this report addresses. SYSTEM 3E’s addressable slice is narrower: premium eco homes, backyard cottages, farm buildings and small commercial work where a cash buyer can accept an uncertified system.

02

Product

  • About 99% expanded perlite. Volcanic glass is popped like popcorn, then bound into a block. The wall is light, self-insulating and fire-resistant.
  • Dry-stack click assembly. No mortar, glue or water. The blocks have a click profile; a small crew can close a shell without a wet trade.
  • Load-bearing for single-storey and low-rise homes, subject to engineering. It is a wall system, not a frame; the load path still has to be designed.
  • No listed South African project. The product works in physics; the certificate is what makes it work in a bank.

Primary sources: SYSTEM 3E product page · B2B page.

What has to be true. The product must pass a South African test, not a brochure. For SYSTEM 3E, the first proof is a certificate and eight paid projects. Everything else is arithmetic.

Verdict

Back behind a certificate

The product is good; the certificate is the business. SYSTEM 3E has the highest gross margin in the study, but it also has the longest cash cycle and the thinnest supply chain. Do not spend the plant capital until the certificate is in hand and eight projects have been paid for. If the certificate slips, the business is a demo company, not a manufacturer.

The study

The order is the answer.

Five good models, one balance sheet. The ranking is not a verdict on the products; it is a verdict on the sequence. AAC first, ICF second, SYSTEM 3E behind a certificate, perlite when the channel is proven, SIPs as a specialist arm.

Weighted scorecard. Capital 20%, gross margin 20%, speed 15%, regulation 15%, competition 15%, scalability 10%, supply 5%.

Scores are 1–5, where 5 is best. Capital: 5 means low capital intensity. Regulation: 5 means easy approval. Competition: 5 means a weak competitive field. Supply: 5 means a resilient supply chain.

The ranking is an order of operations, not a beauty contest. AAC wins because it can earn next quarter. SYSTEM 3E has the best margin and the worst timing. A portfolio that starts with SYSTEM 3E runs out of cash before it runs out of market.
01

The order

  1. 01
    AAC — Start here.

    It funds the rest. Lowest regulatory risk, lowest capital, fastest revenue, and a product that already has local approval. The first 20 projects teach you the customer, the crew and the cash cycle.

  2. 02
    ICF — Add it next.

    Same customer list, different trade. Premium homes, pools, safe rooms and small commercial. The margin is better than AAC and the certification path is shorter than SIPs. Concrete supply is the constraint.

  3. 03
    SYSTEM 3E — Certify with the cash flow.

    Do not build the plant. Use AAC and ICF cash to fund the Agrément file, one container and two demo units. The plant only after the certificate and eight paid projects.

  4. 04
    Perlite block — Build the plant later.

    The best gross margin and the best localisation story, but R7.8m before a rand and 12% utilisation to break even. It is a second-stage asset, not a first venture.

  5. 05
    SIPs — Quote it, do not build around it.

    A useful specialist arm for the client who asks for it. The lender is the real customer, the fire test is expensive and the local panel supply is thin.

Method

How the numbers were built.

Every model uses the same method so the comparison is fair. The inputs differ; the arithmetic does not. Where a number is modelled, it is marked. Where a supplier publishes a price, the link is in the sources.

Market

TAM, SAM, SOM

TAM is the annualised value of private residential building plans passed, from Stats SA P5041.1 (July 2026). The wall-system slice is 20% of TAM. SAM is a segment count × average size × model price; SOM is Stage 1 volume × model price. The segment counts are planning assumptions, not census data.

Financials

Pre-tax, pre-interest

Revenue is m² × price. Direct cost is m² × cost. Gross profit is revenue minus direct cost. EBITDA is gross profit minus opex, before depreciation, interest and tax. Capex excludes working capital.

Pricing

Ex VAT, 2026

All prices are ex VAT. Where a supplier quotes a range, the model uses the midpoint or the conservative end. The price-floor table tests the same cost base at four prices.

FX

30 October 2026

1 USD = R16.69. 1 EUR = R18.65. 1 PLN = R4.234. The SYSTEM 3E landed cost uses PLN and USD; the perlite plant uses USD. A 10% FX move changes the import models more than the local ones.
