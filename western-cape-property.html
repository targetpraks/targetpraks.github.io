03 Shark Bay

You asked about Shark Bay, by Saldanha Bay. It is 82 hectares of freehold eco-estate on 2.5 km of Langebaan lagoon frontage at the West Coast National Park gate, about 75% of it given to SANParks — and it is stricter than the brochures imply.

Building loses to buying, and now it is priced Western Cape construction runs R18 000–26 000/m² for upmarket estate work, excluding VAT, land and professional fees ( JXT, Aug 2026); a 2026 schedule puts exclusive private houses at R26 450/m² ( BVWT). The R2.6m entry plot plus 249 m² at that rate plus 15% VAT is R7.75m–R10.05m before fees and contingency — and that is a 249 m² build. Go to 270 m², or the estate's 390 m² ceiling, and it climbs further. The finished house asking R7.7m is the cheaper way to own it.

So the estate fails as an investment. Every plot scored 2.38–2.93 and every house 3.71–4.61, against finished space in the same lagoon node at R12 641–19 097/m² — Blue Lagoon, Middedorp, Myburgh Park — versus R28 500–38 300/m² inside the estate. The levy has also moved: a local guide quoted R1 500/month in 2024 against R2 220 on today's adverts.

But the lagoon pays as a hospitality product. The gate's highest score in all 107 — 6.89 — is the Shark Bay Villas two-bedroom leaseback at R2.2m, advertised at about R230 000 a year and 10.5%, with 28 days of own use. A R725 000 hotel-share (two shares, ~R76 000/year) scored 3.48. Two questions stay open and matter: whether the advertised 10.5% is gross or investor cash after fees and tax — the brochure does not say — and whether a R1 751-a-night retail rate (LekkeSlaap) supports R230 000 a year. Touch Down is the leaseback operator, not a published guarantor.

Three facts that decide the category 1. Pool units are cash-only — banks do not finance units inside a rental pool; the Colosseum listing says so outright.
2. The split is 71/29 at Century City — the owner receives 71% of rental and the hotel pays the levies from it. An Island Club owner reports ~R4 500/month net on R1.2m (4.5%) against a listing claiming 6.5% — demand three years of owner statements.
3. Levies are the trap — Taj Hotel Residences charges R11 612/month plus R4 126 rates; Pepperclub needs R35 463/month of revenue to return 5% after its carry.

Where the price per metre is lowest per rand of income — Century City at R26 000–31 250/m² against R63 000–65 700/m² in the CBD and Sea Point — the arithmetic is closest to working. Both independent research runs computed The Rockefeller's head-lease studio at 5.8% net from the same published figures; when two models land on the same number from a listing, the arithmetic is probably right.

Duration is the thing worth buying. KLEI pays 8% for three years; everything else pays 9–10% for twelve months. And the twelve-month guarantees are visible in the price: the Marlow's R1.72m entry buys about 21 m², R81 905/m² against a Newlands resale median of R63 761/m² across 16 live listings. The R172 000 guarantee sits inside a premium of roughly R381 000 — you pay about 2.2 times the guarantee in price.

The test for any guarantee Who is legally on the hook, for how many months, on what base (price, or price plus extras), and what the unit earns in month thirteen. Then price it: the guaranteed unit's R/m² against the same street's unguaranteed resale. If the gap is roughly the guarantee divided by the term, you are being paid with your own money.

09 The decision

  1. Buy the cash flow where it exists. Parow, R799 000, 9.6% net, 5.6% after tax, bondable, no operator. The only deal that pays on day one. Interrogate the R1 988 levy (3.0% of price a year) and the body corporate reserves before you offer.
  2. If the corridor is the real interest, buy land or an early phase, not a R46 000/m² apartment. Azalea Acres sells 45 plots from R1.86m; Twin Oaks is R1.679m for 64 m².
  3. Treat the beach as a use decision. Hermanus Beach Club is the only coastal property that clears the bank on letting alone — the seven-body-corporate structure is the price of that.
  4. Take KLEI's three-year guarantee to a conveyancer before KLEI's show house. Ask who guarantees it, and what the unit earns in year four.
  5. Do not buy a hotel pool unit with money you might need. No bond, a 71/29 split, and an exit that depends on the operator's balance sheet.
  6. Shark Bay: only as the leaseback, and only after proving the 10.5%. The plots contradict themselves — a 60-month build obligation, penalty levies at 8×, a 1% resale levy, and a build that costs more than the finished house next door.

10 Method, gaps and evidence

Four research legs: my own Property24 and AirROI sweeps; Codex and Claude on identical written briefs in isolated directories, blind to each other, live sources only, a URL required on every figure; then a dedicated Shark Bay round for each, and a gap-closing pass by Codex after its metered search server ran out of credit — run instead on native search plus a local curl fetcher. Candidate JSON from each leg was validated on merge: a URL that does not resolve or a price with no source was dropped. Scoring: one gate call per property, four-band rubric, weighted to a score out of ten.

Gaps, stated: 0 candidate(s) failed to score and are listed in the working files; levy and rates are missing for several entries, which moves their scores unpredictably; no long-let comparison was obtainable for the coastal towns; the Shark Bay leaseback's 10.5% basis and the HOA's levy history remain unpublished; and the Newlands comparison sets a new-build asking price against a suburb resale median, which is indicative rather than like-for-like.
