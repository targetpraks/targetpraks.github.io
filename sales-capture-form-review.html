01 · What One Brand Asks For

The KPI set is identical on every single brand

This is the first thing the form tells you, and it is the important one. All twelve brands carry exactly the same KPI rows — nine for the SDR, seven for the Deal Maker, nine for Marketing. There is no lighter version for a service brand and no heavier one for a pipeline brand. A franchisee SLA is measured against the same numbers as a brand-new cold market.

Twenty-five rows per brand, and every brand carries all 25. Multiply that by the brands each person owns and the shape of the problem appears immediately.

Two things the form does not capture

CRM hygiene is not a row. The form asks for nine SDR KPIs and seven Deal Maker KPIs, and daily record hygiene — which is the thing that makes the rest of it true — is not one of them. It has to happen; it is simply not measured or credited.

Travel is not a row either. A closer who drives to site loses 30 minutes a trip and the form has nowhere to put it. The person doing the most driving appears identical to the person doing none.

The form is asking two people for four months of work

1,009 hours of target across five people who have 840 hours between them — and 595 hours of work that actually needs doing.

The gap is not effort. It is the same hunting target counted once per brand: 160 cold calls on INF Consultancy, 160 more on each of the four SLAs, 160 more on each FND brand. Olwethu is being asked for 1,280 cold calls a month because the form prices the outbound separately on every brand he owns.

04 · What Has To Change on the Form

Six edits, in priority order
#ChangeWhyEffect
1Give the SLA brands their own KPI set. Replace the hunting rows on the four SLA brands with account-management rows: client meetings held, renewal rate, upsell value, account health, response time. An SLA is sold to franchisees already in the network. There is no cold list to call, yet each SLA currently carries 160 calls, 80 emails, 40 LinkedIn touches and 20 discovery calls. −96.8 h
2Pool the outbound rows. One group-level row for calls, emails and LinkedIn — hunted once across the book, not repeated per brand. The same prospect list is being worked on every brand row Olwethu owns. −69.2 h
3Add CRM hygiene as a real row on both the SDR and Deal Maker sets. It is required daily and currently measured nowhere, so it competes invisibly with every other target. visibility
4Add a travel row per book — 30 minutes a trip, costed only where it happens. The closer who drives to site looks identical on this form to the one who does not. visibility
5Delete the three dead rows: FluxFlow, Papa Pasta, and resolve CLI – KPD's missing owner or remove its target rows. Three brands carry full target sets — one for a product that is no longer sold, one not launched, one with Owner = "None". clean totals
6Cap brands per person at 2–3 pipeline books, with SLA accounts held separately. No single brand is unreasonable. Eight of them on one desk is. structural

The good news on the form itself

The form is well built — it is just pointed at the wrong job

The capture discipline is already designed in. Every row asks for a weekly number and the proof link beside it, and the sheet states the rule outright: "No proof = the KPI does not count." That is exactly right, and it is why the Marketing side scores 100% while everything else stalls.

The Marketing rows are being captured properly. Static posts, reels, stories, blogs, campaigns and the monthly report all carry a target and get filled — that side of the business runs on this form and it works.

One thing to reconcile: INF SLA – Finance has a different Marketing owner from the other eleven brands (Ena Dizon vs Ena Olivares). Worth confirming that is intended and not a copy-paste carry-over from an older block.

05 · The Two Enas

And a note on what the form reveals about Marketing

Marketing carries the least risk on this form — the targets are modest, the rows are always filled, and proof is always attached. But the target mix differs per brand in a way that is worth a look, because it changes the volume of work materially.

The Marketing owner field is worth tidying at the same time: eleven brands point at one person and one points elsewhere, and the same name is written two different ways across the sheet.

The one-line summary

The form is doing its job — it is capturing, it is asking for proof, and Marketing is fully compliant on it. What it is not doing is describing the work accurately: it prices a franchisee SLA like a cold market, it counts the same outbound campaign once per brand, and it leaves out the two things that actually consume the day — hygiene and travel. Fix those and the same form becomes a fair scorecard instead of an impossible one.
