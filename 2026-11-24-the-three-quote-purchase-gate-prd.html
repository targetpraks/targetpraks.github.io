2. The problem

Approvals on purchase orders are already switched on in this org — but they are not Ricardo's, and they carry no quotation evidence.

Correction to the first edition of this PRD. It said no approval rule existed. That was wrong, and the error is worth recording: GET /inventory/v1/settings/approvals?entity_type=purchaseorder lists only custom approvals, and that list is empty — but the module's Multi-Level flow is live elsewhere. Verified on 2026-11-24:

  • Submitting draft PO-00085 produced status: pending_approval with approvers_list = Anne Fourie (level 1) → Jaques Mulder (level 2) and can_send_in_mail: false.
  • Recently issued POs (PO-00088, PO-00089) carry approval paths with Jaques Mulder's and Anne Fourie's decisions.

So the real gap is not "no gate". It is:

  1. The gate signs off a PO with no requirement that three quotes were obtained or attached.
  2. The approvers are the admin and CAO chain, not the person buying; nobody is looking at the quotes.
  3. There is no place on the purchase order to record the three quotes, so the approval decision has nothing to stand on.
  • A custom module cannot fix that. Custom modules are UI-only: /inventory/v1/custommodules → 404 Invalid URL Passed. Nothing inside a custom module can block a purchase order, count records, or be read by an automation.
  • Custom fields on the purchase order cannot be created through the API either — POST /settings/customfields returns 405, PATCH returns 501. That part is a UI step.

So the gate lives on the purchase order; the three quotes are the evidence the approver checks at that gate.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. No purchase order reaches a supplier until a named person has approved it.
  2. The three quotes are on the record before approval — comparable, attributable, and readable a year later.
  3. The click count for a buyer stays low: one approval step per purchase, then normal work.

Non-goals

  • Machine-counting "three". Zoho Inventory cannot count attachments or child records on a transaction; see §5.
  • Per-vendor or per-category rule sets. The gate applies to all purchase orders.
  • Any change to vendors, items, prices or accounts.

4.1 The gate — what already exists, and the one choice left

Purchase order approval is already on in this org. What is not decided is who signs.

Option What it means Consequence
A. Keep the existing chain — Anne Fourie (level 1) → Jaques Mulder (level 2) Three quotes are added to the PO as evidence; the same people sign Smallest change, no policy shift, but the approvers are not looking at the purchase
B. Switch the module to Custom Approval — the staged rule Three-quote purchase gate (id 2270401000010838744, criteria total > 0) puts Ricardo at level 1 That rule already exists and was created over the API Buying waits on Ricardo; Anne and Jaques drop out of the loop

The switch between Multi-Level and Custom Approval is UI-only: the API creates and edits custom rules (POST /settings/approvals → 201), but it cannot make a rule the default (is_default comes back false on every write) or change the module's approval type. So Option B is one radio button in Settings → Purchases → Purchase Orders → Approvals, and it is Ricardo's call, because it moves who can authorise money.

Whichever option is chosen, the gate behaves the same: send, receive, convert to bill are unavailable until approved. Verified: can_send_in_mail: false on a pending PO.

4.2 The evidence — three quotes on the PO

Two parts, both on the purchase order itself:

a. Attachments. The buyer attaches the three supplier PDFs to the PO before submitting. All three are visible to the approver on the same screen, and stay on the record.

b. Three quote fields. Add three custom fields to the Purchase Order form:

Field Type Purpose
Quote 1 — supplier and amount Text e.g. Acme — R 12,450 ex VAT
Quote 2 — supplier and amount Text
Quote 3 — supplier and amount Text
Selected quote Text Which one, and one line of why
Exemption reason Text Empty for a normal buy; see §4.4

These fields make the three-quote condition visible on the record the approver is already looking at, and they are what an auditor reads after the attachments have been buried.

4.3 The optional register (phase 2)

A Custom Module "Quotation Register" — one record per quote, with supplier, amount, currency, validity date, PO number and the PDF — gives a searchable quote history and a monthly view of what was compared against what.

It is not the gate, and should not be sold as one: it cannot block a PO, and nothing can read it from outside the UI. If you want it, it comes after the gate is live, and it is loaded by the buyer as a second step. My recommendation: skip it for phase 1 — the PO already carries the three quotes, and a register nobody fills is worse than no register.

4.4 Exceptions that keep the rule usable

Case Handling Recorded as
Urgent breakdown / machine down Buy first, submit the PO for approval the same day with the three quotes or a written reason Exemption reason filled
Sole supplier (OEM part, licence, regulated item) One quote, written reason Exemption reason filled
Repeat order inside an existing contract or price list No re-quoting; attach the contract or the last quote Exemption reason = "contract / price list"
Petty purchase under a floor you set (suggest R1,000) No approval needed Out of scope of the rule

The exception field is what makes this survivable: without it, staff invent workarounds; with it, exceptions are visible and countable at month end.

4.5 Why not the custom module as the gate

Because it cannot do the job, and the PRD should say so plainly:

  • Custom modules have no API (verified 404), so no rule, function or report outside the app can read them.
  • Custom modules cannot be required on a purchase order — nothing links the two.
  • Approvals on a custom module only gate that module's records, not the buying that follows.

A module can hold the paperwork. Only the purchase order approval can stop the purchase.

The honest sentence: the system guarantees nothing is sent without an approval; it makes three quotes with PDFs the condition of that approval, checked by a person, on evidence sitting on the same screen. That is the ceiling inside Zoho Inventory.

6. Daily flow

  1. Buyer gathers three quotes.
  2. Buyer raises the purchase order as they do today.
  3. Buyer attaches the three PDFs, fills the three quote fields and the selected quote.
  4. Save and Submit.
  5. Approver opens the PO, sees three attachments and three quote lines, approves or rejects with a reason.
  6. Approved → buyer sends the PO, receives stock, bills. No further gate on that purchase.

Rejected → the buyer edits and resubmits; every attempt stays in the approval history.

9. Acceptance tests

  1. Raise a PO with no attachments and empty quote fields → submit → it still reaches the approver (expected: fields are advisory), and the approver sees the gaps on screen.
  2. Raise a complete PO with three PDFs and three quote lines → Save and Submit → appears in the approver's Pending Approvals, email arrives.
  3. While unapproved: Send, Receive and Convert to Bill are unavailable.
  4. Approve → Send becomes available; approval history shows approver and timestamp.
  5. Reject with a reason → buyer can edit and resubmit; both attempts visible.
  6. Buyer tries to approve their own PO → refused ("allow approvers to approve their own transactions" off).
  7. A second admin checks that Final Approve exists and is understood as an exception.
  8. After approval, the buyer completes send → receive → bill with no further approval prompts.

How this was done

  • The approval rule and its criteria: Zoho Inventory API (POST/PUT /settings/approvals), read back after every write.
  • The approval type and the five fields: Zoho Inventory's own settings UI, driven in Dia through AppleScript execute javascript (the desktop AX lane was refused by the browser-consent gate; the browser profile refused real-profile attach because Dia is the default browser).
  • Test record: PO-00085 (ZZTEST-Handoff, R1.00) submitted twice to prove routing, then rejected both times so it never sat in an approver's queue. Final state rejected; it is a test record and may be deleted by Ricardo, but nothing has been deleted.

Still open, and deliberately not done

  • Bills are not gated. A vendor bill entered without a PO still never touches PO approval. Phase 2 decision, §8.
  • No petty-purchase floor, no value threshold, no second level: every purchase order goes to Ricardo. Adding a level 2 above a value is a two-minute change to the same rule.
  • The approver may approve a PO they created themselves; Zoho's "allow approvers to approve their own transactions" preference is untouched. Only matters for purchases Ricardo raises himself.
