01

What I got wrong

“I didn't reject it all — I asked for it not to be dangerous.”

Fair. I heard dangerous and answered unattractive. Then I built a whole second document on the assumption that the only alternative to a Cape Flats flat was a Sea Point one — and that framing was mine, not yours.

It also produced a wrong conclusion. My good-area median was 3.85% net against a 5.31% after-tax bond, and I read that as “residential loses to the bank.” But a median across fourteen suburbs is not the same thing as a well-chosen unit in an ordinary, safe suburb. Those are two different claims and I conflated them.

So here is the filter you actually asked for, applied properly: is the area dangerous, and does the deal still beat the bank? Both questions have real data behind them. Only one of them had been answered.

02

The measure that matters

SAPS publishes crime by police precinct. The tempting column is total reported crime — and it is close to useless for personal safety, because a dense shopping strip generates theft and commercial crime all day long without being dangerous to live in.

Sea Point proves it. It ranks 33rd of 63 on total crime (4,727 per 100,000 — grade F) but its violent-contact rate is 381, which is the 15th safest in the metro. Meanwhile Parow looks milder on paper and carries 1320 violent crimes per 100,000 — 2.3 times Sea Point's.

So the test is violent contact crime per 100,000 residents. The Cape Town median across all 63 precincts is 1 224. A deal is “safe enough” here if its precinct sits at or below that line — a mid-metro suburb, not a fortress, but not a gamble either.

The market prices safety almost exactly to the point where it stops paying. You are not choosing between safe and profitable; you are choosing how much of a 5.31% certainty you want to give up for a number that might be 7.20%.

Commercial and listed: the safety filter barely applies

This is the part that changes the shape of the answer. Personal-safety risk is mostly about being there — viewings, tenant meetings, midnight repairs. You do not live in a commercial unit, a managing agent runs the site, and the tenant signs a three-to-five-year lease and pays the rates, insurance and repairs.

07

What I would do

If you want one brick, make it Brackenfell. 7.20% net, 426 violent crimes per 100,000, +1.89% over the bank after tax. It is the only deal on the list that clears both tests with room on both sides.

If you want the yield without the worry, buy the listed version. Fill the TFSA first — R46 000 a year, tax-free — then the REIT sector at 7.09%. No precinct, no tenant, no 2am phone call.

If you want commercial, the entry ticket is R1 850 000. The cheapest credible unit in the two nodes is 100 m² at R18 500/m² in Woodstock. Below that size the stock is a parking bay with a storeroom attached.

What I would not do: pay Sea Point prices for a 3.68% net yield in a precinct that is safe but not free, or take Fisantekraal's 9.90% on a suburb whose crime cannot even be separated from its precinct's. Unmeasurable is a reason to look harder, not a reason to assume the best.

Nothing here is advice on your money. The bank rate is the only number on this page that is guaranteed — everything else is a risk you carry for a price, and the prices move.
