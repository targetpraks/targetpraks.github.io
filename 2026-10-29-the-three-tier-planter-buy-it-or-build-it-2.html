The referenceWhat we are actually comparing

The photo shows a free-standing staircase planter: three boxes stepping back, roughly 1.2 m long, 0.9 m deep and 0.9 m high, each wall three 100 mm planks high, lifted on small feet so it can stand on paving. Every build figure below is sized to that. Nothing in the local chains matches it exactly — the cheap local units are either low raised beds or two-tier stands — so the buying column is “closest thing at each price”, not the same product.

Option twoBuild it

We would get a heavier, taller planter in exactly the size we want, in treated timber that will outlast anything on the buying list. The catch is the timber bill: treated decking board is about R120 for a 3 m length, and this build needs fifteen of them before a single screw.

Cut list — 1.2 m wide, 0.9 m deep, 0.9 m high

  • 27 boards at 1.2 m — the long walls and the box bottoms
  • 18 boards at 0.35 m — the box ends
  • 12 corner posts at 0.35 m — 38 × 38 mm batten
  • That is 38.7 m of 22 × 102 board, which packs into 15 lengths of 3 m plus two 2.4 m posts

One thing worth knowing before we use treated pine for vegetables. CCA-treated timber is the cheap, rot-proof option and it is what the decking boards are, but extension services recommend a heavy plastic liner between treated timber and soil in a food bed — which is why the liner is in the bill. If we would rather avoid treated wood altogether, untreated saligna is the local alternative at roughly double the timber cost, or we line this one properly and accept a 10–15 year life.

Option threeWhat Dixon adds

The job is 45 cuts, pre-drilling, three boxes assembled, then screwed together and set on feet. Four to six hours for anyone comfortable with a drill and a saw — half a day to a full day. Casual garden labour in Cape Town runs about R300–450 a day; salaried gardeners average R6 237 a month, roughly R290 a working day.

We have used R400 for a full day in every total above. If Dixon’s rate is different, the build numbers move by that difference and nothing else does.

What we would do

If it is going on the brick paving like the photo: buy the Dawnyard at R1 404. It is the only ready-made unit that is tall, stepped and free-standing, and it costs R1 500 less than building the same thing.

If it is going on soil in the garden: the Botanical Cascade at R925 is the cheapest thing that does the job — three tiers, free delivery, no assembly beyond screwing the flat-pack together.

If we want the exact 1.2 m size, thicker boards, and something that will still be standing in fifteen years: build it. About R2 950 with Dixon, plus soil. That is the only route where we are paying for quality rather than saving money.
