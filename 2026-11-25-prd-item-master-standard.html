2. The problem

The item master had grown by import. It worked well enough to sell from, but it could not be reported on or trusted:

  1. The SKU said nothing reliable. 101 of 748 tracked items failed the prefix rule: 60 on an unregistered FRA-, 14 on CON-, 8 on MEN-, one on SUN-, 16 with no prefix and 14 with no SKU at all. Two brands in the group had no items.
  2. Category was invisible in the code. A SKU like EXP-PEN-B did not say whether the item was stationery or promotional, so nobody could slice the catalogue from the code alone.
  3. Duplicates existed in parallel. 26 golf shirts and hoodies existed twice — an older AC/DC Express … group at a 2% markup and a newer Express … group at 12% — and 9 of those pairs were both live on the storefront.
  4. Price lists were empty or opaque. The brand lists were percentage multipliers and stored no items at all; on the surface that reads as "no catalogue".
  5. Zero-rate noise. Ten active items carried no rate — which turned out to be charge lines, not products (§7.4).

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. Every item you can trade today carries a SKU that states brand, category and variant, and no two items share a SKU.
  2. Every rename is reversible and leaves the item's history intact — same item id, rate, stock and documents.
  3. Each brand's price list can be read as a catalogue: a named subset of items with a price each.
  4. Nothing is deleted. Retiring is a status change; every record keeps its history.

Non-goals

  • No storefront work, no Creator, no Flow, no external app.
  • No renaming of SKUs on retired items — a SKU is immutable once documents reference it.
  • No change to the 20% brand markup rule.

Rules, in priority order

  1. Words are never rewritten. A rename may change case, separators and the prefix, and may insert a code. It may not abbreviate, merge, split or invent a token — verified mechanically: strip EXP- and the inserted code from all 618 full-pass renames and the token list matched the old SKU exactly (0 differences).
  2. One brand per item. Prefix and the item's brand field must agree.
  3. A variant is an item. Zoho Inventory models size and colour as an item group with up to three attributes; each combination is a full item with its own SKU, rate and stock. The SKU therefore lives on the variant, never on the group (EXP-UNI-MSHIRT-S, not "Mens Golf Shirt").
  4. Retired items keep their SKU. The rule applies to items you can still trade: tracked, active, stock. scripts/check_sku_prefix.py enforces exactly that scope.
  5. Services carry no brand prefix. Charge lines (Delivery Fee, SALES: SIMPLEPAY, cost recoveries) are not stock and are excluded.

How the writes were done, and why it matters. Every rename is a single PUT carrying only the sku field (plus category_id on the 18 items that had no category at all). Measured first: a sku-only PUT leaves name, rate, purchase rate, category, unit, status, stock and tax flags untouched. Every run snapshots the org before the first write and compares the whole org after the last one.

Zoho rate-limits the whole organisation at roughly 100 requests a minute (429 code 43); a 618-PUT burst tripped it and cost about half an hour. The writer now paces at 0.5 requests/second with backoff and resumes idempotently from wherever it stopped.

7. Decisions, and the evidence behind them

7.1 A price list can only hold a subset if it is per_item

Measured on throwaway lists (scripts/probe_pricebook_subset.py): a fixed_percentage list ignores the items you send it — it stored none and still priced items that were never added (45.00 → 54.00). A per_item list stores each item with its own pricebook_rate, and returns an empty rate for items not on it. A per-item list also requires currency_id, rejects a missing rounding_type (yet stores no_rounding), and refuses inactive items (400 code 2006).

7.2 The brand retail lists stay multipliers

Converting them to per-item would strip the 20% from every item not explicitly listed — roughly 780 items. The subset requirement is met with separate per-item lists instead.

7.3 "Merge" means keep one and retire the other

Zoho Inventory has no item-record merge: no API call and no UI action folds two items into one. The merge done here keeps the canonical EXP-UNI record, carries across anything only the duplicate had, and sets the duplicate inactive. Item ids, rates, stock and documents are untouched, so it reverses with one status change per item.

The duplicates were not cosmetic: the retired set was an older AC/DC Express … group at a 2% markup (golf shirt R153.25 on R149.95) against 12% on the survivor (R163.25 on R145.95). Nine pairs were listed on the shop twice; retiring the duplicate cleared the second listing, and the survivor was already listed in every case.

7.4 The ten zero-rate items were not a defect

Delivery Fee, SALES: SIMPLEPAY, SALES: SLA SERVICES …, cost recoveries, SALES: ZOHO, bakkie usage and disposals are charge lines priced per transaction. Setting a rate would have been wrong, so nothing changed.

7.5 Two service lines stay tracked

Pad printing and Setup Fees cannot be untracked — Zoho refuses an item-type change on items with transactions (400 code 2034). They are retired and inactive, so they sit outside the rule rather than having their history broken.

10. Risks and rollback

Risk Mitigation
A rename breaks a feed or storefront that keys on SKU Every map carries both sides; the inverse map restores the previous codes in one paced run. Item ids never change
A retired record was the one a document references Retiring is a status change only — the record, its history and its documents stay
Zoho throttles a long run again Writer paces at 0.5 req/s with backoff and is idempotent; a run can be stopped and resumed without duplicating work
A future catalogue edit collides with an existing SKU plan_sku_standard.py refuses to write a plan containing a collision
