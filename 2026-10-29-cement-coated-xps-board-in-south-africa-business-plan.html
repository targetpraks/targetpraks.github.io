Cement-coated XPS board in South Africa: business plan

Prepared for Julie and Lily. Edition 1, 2026-10-29.

The one-page version. We import a waterproof, cement-coated XPS board from China at R114–123 a square metre landed, sell it at R170 to the trade and R280–330 retail, and build a South African brand around the one job where it beats everything else: the wet area. Break-even is about 10,400 m² a year, which is a little over three containers. The base case reaches that in year two and earns R2.0m EBITDA by year three on R7.8m of sales. We need R3.0m to start, most of which is stock and vehicles rather than overhead.

This is not a bet on a clever material. It is a distribution business with a superior product in a market that currently has almost none of it, and the numbers are small enough to test before anyone commits the full amount.

1. The opportunity

South Africa builds in brick. Bricks cost about R2.20 each, plastering R80–150/m², and a bricklayer R165–330/m². That is why a plain wall cannot be won on price.

Wet areas are different. A bathroom wall has to be plastered, then waterproofed with a liquid membrane, then screeded, then tiled — three trades and three to five days of drying, with a leak risk that sits in a painted layer. The board replaces all of that with one product that is waterproof through its core, tiles the next day, and carries an R-value.

There is also a gap in the market. The big retailers — Builders, CTM, Tile Africa — list fibre cement and gypsum but no XPS backer board. The European brands that do exist here are imported at two to four times our landed cost. The Greek brand that started this research is not in South Africa at all.

2. The product

A 20 mm (up to 102 mm) extruded polystyrene core, glass-fibre mesh, and a polymer-modified cement face on both sides. It cuts with a knife, fixes with washers and adhesive, takes tile, stone, plaster or paint, and weighs about 6 kg/m² against 20 kg/m² for cement board.

What it replaces On which jobs Why the customer switches
Plaster, membrane, screed, then tile Bathrooms, showers, wet rooms No drying, no membrane, tile next day
Cement board plus membrane Commercial kitchens, ablutions Lighter, faster, genuinely waterproof
Shutterply or MDF Counters, vanities, bar fronts Does not swell, shapes and tiles
Screed build-up Floor levelling, underfloor heating 20 mm instead of 60 mm, no curing

How we choose. Request quotes from all five for the same basket: 10 mm, 20 mm and 50 mm at 1200 × 600 and 2400 × 600, two containers a year, our own packaging. Then:

  1. Samples from three, tested in the same lab: density at or above 35 kg/m³, face adhesion after 24 hours of water, compression at 300 kPa, water absorption under 0.5%.
  2. A fire certificate that names the factory, not a trading company. Class E is what the product is; any claim of better needs a report with the factory's name on it.
  3. A batch test report with the shipment, not once a year.
  4. Third-party pre-shipment inspection (SGS or Bureau Veritas) on the first three containers.
  5. The landed cost per m², not the FOB price — packaging volume decides the freight, and a cheaper board packed badly can land dearer.

On price. Alibaba quotes run from $2.00 to $4.50 a board for a 20 mm 1200 × 600 panel. The honest position is that the cheapest quote is usually a trading company, not a factory, and the cheapest factory is usually not the one with the certificate. The target is not the lowest price; it is the lowest landed price from a factory whose certificate will survive a South African customer's question.
