01The one-page version

We import a waterproof, cement-coated XPS board from China at R114–123/m² landed, sell it at R170 to the trade and R280–330 retail, and build a South African brand around the one job where it beats everything else: the wet area.

Break-even is about 10,400 m² a year, a little over three containers. The base case reaches that in year two and earns R2.0m EBITDA by year three on R7.8m of sales. We need R3.0m, most of it stock and vehicles rather than overhead.

This is not a bet on a clever material. It is a distribution business with a better product in a market that currently has almost none of it — and the numbers are small enough to be tested before anyone commits the full amount.

02The opportunity

South Africa builds in brick. Bricks cost about R2.20 each, plastering R80–150/m², and a bricklayer R165–330/m². That is why a plain wall cannot be won on price, and why the first version of this research was too gloomy: it compared the board with a wall it was never going to beat.

Wet areas are different. A bathroom wall is plastered, waterproofed with a liquid membrane, screeded and tiled — three trades and three to five days of drying, with the leak risk sitting in a painted layer. The board replaces all of it with one product that is waterproof through its core, tiles the next day, and carries an insulation value.

The gap is real. Builders, CTM and Tile Africa list fibre cement and gypsum but no XPS backer board. The European brands that are here cost two to four times our landed price. The Greek brand that started this research is not in South Africa at all.

03The product

A 20 mm extruded polystyrene core, glass-fibre mesh, and a polymer-modified cement face on both sides. It cuts with a knife, fixes with washers and adhesive, takes tile, stone, plaster or paint, and weighs about 6 kg/m² against 20 kg/m² for cement board.

05The Chinese suppliers

Five factories are worth a request for quotation, ranked on what a buyer can check from outside: how long they have made this product, whether they extrude their own foam, who they already supply, and what they certify.

DRYBASE, and Banarta

DRYBASE does custom sizes, OEM packaging and private label across a full shower range. Banarta, in Nanjing since 2018, runs 6–50 mm cement-coated board and sells through samples — likely the sharpest price, on the thinnest track record.

Private label and price testing

How we choose

One request for quotation to all five, for the same basket: 10, 20 and 50 mm at 1200 × 600 and 2400 × 600, two containers a year, our own packaging. Then samples from three, tested identically: density at or above 35 kg/m³, face adhesion after 24 hours of water, compression at 300 kPa, water absorption under 0.5%. A fire certificate that names the factory rather than a trading company. A batch test report with every shipment. Third-party pre-shipment inspection on the first three containers.

On price. Alibaba quotes run from $2.00 to $4.50 a board. The cheapest quote is usually a trading company; the cheapest factory is usually not the one with the certificate. We are not buying the lowest FOB price — we are buying the lowest landed price from a factory whose certificate survives a South African customer's question.

13What we are asking Julie and Lily for

R3.0m, in two tranches: R1.6m at the start, the balance when month six shows 4,000 m² sold and the first merchant reorder. In exchange, a shareholding to be agreed, a board seat, and a monthly one-page report on volume, margin, cash and stock.

And the question we would want answered before investing: is the conservative case survivable, and is the downside capped? The phase-0 option exists so that it is.
