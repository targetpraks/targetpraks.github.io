01The proposition

South Africa builds in brick, and brick is cheap: R2.20 a brick, R80–150 a square metre to plaster, R165–330 to lay. Nothing imported will beat that on a dry wall, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

A bathroom wall is a different animal. Today it is plastered, painted with a liquid membrane, screeded and then tiled — three trades and three to five days of drying, with the leak risk living in a painted layer that fails quietly behind the tile. Our board replaces all of it with one panel that is waterproof through its core, takes tile the next day, and insulates.

What we sell

One system, not one sheet

Board, washers, sealing tape, adhesive, a pre-formed niche and a tiled shower tray, with a half-day course for the tiler who installs it. The system is what earns 46% — the sheet alone is a commodity.

Why now

The shelf is empty

Builders, CTM and Tile Africa list fibre cement and gypsum, not XPS backer board. The European brands that are here cost two to four times our landed price, and the Greek brand that started this research does not sell in South Africa at all.

What we are not claiming

No factory has been contacted and no quote has been requested yet. The prices here are published listing data and our own landed-cost model. The two large demand numbers are estimates with the arithmetic shown in section 3, and belong in a paid survey before anyone commits the full R3.0m.

02The product, in ninety seconds

A 20 mm extruded polystyrene core, glass-fibre mesh, and a polymer-modified cement face on both sides. It cuts with a knife, fixes with washers and adhesive, and weighs about 6 kg/m² against 20 kg/m² for cement board.

The trap we are avoiding

Cheapest FOB is usually a trader

Alibaba quotes run from $2.00 to $4.50 a board. The cheapest price rarely comes from the factory with the certificate — so we buy the lowest landed cost from a factory whose paperwork survives a South African customer's question.

the listing range →

How we choose

One basket, five quotes10, 20 and 50 mm at 1200 × 600 and 2400 × 600, two containers a year, our own packaging.
Samples from three, tested identicallyDensity 35 kg/m³ or better, face adhesion after 24 hours of water, compression at 300 kPa, absorption under 0.5%.
A fire certificate that names the factoryNot a trading company. Class E is what the product is; any better claim needs a report with the plant's name on it.
Inspection on the first three containersSGS or Bureau Veritas, then a batch report with every shipment.

The ask: R3.0m in two tranches. R1.6m at the start; the balance when month six shows 4,000 m² sold and the first merchant reorder. In exchange, a shareholding to be agreed, a board seat, and a monthly one-page report on volume, margin, cash and stock.

Or start smaller. R1.6m buys one container, a van, samples and marketing, with no warehouse — a third-party logistics yard does the same job — and no certification. That tests the trade channel in six months before the rest is called.

The question we would ask in your chair

Is the conservative case survivable, and is the downside capped? That is why the phase-0 option exists: it can lose R150,000 and still answer the question, instead of losing R3.0m to find out.
