The verdict, in plain terms

  • As a worker — the thing that runs in a loop, calls tools and comes back — MiniMax-M3.1-Flash wins clearly. It holds 28.8 on long-horizon agentic work against Step's 19.4, and 80.6 vs 24.8 on browser/tool tasks. That is not a rounding difference.
  • For one-shot "write me this function", they are effectively tied. Step 5 is 1.7 points better on LiveBench coding and about twice as fast when the answer is short — 23 s vs 66 s on merge_intervals.
  • Step 5 has a hard failure mode: long specs. It thinks itself into the token cap and returns nothing, and past ~5,000 output tokens NVIDIA's gateway kills the request at 303 s. A worker that silently returns empty code on the fourth turn is worse than a slower worker.
  • MiniMax's weakness is verbosity at speed. 126 s for the parser task, and it needed 1,625 tokens where a human would use 300. Fine for background work, too slow for interactive completion.

If you wire it in as a worker — two operational facts

  • One job at a time. Ten concurrent calls to minimaxai/minimax-m3.1-flash returned nothing usable; three sequential calls then came back {"status":429,"title":"Too Many Requests"}. The free pool is per-account throttled — Nemotron 3.5 Lightning kept answering on the same key while MiniMax was throttled, so it is per-model pressure, not a broken account.
  • Cap the output or lose the job. Step 5 needs max_tokens ≤ 3,000 to stay inside the gateway's 300-second window; anything larger 504s. MiniMax finished every task under 1,700 tokens.
