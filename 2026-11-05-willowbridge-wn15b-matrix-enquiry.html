Why it is written this way

The moveWhat it buys you
It names WN15B in the subject lineA manager forwards a specific bay question to the right person in ten seconds. A general "space available?" email gets parked.
It asks about the lease position, not the tenant's turnoverYou get an answer instead of a wall. And you are not asking anyone to break a confidence about a competitor's business.
"If it becomes available, keep my details"Turns a no into a place in the queue. Landlords do remember the operator who asked before the bay emptied.
It asks which route to take — centre or BrightwaveStops you running the same conversation twice, and it flatters the person who owns the process.
No rate and no budgetThe first number spoken becomes the anchor. You ask for their number; you do not offer yours.
Concept note attached, one page, no financialsEnough to be taken seriously, not enough to be negotiated against before you have seen the kitchen.

The other route: ask the chain, not the landlord

There is a second way to find out what is happening at that bay, and it is worth knowing about even though I would not use it first.

Head office can tell you something the landlord cannot: whether the branch is closing, relocating or renewing. But the call has a price. A chain that hears an operator is asking about its site becomes a tenant that digs in, and it may well mention the call to the landlord — which turns your quiet enquiry into a negotiation between two parties who both now know your hand. Use it only if the landlord route stalls, make it a general question about the Cape Town footprint rather than that one bay, and do not run it from the same week as the email.
