Verdict first

This is a strong plan built on one fatally wrong number and three unresolved contradictions that sit directly under a PIP trigger. It is not ready to build. The architecture survives; the feasibility claims do not.

1. Structurally right — protect these from tidying

  • The two-axis spine (Brand × BU). The evidence proves the axes are orthogonal (BND1 has 23 of 33 KRs under INF), and the ALL × BND1 rule against double-counting is exactly correct. This is the intellectual core; any "simplification" back to one axis re-breaks the merge.
  • Rule of one writer, and no computed number in ClickUp. The template's three competing progress fields (Progress, 💯Progress %, OKR Point) are measured proof of what happens otherwise. "The moment a computed number becomes typable, it becomes a second truth" is the best sentence in the document.
  • The four words, and the two hard rules (no Action-only scorecard; Action ≠ Outcome achieved). V3's 52 type spellings and the 70% Action legacy composition show what happens without it.
  • Monthly as the management unit, period lock, Retired-not-deleted, verifier ≠ owner, no averaging employee scores into BU results. All five are load-bearing.
  • The activation gate as a concept — blank-with-named-owner beats a plausible guess. The concept is right; its arithmetic is wrong (below).

2. Structurally wrong

(a) The plan's central feasibility number is contradicted by its own evidence. §7.1 claims the gate passes 265 of 375 (71%), failing 110 "whose metric value contains no number." Annex A1, measured from the live sheet: rows with blank metric value = 0, rows whose metric value contains no digit = 372 of 375. The real gate pass-rate is approximately 3, not 265. Either the PRD read a different column, a different version, or invented a plausible number — which is precisely the sin the document forbids. Everything downstream calibrated on "71% pass" — Stage S3, the pilot scope, the "useful starting position" framing — is built on sand.

(b) The "Owner: one named person" gate condition fails wholesale. Annex A1's owner table shows PeopleResponsible holds unit codes, not people: 'MKT', 'DAS', 'BND 1', plus 'FXS\nADM' (two units in one cell) and 'FXL' (a brand in the owner field). Not one of the 375 key results has a human owner. Gate condition 2 fails for essentially the entire sheet, and the PRD never mentions it.

(c) The HOD weight split is stated two ways and the plan proposes to score people anyway. Config/ITC/MKT say 10 leadership + 5 EXCO; the HOD Master and Composite Scoring say 15 leadership. A 5-point swing in the leadership block moves real HOD scores across the 65/66 and 69/70 PIP boundaries. Decision 12 is open; the bands must not be built until it is closed. Same for the individual layer: 0 of 11 roles balance (0.40–0.80), yet §7.3's PIP path is specified to the point.

(d) The join is free text. KPI ID as a short-text field in ClickUp pointing at Zoho IDs has no referential integrity. 375 hand-typed IDs against a register whose own source contains INF-BND2291 (space lost through a CONCAT) will drift within one quarter — this is the 52-spellings failure reborn one level up.

(e) Quarterly targets, monthly close, no decomposition rule. The OKR sheet is quarter-keyed (Q1 130 / Q2 85 / Q3 84 / Q4 76); the close is monthly; the PRD never says how a quarterly target becomes a monthly one. At the first close, "is November on track against a Q1 target?" has no defined answer.

(f) Weights are authored in ClickUp but "enforced by the engine." The check happens after entry, in another system, by a person looking. That is enforcement by eye with extra steps, and it quietly violates the one-writer rule for the single most consequential field in the model.

(g) Decision 1 contradicts decision 9. Carried-forward decision 1 says the OKR sheet is canonical; decision 9 makes ClickUp the authoring surface. §14 flags it in a parenthesis, but a "yes" to both is incoherent and the owner will not notice.

3. What fails first at the first real close

The activation gate, on day one of Stage S3 — before a single number is captured. The team will run the seven-condition test expecting 265 passes and get roughly 3. 372 key results have a named source and no number; none has a human owner. The close cannot fail at verification or capture — it fails earlier: there is nothing Active to close. The scorecard is empty, the engine computes nothing, HR receives nothing, and 55 staff who were told they'd be scored monthly see a blank system in month one. That is the credibility event the PRD itself warns about, arriving through the front door.

If the gate were somehow waved through, the second failure is verification on working day 2: 21 HODs verifying ~55 staff against a culture with 0 of 4,200 sign-offs ever completed — the close dies at the same step the tracker did.

4. Five highest-leverage changes, ranked

  1. Re-measure the OKR sheet and restate the gate arithmetic before anything else. The 110-vs-372 error invalidates the plan's own feasibility claim and the owner's decision basis (decisions 1, 3, 5 are being answered against a false number). Nothing outranks a wrong headline number in a document whose stated rule is "no number is invented."
  2. Close decision 12 and freeze one HOD weight model, in writing, before any band exists. A 5-point ambiguity sitting directly on the PIP threshold is an employment-law problem, not a tidying problem. Refuse to build L2 scoring on the contradiction.
  3. Build and balance the individual layer (L3) before any score reaches Zoho People. 0 of 11 roles balance; ~55 roles need definition; the PIP consequence is specified but its input does not exist. Narrow to a first wave if needed — but no HR feed until weights sum.
  4. Make the join structural, not textual. KPI ID as a relation/lookup against a register, IDs frozen as values before import (the CONCAT/VLOOKUP trap is documented in §10 and then ignored in §9.3), and a monthly decomposition rule for quarterly targets. This is what stops the system re-rotting into free text.
  5. Design verification as a capacity problem, not a rule. Named verifier per BU, a realistic day-2 load, and an exception SLA — because the measured history (0/4,200 signed) says the close fails at sign-off, not at capture.

5. The thing almost everyone would get wrong

Trusting the PRD's headline numbers over the evidence pack. The 110/265/71% story is plausible, well-written, and quoted three times — and the machine-measured annex says 372/375. Almost any reviewer would reconcile the tone, not the figures. I would insist: every count in the plan gets a query and a row list in the evidence index before the owner answers any of the twelve decisions — because decisions 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 are all being asked against a pass-rate that is off by two orders of magnitude. First thing Monday: re-run the metric-value read on the OKR sheet, publish the corrected gate-pass count, and put the twelve decisions back to Ricardo with honest numbers. If the plan's own evidence section can't be trusted, nothing built on it can be.

The two-axis spine is the single best insight in the document, and it is correct. Also right and worth protecting:

  • The verifier separate from the owner — 0 of 4,200 capture rows have ever been signed off. The PRD's diagnosis is exactly right.

  • Retired status, no deletion — the 59 ID holes in the OKR sheet are the proof that deletion is how a file loses its integrity.

  • The restraint — no quota, no invented numbers, no scoring until the pilot closes, no averaging employee scores into BU results. These are the rules that keep the system honest.

2. What is structurally wrong

The gate math is false by 3.4×, and it invalidates the entire stage plan. The PRD says 110 of 375 KRs fail the gate, so 265 pass (71%). The evidence in Part 3, Annex A1 says: "Rows whose metric value contains no digit at all: 372." The PRD's own evidence annex contradicts its own body text. This is not a rounding error. The gate would pass approximately 3 key results, not 265. Every stage from S3 onward — gate pass, pilot close, all-BU close, HR live — is built on the assumption that 265 KRs are Active. That assumption is false. The PRD's "useful starting position, not a disaster" is a fiction.

"Adopt V3 unchanged" is false for the layers that score people. The PRD says V3's weights and bands are "already correct" and should be kept unchanged. That is true only for L0 (enterprise) and L1 (BU outcomes). For L2 (HOD), 10 of 21 roles sum to 1.00, 69 rows have blank or zero weight, and the HOD split is stated two contradictory ways (50/35/15 vs 50/35/10/5) — the PRD itself flags this as decision 12. For L3 (individual), 0 of 11 roles sum to 1.00. The layers that actually determine a person's score are not correct. Adopting V3 unchanged means adopting an engine with contradictory rules for the people who will be scored first.

The quarterly-to-monthly conversion is never addressed. The owner's constraint 3 says cadence is monthly. The OKR sheet's 375 KRs are quarterly only — 130 Q1, 85 Q2, 84 Q3, 76 Q4, with no month column. The PRD says "monthly is the management unit" and builds a monthly close calendar, but never explains how a quarterly KR ("Increase by 10% in Q1") becomes a monthly capture object. A quarterly target has no monthly number. The close calendar assumes monthly capture exists; the source data has none.

The individual layer cannot be built from existing data without violating constraint 2. The evidence: 574 legacy lines, 404 Action (70%), 112 vague/non-quantified, 76 duplicates, 64 outcomes needing targets. Building ~55 roles with 70/20/10 splits, each with at least one Outcome, from this material requires inventing baselines and targets that do not exist anywhere in the three files. Constraint 2 says no invented numbers. Stage S4 is therefore impossible as scoped — it requires the owner to either narrow to a first wave (decision 10) or explicitly authorise the creation of new measures.

ClickUp's own §9.3 violates the rule of one writer. The PRD says "No Actual, no Progress %, no Score %, no Band field anywhere in ClickUp. Those are computed in the engine and read back." Then §9.3 adds Baseline and Target as number fields on the Objectives list. A target typed in ClickUp is a second writer for a number the engine should own. The PRD's own core rule is broken before the system is built.

The close calendar assumes verification will happen. The evidence says it won't. Day 2 of the close requires the verifier to check evidence and approve. The S&M tracker has 0 of 4,200 rows signed off in six months. The PRD names this as the problem, then designs a process that depends on the exact behaviour that has never once occurred. The first close will stall at day 2, not at capture.

3. What fails first at the first real monthly close, and why there

Day 1 fails. There is nothing to close.

The close calendar says "Week ending Friday: capturer types the week's number." The capturer cannot do this, because no KPI has passed the activation gate. With 372 of 375 KRs containing no digit, at most 3 are even eligible, and those 3 would still need owner, baseline, verifier, and weight — conditions the OKR sheet does not carry. The close tasks get created for 9 BUs, and each BU's capturer is handed a list of zero Active KPIs.

The pilot makes this concrete. Stage S5 runs S&M only. The S&M tracker has 25 KPIs, of which 22 are Actions (activity counts) and 3 are Outcomes. The gate would pass only Outcomes. Those 3 Outcomes have no baseline, no weight, no verifier, and their sign-off column is 0 of 4,200 rows. The tracker was never designed as a scoreable surface — it is activity capture. The pilot close's capturer would open the Close task and have nothing to submit, because the gate has never been run and the 3 Outcomes were never activated.

It fails there because the PRD treated the gate as a formality — 265 pass, 110 fail — when in reality the gate is the main event. The gate does not validate the sheet; it reveals that the company's FY2027 commitments are intentions, not measures. The first close fails at day 1 because the system was designed to close measures that do not exist.

4. The five highest-leverage changes, ranked

1. Correct the gate math and rebuild the stage plan around ~3 Active KRs, not 265. This outranks everything because every stage from S3 to S7 depends on the false 265 number. The PRD's exit conditions for S3, S5, S6, and S7 all assume a working population of Active KRs. With 372 failing, S3's exit condition ("every Active object has all seven") is trivially met by having almost no Active objects — which means the stage plan collapses into a measure-authoring program, not a system-build. Fixing this number forces the owner to confront that FY2027 scoring requires writing ~372 measures from scratch. That is the real program.

2. Resolve the HOD split and the 69 blank weights before adopting V3. This outranks the next because 21 HOD/EXCO roles are the first people scored, and the engine currently has two contradictory weight models (50/35/15 vs 50/35/10/5) plus 69 rows with blank or zero weight. Until decision 12 is answered and the 69 rows are closed, no HOD can be scored correctly, and the PRD's "adopt V3 unchanged" is a claim about a layer that is not correct. The enterprise and BU layers can be adopted unchanged; the HOD layer cannot.

3. Design the quarterly-to-monthly conversion. This outranks the next because the owner's non-negotiable cadence rule (monthly) conflicts with the source data's structure (quarterly only), and the PRD ignores the conflict entirely. Every close for every BU depends on having a monthly number. Without a defined conversion — a quarterly KR decomposed into monthly milestones, or a separate monthly target — the close calendar is a process with no inputs. This is a structural mismatch that breaks every close, not a field-level fix.

4. Solve the individual layer's raw material problem — narrow to a first wave or get explicit approval to create measures. This outranks the next because S6 (all BUs close) and S7 (HR live) depend on S4 (individual layer built), and S4 is currently impossible under constraint 2. The evidence shows 404 of 574 legacy lines are Actions, 112 are vague, 76 are duplicates. Building 55 balanced roles from this material requires inventing outcomes, baselines, and targets that do not exist. The PRD must either narrow to a first wave (decision 10) or get the owner's explicit written approval to author new measures — which is a constraint-1 event.

5. Remove Target and Baseline from ClickUp's Objectives list fields. This outranks anything else because it is a small fix that prevents the exact dual-truth problem the PRD identifies as the core disease. The PRD's §9.3 adds these fields to ClickUp while simultaneously declaring that ClickUp "never owns" numbers. A target typed in ClickUp and a target typed in the engine are two truths. The fix is to remove these two fields and read targets from the engine, which is what the PRD says it wants anyway.

5. The one thing almost everyone would get wrong that I would insist on getting right

The PRD itself does this: "the gate would pass 265 of 375 key results (71%) and fail 110 — a useful starting position, not a disaster." The evidence says 372 of 375 contain no digit at all. The gate does not validate the sheet; it reveals that the company's FY2027 commitments are a list of intentions, not measures. The gate is not a filter — it is a diagnostic that shows the content does not exist.

I would insist on running the gate against the live OKR sheet on Monday morning, producing the real number — approximately 3 scoreable KRs — and reporting it to Ricardo before any ClickUp structure is built. The report would say: the structure is the easy part; the content is the program. FY2027 scoring requires authoring ~372 measures from scratch, which is a multi-month content effort, not a system-build. Most people would build the system first and assume the content follows. The evidence says the content does not exist, and constraint 2 says nobody may invent it. The right move is to make the gate the first deliverable, force the real number into the open, and let that number reshape the stage plan before a single field is created in ClickUp.

Verdict: do not build this as written, and do not connect it to Zoho People. The architecture is substantially better than the artefacts it replaces, but the plan currently confuses a sound target structure with an executable scoring and close process. Its central readiness claim is contradicted by the evidence, its scoring model is unresolved, and its close can turn missing evidence into apparently authoritative people scores.

On Monday morning I would freeze implementation—not investigation—reconcile the activation evidence, and get the scoring and close contract decided before importing anything into ClickUp.

1. What is structurally right and must be protected

  1. Separation of authoring, evidence, computation and HR record.
    ClickUp authors commitments and manages work; source systems hold evidence; the engine calculates; Zoho People holds the formal HR record. In particular, the prohibition on typed Actuals, scores and bands in ClickUp is correct. The existing three progress fields and typed OKR Point demonstrate why this matters.

  2. The Brand × BU spine.
    This is a genuine structural correction, not taxonomy tidying. BND1 and BND2 are absent from ClickUp’s 13-option Business Unit field, while five brand codes are incorrectly present. Keeping Express as a brand while distinguishing Express operations, Express marketing and Express finance is the right model. The documented FXS exception is also preferable to pretending the coding scheme is perfectly symmetrical.

  3. Adopting V3 where V3 is already sound.
    The nine enterprise KPIs and 54 BU outcomes already balance to 1.00. Keeping those, the consequence bands and the Action/Outcome distinction avoids inventing a fifth system. The rule that BU results are business metrics, never averages of employee scores, is especially important.

  4. Immutable identity, effective dating and history.
    Freezing legacy IDs as values, effective-dating owners and targets, retaining retired objects and preventing silent recalculation of closed periods are all necessary. The CONCAT(B,K,C)/VLOOKUP trap and the BND2 spelling failure show that these are operational controls, not elegance.

  5. The activation philosophy.
    No invented numbers, no quota, visible Draft status and named gaps are exactly right. So is the principle that an Action cannot be a person’s entire score and that completing an Action does not prove an Outcome.

  6. Evidence, independent verification and exceptions as normal work.
    The plan correctly identifies that capture without review is not management: November capture is moving, while sign-off remains 0. Keeping bands suppressed until a rehearsed close is also right.

  7. Stage exits based on conditions, with copies and rollback first.
    Protect that from pressure to substitute dates for readiness. The problem is that several exit conditions currently certify the wrong thing; the remedy is to strengthen them, not abandon stage gating.

2. What is structurally wrong

A. The central activation claim is false

The PRD says 110 of 375 key results contain no number and therefore 265 of 375, or 71%, would pass the activation gate. Annex A1 says 372 of 375 contain no digit. Only three contain a digit at all.

More fundamentally, containing a digit tests only one of seven conditions. The sheet is quarterly, has no verifier or scorecard-weight evidence, and its PeopleResponsible entries are unit codes such as MKT, DAS and BND 1, not named individual owners. There are no blank owner cells, but that is not the same as satisfying “one named person.”

On the supplied evidence, zero of 375 can be demonstrated to pass all seven conditions. That does not prove none could eventually pass; it means the claimed 265-pass starting position has no evidentiary basis. S3 cannot be planned as a cleanup of 110 exceptions.

There is a smaller evidence-control warning too: Annex A3’s JSON reports 47 positive November rows, while its table and the PRD report 65. That may be a read-time difference, but it must be reconciled rather than silently choosing the more favourable number.

B. The five-layer straight line is incompatible with the rest of the design

The rule L4 → L3 → L2 → L1 → L0, with no skipped levels and one parent, cannot operate as stated:

  • An enterprise cash KPI needs capture directly at L0; it does not acquire meaning by passing through an individual employee.
  • A BU outcome needs capture at L1, regardless of whether an L3 role exists.
  • There are only 14 individual rows against 294 HOD rows, 54 BU outcomes and nine enterprise KPIs, so the chain is not populated.
  • A KPI definition can legitimately be referenced by several period commitments, while a scorecard line needs one effective-dated weighting parent. Those are different relationships.
  • The strict tree sits uneasily with the correct rule that business results are not employee-score roll-ups.

The model needs separate objects for KPI definition, period target/KR, scorecard membership, capture observation and verification. A single immutable KPI registry with controlled relationships would work; a compulsory five-level tree will not.

The same conflation appears in the requirement that KR weights sum to 1.00 under each objective. OKRs are not automatically scorecards, and objective weight is not necessarily HOD scorecard weight.

C. The monthly close is not an executable control process

Ten BU/group close tasks cannot establish completeness across hundreds of measures. There is no expected-versus-received checklist, item-level verification status, evidence sampling rule, named capturer/verifier roster, backup capacity or reconciliation between Zoho capture and ClickUp submission.

The dangerous cases are unspecified:

  • Does missing evidence produce no score, zero, or a renormalised score over whatever arrived?
  • Can HR receive a score while exceptions remain open?
  • Can a BU task be Captured when only some KPIs arrived?
  • Who signs after verification, and what exactly does Signed off certify?
  • How is a locked month corrected when a source system subsequently proves wrong?

The timetable sends scores to HR on day three while Ricardo is only then seeing exceptions. The period lock can therefore make an unverified or incomplete result permanent.

There is also a direct independence contradiction: §5 says verifier cannot be owner, while §11 has the HOD proposing and signing L1 BU outcomes. Where the HOD owns the outcome, that is self-verification.

D. The scoring contract is not settled

Decision 12 is an absolute build blocker: 50/35/10/5 and 50/35/15 produce different scores. “Keep V3 unchanged” is impossible until one version governs.

The wider arithmetic is already demonstrably unready:

  • Only 10 of 21 HOD roles balance to 1.00.
  • CFO and HR sit at 0.50 by design.
  • 69 HOD rows have blank or zero weight.
  • 0 of 11 demonstrated individual roles balance.
  • The individual layer has only 14 rows, yet the plan proposes building roughly 55 roles before the pilot.

The PRD also does not validate how quarterly OKR targets become monthly targets, how higher/lower/binary measures score, how Action/Control evidence is graded, or how missing and partial periods are treated. V3 may contain useful formulas, but those behaviours must be tested for this new operating model before anyone is scored.

E. “One writer” is asserted but not consistently designed

Weights live in the engine according to §3, yet Weight % is authored in ClickUp according to §9. KPI definitions and baselines live in Zoho according to §6, while ClickUp also carries baseline and target fields. There is no field-by-field system-of-record matrix, interface, reconciliation report or failure mode for the promised read-back of computed values.

Decision 1’s “OKR sheet is canonical” wording also conflicts with importing and freezing that sheet, making ClickUp the authoring surface and extending V3 as a writable engine. Decisions 1, 7 and 9 need to be rewritten as one coherent source-of-truth decision.

F. The rollout learns too late

S4 builds all approximately 55 roles before S5 tests one close. That is a large amount of consequential design work against an unresolved model.

The S&M-only pilot is useful as a capture-repair rehearsal, but it cannot validate the whole system. Its tracker is 88% activity, has 0 sign-offs across 4,200 rows and has 176 pre-seeded zero rows in each future month. It does not adequately exercise BU outcomes, HOD inheritance, individual scoring, cross-BU verification or the HR boundary.

G. The document violates its own governing rules

  • “Archive, then purge” and decision 9’s proposed purge violate the standing nothing is deleted rule. Archive and rename; do not purge.
  • §1 refers to nine decisions, S0 to ten, and §14 contains twelve.
  • S6 requires nine signatures inside two working days, while the engine and HR steps occur on day three.
  • The claim that 59 ID holes prove deletion is stronger than the evidence; they prove missing content, not the mechanism.

These are not copy-editing issues when the document is intended to function as the system constitution.

3. What fails first at the first real monthly close

The day-two verification and completeness step fails first—not the weighting arithmetic.

The likely sequence is:

  1. Capture remains partial. November already shows only 65 positively measured rows among 213 rows carrying week numbers, with no sign-offs. Staff who did not design the model now have to interpret definitions, evidence requirements and monthly targets simultaneously.
  2. A capturer marks one of ten aggregate ClickUp tasks Captured. Nothing proves that every expected KPI for that BU was submitted, or distinguishes a genuine zero from an untouched pre-seeded zero.
  3. The verifier receives no KPI-level completeness statement and has no workable sampling or rejection standard. Several HODs are also being asked to verify results they own.
  4. By day two, the choice is to miss the close or approve an incomplete one. Exceptions become the parking lot for ordinary unfinished capture.
  5. On day three, the engine computes anyway and HR is scheduled to receive scores while exceptions remain unresolved. Missing evidence can become a low score; the period lock then preserves it.

That is how the programme discredits itself in month one: not because ClickUp has too many fields, but because a plausible-looking score reaches a person without a complete, independently verified evidence set. A close that slips is recoverable. A false PIP trigger is much harder to recover from.

4. The five highest-leverage changes, ranked

1. Replace the readiness claim with a reproducible gate report and settle the scoring constitution

Re-run the gate against all 375 KRs and publish each of the seven conditions separately. Reconcile 372 versus 110, and 47 versus 65, with source version and read time recorded. Treat 0 as the number currently proven Active, not 265.

Before scoring design continues, resolve decision 12 and approve worked scoring examples covering higher-is-better, lower-is-better, binary, mandatory zero-weight controls, missing evidence and open exceptions.

This outranks everything else because the present plan’s workload, readiness and import assumptions rest on a contradicted number, and no structural work can make an undecided scoring model safe.

2. Specify the close as a controlled accounting process, with an explicit HR safety gate

For every BU and period, generate an expected-item roster with capturer, owner, independent verifier, evidence requirement and state. Define:

  • complete, incomplete and not-applicable states;
  • genuine zero versus missing;
  • evidence rejection and resubmission;
  • which exceptions block sign-off and HR transfer;
  • who finally signs;
  • controlled late correction/reopening of a locked period;
  • employee review, dispute and appeal before a consequential HR process.

No score should leave the engine unless its scorecard is valid and its close is signed. Add named backups and measure verifier workload.

This outranks data-model refinement because it directly prevents false people consequences and addresses the first observed point of failure.

3. Replace the rigid layer chain with a registry-and-relationship model, then define one writer per field

Use immutable surrogate KPI IDs with legacy IDs retained as aliases. Model separately:

  • KPI definition;
  • effective-dated scorecard line and weight;
  • period target/KR;
  • capture observation;
  • verification and close;
  • score and HR transfer.

Permit capture against L0, L1, L2 or L3 as appropriate. Keep one scorecard parent for weighting where required, but do not force measurement lineage through individuals.

Then publish the field ownership matrix: ClickUp owns objective wording and approved period commitments; the engine owns weights, calculations, scores and locks; source systems own observations. Add automated count, orphan, duplicate-target and weight reconciliation between systems.

This outranks rollout changes because otherwise the pilot will rehearse double-counting, duplicate weight ownership and broken lineage.

4. Narrow the build and make the pilot representative before scaling to 55 roles

Keep December S&M work as a capture-repair exercise, but do not treat it as proof that the performance system works. Build only a pilot wave of roles and run a representative shadow close containing enterprise/BU/HOD/individual linkage, at least one genuine Outcome, a Control, a missing-evidence case, a late correction and an independent verification.

Require reconciliation and employee-facing review to succeed before S4 expands. Keep Zoho People on test cases until a real shadow score has been inspected without consequences.

This outranks document governance because it limits blast radius and discovers operating defects before they are replicated across approximately 55 roles.

5. Make the PRD internally enforceable and compliant with the owner’s rules
