Build guide · Donatello's figure

Dummy 13, from two beds to a robot

Twenty-six official steps, seven stages, no glue. The armour came off in sapphire silk, the skeleton is the frame plate, and the six hands travel with the armour. Everything here follows soozafone's own assembly guide — the pages are reproduced below so you never have to hunt for the PDF.

01What is on the two plates

Dummy 13 is a skeleton wearing armour. The skeleton does the moving; the armour is what you see. They print separately, and they are the only two things you need to build the figure.

PlateWhat's on itColourTime / weight
Armour Outer chest, inner chest, waist, abdomen, crotch, neck, head, two shoulders, two upper arms, two forearms, two hips, two thighs, two shins, two knees, two feet, two toes — plus all six hands Sapphire silk 2 h 36 · 20 g
Frame Head, neck, chest, abdomen, waist, hips, two clavicles, four hip/shoulder joints, four knee/elbow joints, two upper arms, two forearms, two thighs, two shins, two ankles, stand adapter PLA 2 h 01 · 16 g
One honest caveat. soozafone recommends ABS, ASA or PETG for the frame and specifically warns against PLA: it is stiff, brittle, and it creeps under load, so the ball joints loosen with use. Yours is PLA because that is what was on the spool — it assembles and poses, but it is the short-life version. A PETG frame later costs 16 g and two hours.

02Before you start

Tools

  • Your fingers, for snapping parts off the runners
  • A hobby knife or flush cutters, for the nubs
  • A shallow tray or a bowl — there are 21 frame parts and 16 armour parts, and the small ones vanish
  • Nothing else. No glue, no screws, no tools

Two rules that save the build

  • Snap, don't force. v1.0 replaced almost every press-fit with a snap-fit, so a part that resists is usually the wrong part or the wrong way round, not a stiff one
  • Keep left and right apart. Hands, arms, legs, shoulders and hips are handed; the files are pre-mirrored, so there is nothing to flip in the slicer

03The build, in seven stages

The numbers below are soozafone's own step numbers, 1 to 26. Work in this order — the torso is built bottom-up and each stage only fits once the one before it is seated.

  1. Head and neck · step 1 Snap the orange neck collar onto the neck frame's lower ball, set the head frame on top, then push the orange head armour down over the head frame. It clicks, it does not screw.
  2. Chest · step 2 Seat the chest frame into the inner chest bracket, then lower the outer chest armour over it. The inner chest is symmetrical front-to-back now, so the backpack hole cannot end up on the wrong side.
  3. Waist, hips and the ball chain · steps 3–5 Push the abdomen frame's ball up into the waist armour, then build the hip: the three-ball chain and its C-clip go into the orange hip shell so the outer balls sit proud on each side as the leg sockets.
  4. Stack the torso · steps 6–7 Head down into chest, chest down into abdomen, abdomen down into the hips. Each is a ball into a socket — press until it pops through, then check the joints turn freely before you go on.
  5. Legs · steps 8–13 Per leg: snap the C-socket onto the thigh frame, drop the thigh frame into the thigh armour, push the shin frame up into the shin armour, then bridge the two with the figure-eight knee clip and cap it. Push the finished leg up onto the hip ball and press the hip dome over the joint. Repeat for the other leg.
  6. Feet · steps 14–17 The grey ankle clamp seats in the heel cradle and the toe cap clips onto the front. Then push each foot up onto its ankle ball — and check the two ankles are symmetrical before pressing them home, because a foot that goes on crooked stays crooked.
  7. Arms · steps 18–22 Per arm: cradle into the upper-arm frame with the orange shell under it, ball link up into the forearm housing, the S-clip joining both halves, and the three-prong gripper on the wrist. Add the shoulder ball and the small shoulder cap, then push the whole arm onto the shoulder ball. Repeat for the other arm.

Steps 23–26 — hands, and the stand

Every hand is a push-fit on the same wrist ball, so they swap in a second and Donnie can re-arm the figure as often as he likes: open, grip, fist, left and right of each. The stand adapter (step 26) plugs into the port on the figure's side and is what a display base grips — it is not needed to stand the figure up.

05When it fights you

SymptomWhat it usually is
The joint will not clickWrong orientation — the ball's flat neck gives it away. Rotate 90° and try again before adding force.
The joint clicks but flopsA PLA frame joint that has already crept. Squeeze the socket ring gently, or move that limb less.
A part cracked on the runnerSnapping too fast. Cut the two runner gates with a knife first, then lift.
The armour will not sit flushThe frame under it is not fully seated. Take the armour off, press the frame home, refit.
Legs look unevenAnkles pressed on at different depths. Pull both feet, check step 15's alignment, refit.
