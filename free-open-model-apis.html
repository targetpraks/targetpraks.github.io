Bottom line

Three kinds of lane. (1) Brokers on the Gonka network — a decentralised GPU network whose chain keeps proof-of-compute records; brokers resell the same capacity at different mark-ups (Dahl, GonkaGate, JoinGonka, Mingles, Chutes, and a directory of about ten more). (2) Big-lab free tiers and free pools — Groq, the Google Gemini API, NVIDIA NIM, Cloudflare Workers AI, and OpenRouter's free-model pool. (3) Card-required trials — Cerebras ($5, 30 days).

How they differ. Weekly-renewing free (Mingles) beats one-off grants (Dahl 100M, JoinGonka 3M) for sustained use; rate-capped free models (Groq, OpenRouter, Gemini) are ideal for overflow; daily allocations (Cloudflare) never expire but run out each day. Paid starts at $0.000245/1M (GonkaGate) and tops out around $0.44 in / $1.32 out (Chutes, where confidential TEE compute is the point).

The play: stack, don't choose. Wire them all into one agent; use free lanes first and let the fallback chain catch every rate cap. The catches: free lanes rotate and die (GitHub Models retired July 2026); Dahl's account is save-once fingerprint with no recovery; top-ups on the Gonka side are crypto-only; and there is no zero-retention promise — keep sensitive data out.

§2 The Gonka family — decentralised brokers

A broker is an independent operator who runs a Gonka gateway and resells inference. From your app's side it is an ordinary OpenAI-compatible endpoint: a base URL, a bearer key, and /v1/chat/completions. Brokers exist because opening a devshard on the chain is permissioned (governance-gated); the broker absorbs the escrow lifecycle and hands you a key.

  • Free lanes: Dahl — 100M one-off, no card, fingerprint-only account (save it); JoinGonka — 3M one-off, email signup; Mingles — 3.9M every week, renewing, no card, 10 requests/min — the sustained-free champion of the whole set.
  • Paid floors: GonkaGate is the cheapest lane seen ($0.000245/1M all-in, USD prepaid, no wallets); Dahl sits at ~$0.03/1M with crypto-only top-ups; Mingles prices per-token below OpenRouter.
  • Uptime is real but uneven: a public broker status page showed 92–100 % per model across day-to-day checks, with occasional 503 "no available backend" spikes. Fine for evaluation; keep a fallback for hard-availability work.
  • Model IDs rotate as models are added or retired (Kimi K2.6 has already been rotated out of some catalogs). Always resolve IDs from GET /v1/models at runtime.

§3 The big-lab free tiers and free pools

  • Groq — very fast inference on open models; a free plan with per-model rate limits (RPM/RPD/TPM tables in their docs), no card. Great for overflow and latency-sensitive calls.
  • Google Gemini API — a documented free tier with per-minute rate limits; an OpenAI-compatible endpoint is available, and Hermes ships a built-in gemini provider for it.
  • NVIDIA NIM — "free inference with leading models": hundreds of open models behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (integrate.api.nvidia.com/v1) for account holders; a built-in nvidia provider exists in Hermes.
  • Cloudflare Workers AI — 10,000 neurons per day free (about a fixed daily token allocation), then $0.011/1K neurons on the paid plan; edge-hosted models, no card for the free allocation.
  • OpenRouter free pool — 19 models priced at $0 (counted live from their models API); free usage is rate-capped (≈20 req/min, 50/day) and requires a non-negative account balance. Built into Hermes — just add the key.
  • Cerebras — $5 in free credits for new accounts, expiring after 30 days; a verified payment method is required, so this is a trial, not a free lane.
  • GitHub Models — retired 30 July 2026. Kept here on purpose: it is the object lesson that free lanes die. Re-check every link before relying on it.

§4 Wire them all into Hermes Agent

Hermes holds every account side by side — each one a named custom provider — so you route per task and keep a fallback chain for when one throttles. Six custom entries cover the Gonka family plus Groq; Gemini, NVIDIA and OpenRouter are already built-in providers in the picker (no custom entry needed, just add their keys). Same OpenAI pattern throughout.

Step 4 — switch by role; verify in two commands

# in session — by alias, or by named provider (triple syntax)
/model weekly                                   # alias → Mingles (weekly free)
/model grant                                    # alias → Dahl (100M buffer)
/model private                                  # alias → Chutes (TEE)
/model custom:groq:MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2.7       # named-provider form
$ hermes config check      # parses config.yaml and flags anything wrong
$ hermes fallback list     # prints the chain in fallback order

Smoke-test any lane

$ BASE=https://router.mingles.ai/v1 KEY=$MINGLES_API_KEY   # swap in any row
$ curl -s $BASE/chat/completions \
    -H "Authorization: Bearer $KEY" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
    -d '{"model":"MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M2.7","messages":[{"role":"user","content":"Hello!"}]}'
# 200 = key works · 401 = bad key · 402 = no tokens allocated on the key

How it behaves once wired

  • Stack the free lanes first. Dahl 100M one-off + JoinGonka 3M + Mingles 3.9M/week + OpenRouter's 19 free models + Groq's free plan — an evaluation budget at $0 before any top-up.
  • One primary, an ordered chain behind it. Fallbacks fire on rate-limit, 5xx and connection errors; a 429 naming its reset time benches that provider until exactly then.
  • Switching persists. A mid-session /model switch survives a restart; stepping back to a built-in provider clears the custom base URL automatically.
  • Keys stay scoped. Each named provider resolves its credential from its own key_env — never from the account that happened to be active before.

§5 How the picks were checked

  • Live probes, not press releases: every lane's model catalog was fetched (or its auth wall confirmed with a clean 401/403), free-tier pages were read that day, the OpenRouter free count (19) was counted from its live models API, and the GitHub Models retirement notice was read from its own docs — all on 8–9 Oct 2026.
  • Picks scored through a local decision lane (a small on-machine scoring model, no cloud): under "maximize sustained free usage, no payment card", the weekly lane beat the one-off grant in mirrored checks (3.9M/week vs 100M one-off: 0.83 vs 0.30), and stacking several free lanes beat betting on any single one (0.79). The field-level "best single lane" pick was weak (all top lanes within noise) — which is itself the finding: these lanes are complements, not competitors.

§6 Watch-outs

Free lanes rotate and dieGitHub Models was retired outright in July 2026. Treat every free tier here as dated: re-check the link before building on it.
Account quirks on the Gonka sideDahl's account is a save-once 32-character fingerprint — there is no email recovery by design; store it in a password manager at signup. Top-ups on that side are crypto-only (effectively no chargebacks).
No zero-retention promiseThe Gonka-side operator's own policy declines to guarantee zero retention or anonymity. Do not route sensitive data through those lanes; Chutes' TEE lane exists for when privacy is the point.
Rate caps everywhereFree tiers carry low caps (requests/day and tokens/day). Stack lanes so a 429 simply falls through to the next provider instead of failing your task.
Mark-ups differ on the same capacityFive brokers resell the very same Gonka network at very different prices — the ranking matters more than the brand. Compare before committing to spend.
