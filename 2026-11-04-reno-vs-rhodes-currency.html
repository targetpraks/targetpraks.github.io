The whole thing in one paragraph

A and B are cash-flow and lifestyle decisions. Rhodes is a currency decision. The two SA options do not care what the rand does; the Greek option barely cares about Greek property — it cares about the ZAR/EUR rate in 2036. If the rand weakens at its long-run pace, almost any euro asset wins and Rhodes ties the best SA option at 0.90% a year of Greek house-price growth. If the rand holds flat, Rhodes needs 5.7% a year — the entire Greek market outperformance — just to tie a fixed deposit. That is a macro view, not a property analysis, and it should be named as one.

Recommendation. Do the cheap reno. Do not do the R3.0m double storey for financial reasons. Rhodes only if you are deliberately buying euro exposure or want the permit itself — and if you do, size it to at least 120 m² at a fair €/m², never a small unit priced to the threshold.

Rhodes: the two things that decide it

1. It is the €800k tier, not €400k — and that is not an overpay

Rhodes has roughly 127,600 residents, so it sits in the top band under Law 5100/2024: €800,000 minimum, a single property, at least 120 m² of main usable area on one deed. Balconies, parking and storage do not count, and you cannot stitch two smaller units together.

So a 240 m² Lindos villa at €800k is market-consistent, not a structural overpay. The trap is specific: buying a small ~120 m² unit priced at €800k to hit the threshold. At the town average that unit is worth about €273k, so you would be paying 2.9× market for the visa wrapper.

2. The Golden Visa property cannot be short-let

This kills the beach-let thesis. Under Law 4959/2022, short-term tourist letting on the qualifying property is prohibited for the life of the permit. Long-term leases of 12 months or more are explicitly permitted and carry no penalty risk. If the Rhodes plan in your head is "villa plus Airbnb income", that plan is closed on a Golden Visa property.

Exchange control is the hard stop

The single discretionary allowance was raised from R1 million to R2 million per person per calendar year in the 2026 Budget, and the foreign investment allowance adds R10 million more via SARS tax clearance. Call it R12 million per person, R24 million for a couple.

R16.59m exceeds one person's annual capacity even on the generous reading. Either use two applicants — a spouse gives R24m and the deal fits without special dispensation — or apply to SARB for approval above the allowance.

The three options, one at a time

A · Welgemoed double storey

R3m buys about 125–156 m² of double storey at R16–20k/m², plus 20% for fees and contingency. Extensions do not recover their cost; kitchens recover 70–80%, bathrooms 45–70%, paint 75–100%, and no South African source I could find publishes a figure for extensions. At a 60% assumption R3m adds R1.8m of value and destroys R1.2m on resale.

Cross-check: a R3m bond at prime over 20 years is about R30,457 a month. Welgemoed lets around R38,000. Building an extension to let it out does not clear its own debt. This is a consumption decision. Fine if you want the house for ten years and fund it from equity; a losing trade if you fund it or build to sell.

B · Trichardt Street, cheap reno

R600k on kitchen, bathrooms and paint, blended recovery about 70%, so roughly R420k of value added for R600k spent — a real R180k of cost, for a materially better house, an extra 10–15% of rent, no approvals and no new debt. Best risk-adjusted rand in this comparison. Cap it at R600k and do not drift to R1.5m.

C · Rhodes

The permit is the product. Net long-let yield is 0.4–1.1%, short-letting is banned and revocable, and the friction is €80,000. Two more gates: Rhodes is a border zone, so non-EU buyers need Ministry of National Defence approval (two to six months, routine but mandatory), and processing runs three to nine months. It grants residency, not citizenship — seven years of presence plus language and civic tests for that.

If you only do one thing

  1. Cheap reno now. R600k, kitchen, baths, paint. Highest returning rand on the page, no approvals, no debt, liveable immediately.
  2. Revisit the double storey in twelve months, after living in the renovated house. That decision is far better made with real use data than on a spreadsheet.
  3. Treat Rhodes as independent. It is gated by permits and currency, not by the other two, so it can run in parallel — or never. The cheap reno is the one that pays regardless.
