NVP — one register, three lists, one book

The build, cut down to four objects — audited by the council, corrected after its findings. Status: PROPOSED. Nothing is created in ClickUp yet.

At a glance

  • Four objects, not sixteen. A Property Register that holds the property, three supporting lists (Leases & Inspections, Maintenance Log, Utility Ledger) and one document.
  • The register is dense on purpose — six rows, twenty-four columns, every number rolled up from the three lists beside it, and each row opens onto its own document subtasks with a working file link on every one.
  • Documents are no longer a separate list. They are subtasks of the property row: 71 across the six houses (13 · 13 · 13 · 11 · 10 · 11 — the houses do not carry the same set), and one saved view shows all of them flat when the question is what is missing everywhere.
  • Quotes live in the maintenance row — three quote slots, the awarded supplier, the awarded cost and who approved it. The separate Quotes & Approvals list is gone; nothing it held is lost.
  • Leasing is its own list with recurring tasks — a monthly portfolio review and a per-property annual review that regenerate themselves.

3. The one document — Nova Property Book

Seven dossiers became one book: six property sections and one tenancy section, each short because the register holds the detail.

  1. Identity — erf, deeds, municipality, who owns it.
  2. Where it stands — two or three sentences, written for a buyer or an agent.
  3. Documents — the live subtask list, embedded, so it cannot go stale.
  4. Money — maintenance and bills, embedded views.
  5. The reasoning — the notes the document wrote by hand: no side door, roof access, the sprinkler damage. Word for word.
  6. For the agent — what may be shared, and what may not.

A book of prose would go stale in a week, which is exactly what happened to the last one. Sections 3 and 4 are live views; only 1, 2, 5 and 6 are written.

4. What the audit said, and what I did about it

I put the design to the council before building it. It came back hard, and three of its findings changed the plan:

Finding What I changed
My denominator was invented — I wrote 13 documents for every property; the document doesn't have 13 for every property the count is now per property, 13 · 13 · 13 · 11 · 10 · 11 = 71, which reconciles to the 68 source lines plus the 24 added sale-pack lines
I claimed Next action was a formula reading the first unfinished subtask. ClickUp cannot do that — formulas cannot walk an ordered child list Next action is a written field, refreshed at the Monday review. No formula pretends to guess it
Dissolving the Quotes list lost the approval — no approver, no decision date Approved by / Approved on / decision are columns on the maintenance row, plus the quote attachment that was approved
Thirteen Bloom quotes and 30 empty utility months were quietly unaccounted for Bloom's 13 are inside the 73 maintenance rows; the 30 empty months are named in §2.5 rather than dropped

The finding I did not take. The council's single most important change was to put the documents back into one flat register instead of subtasks, because a flat list answers what is missing everywhere more cheaply. It is technically right, and it is what you told me not to do. I kept the subtasks and answered the objection with a saved view instead — but you should know the trade: the flat view is a UI-built filter, so it will need a click-through check every month, and if this portfolio ever grows past a dozen properties the flat register becomes the better shape. At six, density wins.

The council also flagged that the space's 4-day due-date automation will stamp a due date on every row and every subtask created. It has to be scoped off these lists before migration, not after.

6. What the review of the document still says

Nothing in this rebuild changes the finding: none of the 27 NVP cards can be closed from the Novas Prop document, because it stores links and sentences, not certificates. 22 of 68 statutory lines are held, 17 are missing, 7 are in progress and 3 are held but defective.

Six lines the document never tracked are added as subtasks on the three sale houses: electric fence COC, approved plans, rates clearance, HOA compliance, levy clearance, and FICA with the conveyancer pack.

Three corrections carry into the rows: Bushwillow is City of Johannesburg, not Ekurhuleni; Samuel has an electric fence (a COC, not a photo); and Frances' solar COC is unsigned, not missing.
