04 · Why It Balances

Three structural fixes, not three lucky numbers
FixWhat it isHours released
Pool the SLA outbound An SLA is sold to franchisees already inside the network — so one outbound campaign serves all four SLA brands at once, instead of four separate ones. 82.7 h → 20.7 h. 62.0
Centralise the hunting Calls, LinkedIn and Apollo-built research run once for the group, not repeated on every book. Five books' worth of hunt collapses into one. 86.7 h → 17.3 h. 69.2
SLA = account management The SLA brands come off the Deal Maker and sit on the SDR side as accounts: check-ins, renewals, upsell. 42.7 h → 18.5 h per brand. 96.8

Why an SLA is not a hunting book

There is nobody to cold-call. The franchisee is already a customer. A pipeline book carries 160 calls, 80 emails, 40 LinkedIn touches, 20 booked meetings and 20 discovery calls; an SLA carries six meetings, a report and hygiene.

Costing an SLA like a hunting book overstates it by 2.3× on the SDR side and 1.7× on the Deal Maker side. That error is what made this look like a hiring problem.

The three structural fixes do the work — 228 hours a month released, while the same amount of real selling happens.

The uncomfortable truth

If the CRM pipelines, email templates and push automation are not loaded, the Deal Maker cannot hit the number — and that is a management accountability, not a salesperson one. The manager owns the tooling. Blaming the closer for a missing automation is blaming the driver for a missing road.

07 · What To Do First

Six actions, in order
  1. Pool the SLA outbound. One campaign for four brands. Releases 62 hours a month, costs nothing.
  2. Centralise the hunting. Run calls, LinkedIn and research once, not per book. Releases 69 hours.
  3. Move the SLA brands to account management on the SDR side. Releases 97 hours of Deal Maker time.
  4. Adopt the matrix at §01. Olwethu 183%→86%, Nkuli 206%→70%, Andile 32%→87%, Alex 37%→70%. No new head.
  5. Attach the proof, then start signing off. Twenty-eight unverified SDR rows and zero sign-offs — the cheapest two fixes on this list.
  6. Hold the cap. Two to three pipeline books per person, SLA accounts carried separately. The rule that stops it recurring.
  7. Rewrite the scorecard. The eleven KPI changes at §08 — without them the load will drift straight back to where it started.

08 · The KPIs That Must Change

Eleven changes — four of them are why the load was wrong

The model above is only sustainable if the scorecard changes with it. These are the specific lines on the tracker that must be altered, added or removed. The first four are structural — they are the actual cause of the overload, not a symptom of it.

A · Structural — the four that caused the problem

#KPITodayMust change toWhy
1Books per person No limit — one SDR carries 9 rows, one Deal Maker 8 Cap at 2–3 pipeline books per person. SLA accounts held separately and not counted as pipeline books. This is the root cause. Nine books on one desk is 183% of a month before anything goes wrong.
2Hunting target — calls, LinkedIn, research Costed once per book: 160 calls × 5 books = 800 calls a month between two people One pooled group target. 160 qualified calls covers the group; the number is hunted once, not five times. The same prospect list is being worked five times over. Releases 69.2 h a month.
3SLA outbound campaign Four separate campaigns — one per SLA brand — 4 × 20.7 h = 82.7 h One pooled campaign for all four SLA brands. 20.7 h, run once. An SLA is sold to franchisees already in the network. There is no separate list to cold-call. Releases 62.0 h.
4Management time new KPI Zero hours costed. The manager's selling book is costed as if running the team were free Reserve 30 h a month — roughly 18% of a month — as protected management time: one-on-ones, sign-off, forecasting, EXCO. Nkuli sat at 206% with no management hours counted at all. With the reallocation that is 118.4 h selling + 30 h managing = 88%, and the job becomes doable.

B · Role definition — the SLA brands are measured against the wrong job

#KPITodayMust change toWhy
5SLA brands must stop carrying hunting KPIs Each SLA carries the full pipeline set — 160 calls, 80 emails, 40 LinkedIn touches, 20 booked meetings, 20 discovery calls Replace with account-management KPIs: 6 client meetings, renewal rate, upsell value, account health, response time. Costing an SLA like a hunting book overstates it 2.3× on the SDR side and 1.7× on the DM side. That single error is what made this look like a hiring problem. Releases 96.8 h.
6New Accounts Researched — time standard 20 minutes per account 10 minutes per account, with Apollo AI as the named tool in the KPI row. The team already builds its lead lists with Apollo. The standard is still priced as if it were manual research — 2.0 h saved per book.
7Travel new KPI No travel line anywhere on the sheet — it appears nowhere and is measured nowhere Add travel as a costed, per-book line at 30 minutes a trip: INF Consultancy 5 h · ACDC 0 h · TLF franchise 0 h · TLF product 10 h · INFX 5 h. Travel is real work. Uncosted, it silently eats the closer's month; the FND books are held at our office and correctly carry none.

C · Data discipline — the KPIs that already exist but are not being met

#KPITodayMust change toWhy
8Proof link on every row 28 unverified rows on the SDR scorecard Zero unverified rows. A number without a dated evidence link does not count toward score. The work is very likely happening — the proof simply is not attached. This is the cheapest fix on the list and it changes no one's workload.
9HOD sign-off 0 / 9 and 0 / 7 — not one line signed off anywhere Every line signed off before month end. No month closes without it. Without sign-off the scorecard is decorative. This is a management KPI, and it sits with the manager.
10Capture completeness Captured rows fell from 240 to 73; verified score fell from 412% to 65% Hold capture above 90% of expected rows every month, tracked as its own KPI. Less is being entered and less of it is provable. A dashboard that only improves because fewer rows are captured is measuring nothing.

D · Sheet hygiene

#KPITodayMust change toWhy
11Remove the discontinued line The FluxFlow row still sits in the Setup tab and in the ownership maps Remove FluxFlow from the tracker and all ownership mappings. Five pipeline books, not six. The team no longer sells it. A live row with no work attached is a standing invitation to miscount the load.
The effect of the changes, together

Four structural changes (cap the books, pool the hunt, pool the SLA outbound, protect management time) release 228 hours a month — without a single person working harder and without a single hire.

Three role changes (SLA account KPIs, Apollo research standard, travel as a costed line) make the numbers describe the job that is actually being done, instead of a job nobody could ever complete.

Where these KPIs should live

Structure (1–4) on the Setup tab, as allocation rules the scorecard enforces. Role definition (5–7) on the KPI Targets tab, per role. Discipline (8–10) on the capture tab, as pass/fail gates. Hygiene (11) once, in Setup.

A matrix, not a hiring round.

Olwethu 183% → 86%. Nkuli 206% → 70%. Andile 32% → 87%. Alex 37% → 70%. Micali untouched at 40%.

Pool the work that repeats, centralise the hunt, treat the SLAs as the accounts they are — and every desk lands inside a working month. Every hour works. Nobody breaks. No new hire.

It holds only if the scorecard changes with it. The eleven KPI changes at §08 are the difference between a plan and a slide.
