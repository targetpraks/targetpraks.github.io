Infinity Brands · ClickUp workspace 4627083 · five-model panel review · 17 November 2026

ClickUp, judged

Five independent models — GLM 5.3, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3.1 Pro, Nemotron 3 Ultra and Mistral Large 4 — were given the same evidence pack, read live from the workspace, and asked one question: is this an operating system for Infinity Brands? A sixth, Kimi, chaired the verdict. None of them saw the others' answers before writing.

The verdict, unanimous

"No-go for a 40-person rollout in its current state. This is an uninhabited scaffold with one live reporting workload, not an operating system — ownership is absent, REP cannot execute its documented close, and an automation is corrupting due dates."

Chair's summary, signed by all five panellists in substance. Not one of them argued it was ready.

2. Where all five agreed

  1. There are no people. One member, fourteen empty teams. Assignment, mentions, notifications and unit accountability cannot function. This is the operating part of the operating system, and it is missing.
  2. REP cannot close a period. No Submitted, no Reviewed, no submission queue, no exception view for missing or late units. The one documented governance loop — owner submits, only Ricardo reviews — exists nowhere but on paper.
  3. A live automation is falsifying due dates. Every new task is stamped "today + 5 days". Four panellists called it data corruption; all five put it in the first two actions.
  4. MKT is a combinatorial trap. Eight disciplines × four tiers × three brands, built as folders. Forty staff will not find the right list, and the same brief will land in different places depending on who files it.
  5. REP has competing sources of truth. Two fields for one thing, twelve narrative text fields duplicating the description template, and a cancelled quarterly cadence still sitting in the field picker.

4. The five moves, in the order the panel signed them

#MoveWhy it outranks the next
1Kill the due-date automation, freeze broad task creation, repair the dates it rewrote in REP.It is live damage. Every task created before it stops carries a fabricated deadline, and every report built on those dates is worthless.
2Give the workspace people — populate the groups, name an owner for every unit, appoint a backup reviewer, settle NVP-without-a-team and DID-without-a-space. Pilot with two owners before inviting forty.No workflow operates without identifiable actors. Nothing else changes a single outcome until this is true.
3Turn REP into an exception-driven close: Draft → Submitted → Reviewed, an owner queue, a Submitted-not-Reviewed queue for Ricardo, a view that shows missing and late units, and one dashboard for EXCO and MANCO.REP is the only live business process, and the whole governance loop hangs on it. It has a monthly deadline; the empty shells do not.
4Collapse the intake hierarchy before real work arrives — Briefed-In, Recurring, Projects per unit; tier, brand and discipline as fields and saved views; MKT cut down hard.Misfiling gets expensive the moment habits form. It is far cheaper to remove 145 lists now than to move misfiled work out of them for a year.
5Publish one operating contract — one business-unit field, native priority and due date, the brief template, the reporting deadlines and the review rule, in a doc inside ClickUp.Documentation sustains a working design. It cannot substitute for the four moves above, which is why it comes last, not first.

5. The thing all five said everyone gets wrong

"They would tidy the workspace and still confuse task completion with governance. A unit sub-task is that unit's obligation for a period. Completed is not a proxy for Reviewed, and a period cannot close until every required unit is reviewed or explicitly excused."

Everyone — including me, in the last two days — reaches first for the visible mess: probe fields, duplicate names, empty folders. That work makes the board prettier and changes nothing about whether a report was ever reviewed. Missing and late submissions must stay visible as exceptions, never dissolve into a completion percentage.

7. Monday, in one paragraph

Kill the automation in the ClickUp automations panel. Then cancel the idea of inviting forty people. Put Ricardo plus two unit owners into their groups, name a backup reviewer, and configure REP's Submitted and Reviewed states by hand in Space settings. Build the three REP views the close needs — owner queue, review queue, exception view — and run one complete weekly close with those two owners end to end. Only when assignments, mentions, deadlines and that close all work should the rest of the staff come in, and only then should MKT and the empty shells be collapsed.
