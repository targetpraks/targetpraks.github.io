2. Problem

Purchases from Mumbai have no controlled paper trail and no sign-off. The gate everyone assumes exists does not:

  • No approval rule is configured at all. GET /inventory/v1/settings/approvals?entity_type=purchaseorder on org 716443079 returns {"approvals": []}. Every one of the 83 purchase orders in the org can be sent, received and billed without anyone approving anything.
  • A Custom Module by itself cannot fix this. Custom modules are UI-only — /inventory/v1/custommodules returns 404 Invalid URL Passed, so nothing inside a custom module can block, trigger or validate a purchase order.
  • What Custom Modules can do is hold the quotation register and run an approval of their own (Blueprints), and what Approvals can do is stop a purchase order from moving forward. Option 1 combines those two facts; it does not pretend a module can count.

The org is on Zoho One / ENTERPRISE_NEW (/inventory/v1/organizations), so Custom Approvals (Professional+) and Custom Modules are within plan. Blueprints are early access and must be switched on by Zoho support.

3. Goals and non-goals

Goals

  1. Every Mumbai purchase has a named sourcing request, three comparable quotations on record, and Ricardo's approval before the PO is sent.
  2. The approval is a system block on the purchase order, not a convention.
  3. The evidence (three PDFs + comparable amounts) survives the purchase, retrievable months later for audit.

Non-goals

  • Automatically counting quotations and blocking the 12th attempt at a PO. Nothing inside Zoho Inventory can count child records on a transaction; see §5.
  • Replacing buying judgement. The approver is the control; the system makes the approver's decision cheap and recorded.
  • Any change to vendors, items, prices or accounts.

6.3 Blueprints

Both are early access — Zoho support must enable Blueprints for the org (§10, step 1).

Blueprint "Quotation check" (module: Quotations)

  • Verify — Received → Verified. Owner: sourcing owner. Mandatory fields: Quote date, Valid until, Amount, Currency. Checklist: "Supplier PDF attached", "Supplier name matches the quote", "Amount is ex-VAT".
  • Select — Verified → Selected. Owner: Ricardo. Checklist: "Compared against the other two quotes on this request", "Validity date covers the PO date".

Blueprint "Sourcing approval" (module: Purchase Requests)

  • Submit — Draft → Quotes Requested → Submitted for Approval. Owner: sourcing owner. Mandatory: Quote 1, Quote 2, Quote 3, Currency, Budget.
  • Approve — Submitted for Approval → Approved. Owner: Ricardo. Checklist: "Three quotes present and comparable", "Three quote PDFs attached to the request", "Budget covers the selected quote".
  • Reject — Submitted for Approval → Rejected. Owner: Ricardo. Mandatory: rejection reason (text field on the form, filled at transition).
  • SLA on Submitted for Approval: 2 working days → notify Ricardo and the backup; 3 working days → email both.

6.4 Approvals on Purchase Orders

Settings → Purchases → Purchase Orders → Approvals → Custom Approval → New Custom Approval.

Setting Value
Approval name Mumbai purchases — three quotes
Criteria Vendor equals each Mumbai supplier (one criterion per vendor, OR-joined). If Mumbai turns out to be a buying desk, the criterion becomes Created By / user instead — the rest of the design is unchanged
Flow Level 1 — Ricardo; Level 2 — backup (optional, recommended)
Priority 1 (above any future threshold rule)
"Allow approvers to approve transactions they create" Off
Notify approvers on submit On

Second rule, recommended in the same screen: Any PO above R50,000 → Level 1 Ricardo. This closes the "buy it from someone else" hole without needing to enumerate vendors.

6.5 Approvals on Bills and Payments Made

Without this, a direct bill bypasses the PO entirely. Recommended: Bills → Approvals → Simple Approval (or a custom rule above R10,000) with finance as approver. Phase 2 if Ricardo wants the first cut small — but then say so out loud, because the gate has a side door.

6.6 Purchase order custom fields (evidence on the PO itself)

Add to the PO form: Sourcing Request No (text) and Three quotes attached (checkbox). They give the approver everything on one screen, and they are what audit reads after the register has moved on.

7. Operating procedure

Happy path. Buyer raises Purchase Request → requests quotes from three suppliers → loads three Quotation records, attaches three PDFs → verifies each, selects one → submits the request → Ricardo approves → buyer raises the PO against the selected quote, fills Sourcing Request No, ticks the checkbox, attaches the three PDFs → Save and Submit → Ricardo approves → PO can be sent.

Exception — sole source / urgent. Request still raised, with Quote 2 and Quote 3 marked Sole source by using a quotation record whose supplier is Sole source (documented) and whose notes carry the reason. Ricardo approves or rejects; the exception is visible in the register and countable at month end.

Monthly audit. Filter Purchase Requests to Approved, sort by date, export; check every Mumbai PO in the same period matches a request and three quotations. Any orphan is a control failure to raise with the buyer, not a system failure.

9. Acceptance tests (all must pass before this is called done)

  1. Create a Purchase Request for a Mumbai item with fewer than three quote references → Submit is refused; the missing fields are named.
  2. Add a quotation without an amount → Verify is refused.
  3. Add three complete quotations, attach PDFs, submit → lands in Ricardo's Pending Approvals; email arrives.
  4. Approve the request → status Approved; transition buttons disappear for the buyer.
  5. Raise a PO to a Mumbai vendor → submit → PO appears in Pending Approvals; Send, Receive and Convert to Bill are unavailable until approved.
  6. Approve the PO → Send becomes available; approval history shows who approved and when.
  7. Raise the same PO to a non-Mumbai vendor under R50,000 → no approval demanded.
  8. Direct vendor bill (no PO) → payment blocked if §6.5 was enabled; if not enabled, this test fails by design and is recorded as accepted risk.
  9. Reject a PO with a reason → buyer can edit and resubmit; both attempts visible in approval history.
