Two bedrooms in Cape Town: the best buy for Airbnb, and what it actually returns

Four real properties — one on auction, one fixer-upper, one new build, one beach flat — costed to the rand against live asking prices, sampled nightly rates, and the City's own seasonality. One of them clears a 10 per cent net return. The rest do not.

Prices: Property24 live stock, 25 Oct 2026 · Rates: Airbnb 2-bed sampling, Dec and Jul windows · Occupancy: AirROI Cape Town monthly · Finance: prime 10.75 % (SARB, 22 Oct 2026)

01 · The verdict

Buy the auction lot in Observatory: 515 Obs Court, 3a Howe Street — 63 m², two bedrooms, two bathrooms, parking, pool. Bidding opens at R1 850 000 on 12 November and the platform charges no buyer commission.

On my numbers it returns R450,223 a year in short-let income and R257,117 after every operating cost — a 11.8% net yield on R2,170,286 all-in. With 80 % debt at 11.75 %, the cash left after interest and tax is 7.1% on R690,286 of your own money, and the ten-year internal rate of return, on 6 % a year capital growth, is 16.5%.

02 · Why not Sea Point

The Atlantic Seaboard earns the highest nightly rate in the city. It also costs four times as much to get in, and the arithmetic does not forgive that.

A two-bedroom in Sea Point asks a median R8,400,000 today — 160 such listings on the market. It earns about R2,962 a night across the year, roughly R499,551 gross. That is a 7.6% gross yield on the capital it takes to buy it. After levies, rates, cleaning, management, utilities and repairs you keep 6.1% before tax — and your bond costs 11.75 %.

The same money in Observatory or Woodstock buys a unit at a third of the price for about 70 % of the nightly rate. That is the whole trade: in short-letting, revenue is capped by the nightly rate, but the return is divided by the purchase price.

The Airbnb route is worth about R99,665 a year more than that, for two hundred turnarounds and a licensing file. Below roughly 32 % occupancy the extra work stops being paid for.

06 · The thing that could end it

Cape Town is writing a short-term letting by-law right now, and it is aimed at exactly this property.

The draft by-law — open for public comment until 5 November — requires every short-let to be registered with the City, moves any property let for more than 183 nights a year out of the residential rates category, and hands neighbours and the City an enforcement route that does not exist today. Commercial rating on a R1.85 m flat is roughly three times the residential bill, and takes effect from 1 July 2027.

Two more, specific to this lot: a sectional-title block can ban short-letting in its conduct rules, and an auction sale is unconditional. Both are checked before you bid, not after.

07 · Before 12 November

  1. Get the conduct rules for Obs Court and a written answer from the trustees on short-letting, plus the levy schedule and any special levy approved but not yet billed.
  2. Bond pre-approval for R1.85 m at 80 % — the auction has no bond clause, so finance is a cash question on the day.
  3. Inspect in person at the 25 October show day: water pressure on the fifth floor, the lift, the balcony's aspect, and whether the second bathroom is a full one.
  4. Register to bid with the platform and read the rules on deposits, reserve and commission — confirm in writing that no buyer commission applies.
  5. Quote the short-let insurance and a co-host for turnarounds — Observatory's guest mix means Friday and Saturday changeovers.
