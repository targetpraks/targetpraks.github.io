Zoho MCP: the authorisation loop, automated

Chrome over CDP · one consent per server · paced, resumable, on a timer

The short version

The three-dot → Authorize → tick → Accept loop is now a script. It reads the console's own connection status, authorises only what is not authorised, verifies every row, and a Hermes cron job (zoho-mcp-keep-authorized, every 2 h) keeps it that way — which matters, because the grants lapse. Evidence for that is below.

How it drives the console

StepWhat it doesTrap it survives
Queue GET /baas/mcp/v1/server/<id>/connections?type=native for all 98 servers The status lives in the connection record; the server record does not carry it
Open Fires one fresh tab per server at #/server/<id>/connection The tab the consent redirect lands in cannot rebind Lyte's handlers — the three-dot goes dead and looks like a permissions problem
Three dots Bubbling MouseEvent('click') on svg.table-threedot-class The real binding sits on the enclosing lyte-td.table-toolkit; coordinate presses land on nothing
Authorize Click on lyte-menu-item[data-zcqa="tableMoreOptionCallback"] Dispatching on a child span does nothing; the item is 0×0 until the menu paints (~2–3 s)
Consent Follow the /baas/…/auth popup through its redirect, tick each unchecked box once, click Accept Accept stays disabled until the box is ticked; a second click toggles it back off; the tab's socket resets on success — that reset is the confirmation
Verify Re-read the row until it says Connected with the authoriser's email The row lags the consent by several seconds; a 200 on the click is not a grant

Pacing is 58 s between consents — Zoho's published token limits allow 10 authorisation codes per 10 minutes per user — and a rate-limit page triggers a cool-off instead of a retry storm.

Each consent takes about 25 s of clicking; the rest of the gap is the waiting Zoho's budget requires. By hand, that same loop is roughly two to three minutes per server, with no verification step.

Two things you need to know

1. The grants lapse — that is why the manual loop never ends

Measured on this estate: 79 servers were Authorized at 16:39 and 60 at 17:15, with no revoke action in between. Servers authorised earlier in the day — Zoho Mail (14:38), Zoho Projects (14:31), Zoho Sheet (15:50) — were back to Authorization Required hours later. Authorising is therefore maintenance, not a one-off: the cron job is the fix, not the script alone.

Controlled checks ruled out the obvious alternatives: authorising one server flipped nothing else to required (54 → 54), and authorising a duplicate of an already-connected service (cliq next to cliq-2) disturbed nothing.
