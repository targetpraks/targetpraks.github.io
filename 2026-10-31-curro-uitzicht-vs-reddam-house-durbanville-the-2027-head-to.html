01The answer, in three lines

trajectory first, belonging second

Uitzicht is the better school for him. Its whole design is his week: a maximum of 12 learners, learning-support teachers and an occupational therapist in the room, assessment accommodations written into the prospectus rather than promised in a letter, and the mainstream national curriculum underneath so the door back to a mainstream school stays open. That is not a nicer version of Reddam. It is a different machine.

Reddam is the better home for him. He started there in Grade R in 2023. His sister is there, his football team is there, and the teacher who flagged the problem is the teacher who knows him. No school's structure manufactures belonging, and this is the real weight on the Reddam side. It is not small.

The decision model still picks Uitzicht. 0.89 in the head-to-head and a clear lead in the three-way, both after the priority order you set on 15 October: trajectory first, confidence and belonging second, the mainstream path third, cost last. What it is choosing is staffed capacity over goodwill: at Uitzicht the support is the school's own staff, in the timetable, in the fee; at Reddam the support is a named package the school has not yet committed to paper.

So the answer is Uitzicht, with one question back at it

The version of Uitzicht that wins is the supported Grade 4 entry, not the repeat. The specialist school has offered the place on condition that he repeats Grade 3; you asked Veronica Handley on 31 October to cost the alternative. If Uitzicht will take him into Grade 4 with the same in-class support, it takes every point in the comparison and gives back the year. If it will not, the repeat is the price of the better structure, and it is still ahead of Reddam on this framing.

03What actually decides it: staffed, or promised

the only row that matters

Strip out the campuses, the sport and the marketing, and the question is whether an adult who is paid to do it will sit with him during the school day. Reddam has said, in writing, that Grade 4 would need a reader, a scribe and ideally a facilitator. That is a professional judgement, and it is the most valuable sentence the school has produced. It is not yet a staffing plan.

The evidence on the Reddam side cuts both ways. Support is real: there is a learning-support function, a speech and language programme, and a reading intervention already running beside his class. But the capacity the school is adding for 2027 is advertised as fixed-term, Early Learning and Junior Preparatory only, reporting to the Head of Early Learning and Junior Prep, closing 2 November 2026. He is in Senior Prep next year. The post being hired is for the phase below him.

The one thing nobody has priced on either side

A parent-contracted facilitator is an employment, not a school fee: salary, UIF, leave cover, and the risk that they resign in March. Budget for the agency and a replacement, not the monthly rate. Uitzicht's fee letter excludes "any external interventions by educational therapists or psychologists" too, so the speech therapy that already sits in his week does not disappear because the school changed.

Two small lines that belong in the decision anyway. Reddam gives 3% off the second and subsequent children, with the eldest charged at the standard rate — so if Donatello leaves, Gianna becomes the eldest and the discount on her fee falls away, about R3,092 a year on the 2026 Grade 2 fee. And Uitzicht's fee excludes textbooks, stationery, outings and any external therapy or psychology; its matric reader or scribe is R460 per exam sitting.

Two caveats, because they are part of the evidence. The three-way confidence was low (0.28) and the pairwise moderate (0.51): the gate separates the schools clearly and does not separate the two Uitzicht entry points well. Treat 0.63 against 0.33 as "the repeat is the more defensible default, and the supported Grade 4 is the better outcome if the school will do it" — not as a settled ranking.

This is a second, separate run from the 26 October one

The 26 October gate was asked a different question — repeat at Reddam or progress at Reddam, assuming the package was committed — and answered Reddam at 0.98. That answer stands for that question. Today's question is which school, and the same model, told that Reddam's support is uncommitted and that its 2027 support hire is junior phase, puts Uitzicht ahead either way. When two runs disagree, read the question, not the number.

4Uitzicht: will you take him into Grade 4 in 2027 with the same in-class support, instead of repeating Grade 3?A yes makes Uitzicht the answer on every criterion and gives the year back. A no keeps the repeat on the table.
5Uitzicht: for the repeat year, what is the written plan — programme, hours a week, who delivers literacy, and what has to be true for him to move to Grade 4 in 2028?A document with a review date. This is the same three-part question you sent Veronica on 31 October.
6Both: will the school run a formal review at the end of Term 1 2027 against his September 2026 reading baseline?A date in the calendar and a named owner, agreed before anyone signs.

One structural risk to put on the record for Uitzicht: the high school's published support team is a curriculum coordinator and a counsellor, not the primary's learning-support teachers and OT, and Grade 8 allows up to 15 in a class. The primary model is the strong one. When you visit, ask what the Grade 8 year looks like for a boy who still needs a reader.
