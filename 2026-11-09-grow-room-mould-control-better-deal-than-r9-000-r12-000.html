5 What the proposal gets wrong

  1. The total is not backed by any quote. Two of the three supplier lines are planning guesses — the document says so itself: "indicate budgets, not current confirmed quotes". The ACDC line is the weakest: a search of their catalogue returns no portable air conditioners, so R8,000–R11,000 has no supplier behind it.
  2. The cheapest line hides the real gap. The R4,999 Alliance unit is a genuine promotion but it is 12,000 BTU, not the 14,000 BTU the proposal specifies. Comparing R4,999 against a 14,000 BTU need is comparing two different machines.
  3. Running cost is ignored entirely. A portable unit is the most expensive way to buy cooling. On a grow room that runs lights and a compressor together, the electricity outweighs the purchase price inside the first year — see below.

7 The flaw in the design, not just the price

A portable unit has one hose. It blows the room's air — the air you just paid to cool and dry — out through that hose. The room then pulls replacement air in through every gap and door crack, and that replacement air is the humid outside air the mould is feeding on. The harder it works, the more moist air it drags in.

The proposal's own warning is the right one, and it points at the same conclusion: exhausting air outside does not by itself hold 50–55% RH. A split unit sits on the wall, cools the room air and never throws it away — no hose, no negative pressure, and it dehumidifies as a by-product. It also runs at roughly half the electricity.

9 Before anything is paid for

  1. Do the load sum. Lighting wattage × 3.412 gives the BTU the lights alone put into the room. If that exceeds about 9,000 BTU, 12,000 BTU is too small and the 14,000 BTU TCL at R6,699 is the honest buy.
  2. Ask for a written quote — Alcell on the TAC-14CHPB, or AC Direct on the Haier Real ECO 12,000 BTU bundle. Model number, price, install scope, delivery date, warranty terms. Every figure here is a live web price, not a quotation.
  3. Ask whether the room is sealed. If it is not, a portable unit's negative pressure is the whole problem. A split removes that variable entirely.
  4. Confirm condensate handling. The TCL is self-evaporating; the proposal is right to flag that this must be confirmed, not assumed.
  5. Do not buy the GMC 14,000 BTU yet. It is the best unit on this list and it is not on sale until end November.
