01 The rules this sheet is built on

One body, one mind

Each child picks one physical thing and one thinking or belonging thing. More than that and the school week plus the reading programme stops holding.

Only in a free slot

An option that collides with the school timetable is not an option, however good it is. Six of the fifteen do.

R600 a month each

the ceiling

Outside school, per child. That buys almost everything on this page except coding, which costs R1 120 on its own.

Why this is not another school-sport list. The school already gives them a Tier 1 sport every term — cricket and hockey for Donatello, Sports Hour and swim squad for Gianna. What a child needs outside that is the thing school cannot give: a club that is not school, an adult who is not a teacher, and a Saturday with a purpose. That is the filter used below. 08 Running the choosing with them

  • Give the cards, not the lecture. Two ticks each, one body and one mind. A child who has chosen the Saturday activity turns up to it; a child who was enrolled does not.
  • Ask what they want to be able to do, not what they want to join. "I want to be able to do a lap without stopping" is a goal you can test in six weeks. "I want to do karate" is a mood.
  • Trial first, pay after. Sheen, Anax and Gym Wizards all offer a free or trial session. Norval and the cricket club let a child come and watch. Use that — it is the cheapest part of this page.
  • One term, then a review. Anything not being asked about by week six gets dropped without argument. The point is the habit, not the enrolment.
  • Protect the empty Saturday. If both children end up with Saturday morning fixtures, the family loses the one morning that has no timetable. Pick one child per season for the Saturday slot.
