The one rule that governs all of it

An SLA is not a hunting book. The franchisee already buys from us. There is no cold list, no discovery, no prospecting — so the SLA's outbound campaign runs once for all four brands together, and the SLA itself is measured on renewals, upsell and account health.

Costing an SLA like a pipeline brand overstates the SDR side by 2.9× and the Deal Maker side by 1.4×. That single modelling error is what made this look like a hiring problem.

What that means in one line

Why the Deal Maker fills up faster than the SDR

The SDR's work is throughput — 5-minute calls, 4-minute emails. Twice as many calls is a longer list, not a harder job.

The Deal Maker's work is diary-bound. Sixty-eight meetings a month means 68 fixed slots, each needing preparation, a proposal and three follow-ups around it. The ceiling is not effort; it is the number of hours a person can sit in a room. That is why the Deal Maker carries 2.5–3 brands where the SDR could theoretically carry more.

Read against the current matrix

Olwethu is carrying 8 live brands. A full month absorbs 3.16. That is why he sits at 253% — not because he is slow, but because the ceiling is 3 brands and the form has given him 8.

Nkuli is carrying 8 on the closing side. A full month absorbs 2.5–3. Same arithmetic, same result: 239% of a month, before a single management hour is counted.

What this does not change

A ceiling is not a plan

A maximum-load month assumes zero slack — no sick days, no leave, no ramp-up, no bad week, no time to think. It is the number to measure against, not the number to run at.

The plan on the next two pages runs people at 70–97% instead, which is where a team can actually be held for twelve months without burning out. Maximum load tells you what is possible; the matrix tells you what is sustainable.

The Sheet — And What Has Not Been Captured

The tracker is well built. The capture discipline is already designed into it: every row asks for the weekly number and the proof link beside it, and the sheet states the rule outright — "No proof = the KPI does not count." That is the right rule. The question is whether it is being followed.

The October position, stated plainly

No KPI row on the current month has been filled. Several rows carry a note such as "Pending until Week 4" or "Not applicable yet" in the comment column rather than a number and a proof link. Under the sheet's own rule, the month currently scores zero across every function — including Marketing, which had been the one function fully compliant in earlier months.

The Scorecard View confirms it

The Scorecard tab is built as "one page per person, per month — for one-on-ones". Opened for Olwethu on INF Consultancy it returns "Verified score: Nothing verified", with the sheet's own note beside it: "Unverified KPIs: 0 — must be zero before this month can be signed off."

Every KPI row on that scorecard — all nine of them, response time, meetings booked, cold calls, emails, LinkedIn, discovery, research, pipeline report and training — returns a blank target, blank actual, blank percentage and blank status. The scorecard has been built, wired and documented; it is simply not being fed.

The five things the sheet does not capture at all

#GapConsequence
1CRM hygiene is not a row on the formIt is required daily — every record clean, every stage change made when it happens — and it is measured nowhere, so it competes invisibly against every other target.
2Travel is not a row on the formA closer driving to site loses 30 minutes a trip and there is nowhere to put it. The person doing the most driving looks identical to the person doing none.
3Management time is not costed anywhereNkuli manages the team and carries eight brands on the same form, as though running the team were free.
4Dead brands still carry live targetsFluxFlow, Papa Pasta and KPD inflate every total on the sheet and can never be scored.
5Ownership is inconsistentOne Marketing owner appears under two different names across the sheet, and CLI – KPD has Owner = None with a full target set beneath it.
The KPI changes the scorecard needs

Structural (the four that caused the overload): cap books per person at 2–3 pipeline brands; pool the hunting target group-wide; pool the SLA outbound into one campaign; cost management time as protected hours.

Role definition: give the SLA brands account-management KPIs instead of hunting ones; price research at the Apollo standard of 10 minutes; add travel as a costed per-book line.

Discipline: zero unverified rows; every line signed off before month end; hold capture above 90% of expected rows; and remove the three dead brands.

Why the form is worth keeping

It is not the form that is broken

It captures. It asks for proof beside every number. It has a scorecard built per person for one-on-ones, and it states its own rule in plain language. On the evidence of earlier months, when the rows are filled, the thing works — Marketing scored 100% and above, month after month, on this exact sheet.

What it does not yet do is describe the work accurately: it prices a franchisee SLA like a cold market, it counts the same outbound campaign once per brand, and it leaves out hygiene, travel and management time. Fix those four modelling errors and the same form becomes a fair scorecard instead of an impossible one.

A matrix, not a hiring round. A form fix, not a performance problem.

Nine brands, five people, one month each. Pool the work that repeats, centralise the hunt, treat the SLAs as the accounts they are — and move two books onto the desks with room. That brings everyone inside a working month at zero cost, and makes the scorecard measure the job people are actually doing.

The only two things left are decisions: which book sits where, and whether the capture rows get filled this month.
