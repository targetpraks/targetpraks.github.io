Why Greece is not South Africa with a different flag

Six things change the arithmetic, and two of them change it a lot.

1. The machine lands cheaper — much cheaper

South Africa charges about 20% import duty on a vending machine under HS 8476, plus NRCS approval and local clearing. The EU charges 1.7% on the same heading. The same Chinese cabinet that lands at R95 000 in Johannesburg lands at about €6 000 in Piraeus — and 24% VAT on the import is recoverable if you are VAT-registered, so it is a cash-flow item, not a cost. That single change moves every payback on this page.

2. The ticket is bigger and the euro is strong

A freddo is €2.50. A Greek snack is €1.30. A protein shake clears €3.50. Convert at €1 = R18.58 and a Greek machine earns more rand per sale than a South African one, from a machine that cost less. That is why the median payback drops from 40 months to 30.

3. Labour and power are dearer

The minimum wage is €920 gross a month from April 2026 and the employer pays 21.79% on top through e-EFKA, so a body costs about €1 120 before you hand them a phone. Industrial power sits near €0.23/kWh against roughly €0.14 in South Africa, which punishes every refrigerated machine.

4. Tax is lower, and the islands pay less VAT

Corporate income tax is 22% against 27% in South Africa. And Greece still applies a reduced VAT regime on a list of islands that includes Rhodes — 17% instead of 24% — which is a small gift on every consumer sale there.

5. The country has a season

Athens trades twelve months. Rhodes trades from March to November and then largely stops. The model runs an island at 84% of an Athens year: nine months at full, three at 35%. That is applied to revenue, not to cost — the rent and the compressor do not take the winter off.

6. The competition is the periptero

Greece already has a national convenience network: the kiosk on the pavement, open all hours, selling water, snacks, coffee, phone credit and cigarettes, with a licence that costs nothing per square metre because it is standing on the public footpath. You cannot beat a periptero on a snack. You beat it where it is not: inside a factory, a hospital, a hotel lobby, a marina, a campsite, a beach with no kiosk.

Where it actually goes

The Greek site list is not the South African one. These are the addresses that matter.

Factories and industrial estates — Aspropyrgos, Elefsina, Oinofyta, Thessaloniki

The PPE and tool-crib market. Greek industry is smaller than South Africa's mining complex but it is concentrated around Attica and Thessaloniki, which is easier to route than a mine in Limpopo. Same argument as the South African page: the plant buys control of its consumables, not gloves.

Hospitals, clinics and universities

Sanitary products, water, coffee, condom dispensers. In Greece these are usually funded installations — a municipality, a university or an NGO pays for the machine and the service. That is a tender market, not a route market.

Hotels, marinas, campsites and ferry ports

Beach kit, ice, water, freddo, adapters. Six dense months, then a closed gate. Only worth it if the site pays the power and you can lift the machine out in October.

Gyms — Athens, Thessaloniki, Patras, and the big island towns

The protein machine. Greek gyms are smaller and more seasonal than South African ones, and the shake is competing with a freddo at half the price. It still works at €3.50, but not at a 200-member gym.

Office buildings and co-working

Bean-to-cup, but be careful: in Greece the coffee distributor will place the machine for free on a pay-per-cup contract exactly as Bunacoffee does in South Africa. Assume you are selling the placement, not the cup.

The pattern

Greece wins on capital — the machine lands at a third of the South African price — and loses on volume and season. A Greek format that pays back in 5 months in Athens pays back in 6 on Rhodes; a South African one that pays back in 17 pays back in 17 all year, because Johannesburg has no off-season. If the plan is a route business with a bakkie, South Africa is the better market: bigger, denser, no winter. If the plan is one good machine in one good site, Greece is cheaper to prove.

What I would not do in Greece

Snacks and cold drinks

The periptero sells the same can at the same price with no rent and no machine. Skip it.

Water refill

Athens tap water is drinkable. The whole South African water-ATM case rests on a supply problem Greece does not have.

Parcel lockers as a startup

Greece already has a national locker network with the courier contracts signed. You would be building the fourth network into a market that has three.

Anything that needs a winter

Ice, beach goods, ice cream on an island: these are a season, not a company. If you do it, do it with a site that pays the power and a van that can move the machines in October.
