02 The three ways to buy one from Cape Town

Same frame, three doors. The price gap between them is not small — for an Aura Carver it is more than R3,000.

The one thing that makes route 3 work: Aura does not ship to South Africa from its own site (US / UK / Canada only), but the Aura Home storefront on Amazon UK does ship here — the checkout line reads “£35.71 delivery to South Africa”. Duty is nil on this tariff line; the 15% import VAT is what lands on top, collected at checkout or by the courier.

What the Times Frame actually is

A transparent screen, not a photo printer

  • 10.1″ transparent IPS in a three-layer tempered-glass body, so pictures appear to float in the frame. 64GB internal, Wi-Fi 6, dual-band.
  • Does the job a desk frame does: photos and video from the phone via the free Divoom app, no subscription, multi-user sharing, cloud upload.
  • Plus being a desk object: 400+ clock faces, weather, stock and crypto tickers, countdowns, tomato timer, and pixel art from a 10-million-user community with RGB accents.
  • Not a Google Photos frame. Uploads go through Divoom's app and cloud — no account-level library sync like Aura or Cozyla.

The reviews say the same two things

Best-looking on the desk, dimmer than an LCD

  • The draw is the illusion. Reviewers consistently lead with the floating depth — MacSources calls it “the coolest digital picture frame around”.
  • The catch is clarity. A transparent panel passes light through, so photos read less punchy than an opaque matte LCD — OSReviews notes practical limits on clarity, and The Awesomer flags a flimsy rear backer panel.
  • Which decides the case. For a bright office desk where photos are the point, an opaque Aura still wins on picture. For a desktop piece that shows photos and tells the time, weather and countdown, this beats everything else at its price.

Buy it on Takealot at R2,999 — cheaper than Divoom's own R3,542, with a local 12-month warranty. Direct adds US$15 shipping to South Africa.

Worth knowing: the brand is genuinely one ecosystem — the same app draws the frame's pixel art, runs the karaoke mics and paints the backpack's LED panel. That is why it felt like one brand and not three.

07 The pick, and what it costs you to get it wrong

I put the shortlist to the local decision gate with the priority order spelled out — cloud library first, no subscription second, landed rand cost third, South African availability fourth — because that is the order the August brief set.

Buy this

Aura Carver 10″ Gravel — £124 + £35.71 delivery, ≈R4,178 landed

  • Why: it is the only sub-R4,500 option that pulls your real Google Photos library with no per-photo sending and no subscription, ever.
  • What you give up: landscape only, no local warranty card (Aura support is US/UK-based), and returns would go back to the UK.
  • Alternative colour: the matted Carver Mat at £138 (≈R4,544) if the paper-mat look matters — that is £14 for the better-looking frame.

If you would rather keep it simple and local: Volkano Agenda 15.6″ at R2,199 — big screen, next-day, local warranty, but you send photos to it by hand.

Runner-up

Cozyla Frame 10.1″ — R4,174 on Amazon.co.za

  • The only rival with native Google Photos at the account level, free unlimited storage, long video, no subscription.
  • Priced within R4 of the Aura once VAT is counted, and it comes with Amazon.co.za returns and an earlier delivery.
  • It lost the head-to-head 0.90 / 0.44 — the gate read the Aura's longer track record and unlimited storage as the safer buy for a desk that has to just work.

If local returns matter more than brand pedigree, this is a defensible swap, not a mistake.

Three things to avoid this week

  • Buying Aura from Amazon.co.za. Same product, +78% on the Carver, and a later delivery. The only case for it is the local return address.
  • The 15″ Walden via Amazon UK right now. It quotes 21 December — three weeks slower than every other size — while the price saving over the local reseller (R1,951) is real but the timing kills it for a gift.
  • Treating “Black Friday” on the reseller lines as a discount. Every Desertcart listing carries the banner; every Desertcart price is higher than August's.
The catch on route 3, stated plainly: the parcel lands in South Africa with 15% import VAT payable — collected at checkout by Amazon or by the courier on delivery, depending on the line. There is no local Aura distributor, so a warranty claim is a conversation with Aura support, not a walk-in. That is the price of the R3,280 saving.
