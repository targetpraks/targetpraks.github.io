01 How to use this pack

The vacancy is a new position in a new department — the central kitchen does not exist yet, so there is no incumbent to compare against. Judge the candidates against section 04, not against each other.

  • Post section 03. It is written to be copied straight onto a job board; a plain-text copy sits beside this page as head-chef-advert.md.
  • Screen with section 07. Eight questions, in order. A "no" on question 1 or 6 ends the call politely.
  • Offer inside section 05. Nothing above the ceiling without Ricardo's written approval.
  • Run section 08. The work-profile step is one input to a human decision, never a rejection on its own — the reason is legal, not philosophical.

The one thing to get right

This is a production kitchen, not a restaurant. A brilliant à la carte chef who has never weighed a batch, written a card or signed a temperature log will fail here — and will look excellent in the interview. Question 1 and the practical exist to catch exactly that person.

What the kitchen produces

  • Nine core sauces in batch, portioned for stores — Napoletana, Bolognese, Alfredo, and the six beside them.
  • Fresh pasta for service, extruded and sheeted, in the four formulas the menu runs on.
  • A retail line — fresh artisanal pasta in 500 g packs, labelled and dated, for store shelves.

The head chef owns the standard for all three. Stores cook and plate; they do not make sauce and they do not make pasta. That separation is the whole design — and it means the person in this seat is the brand's palate, not one store's cook.

03 The advert — copy and post

Also as plain text: head-chef-advert.md. Change nothing except the application address.

Head Chef — Central Kitchen (Papa Pasta)

Bedfordview, Johannesburg · Full-time, permanent · R38 000 – R48 000 per month basic · R41 600 – R52 200 cost to company

Papa Pasta is a new pasta concept from Infinity Brands. We make fresh pasta and nine signature sauces in one central kitchen and supply our stores with both — plus a retail line of fresh artisanal pasta in 500 g packs. The kitchen is small on purpose: three people and a driver.

The seat

You own the food standard for the whole brand. Every plate in every store is your batch card, cooked by someone else. That means the pasta is made the same way on a Tuesday in month three as it was on day one, the nine sauces taste the same in every pack, and every batch is weighed, recorded and signed.

You will:

  • run the kitchen day to day — production plan, roster, stock, hygiene, equipment
  • write and hold the batch cards: nine sauces, four pasta formulas, the fresh-pasta pack spec
  • make sauce and pasta with your own hands until a second cook can repeat it
  • own yield and waste: output weight, servings per batch, cost per portion
  • hold the cold chain and the records that prove it — chill, hold, pack, dispatch
  • build the kitchen's food-safety system with the GM, and pass the first audit
  • hire and train the two people who will work beside you

What you must have

  • 5+ years in a professional kitchen, at least 2 in a production, commissary, central or high-volume kitchen — not only à la carte service
  • A recognised culinary qualification at NQF 4 or above (for example the CATHSSETA National Certificate: Professional Cookery, or the Occupational Certificate: Cook) or six or more years of verifiable production-kitchen experience in place of a formal qualification
  • Fresh pasta by hand and by machine — sheeting, extruding, shaping, and the feel for hydration
  • Sauce production at volume — emulsions, cream bases, reductions, and the discipline to hold them
  • Food safety in practice — HACCP principles, temperature control, allergen control, cleaning records
  • Own reliable transport — the kitchen starts at 05:30 and we are not on a taxi route
  • Medically fit to handle food — a certificate from a medical practitioner, arranged after a conditional offer, at our cost

What will set you apart

  • Commissary or central-kitchen experience supplying multiple sites
  • FSSC 22000 or an accredited food-safety management system, not only HACCP awareness
  • Retail or fresh-pack production: weights, seals, dates, shelf-life trials, label checks
  • Costing: recipe costing, yield testing, waste measurement, portion control
  • Having trained a cook from scratch and made them good

The process

  1. Send your CV and a short note on the last kitchen you ran and what it produced.
  2. A 20-minute screening call.
  3. A 90-minute practical in our kitchen, paid at our standard rate — pasta from flour, and one sauce from our card.
  4. A panel conversation and a work-profile questionnaire.
  5. Two references, contacted only with your permission.
  6. Offer.

We read every application. If you have run a production kitchen and can prove a batch card, we want to see it.

Apply: send your CV to the Infinity Brands recruitment desk.

Appointment will be made in line with the company's employment equity plan. The requirements listed above are the inherent requirements of the position.

What is deliberately not on the list

No age, no gender, no language requirement beyond working English, no "own vehicle" as a proxy for anything but the 05:30 start, no years-of-service ceiling, no particular culinary school. Every one of those is either unlawful to require or irrelevant to whether the sauces taste right in month three.

06 The legal frame — what binds this hire

Five rules shape the advert, the process and the offer. None of them are bureaucracy: each one is a place where a badly run hire becomes a dispute.

1 · Hours and overtime — BCEA

Ordinary hours are 45 a week and 9 a day on a five-day week, with a meal interval after five hours of continuous work. Overtime is voluntary and capped at 10 hours a week. (Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997)

The catch: at R38 000+ a month this seat sits above the BCEA earnings threshold (revised every year, and far below this band), which means the statutory overtime and Sunday entitlements do not apply automatically. The contract therefore has to state them. Recommendation: 1.5× overtime, 2× Sunday and public holiday — the market norm, and cheaper than the argument.

2 · The floor — national minimum wage

R30.23 an hour from 1 March 2026, which is about R5 894 for a 45-hour month (2026 rate). Irrelevant to the head chef's band, binding on the peak-hour kitchen assistant, and the reason that seat is costed at R2 620 for 20 hours a week and not a flat R2 000.

3 · The work profile is a psychological assessment — EEA s8

The Employment Equity Act permits psychological testing only where the test is scientifically valid and reliable, applied fairly to all applicants, and not biased against any group. The Constellation work profile qualifies on design, but its norms are still demo norms — statistically provisional.

So it cannot be an automatic rejection. It ranks the shortlist and it hands the panel one question to ask. A "not fit" verdict on a candidate who has already passed the practical is a prompt for a second look with the reason written down — never a silent filter before a human has read the CV. Company pays for the assessment; here, that means it is free and in-house.

4 · Medical testing comes last — EEA Code of Good Practice

Testing to establish health status may only happen once the employer has established the person is competent to do the job, and a conditional offer has been made — and the employer bears the cost (Code of Good Practice, s8). That is why the advert says the food-handling certificate is arranged after a conditional offer, at our cost. It is also why no medical question appears anywhere in the screening script.

5 · Food handlers and the premises — R638

The regulations place the duty for food-handler training on the person in charge of the premises — there is no prescribed course to demand on a CV. A handler who has had a contagious illness may only return to food handling with a medical practitioner's certificate of fitness.

Practically: HACCP or food-safety certificates are a desirable item that improves a candidate, not a legal gate; and the "medically fit to handle food" line in the advert is the company's own standard, applied after offer. (Regulations R638 of 2018)

The eight screening questions

  1. Which kitchens have you run or worked in, and what did each one produce — dishes, or batches?
  2. How many portions a day, and what did you personally weigh or count?
  3. Have you written a batch card, spec sheet or prep list? Could you send me one, with the brand name removed?
  4. Fresh pasta: by hand or machine? What hydration do you work at, and what do you do when the sheet tears?
  5. Biggest sauce batch you have held — how big, in what, at what temperature, and who signed the record?
  6. Our kitchen starts at 05:30 in Bedfordview. How do you get here?
  7. Tell me about the last food-safety audit or inspection you were part of and what it found.
  8. Notice period, and what salary are you looking for?

The six interview questions

  1. Tell me about a batch that went wrong at two hundred servings. What did you do in the next hour, and what changed after?
  2. A cook repeats the same step wrong twice. Walk me through what you say the second time.
  3. You open the walk-in at 05:30 and it is reading 8 °C with 40 kg of sauce inside. Next hour, minute by minute.
  4. Your sauce costs R4.20 a portion and the target is R3.60. What do you change first, and what do you refuse to change?
  5. The GM wants a new sauce on the menu on Friday. You have no card for it and the kitchen is at capacity. What happens?
  6. Tell me about the last person you trained from nothing to working on their own. How long did it take, and how did you know?

Red flags, in the order they usually appear

  • Talks about menus and plating, never about process, weights or records.
  • Cannot name a single temperature, or a single number, for anything they have run.
  • Every failure belongs to someone else — the supplier, the owner, the staff.
  • Wants a head chef title on a chef de partie record, and says so in the first ten minutes.
  • No written artefact of any kind. In a production kitchen that is the whole job.

08 Where the work profile fits

The Constellation profile is not a personality quiz bolted onto the process. It answers a question the interview cannot: will this person hold a written standard when nobody is watching, and can they still taste?

  • The recruiter's output is one page: the verdict, the three heaviest dimensions, the one flag, and the one question the panel should ask.
  • Weighted, not decisive. Strong and fit support the practical result; stretch and not-fit are grounds to look again, in writing, before the panel decides.
  • The practical outranks the profile. Food another cook can repeat beats any score. The profile explains the gap between two candidates who both cooked well.
  • Consent and fairness: explain what it is, that it is not a test of intelligence or personality type, that the company pays, and that a result is only ever one input.

The design behind it — 27 measured dimensions across six instruments, eleven rules, four worked candidates through the app's real verdict — is in the role-design report. That document is for the executive; this one is for the recruiter.
