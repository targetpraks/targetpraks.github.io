Why this is the root cause. Tasks inside an undated container inherit no programme date, which is how 72 open tasks ended up with no date at all. Dating the containers addresses the whole class; chasing individual tasks does not.

§6 Recommendations

  1. Date the containers. Set start and end dates on the 11 undated projects. This is a single sitting of work and removes the structural cause.
  2. Sweep the recoverable band. The 183 tasks 5–10 days late are the cheapest win; most will be a status update rather than real work.
  3. Decide the recurring block. The largest project's overdue load appears to be recurring checklist work. It needs a rules decision — close-and-recreate, or roll forward — not individual chasing.
  4. Treat the two site programmes as one. They share a task set across several contributors; report on them together.
  5. Clear unowned work. Work sitting against placeholder assignees is invisible to every owner, and should be assigned or closed.
  6. Re-baseline before the next review. Agree the definition of overdue (due date plus working days) so the number is comparable month to month.
