The pick — best quality

Suēle Soleira Pro Panel 1500W — R24 897

It is the only device here you can verify rather than believe: independently measured irradiance at two distances, published EMF and spectrum reports, TGA ARTG 452667 plus CE and FDA marks, a three-year local warranty, and the rolling stand in the box. The decision gate put it first at 0.94 probability (confidence 0.86).

If R24 897 is more than you want to spend, the honest second answer is the Soleira 350W set at R5 169 — same lab reports, same three-year warranty, a fifth of the price, a quarter of the coverage.

The one thing that decides this

Wattage on these pages is a sales number — most of it leaves the panel as heat. What reaches your skin is irradiance, in milliwatts per square centimetre, at a stated distance. So the first question is not “how many watts” but “is the number they publish even possible?”

You can bound that from the device's own figures. If a panel draws 95 W and spreads its light over 1.22 m², then even converting every last watt to light — which no LED does — the average across that area tops out at 7.8 mW/cm². That is arithmetic, not opinion.

What this does not prove. These are published figures, not my measurements, and irradiance stated at 10 cm is not directly comparable to one stated at 3 inches or at no distance at all. The ceiling test only catches claims that are impossible — it does not certify the ones that pass. Suēle is the only seller here that publishes the meter work behind the number.

Why the Sports Mat finishes last

It is the most expensive thing after the Pro panel, it covers your whole body, and it is the only one I would steer you away from. The 400 mW/cm² headline is not a rounding error or a generous reading — it is off by one to two orders of magnitude for a 95 W device covering 1.22 m². Two further details point the same way: 1328 LEDs drawing a total of 95 W means 0.07 W per chip, and the warranty is not stated at all.

None of which makes it useless. A wrap works by contact and coverage rather than intensity, and it is the only device here that treats your back and legs at once. But you would be buying it on the strength of a number that is not true, at a price where Nuera’s own X900 — R11 390, 900 W, 180 LEDs, 214 mW/cm² at 3 inches, MDSAP/UKCA/CE/FCC/RoHS certified, three-year warranty, in stock — is R270 cheaper and better documented on every axis except coverage. That is the swap I would make.

Quality and value are different questions

Ranked on value — documented dose per rand — the order changes. The Pro panel is the best device and roughly the worst buy per rand; the 350W set is where the evidence is cheapest.

The quality pick ran twice and agreed across both lanes — 0.92 on the cloud leg, 0.94 on the local model — so it does not rest on one roll. The value question did not settle: the multi-way put the 350W set a hair ahead, and the confirming pair both leaned to the Pro panel instead, on answers of 0.49 and 0.56 that are barely off a coin flip. Read value as a genuine toss-up between those two, not as a verdict. The quality answer — which is what you asked — is not close.

If you buy

  • Pro panel, colour. Black is sold out; the white variant is the one in stock at R24 897. Both are one variant of the same SKU, so confirm the colour before you check out.
  • The 350W set beats the panel alone. R5 169 for the panel plus two Solīs Red bulbs, against R5 595 for the bare panel. The set is cheaper than the single unit — buy the set.
  • Ignore collection pages for stock. Suēle’s catalogue grid printed “Sold out” on every product while the product pages were taking orders. What I checked was the variant-level availability, not the tile.
  • The NeuroActive needs its stand. R9 425 is the panel alone; the adjustable stand is a separate R4 549 and the page marks it “sold separately”. The buyable unit is R13 974, and the stand carries only a one-year warranty.
  • Nothing here is a medical device purchase made on my advice. This is a comparison of published specifications. Decide on your own use with your own doctor if you are treating something.
