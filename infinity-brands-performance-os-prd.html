1. The decision, in one page

Infinity Brands does not have a KPI problem. It has four systems, each doing a different job, and no spine between them.

  • The OKR sheet says what the company committed to — 375 key results across 5 brands and 4 quarters — and carries no actuals, no percentages, no scores. It has never been measured.
  • The S&M tracker captures weekly activity in 4,200 rows — and 22 of its 25 KPIs are activities, not results.
  • Luke's V3 is the best-built artefact in the set: 9 enterprise KPIs weighted to exactly 1.00, 54 BU outcomes weighted to exactly 1.00 per unit, 294 HOD accountabilities, a scoring band model, a PIP/PGP path. It is also entirely empty — zero actuals, 14 individual rows, an empty exceptions register, and a live state that says "Design / Prototype — scoring is not yet used for live decisions."
  • ClickUp MANCO now holds a generic OKR template — 5 lists, 27 parent tasks, 77 tasks including subtasks — of invented Silicon Valley objectives that belong to nobody. Not one row is Infinity Brands.

The company has been trying to make one of these systems do all four jobs. That is why each of them half-works.

The proposal: keep four systems, give each exactly one job, and put a single ID spine through the middle so a KPI is defined once and referenced everywhere. Adopt V3's ID scheme and weight model as the spine — it is already correct, and inventing a second scheme would be the fifth system. Put authoring and the monthly close in ClickUp, where people already work. Keep the numbers in Zoho, where the evidence lives. Delete the ClickUp template content, not the folder.

What it buys: one place to state a target, one place to type a number, one place to see if the month closed, and an audit trail that survives a person leaving.

What it costs: about 55 roles' worth of KPI definition work, one monthly close cycle run end-to-end by hand, and your nine decisions in §14.

2.5 The one sentence that explains all of it

Across three files there are 52 ways to spell a KPI type, 17 ways to spell a pillar, 13 ways to spell a unit, and 59 IDs with no content. Every defect downstream — a formula that reads the wrong column, a dashboard showing 273%, a quarterly plan with no monthly number — is a symptom of free text standing where a register should be.

3. Four systems, one job each

The fix is not a fifth spreadsheet. It is to stop each system doing someone else's job.

System Its one job It must not
Zoho Sheet — V3 engine Compute. Hold weights, formulas, bands, roll-ups, history. Be the single arithmetic truth. Author objectives. Accept typed totals. Hold a second copy of a number.
Zoho — source systems & capture Hold the evidence: CRM deals, Euphoria calls, Xero cash, WorkDrive proof. Capture the weekly number where it is born. Score anyone. Roll up a BU. Decide a band.
ClickUp — MANCO Author the objectives and key results. Run the monthly close. Hold the exceptions and the work that moves a KPI. Hold an Actual, a percentage or a computed score. It reads them; it never owns them.
Zoho People The HR record: scores at month close, PIP/PGP cases, the formal process. Be a design surface. Be edited by anyone but HR.

Rule of one writer: each object has exactly one writable home. If an objective can be typed in two places, it will be typed in two places, and one of them will be wrong by the end of the quarter. This is not a theory — June 2026 already exists twice in the S&M tracker under two spellings.

4. The spine: two axes, five layers, four words

4.1 The two axes — and why the two files cannot merge without them

The OKR sheet is keyed by brand: INF, EXP, TLF, FXS, FXL. The V3 workbook is keyed by business unit: FIN, HRM, ADM, ITC, MKT, SAL, BND1, BND2, FXS.

These are not two spellings of one thing. They are orthogonal: a brand is who the result belongs to, a BU is who delivers it. A marketing result for Express is EXP × MKT. A finance result for the group is ALL × FIN.

This is the reason a straight merge of the two files always failed. Neither axis is wrong; both are incomplete alone.

The spine:

Axis Codes Used for
Brand INF · EXP · TLF · FXS · FXL · PPP · DID · ALL Commercial vertical. Required on BU outcomes, key results and capture rows. ALL at enterprise and HOD level.
Business Unit FIN · HRM · ADM · ITC · MKT · SAL · BND1 · BND2 · FXS Function that owns delivery. Required on every object.

Every object in the system carries both. The pair is the cell; the enterprise result is the row total; the BU scorecard is the column total.

4.2 The five layers

Layer ID pattern Count today Owner Cadence
L0 — Enterprise result BUS-REV-01, BUS-CASH-01 … 9 EXCO / MD Monthly capture, quarterly review
L1 — BU outcome FIN-01, MKT-03, FXS-02 … 54 HOD Monthly
L2 — HOD accountability CFO-01, RKPI-MKT-03 … 294 HOD (personal) Monthly
L3 — Individual outcome RKPI-{BU}-nn 14 built, ~55 roles needed Employee, approved by HOD Monthly
L4 — Capture record one row per KPI × week × month 0 today Capturer Weekly, closes monthly

The chain is a straight line and every layer points up exactly one level: L4 → L3 → L2 → L1 → L0. No object may skip a level, and no object may point at two parents.

V3 already names these layers and already has the IDs. We adopt them as-is. A new scheme would be the fifth system.

4.3 The four words

V3's Read Me defines four types. The file itself uses 52 spellings of them. We fix the vocabulary and nothing else:

Type The question it answers Required fields
Outcome What actually changed because the work happened? Baseline · Target · Direction · Unit · Source
Action Did I do the required work, by the required time? Deadline or SLA · Evidence
Control Was the work correct, controlled, supportable? Standard · Sample · Pass/fail rule
Leadership Did the team or process become measurably better? Baseline · Target · Named team · Source

Two hard rules from V3's own Golden Rules, kept: an Action may never be the only thing a person is scored on (minimum one Outcome per role), and completing an Action never implies the Outcome was achieved. That distinction is the single most valuable thing in Luke's workbook and it must survive the rebuild.

5.1 Fields every object carries

Retired is how anything leaves this system. There is no delete. The 59 empty ID holes in the OKR sheet are what deletion leaves behind, and they took a quarter of the file's integrity with them.

5.2 What is genuinely new in this model

Three things V3 does not have and the company has never had:

  1. A Verifier separate from the owner. Today every KPI has an owner and nobody checks it. That is why 4,200 capture rows sit unsigned.
  2. An Effective From date on targets and owners. V3 has Effective From on the individual master but not on the BU or HOD layers. Without it, changing an owner rewrites history — and in the OKR sheet, changing an owner rewrites the ID and breaks all 18 unit tabs.
  3. A period lock. A closed month never recalculates. A target changed after the period opens requires an approver and is logged.

6. OKRs and key results: how they join to KPIs

This is the part the ClickUp template gets structurally wrong, and the part that decides whether this rebuild lasts.

An OKR is a bet. A KPI is a measure. They are different objects and they never duplicate each other.

Object What it is Where it lives Count
Objective The bet for a period, in words, owned by one person ClickUp, Objectives list 52 today
Key Result A measure bound to a period and a target — the test of whether the bet landed ClickUp, subtask of its objective 375 today
KPI The measurement definition: unit, direction, source, baseline Zoho engine 9 + 54 + 294 + ~55×3

The join: every Key Result must carry a KPI ID. A key result is not a new measure — it is a KPI, bound to a quarter, with a target and an owner. If two key results in the same period name the same KPI with different targets, that is a conflict and the gate catches it. No one in this company has ever been able to see that conflict, because 375 key results have never been linked to anything.

The consequence for the template's model: objectives as tasks and key results as subtasks is the right shape. What is wrong is that the template creates 375 free-floating text statements with no field pointing back at a measure. We keep the shape and add the field.

The restraint the panel imposed last round still stands: of the 375 key results, the ones that pass the activation gate in §7 become Active; the rest stay Draft and are visible as Draft. There is no quota — whatever passes, passes. The gate decides, not an editor with a target for how many objectives a company should have.

7. Targets and weights — the activation gate

7.1 A target is not a target until seven things are true

A key result or KPI may move to Active only when all seven are present and approved:

  1. Target — a number, in a defined unit.
  2. Owner — one named person.
  3. Cadence — monthly capture, weekly where the evidence demands it.
  4. Source — the named system the number comes from.
  5. Weight — a percentage that makes its parent sum to 1.00.
  6. Baseline — the number the target is measured against, with a date.
  7. Verifier — the department head who will sign the month.

Fail any one and the object stays Draft, visibly Draft, with the missing thing named. Blank plus a named owner beats a plausible guess. No number is ever invented to make a sheet look complete.

Against today's data this gate would pass 265 of 375 key results (71%) and fail 110 — the ones whose metric value contains no number. That is a useful starting position, not a disaster: it tells us exactly which quarter's work is unwritten.

7.2 Weights

  • Every level sums to 1.00. The engine checks it; a person does not.
  • L0 enterprise: nine KPIs, 0.20 / 0.20 / 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.18 / 0.12 / 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.10 — already exactly 1.00, grouped 40 / 30 / 30 by pillar. Keep unchanged.
  • L1 BU: each BU's outcome weights already sum to exactly 1.00. Keep unchanged.
  • L2 HOD: 50% BU result + 35% direct outcomes + 15% leadership drivers. Two approved exceptions (the Finance Managers, 50/35/15 with no BU inheritance). 10 of 21 roles currently balance; the other 11 have 69 rows with blank or zero weight to close.
  • L3 individual: 70% outcomes + 20% actions/drivers + 10% quality/controls. 0 of 11 roles currently balance — the individual layer is a demonstration, and this is the largest single piece of build work in the programme.
  • Scores cap at 100%. Stretch is recognised separately, never by inflating a score.
  • BU and business results are objective business metrics — never the average of employee scores. Kept verbatim from V3. It is the rule that stops the whole model collapsing into a popularity contest.

7.3 Consequences

The bands are Luke's and they are good. Keep them exactly: ≤65 PIP trigger · 66–69 At Risk (HOD + HR) · 70–79 PGP · 80–94 On Track · ≥95 High Performance. Each one triggers a review, never an automatic employment decision — that stays in the wording, because the wording is what protects the company.

Nothing scores until the pilot closes. The engine stays in design mode with the numbers visible and the band column suppressed. A band shown before the process is trusted is how a performance system loses the room in month one.

8. The monthly close

Monthly is the management unit. Quarterly is a reporting grouping — never where work is managed. A quarter is what you show the bank; a month is what you run.

Working day Who What happens Where
Week ending Friday Capturer Types the week's number and attaches the proof link Zoho capture
1st working day Capturer Submits the month for verification ClickUp close task → Captured
2nd working day Verifier (department head) Checks evidence, approves or returns ClickUp → Verified, or an Exception
3rd working day Ricardo Sees only what did not close ClickUp Exceptions + his morning brief
3rd working day Engine Computes scores and bands. Closed months never recalculate. Zoho engine
3rd working day HR Receives scores; opens PIP/PGP only where the band requires a review Zoho People

Three rules that make the close real:

  1. A number without proof does not count. V3's Evidence Inbox is 6 rows and the tracker's sign-off column is 0 of 4,200. Both are proof that the rule has never been enforced.
  2. The verifier is not the owner. No one signs their own month.
  3. An exception is a normal outcome, not a failure. The Exceptions list is where "the CRM report was wrong" or "we missed by 12% and here is why" lives — visible, owned, resolved. A close that produces zero exceptions in month one is not a clean close; it is an unread one.

11.2 Change control

  • A target changed after its period opens requires the layer's approver and is logged with a reason. The old target survives in history.
  • An owner change is effective-dated. It never rewrites what came before. This is the fix for the ID-break trap.
  • A retired object keeps its ID and its history. It stops appearing in the live scorecard. It is never deleted.
  • The Rules & Change Log is the constitution. Any change to a definition, a weight model or a band is a row in it, with who approved it. Without that list, this design drifts back to free text within two quarters — which is precisely what happened to the 52 type spellings.

11.3 Access

MANCO is private with 18 members. That does not change. What changes is that the OKR lists become the only place a commitment is stated, so membership becomes consequential for the first time. Two lists are visible to the whole 18; Exceptions is visible to the owner, the verifier and Ricardo.

12. What we will not do

  1. No fifth spreadsheet. If a new file is needed, an existing one is retired first.
  2. No actuals or percentages in ClickUp. It reads computed numbers; it never owns them.
  3. No quotas. Whatever passes the gate passes. A target number of objectives is how a framework becomes theatre.
  4. No invented numbers. Blank plus a named owner beats a plausible guess, every time.
  5. No averaging employee scores into a BU result. Business results are business metrics.
  6. No deletion. Retirement is a status.
  7. No scoring in design mode. Bands stay suppressed until the pilot closes and someone has read a month end-to-end.
  8. No write to a live business system without your word. Copies, rehearsal, rollback proof, then ask.
