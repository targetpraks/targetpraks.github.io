1. The decision, in one page

Infinity Brands does not have a KPI problem. It has four systems, each doing a different job, and no spine between them.

  • The OKR sheet says what the company committed to — 375 key results across 5 brands and 4 quarters — and carries no actuals, no percentages, no scores. It has never been measured.
  • The S&M tracker captures weekly activity in 4,200 rows — and 22 of its 25 KPIs are activities, not results.
  • Luke's V3 is the best-built artefact in the set: 9 enterprise KPIs weighted to exactly 1.00, 54 BU outcomes weighted to exactly 1.00 per unit, 294 HOD accountabilities, a scoring band model, a PIP/PGP path. It is also entirely empty — zero actuals, 14 individual rows, an empty exceptions register, and a live state that says "Design / Prototype — scoring is not yet used for live decisions."
  • ClickUp MANCO now holds a generic OKR template — 5 lists, 27 parent tasks, 75 tasks including subtasks (77 at the first count, two days earlier: the template is being edited while it sits in review) — of invented Silicon Valley objectives that belong to nobody. Not one row is Infinity Brands.

The company has been trying to make one of these systems do all four jobs. That is why each of them half-works.

The proposal: keep four systems, give each exactly one job, and put a single ID spine through the middle so a KPI is defined once and referenced everywhere. Adopt V3's ID scheme and weight model as the spine — it is already correct, and inventing a second scheme would be the fifth system. Put authoring and the monthly close in ClickUp, where people already work. Keep the numbers in Zoho, where the evidence lives. Delete the ClickUp template content, not the folder.

What it buys: one place to state a target, one place to type a number, one place to see if the month closed, and an audit trail that survives a person leaving.

What it costs: reconciling the scorecards that already exist rather than writing 55 new ones, one monthly close cycle run end-to-end by hand, and your sixteen decisions in §14.

2.5 The one sentence that explains all of it

Across three files there are 52 ways to spell a KPI type, 17 ways to spell a pillar, 13 ways to spell a unit, and 59 IDs with no content. Every defect downstream — a formula that reads the wrong column, a dashboard showing 273%, a quarterly plan with no monthly number — is a symptom of free text standing where a register should be.

3. Four systems, one job each

The fix is not a fifth spreadsheet. It is to stop each system doing someone else's job.

System Its one job It must not
Zoho Sheet — V3 engine Compute. Hold weights, formulas, bands, roll-ups, history. Be the single arithmetic truth. Author objectives. Accept typed totals. Hold a second copy of a number.
Zoho — source systems & capture Hold the evidence: CRM deals, Euphoria calls, Xero cash, WorkDrive proof. Capture the weekly number where it is born. Score anyone. Roll up a BU. Decide a band.
ClickUp — MANCO Author the objectives and key results. Run the monthly close. Hold the exceptions and the work that moves a KPI. Hold an Actual, a percentage or a computed score. It reads them; it never owns them.
Zoho People The HR record: scores at month close, PIP/PGP cases, the formal process. Be a design surface. Be edited by anyone but HR.

Rule of one writer: each object has exactly one writable home. If an objective can be typed in two places, it will be typed in two places, and one of them will be wrong by the end of the quarter. This is not a theory — June 2026 already exists twice in the S&M tracker under two spellings.

4. The spine: two axes, five layers, four words

4.1 The two axes — and why the two files cannot merge without them

The OKR sheet is keyed by brand: INF, EXP, TLF, FXS, FXL. The V3 workbook is keyed by business unit: FIN, HRM, ADM, ITC, MKT, SAL, BND1, BND2, FXS.

These are not two spellings of one thing. They are orthogonal: a brand is who the result belongs to, a BU is who delivers it. A marketing result for Express is EXP × MKT. A finance result for the group is ALL × FIN.

This is the reason a straight merge of the two files always failed. Neither axis is wrong; both are incomplete alone.

4.1a The unit register — BND1 and BND2 are brand operations, not departments

This is the split that has to be exactly right, so it is taken from two files rather than assumed. Both V3 and the OKR sheet describe the same two units, independently:

Code The unit Serves brand V3 says OKR sheet says
BND1 ACDC Express brand operations — franchise network performance, compliance, operational enablement EXP BND1 tab, "BND1 / ACDC EXPRESS BUSINESS UNIT", owner Lauren, 11 mentions of Express 33 KRs — 10 under EXP (EXP-BND 1302 … 1314), 23 under INF
BND2 The Local Farmer brand operations — commercial sustainability, production, fulfilment TLF BND2 tab, "BND2 / THE LOCAL FARMER BUSINESS UNIT", owner Thembi, 21 mentions of TLF 15 KRs — 9 under TLF (TLF-BND 2348 … 2360), 6 under INF
BND3 does not exist yet — brand operations for Titan TITAN none On Hold Titan ZOR tab, 9 rows, waiting for a home

Three things follow, and they are the answer to "is the BND split right":

  1. The unit is fixed to its brand, and still not the same thing as its brand. BND1 is Express operations, but Express marketing is EXP × MKT and Express finance is EXP × FIN. So BND1 is a unit code, EXP is a brand code, and Express work appears on both axes — which is the correct, not the confusing, answer.

  2. BND1's INF rows are group oversight, not Express work — mostly. 23 of its 33 key results sit under INF. Reading them row by row: many name Express network growth, Express participation rates and Titan tie-ins explicitly, so turning all 23 into ALL would erase the distinction the row itself makes. The count is right; the blanket rule is not. Each of the 23 is classified on its own line into ALL × BND1 (group franchise governance) or EXP × BND1 (Express-specific), and the crosswalk is signed by the BND1 owner before import (decision 15).

  3. The label is already broken once. One row spells the unit BND2 with no space (INF-BND2291, Q4) against 14 spelled BND 2. Because the ID is CONCAT(B,K,C), that row's ID loses the space too — so a filter on either the label or the ID pattern silently drops 1 of 15, and the row is invisible in every BND2 total.

  4. One unit has no home at all, and the audit is the first thing to say so. DAS carries 68 of the 375 key results (18.1%) — INF 64, EXP 3, TLF 1 — and DAS appears nowhere in this nine-BU register, in V3's BU list, or in ClickUp's options. It cannot be quietly folded into ADM: 68 rows is not an accounting detail, and the two units do different work. (decision 14.) Until it is answered, 18% of the plan has no delivery unit and the gate refuses those rows — which is the correct behaviour, not a bug.

  5. The register is a primary brand per unit, not an exclusive one. V3's BND2 tab carries a Papa Pasta responsibility (LEB-A05) even though BND2's brand is The Local Farmer, and several BND1 rows name Titan. A unit serves its own brand first and other brands by agreement. The field pair therefore reads: Brand = who the result belongs to; BU = who delivers it — and a unit delivering outside its brand is a real row, not a data error.

And in ClickUp, neither code exists. The Business Unit field carries 13 options at space level and 10 at list level, and BND1 / BND2 are in neither list — while EXP, TLF, PPP and DID, which are brands, are in both. So today an Express operations task would be filed BU = EXP, and the delivery unit disappears: you keep the client and lose the team.

The spine:

Axis Codes Used for
Brand — who it belongs to INF · EXP · TLF · FXS · FXL · PPP · DID · ALL · (TITAN pending) Commercial vertical. Required on BU outcomes, key results and capture rows. ALL for group and HOD-level results.
Business Unit — who delivers it FIN · HRM · ADM · ITC · MKT · SAL · BND1 · BND2 · FXS Function that owns delivery. Required on every object.

FXS is the one code that is legitimately both: Franchise Services is a brand and the unit that delivers it. Documented as the single shared code, not as an accident.

Every object in the system carries both. The pair is the cell; the enterprise result is the row total; the BU scorecard is the column total.

L0 — Enterprise result BUS-REV-01, BUS-CASH-01 … 9 EXCO / MD Monthly capture, quarterly review

Two different arrows, and confusing them is the most expensive mistake available in this document. An alignment arrow says who a result belongs under. A computation arrow says which numbers are added together. They are not the same edge and they do not run the same way:

  • Alignment — L4 evidence under an L3 role KPI under an L2 accountability under an L1 BU outcome under an L0 enterprise KPI. Every object has one parent here, and the parent is a home, not an arithmetic rule.
  • Computation — runs the other way, per composite, and is defined explicitly in §7.2. A BU score is its own measured outcomes. A HOD score is 50% inherited BU result + 35% direct outcomes + the leadership component. An enterprise KPI is measured from the business, never summed from employee scores — §7.2 forbids that in V3's own words.

So: no object skips a level in alignment, and no object has two alignment parents. Nothing in this document infers arithmetic from an alignment edge. A builder who sums a parent from its children has built a different, wrong system.

V3 already names these layers and already has the IDs. We adopt the scheme — after one audit, not as-is. The scheme is sound; the data under it is not yet unique. Measured in 04 Individual KPI Master: 304 rows, 296 distinct IDs — eight strings are used twice (MAR-A01–A05, MAR-O01–O03, Maria and Marie-Claire), and RKPI-MKT-03 / RKPI-SAL-02 appear in both the HOD master and the individual master as separate objects. A global lookup keyed on those strings will attach one person's evidence to another. The audit comes first: legitimate cross-layer references are separated from genuine collisions, collisions get replacement IDs, and the source ID is kept in a crosswalk column so nothing is lost. Then the scheme is frozen and adopted.

4.3 The four words

V3's Read Me defines four types. The HOD master alone uses 51 distinct type strings (50 non-blank) for them — Outcome, Action, Action / Control, Leadership Driver, Outcome / Leadership, Leadership / Enterprise Accountability and 45 more. We fix the vocabulary and nothing else:

Type The question it answers Required fields
Outcome What actually changed because the work happened? Baseline · Target · Direction · Unit · Source
Action Did I do the required work, by the required time? Deadline or SLA · Evidence
Control Was the work correct, controlled, supportable? Standard · Sample · Pass/fail rule
Leadership Did the team or process become measurably better? Baseline · Target · Named team · Source

Two hard rules from V3's own Golden Rules, kept: an Action may never be the only thing a person is scored on (minimum one Outcome per role), and completing an Action never implies the Outcome was achieved. That distinction is the single most valuable thing in Luke's workbook and it must survive the rebuild.

5.2 What is genuinely new in this model

Three things V3 does not have and the company has never had:

  1. A Verifier separate from the owner. Today every KPI has an owner and nobody checks it. That is why 4,200 capture rows sit unsigned.
  2. An Effective From date on targets and owners. V3 has Effective From on the individual master but not on the BU or HOD layers. Without it, changing an owner rewrites history — and in the OKR sheet, changing an owner rewrites the ID and breaks all 18 unit tabs.
  3. A period lock. A closed month never recalculates. A target changed after the period opens requires an approver and is logged.

6. OKRs and key results: how they join to KPIs

This is the part the ClickUp template gets structurally wrong, and the part that decides whether this rebuild lasts.

An OKR is a bet. A KPI is a measure. They are different objects and they never duplicate each other.

Object What it is Where it lives Count
Objective The bet for a period, in words, owned by one person ClickUp, Objectives list 0 today — the sheet contains none; they must be authored
Key Result A measure bound to a period and a target — the test of whether the bet landed ClickUp, subtask of its objective 375 today
KPI The measurement definition: unit, direction, source, baseline Zoho engine 9 + 54 + 294 + ~55×3

The join: every Key Result must carry a KPI ID. A key result is not a new measure — it is a KPI, bound to a quarter, with a target and an owner. If two key results in the same period name the same KPI with different targets, that is a conflict and the gate catches it. No one in this company has ever been able to see that conflict, because 375 key results have never been linked to anything.

The consequence for the template's model: objectives as tasks and key results as subtasks is the right shape. What is wrong is that the template creates 375 free-floating text statements with no field pointing back at a measure. We keep the shape and add the field.

The restraint the panel imposed last round still stands: of the 375 key results, the ones that pass the activation gate in §7 become Active; the rest stay Draft and are visible as Draft. There is no quota — whatever passes, passes. The gate decides, not an editor with a target for how many objectives a company should have.

7. Targets and weights — the activation gate

7.1 A target is not a target until seven things are true

A key result or KPI may move to Active only when all seven are present and approved:

  1. Target — a number, in a defined unit.
  2. Owner — one named person.
  3. Cadence — monthly capture, weekly where the evidence demands it.
  4. Source — the named system the number comes from.
  5. Weight — a percentage that makes its parent sum to 1.00.
  6. Baseline — the number the target is measured against, with a date.
  7. Verifier — the department head who will sign the month.

Fail any one and the object stays Draft, visibly Draft, with the missing thing named. Blank plus a named owner beats a plausible guess. No number is ever invented to make a sheet look complete.

Against today's data, 0 of 375 key results pass that gate. Not 71% — none. That is the single most important number in this document, so here is the arithmetic column by column:

Source — the named system Metric Source (I) 375 of 375

Two conditions are met and five are not. The gate is doing exactly its job: it is telling us that the FY2027 plan as written cannot be measured, cannot be owned and cannot be closed. That is not a reason to lower the gate. It is the work.

What that means in practice, and it is the correction that matters most: the first task is not a ClickUp build. It is a management act — putting a person's name against a key result, and a number against the ones that have none. 375 key results ÷ the 21 HOD roles that would own them is roughly 18 each, which is not a reasonable personal load; the honest answer is that a large share of these 375 are not key results at all but activities, and the gate will say so out loud as it is applied.

7.1a Quarterly to monthly — the rule that was missing

The sheet is quarter-keyed and the close is monthly. Without a stated rule, the first monthly close arrives with nothing to measure, because no single month has a target. The rule:

  • Every quarterly key result carries a monthly target sequence, authored at activation. Three months for Q1–Q4, each with its own number, summing by the metric's own logic — cumulative for a count or a total, a monthly rate for a percentage, a milestone date for a binary.
  • A key result with a monthly sequence missing cannot become Active. It stays Draft with the gap named, exactly like a missing target.
  • The monthly sequence lives in the engine, one row per month, and is what the capture row points at. ClickUp shows the period, never the monthly number.

Two things the rule above does not yet say, and a builder will invent both.

  • Which months are Q1–Q4. The fiscal calendar is stated once, in the engine, and the close reads it: financial year start month, the four quarter boundaries, and what happens to a 53-week year. Today the sheet says Q1 and nothing more.
  • How each metric type decomposes. The five cases, decided per metric, not per key result: flow (a count or a total) splits into three monthly amounts that sum to the quarter · cumulative (year-to-date) stores three cumulative values and never sums — summing them triples the result · stock (headcount, ARPU at a point) takes a monthly value, no split · rate (a percentage) needs its numerator and denominator stored separately, because averaging three monthly percentages is not the quarter's rate · milestone stores a date and a binary, and cannot populate a numeric target at all.
  • Weekly capture into a month: a week belongs to the month holding its last working day, stated once, or two people count the same week in different months.

This is the reason the pilot is one BU for one month: it is the only way to find out whether a month's worth of these sequences can be written at all.

  • Weights are validated by the engine at import, not by eye in ClickUp. A batch whose parents do not sum to 1.00 is rejected whole, with the offending parent named. Typing weights into ClickUp and trusting someone to add them up is how a 0.85 scorecard reaches a person's review.
  • "Sums to 1.00" means the final composite, not the personal rows. Nine HOD roles carry personal rows totalling 0.50 by design, because they inherit the other 0.50 from their BU. Filling those rows to 1.00 would produce 1.50 and double every one of those scores. Blank weight and intentional zero weight are also different things: a zero-weight row is a mandatory control that must be evidenced but not scored, and the engine treats it as required, not optional.
  • Scores cap at 100%. Stretch is recognised separately, never by inflating a score.
  • BU and business results are objective business metrics — never the average of employee scores. Kept verbatim from V3. It is the rule that stops the whole model collapsing into a popularity contest.

7.3 Consequences

The bands are Luke's and they are good. Keep them exactly: ≤65 PIP trigger · 66–69 At Risk (HOD + HR) · 70–79 PGP · 80–94 On Track · ≥95 High Performance. Each one triggers a review, never an automatic employment decision — that stays in the wording, because the wording is what protects the company.

Nothing scores until the pilot closes. The engine stays in design mode with the numbers visible and the band column suppressed. A band shown before the process is trusted is how a performance system loses the room in month one.

And the rule that protects the first month from becoming a false verdict on real people: no evidence, no score. A month whose evidence is missing, late or unverified is recorded as unscored — the engine writes a null, not a zero. It never redistributes that weight across whatever did arrive, because that manufactures a performance finding out of an absence of data. An unscored month cannot trigger a PIP/PGP review. The underlying business miss is reported separately, in the results ledger, where it belongs. The first month this runs will have missing evidence; the only question is whether the system says so or invents a number.

Where the null travels — the sentence the first edition was missing. "Writes a null" describes a cell, and a builder has to know what it does to a score. With weights 0.60 / 0.40 and results 100 and null, a composite that sums what is present reports 60 — a performance finding produced entirely by missing data. So:

  • A composite is null when any required input is null. Not 60. The cell holds "not scored", and the band column stays empty for that object that month.
  • "Required" includes inherited inputs. A HOD's 50% BU component is required: if the BU result is unscored, the HOD is unscored, however much of their own 35% arrived.
  • Partial evidence is not a discount. V3 demos a Partial-evidence month at a discounted 45. That mechanism is removed. Partial is either verified as complete or unscored.
  • An override cannot manufacture a score. A HOD override may annotate, and may never lift a null to a number. Any override is logged with the name, the reason and the month, and appears in the close report.
  • A zero-weight mandatory control blocks the score it does not weight. If the control is unevidenced, the composite is null — the control's weight is zero, its requirement is not.
  • A month cannot be both unscored and banded. No band, no ranking, no HR export for an unscored month. That is what makes an unscored month cost something and stay honest at the same time.

8. The monthly close

Monthly is the management unit. Quarterly is a reporting grouping — never where work is managed. A quarter is what you show the bank; a month is what you run.

Working day Who What happens Where
Week ending Friday Capturer Types the week's number and attaches the proof link Zoho capture
1st working day Capturer Submits the month for verification ClickUp close task → Captured
2nd working day Verifier (department head) Checks evidence, approves or returns ClickUp → Verified, or an Exception
3rd working day Ricardo Sees only what did not close ClickUp Exceptions + his morning brief
3rd working day Engine Computes scores and bands only for months that reached a disposition. Closed months never recalculate. Zoho engine
3rd working day HR Receives scores; opens PIP/PGP only where the band requires a review Zoho People

Three rules that make the close real:

  1. A number without proof does not count. V3's Evidence Inbox is 6 rows and the tracker's sign-off column is 0 of 4,200. Both are proof that the rule has never been enforced.
  2. The verifier is not the owner. No one signs their own month.
  3. An exception is a normal outcome, not a failure. The Exceptions list is where "the CRM report was wrong" or "we missed by 12% and here is why" lives — visible, owned, resolved. A close that produces zero exceptions in month one is not a clean close; it is an unread one.

Three more, added because the panel was unanimous that verification is where this dies:

  1. A month cannot close unverified. If the verifier has not signed by the second working day, the month stays open, not passed. Day three it lands with Ricardo as a Late verification exception, and on day five it escalates to the verifier's own manager. An unsigned month is the one failure mode that quietly becomes a score.
  2. The verifier has a named incumbent per BU before the pilot starts, and the name is in the rules log. Today the verifier is a role with no person in it — which is why the S&M sign-off column reads 0 of 4,200. A role with no incumbent is a promise, not a control.
  3. Nothing in the close depends on one person being at their desk. Every step has a named deputy, written beside the incumbent. The whole design assumes a company where people take leave.
  4. The month always reaches a disposition — that is a duty, not a hope. Rules 1–6 protect the number. They do not, as written, stop a month hanging open forever, and they can produce a loop: the HOD owns the BU result, the HOD is also the department-head verifier, self-signing is forbidden, so the month escalates to Ricardo — and stays open. So the close has one more seat and one more deadline.

The close authority. One named person per group, independent of every BU owner, is the close authority. Their only job is to end the month, and they hold two signatures, not one:

They sign When Effect
Verified The evidence is present and correct The month proceeds to scoring
Unscored — disposition The evidence did not arrive, or arrived unverifiable The month proceeds to a null, published and visible, with the reason and the outstanding evidence named
  • The duty is due on the fourth working day. Missing that date is an exception against the close authority, escalated by rule 4 exactly like a verifier's.
  • Neither signature can be given by the object's owner. If the close authority is also a BU owner, the deputy signs for that BU — and the deputy is named in advance, not found on the day.
  • Inheritance never confers signing rights: a HOD who inherits 50% of a BU result is still not allowed to sign that BU's month.
  • No disposition, no score. The engine will not compute an undismissed month, and an open month produces neither a score nor a band — it produces a visible debt on the close report, which is the honest outcome.
