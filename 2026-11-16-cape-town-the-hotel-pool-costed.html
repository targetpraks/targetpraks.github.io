02

The number that decides the deal

The operator runs sales, pricing, housekeeping, maintenance and the guest relationship. It takes a share of room revenue and you take the rest. That split is the entire business case, and it is not in any of the public material I could find — not on the developer site, not on the operator's partner page, not in the investor brochure. It lives in the sale agreement.

Your unit's income is a product of those two lines, and one of them is now working against you. That is the operating environment the guarantee is protecting you from, and it is why the guarantee exists.

04

1 July 2027: the rules change

Any unit in a rental pool will be rated commercial. Certainty, not risk.

The draft by-law rates a property commercial when it is available to book for more than half the year — measured on nights listed, not nights booked. A pool unit is listed all year. It crosses the line even at low occupancy.

The City's own worked example, on a R2-million studio, using the 2026/27 rates schedule:

Residential, owner's primary residence R806 a month
Residential, not primary residence R1 160 a month
Reclassified business / commercial R2 818 a month
Commercial ratio vs residential base 2.35× — up to +135%

Changes take effect 1 July 2027, from the registration data. Registration on a booking platform becomes mandatory and the registration number must appear on the listing. In my model the Delaporte studio already carries the commercial number — so this is not a cost I am guessing at later, it is the cost you will actually pay.

One thing the by-law does not do: it does not make short-letting unprofitable. The City's own critics say so — the ratepayer association argues landlords will absorb the increase and keep letting. Read it as a margin tax on the model, not an end to it.

The guarantee is a subsidy, not a return

KLEI guarantees 10% of the purchase price for three years. On R3 350 000 that is R335 000 a year — gross, before the costs you still own:

Three years of guaranteed income nets 5.40% — +0.09% against the bank. Then the guarantee ends and the unit trades on its own performance: at the same upscale ADR and a 60/40 split, it nets 3.36%. That is below the bank.

The brochure says gross returns are “expected to reach at least 12% after the third year and increase exponentially thereafter.” Back-solve that: 12% of R3 350 000 to you requires R2 923 ADR at 62.8% occupancy. That is 52% above the upscale-class benchmark and at the very top of the Cape Town city average. It is a number you should ask the developer to show you the working for.

And the guarantee runs “from first transfer” on the rental amount — so it covers the ramp-up years when a new aparthotel has no reputation and no repeat guests. Those are precisely the years it would otherwise lose money. That is what it is for. It is not evidence the asset performs.

The shape of it: a hotel pool is only worth doing at the top of its range. At a good split with genuinely upscale rates it competes with commercial property — real money, real work by somebody else. At a 50/50 split, or at midscale rates, it loses to a government bond, and you carry hotel-level costs, a compulsory furniture pack, parking you rent separately, and a unit you cannot easily live in or sell.

08

What I would ask before signing anything

Four questions, and I would get the answers in writing in the sale agreement rather than in a launch conversation:

1. The split what % of room revenue is mine, and is it before or after the operator's own costs?
2. The levy what is it now, what does it cover, and is the hotel operation inside it?
3. The pack furniture pack price, and what happens to it on resale?
4. The exit can I take the unit out of the pool, and what does that do to the levy and the rates?

If the split is 60/40 or better and the operator will put it in writing, the Delaporte maths works and it beats the bank. If it is 50/50, it does not — and no amount of rooftop pool changes that. The building is not the investment. The contract is.

And my honest preference, unchanged: Brackenfell at 7.20% with no operator between you and the rent, or Woodstock commercial at 8.21% where the tenant pays the rates and you never think about occupancy. The hotel pool is the only lane here where a stranger's pricing decisions determine your return — and where the one number that matters is the one nobody prints.
