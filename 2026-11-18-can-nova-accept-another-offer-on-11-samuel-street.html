Can you accept another offer on Samuel?

Reading of the signed Offer to Purchase dated 16 November 2026 — clause 6, the competing-offer machinery, and the window you have.

Yes — but only through clause 6, and not yet. The property is sold to DMVPP (Pty) Ltd / Dean Vercueil at R4 000 000, and that contract binds you from 16 November. Clause 6 keeps the door open: you may keep marketing and you may end up taking a better offer — after you serve a written notice on the purchaser and they fail to make the sale unconditional within three business days.

You cannot simply sign a second offer while this one is alive. Doing that is a breach, not a substitution.

01 What clause 6 actually gives you

It is the standard Seeff continued-marketing / "competing offer" clause, and it was left completely unamended in this contract — no initials, no strike-through.

Clause 6 — Continued Marketing “The Seller may continue to market the Property until fulfilment of all suspensive conditions contained herein, payment of any deposit due and the fulfilment of any suspensive conditions in the agreement of sale of the other property, where applicable. Should the Seller, during this time, receive another acceptable unconditional offer ("the competing offer") to purchase the Property, he / she will have the right to call upon the Purchaser, by notice in writing, to waive or fulfil all suspensive conditions to which this offer is subject, thereby making this sale unconditional, within 3 (three) days (excluding weekends and public holidays) of the date when such notice is delivered to the Purchaser … Such notice shall include a copy of the competing offer. If the Purchaser fails to waive his / her rights or advise the Seller that the suspensive condition(s) have been fulfilled, in writing within the waiver period, the Seller shall be entitled to, but not obliged, to accept the competing offer, upon which this sale shall lapse and be null and void.”

02 The sequence you must follow

  1. Get a second offer in writing, and unconditional. “Acceptable unconditional” is the gate. A second buyer who also needs a bond does not unlock clause 6 — their offer is conditional, so you cannot use it to force the first purchaser to decide.
  2. Serve written notice on the purchaser, with a copy of that offer attached. Not a phone call to Landon, not a WhatsApp. Written notice on the purchaser, which per clause 22.4 can be delivered by hand or email.
  3. Wait three business days (weekends and public holidays excluded). If the purchaser waives the bond condition in writing, this sale becomes unconditional and binds you at R4 000 000 — the competing offer falls away. If they stay silent, this sale lapses and you are free to accept the other offer.

What you must not do: sign a second Offer to Purchase before that three-day period has run out. The purchaser could then claim specific performance at R4 000 000 or damages, and under clause 22.3.2 your full brokerage — 3% plus VAT, R138 000 — becomes payable by you immediately on your breach, plus costs on the attorney-and-own-client scale under clause 22.5. A second offer is only safe as a written back-up expressly conditional on the first sale lapsing.

03 How long the window stays open

The window closes on the last of three events: all suspensive conditions fulfilled, the deposit paid, and any conditions in the purchaser's own sale of another property fulfilled. Until then, you may market.

06 Loose ends on the signed document

  • Clause 31.1 is blank for the seller's registered entity name and registration number. You signed as authorised signatory for RM Nova Properties (Pty) Ltd, which is described as agent for the registered owner — have the conveyancer insert the entity, registration number and the registered owner's details before lodgement.
  • Clause 25.4 reads inconsistently: “R 380 000” in figures against “Three hundred and eight thousand rand only” in words. In a dispute the words usually govern — have it rectified by addendum, not left as is.
  • Clause 20.1 was amended by hand from a printed 7.5% to 3% and initialled. Landon confirmed in writing on 16 November that the brokerage is 3% plus VAT with no additional costs. Keep that email.
  • Clause 27 gave acceptance until 17:00 on 18 November 2026. You accepted on 16 November, so the contract concluded then — the expiry date no longer limits anything, but it is why nothing should be left unsigned.

This is a reading of your own signed contract, not legal advice. Before you serve a clause 6 notice or sign anything else on this house, put it in front of the conveyancer or your attorney — the three-day waiver period is a hard deadline and it is not one to run on a summary.
