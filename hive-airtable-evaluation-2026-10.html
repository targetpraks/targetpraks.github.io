The one-line verdict

Hive Teams, R136,305 a year, replaces Projects and Filestage together — same money as ClickUp, but with an approval round that ends in a stamped signature instead of a comment thread. HiveThe proofing is real, and it is the whole story

What Hive actually proofs — from its own supported-file list

This is the finding that changes the recommendation. Nifty does images and video. ClickUp does video, images and PDF. Hive does all of those plus the Office formats your teams live in, and it wraps them in an approval workflow rather than a comment thread.

How the approval itself works

  • Rounds and stages. You add a proof, then route it. Each round can hold several stages so first-round reviewers and final sign-off do not collapse into one pile.
  • Three approver types. Internal, external, or a whole team at once — adding a team as approver notifies every member.
  • External approvers need no account. Enter an email address and Hive sends a link to review in the proofing window. That is the single biggest gap between this and the rest of the field.
  • It ends in a mark, not a mood. Stamps — including a custom "Approved" stamp that auto-fills who signed and when — signatures, callouts, carets, text boxes, links and freehand annotation.
  • It moves the card for you. "Change status after approval" pushes the action to the next status the moment a decision lands, so the board reflects the sign-off without anyone touching it.
  • Its own queue. Anything routed to you sits at the top of My Actions in a separate My Approvals section — you do not hunt for it in a feed.
  • AI review is optional and separate. Proofing and Approvals is an app you switch on; AI Proofing is a second app your workspace admin has to enable.

The honest caveat on video

Hive accepts .mp4, .mov and .webm in the proofing viewer and annotates on top of them. What I have not verified with my own eyes is frame-accurate timecode commenting the way Filestage does it — annotations that pin to a second and follow the playhead. Trial one 60-second video and one 20-page PDF against a real client round before you cancel Filestage. Everything else about this workflow is confirmed from Hive's documentation.

Where Hive will pinch

  • Nobody gets in cheap. Free and Starter both cap the workspace at 10 members, so 50 staff means Teams at $12 — there is no mixed-seat story here, and Starter's $5 is theoretical at your size.
  • Client access is a paid premium app. External Users is priced separately — quote on request, not on the public price page. Budget for it before you promise a client portal.
  • A client cannot see a child project. Their access is one project at a time — even if you gave them the parent, child projects stay shut. So "client folder" means one project per client, not a tree of them.
  • Clients can log time but cannot submit a timesheet, and they cannot change project settings, add members, or message people outside their own projects.
  • The API question. API credentials are self-serve in every account and their MCP server is public, yet the price page sells "Enterprise API" as an Enterprise feature. Get the Teams-level rate limits in writing before you build anything on it.
  • Airtable's own trap, for contrast: read-only collaborators, form submissions and share links are free — which is why 15 editors plus 35 readers costs R68,152 rather than R227,174. Hive has no equivalent.

Airtable and the cost of all of itWhat it is for, and what it costs at 50

Airtable is the Superhuman Docs replacement, not a project platform

Coda became Superhuman Docs on 8 July 2026, and its billing is the model you asked me about weeks ago: you only pay for Doc Makers — editors and viewers are free. Airtable runs the same idea with different nouns. Paid seats are charged only for people who can edit at least one base; read-only collaborators, form submissions and share links cost nothing on any paid plan. That is exactly the mixed-seat structure you wanted, and it is the cheapest version of it on the market.

Portals — the client-scoped space — is an add-on on every plan and is not priced on Airtable's public page. Third-party reporting puts the Team portal package near $120/month for 15 guest users, about R27,261 a year including VAT. Treat that as indicative and get it quoted; I would not budget a client portal off a blog.

What I would actually do

One platform for the work: Hive Teams at R136,305. It is the only tool that passes the review test with a real approval — PDF, Office and video, staged rounds, an external approver who needs no seat, and a stamped "Approved" that moves the card. Filestage at R37,673–R62,284 comes off, so the net is R74,021 to R98,632 — the same net as ClickUp, for a better review loop. Trial one video and one PDF first.

One platform for the documents: Airtable Team at R68,152 for 15 editors and 35 readers. It is the closest thing to Superhuman Docs — bases, interfaces, automations, and the best MCP of any tool I have reviewed for you. Add Portals if a client must see their own space. It has no timer and no proofing, which is why it is not the work platform.

Both bills together: R204,457 a year — R90,870 less than putting all 50 on Airtable Business, and roughly what two of the three reviews already cost you.
