One link, your name on it

A single page that a stranger can land on from any social bio and understand in five seconds: who Ricardo Maio is, what he runs, and how to reach him. Not a blog, not a store, not a funnel — the front door. This PRD covers what comes off the current page, what replaces it, and what has to be true before the link in your bios changes.

01

Summary

replace a rented page with an owned one

Today the single link is beacons.ai/ricardomaio on Beacons' Free plan: your name, a personal tagline, six company buttons and an empty Hobbies block, under Beacons' URL and logo. It reads as a creator profile, which is what the platform is for.

This PRD specifies its replacement: one static page at ricardomaio.co.za, hosted free on GitHub Pages, built and published by the fleet from a single data file. The platform reasoning and the numbers behind that choice sit in the review document beside this one — the gate picked owning the page at 0.87, with Carrd as the only credible off-the-shelf runner-up.

02

Objectives

what success looks like
  • Replace six company buttons with the four that are still true, and add a personal layer so the page answers *who is this* before it answers *what does he run*.
  • Put the address in your name. `ricardomaio.co.za` instead of a third-party path, with no platform branding and nothing that can be repriced or shut down under you.
  • One link that works everywhere — Instagram, LinkedIn, X, Facebook, WhatsApp, email signature, business card QR — with a share preview that looks deliberate, not default.
  • Five-second comprehension on a phone in a queue: name, what he does, what he runs, how to reach him.
03

Non-goals

kept off on purpose
  • No blog, no newsletter, no email capture, no course, no store, no booking calendar.
  • No login, no member area, no CMS, no database, no server-side anything — a static page cannot rot.
  • No per-company detail pages in v1. Each company links out to its own site.
  • No change to any company's own identity, domains or branding. This is Ricardo's page, not a group portal.

05

How the page is laid out

one column, top to bottom
  1. Identity Portrait, name, one positioning line, Cape Town, South Africa. No carousel, no animation, no stock photography.
  2. The companies The four keepers as full-width rows: name, one line of what it does, outbound arrow. Order chosen by you, not by when each was added.
  3. Reach me Email, WhatsApp click-to-chat, LinkedIn, and one social. Four taps, no forms, no login walls.
  4. Elsewhere Optional third layer — press, talks, boards, community work — only if something real exists to put there.
  5. Footer One line of provenance and the year. No platform branding, no "made with" badge.
R1 P0

Own the address and the hosting

`ricardomaio.co.za` registered in Ricardo's name, DNS pointed at GitHub Pages, TLS issued automatically, apex and `www` both resolving to the same page. `ricardomaio.com` is not ours — it is another Ricardo Maio's photography portfolio — so nothing in the page, the copy or the metadata may imply it is.

  • `https://ricardomaio.co.za` returns 200 over TLS from a phone on mobile data
  • `www.` issues a single 301 to the apex, no loop
  • No platform branding, badge or third-party footer anywhere in the HTML
R2 P0

Identity block that reads in five seconds

Portrait, name as Ricardo Maio, one positioning line, and the city. The current tagline — *Entrepreneur! Proud Dad. Getting Shit Done the right way vs the quick way* — is the personal voice and can survive verbatim; what is missing is one plain sentence a stranger can file you under.

  • Name, role and city are all visible without scrolling on a 390 px screen
  • Portrait is a single file under 120 KB, cropped square, no text baked in
  • Exactly one positioning line above the companies — not three
R3 P0

The company directory: four, in your order

Infinity Brands, ACDC Express, The Local Farmer, INFX Solutions. One row each: name, a single line of what the business does, and an outbound link that opens in a new tab. Each line is written for a stranger, not for someone inside the group.

  • Exactly four rows render; a fifth appears only when you add one deliberately
  • Every outbound link was fetched and returned 200 on the day of publish
  • Each row is a single line on a phone, with no truncated word
R4 P0

The two removals are total

Codeblaze and Titan Electrical — the second read from your note as “Tights and Electric” — come off the page. Not hidden, not moved to a footer: gone from the markup, the page description, the share card and any alt text.

  • A case-insensitive search of the published HTML for both names returns zero hits
  • The share-preview image and description carry neither name
  • The Beacons page no longer lists them either, so the two pages do not disagree
R5 P0

A personal way in

Email, WhatsApp click-to-chat, LinkedIn, and one social profile. Direct links, no contact form — a form on a static page either goes nowhere or needs a third-party handler for no benefit on a page this small.

  • Four contact actions, each opening the right app on a phone
  • The WhatsApp link uses `wa.me` with the country code, pre-tested from a phone that is not yours
  • No email address is exposed in plain text to scrapers — obfuscated or rendered from script
R6 P0

A share card that looks deliberate

Open Graph and Twitter meta tags plus a 1200 × 630 image, so the link renders as a card with your name in WhatsApp, LinkedIn and X instead of a grey default.

  • The card renders in a link debugger with the intended title, description and image
  • The image is under 300 KB and legible at thumbnail size
  • The description is a sentence a stranger understands without context
R7 P0

Phone-first, and provably so

The page is judged at 390 px, on mobile data, on a mid-range phone. One self-contained file, no remote fonts, no framework, no external scripts beyond the optional analytics beacon.

  • Document scroll width equals client width at 390 px — no sideways scroll
  • Page weight at or under 150 KB with images included
  • Lighthouse mobile performance at or above 95, checked on the live URL, not a local file
R8 P1

Counting without tracking people

Per-link click counts and page views through Cloudflare Web Analytics or GoatCounter — cookieless, no banner, no personal data. Enough to answer *which company button do people actually press*.

  • A dashboard exists and shows the first week of real clicks
  • No cookie banner is required and no consent prompt appears
  • The beacon is the only third-party script on the page, or there is none
R9 P1

The cutover, everywhere at once

The new URL replaces the Beacons one in every place the old one appears — and each placement is ticked off, because a half-swapped bio is worse than either version.

  • 8 placements checked: Instagram, LinkedIn, X, Facebook, WhatsApp business profile, email signature, business card QR, and any deck or proposal template
  • The old Beacons page either points at the new domain or is retired after a 90-day dormancy
  • Beacons click history is exported before anything is deleted
R10 P1

Upkeep that costs you nothing

All copy, links and portraits live in one JSON file next to the renderer. A change is one line plus one command, run by the fleet on request; the page is rebuilt and published, and the data file is filed in the vault so the source of truth is not a scratch folder.

  • Ricardo asks for a copy change and it is live the same day
  • A quarterly review is on the wheel — the date exists, not just the intention
  • Domain renewal is on auto-renew and the renewal date is in Ricardo's calendar

Build and cutover

three days, then a quiet week
  1. Day 1 — domain Ricardo registers `ricardomaio.co.za` (R197 first year, R109 to renew; xneelo lists R110 flat). DNS to GitHub Pages, repository created, page rendered from the data file, share card made.
  2. Day 2 — copy and proof Positioning line, four company lines and the contact block approved by Ricardo. Every outbound link fetched. Phone check at 390 px, Lighthouse run on the live URL, analytics switched on.
  3. Day 3 — the swap 8 bio placements updated in one sitting. The Beacons page is set to point at the new domain rather than deleted, so old QR codes and printed links still land somewhere sensible.
  4. Week 2 — tidy Beacons click history exported, the old page left dormant, the data file filed in the vault, and the quarterly review dated on the wheel.

Decisions only you can make

1. The name. Is “Tights and Electric” Titan Electrical? If not, which business did you mean?

2. The domain. Approve `ricardomaio.co.za` at R197 for the first year and R109 a year after — you buy it, since it is your money and your name. `.me`, `.net` and `.io` are also free if you prefer one.

3. The four keepers, in order. I have listed them alphabetically for now; the page should follow the order you would introduce yourself.

4. Your handles. LinkedIn URL, the one social profile you want shown, the WhatsApp number and the email address to publish.

5. The positioning line. Keep “Entrepreneur! Proud Dad. Getting Shit Done the right way vs the quick way” as the voice, with a plain sentence above it — or lead with the plain sentence only.

6. Hobbies. Fill the block with two or three real things, or drop it. An empty block is the one thing on the current page that looks unfinished.

What this PRD does not claim

No company website was fetched for this document, so the one-line descriptions of the four businesses are still to be written against their own sites. The `.co.za` availability was checked against the registry from this Mac on 21 November 2026; availability is not a reservation, and another buyer can take the name at any time before it is registered.

Nothing here has been built or purchased. No domain has been bought, no repository created, and the Beacons page has not been touched — every change in this document waits on your go.
