How the week holds together

  • Ricardo is home all seven days. No away week, no rotation — the school run, the sport lifts, the pool and the garden sit with him every day they appear.
  • He does not cook. The kitchen belongs to Julie and Lily. He orders the groceries, tops up mid-week, and closes the kitchen on Wednesday and Friday.
  • He drives the big kids both ways — drop-off at 07:30, pickup at 13:30 for Gianna and 14:00 for Donnie, 12:45 and 13:15 on Friday — and takes every extra-mural: hockey Monday and Thursday, tennis Tuesday, swimming Wednesday and Friday.
  • Homework is 2 of the 4 days: Ricardo takes Donnie on Monday and Wednesday, Julie takes Gianna on Tuesday and Thursday.
  • 17:00–20:00 is Julie's. Cluster feeding. Nobody books over it; dinner, baths and bedtime belong to everyone else in that window.
  • Nights split every night: Ricardo 22:00–02:00, Julie 02:00–06:00.

Worth confirming

  • Gardener Two days assumed as Tuesday and Friday — give me the real pair and two cells move.
  • Domestic Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, cleaning and laundry on her days. Confirm whether bed linen is hers or Lily's.
  • Lily Three cooking days, three laundry days and Sunday's kitchen — that is the load to check with her first.
  • Pool Assumes no service contract. A pool service empties three Ricardo cells.
  • Bins Refuse goes out Tuesday. Cape Town now collects refuse and recycling on the same day in Welgemoed — confirm your street on the City's schedule change.
