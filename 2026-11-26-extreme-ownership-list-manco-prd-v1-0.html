1. The product in five lines

  1. One board in MANCO where every failure that hit the company gets owned in full — What. Why. Fix.
  2. Every card carries Brand, Business Unit and Section, so a failure is findable by where it lived.
  3. Every card carries a Recurrence flag — First time, Repeat, Chronic — so the twice-rule is visible, not remembered.
  4. The Section filter is the payoff: when the same thing threatens to happen again, the history of that section failing across every BU is one filter click away — before it happens, not a month after.
  5. Every failure is logged the moment it happens and the fixer is tagged on the card at filing; nothing repeats without a system change on record.

3. The card

Part What goes in it Who sets it
Title The failure in plain words, no code: "Diesel delivery missed the Saturday drop" Finder
WHAT One sentence, facts only. No blame, no adjectives. Finder
WHY The cause, not the excuse. "Friday's stock check ran at 15:00, after the order cut-off." Finder or Fixer
FIX Named person and a date. "Ryno — reorder Monday 07:00, Saturday drop secured." Fixer

Due date When the Fix lands Fixer
Brand / Business Unit / Section / Recurrence Where it lived (table §4) Finder, on filing

Filing takes about 90 seconds. What. Why. Fix. stays three paragraphs in the description — not three custom fields — because typing beats clicking when something is on fire, and free text carries the nuance a dropdown kills. The department and section, the things we filter on, are the fields.

6. The protocol (how the rule lives after the build)

  1. Filing — immediate: the finder files the card the moment the failure is found — right there, right then — and tags the person who must fix it; the fixer owns it from the second the card exists. A Repeat card links to the first card (native task link) — the paper trail of a pattern is two clicks deep.
  2. Review — on demand, not on a schedule: whoever opens V4 Repeat Watch sees every failure that has landed twice, one screen. A Repeat closes only when the Fix names the system change; a Chronic comes to Ricardo with the system-change proposal attached, because three strikes are a business decision, not a task.
  3. Close rule: no card closes without a Fix line showing a person and a date; a Repeat or Chronic closes without a system change recorded in the Fix is not a close — reopening is one click and MANCO sees it happen.
  4. The promise the list makes: the first failure is training; the second is a decision. Nobody at MANCO should ever meet failure number three unannounced.

EXTREME OWNERSHIP — how this team runs from today

Open the list

This is not a suggestion box. Not a wellness poster. This is where an elite team is built: every f*ck-up gets owned — by you, in writing, the moment it happens. Elite teams don't do quiet failures. Elite teams don't do excuses.

The rule: a f*ck-up happens, you log it. Right there. Immediately. You don't wait for a meeting. You don't wait for permission. You don't wait for someone else to say it first.

The card — 90 seconds, no essays:

  1. WHAT — what happened. Facts. One sentence.
  2. WHY — the cause. Not the excuse.
  3. FIX — who fixes it and when. Tag that person on the card as you file it — they own it from that second.

Tag Brand · Business Unit · Section, set Recurrence — First time, Repeat, Chronic.

Before you act twice, search it. Same section, any brand — the whole history of that failure, one click. If it's sitting on the board and you still hit it again, that one is on you.

The line:

  • Once is training.
  • Twice, the fix must change the system — not the person.
  • Three strikes is a boardroom decision.

It's not what we preach, it's what we tolerate — this list is where we stop tolerating silent f*ck-ups. That's how this team runs with superstars: owned misses, bigger work to the ones who own them, zero hiding.

Good. Now we fix it — and it never happens twice.
