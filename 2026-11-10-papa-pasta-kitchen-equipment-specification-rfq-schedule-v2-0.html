How to quote this pack

Send back one row per line

Complete the response table at the back of this pack (or the same columns in your own sheet). Add rows where you offer an alternative. Attach the specification sheet, the electrical load sheet and a photograph or brochure for every powered item. Quote in USD FOB and state the port.

Every quotation must state

  • Brand and model per line — premium commercial-grade equipment, not entry-level or domestic.
  • Unit price and quantity in USD FOB, line total, and volume discount tiers from 2 units upward.
  • Electrical detail — rated kW, voltage, phase, frequency and the breaker size the unit needs. Every powered item.
  • Dimensions and weight per item, packed and unpacked, for shipping volume and site planning.
  • Certifications — CE with test report, ISO 9001, food-grade material certificates, enclosed. For electrical goods, the South African NRCS letter of authority status and the SANS/IEC 60335 test report — CE alone is supporting evidence, not a substitute for the LoA.
  • Nameplate and drawing — a photograph of the rating plate and a dimensioned drawing to ±5 mm for every powered line. A brochure photograph is not a drawing.
  • MOQ, lead time, packing — production time to FOB, export documentation, and whether you consolidate a mixed container.
  • South African import support — 400 V / 50 Hz, three-phase plus neutral and earth, IEC 60309 (Commando) plug or gland tails — not a Chinese or Australian plug. State the HS code and whether you will quote CIF Durban as well as FOB.
  • Payment terms — deposit percentage and balance terms (T/T), and whether you accept a letter of credit.
  • After-sales — spare-parts list with part numbers, remote commissioning support, warranty period.

Automatic rejects — do not quote around these

  • Bronze dies only on the extruder (A1/A3). A Teflon die is rejected without negotiation.
  • A rack, not a machine (A4) — 600 mm, trays, castors. A freeze dryer, a refrigerated cabinet or a PLC humidity cabinet substituted for the rack is rejected. If you want to offer the cabinet as a priced option, quote it as a separate line.
  • Dried-pasta cook cycle, 8–12 minutes (B1). A fresh-pasta or rapid-cook cooker is rejected.
  • Induction that sears (B2). A bowl that will not reach 165 °C from cold in 90 seconds, or a flat hob with a trivet under the wok, is rejected.
  • Dual-tank, non-carbonated slush (E1). Carbonated machines are rejected.

What changed since September

The wok is now a specified machine, not a category

B2 is written as an induction automatic wok with a measurable heat floor, a magnetic bowl, programmable cycles and a throughput test — because “automatic stir-fry machine” covers everything from a real wok to a heated bowl with a lid. The 304 bowl in the September pack was wrong: 304 is non-magnetic and will not couple to an induction coil, so the line now asks for carbon steel or magnetic 430/clad. Suppliers answer the heat question with a number and a test method. The quantity is now resolved: three units at 8 kW is the most the 60 A board carries, and the pack prices two 10 kW units and four 5 kW units against it.

Every line carries a photograph

Each card carries a reference image of the machine class, and no line is left with an empty frame. If a supplier's machine does not match the photo, the specification still governs — but they must say, line by line, where theirs differs. The photo is indicative; the text is the contract.

Two lines carry an open decision

B2 quantity — three 8 kW units fit the board with room to spare; whether a fourth 5 kW unit earns its price is our decision, not the quote's. E2 can sealer — carried in this pack, but it only earns its place once a can supplier and a fill line are named.

The seven machines that carry the store

A1 vacuum extruder · A4 600 mm drying rack · B1 nine-basket pasta cooker · B2 three induction automatic woks · B3 four-well sauce warmer · E1 three-tank slush machine · E2 automatic can sealer. Zones C to F support them — refrigeration, wash-up, smallwares. If the budget is cut, it gets cut here last, because these seven are the product the customer pays for.

B2

Induction automatic wok — open-bowl auto stir-fry station

QTY 3 × 8 kW (price 2 × 10 kW as the alternative) POWER 8 kW each · 400 V 3φ

Ricardo's specified machine: induction, automatic. Set into the pass, bowl visible to the customer line.

Heavy-duty commercial induction automatic wok with a motorised open bowl and programmable cooking cycles, set into the pass at 950 mm so sauce and pasta are tossed in full view of the queue. Three units at 8 kW is what a 60 A board carries; price two 10 kW units as the alternative, because a 10 kW single-wok station is the modern high-output build and two of them draw less than three 8 kW units. On a static hob sauces get held that should never be held — this machine exists so they are finished instead. It must sear and reduce, not warm, and the bowl must stay on its coil while it works. It must also empty itself: the bowl tips forward over a pan, a wash arm cleans it between services, and the waste water goes into a dump section and out through a strainer to the grease trap. Three things a flat induction hob cannot do, and the reason this is a station and not a hob.

Desired specifications
  • Induction, 380–400 V three-phase, 50 Hz, 8 kW continuous per unit (10 kW if quoting the two-unit alternative). State nameplate kW — a nameplate photograph is required with the quote
  • Bowl surface 165 °C from cold in under 90 seconds at the bowl base; recovery within 60 seconds after a 1 kg load. State the test method and the probe used
  • Eight power levels minimum with real-time level display and a sensor-touch panel; energy efficiency 90% or better — state the measured figure, not the marketing figure
  • Bowl: seamless spun carbon steel or magnetic 430 / clad stainless, 3.0–4.0 mm, Ø 350–400 mm, 5–8 L usable. 304 alone is non-magnetic and will not couple — do not offer it
  • Concave induction coil matched to the bowl radius with full coverage. A flat hob with a trivet under the wok is rejected
  • Motorised rotation or auto-toss, 10–40 rpm variable, bowl seated on the coil throughout; manual tilt and manual toss override for the cook
  • Programmable recipe cycles — power level, rotation speed, time — 20 stored recipes minimum, batch timer, audible alarm, temperature log export
  • Throughput 45 portions per hour per unit minimum on a 140 g dried-pasta plus 180 g sauce finish; state cycle time and test conditions
  • Bowl tips forward to empty — power tilt through at least 90°, over a collection pan at the pass. State the tilt actuation, the angle and the pan size; a wok that has to be lifted off a hot coil to be emptied is an automatic reject
  • Automatic wash: built-in spray ring or wash arm under the bowl rim with a timed wash and rinse cycle and a detergent dosing point. State water connection, pressure, litres per cycle and cycle time
  • Waste water dump section: sump under the bowl of 5–10 L, removable mesh strainer, and a drain piped to the grease trap. State sump capacity, whether it lifts out for cleaning, and the drain size
  • Open bowl visible from the customer side, splash guard to the queue, brushed 304 body, stated noise level at 1 m
  • Over-temperature cut-out, dry-burn and no-load protection, rotation interlock, emergency stop, IPX4
  • State smoke and fume generation and the canopy airflow required per unit; unit must fit under a 1.5 m hood at 950 mm pass height
  • CE with test report, SANS / IEC 60335-2-9 alignment, NRCS letter of authority support; English manual, wiring diagram, spares list, two-year commercial warranty
Automatic reject: Gas; flat hob with trivet; 304-only bowl; under 6 kW continuous; timer-only control with no temperature feedback; bowl that lifts off the coil; no forward tilt; no built-in wash cycle; no waste water sump or drain

Factory acceptance test — run it before you ship

  • Cold start to 200 °C at the bowl base in under 90 seconds, measured with a contact probe, and held there through three consecutive 3 kg batches.
  • Three consecutive 3 kg batches tossed at full rotation without spill or stall, with the bowl at temperature throughout.
  • Twenty consecutive finish cycles with no power derate and no control fault.
  • Full cook of one portion in under three minutes, filmed, with the cycle program and the wattage shown on the panel.
  • Ship only against a signed test sheet and the video.

What "automatic" must mean on this line

A rotating bowl and a timer is not automatic. The machine must run a stored recipe cycle — power level, rotation speed and time — that a cook selects with one touch, and it must hold that cycle repeatably across a service. Manual override stays available, because the pass is a show station and the cook still finishes by eye. If the only control is a dial and a switch, it is a heated mixer, not an automatic wok.

Automatic pasta machine — vacuum-assist extruder, bronze die

QTY 1 POWER 400 V 3φ · 2.2–4 kW

The Pasta Theatre engine. Output is dried in A4 and cooked in B1 as dried pasta.

A commercial single-screw extruder that mixes, extrudes and cuts in one pass, with a vacuum chamber on the barrel so the dough is de-aerated before it leaves the die. That is what stops the chalky white specks inside the pasta and gives a dense, translucent dried shape that survives ten minutes in water. Bronze die only — the rough surface is the texture promise. It must hold a 120-cover hour with the drying cabinet behind it; a tabletop domestic extruder, or a fresh-pasta-only machine with no drying path, is the wrong product for this store.

Desired specifications

  • Vacuum-assist extrusion chamber with gauge, held at −0.08 MPa or better. No vacuum is an automatic reject on this store's texture standard
  • Bronze die, Ø 55–70 mm inserts, six dies minimum (spaghetti, penne, rigatoni, fusilli, tagliatelle, bucatini). Teflon / PTFE is an automatic reject
  • Water-jacketed barrel and head holding dough at or below 45 °C; state the cooling method and the temperature it holds at 25 °C ambient

A benchtop automatic can seamer that takes a pre-filled 202 or 211 aluminium can, seats the end and rolls a double seam the operator can tear down and measure. Two very different machines hide behind the word "sealer": the store-level trial unit costs about US$350 and seals what a person has already filled; the aseptic fill-and-seam line starts at US$60,000, fills and seams 80 cans an hour upward, and is a network asset bought once, not a store asset.
