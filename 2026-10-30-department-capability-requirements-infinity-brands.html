The problem

Every one of the 11 departments in the Infinity Brands company had an empty capabilityRequirements. The team report's coverage heat map had no rows, the gap list was always empty, and the placement board and team composer had no bar to measure against. The capabilities existed in the library (18 of them); what was missing was what each team needs, and at what percentage.

How the numbers were decided

The local decision lane (d3-flash, Decision 3.0 — the model behind the §8 gate) answered for every department × capability pair, in four passes:

Pass Question Calls
1 How much does this department need this capability? (5-level score) 198
2 Is it core, supporting or not required? (3-level choice) 198
3 Which of these two matters more? (every pair asked twice, options swapped) 422
4 Which one is the headline requirement? (choice over the shortlist) 11

Every answer was logged. Pass 3 is the one that matters: a two-option choice is the lane's most reliable form, and asking each pair in both orders removes position bias — 82% of the 211 pairs gave the same answer with the options swapped, and the 38 that split went almost always to whichever option was listed first (33 of 38), which is exactly what the swap catches. Those pairs were settled by the pass-1 score, not by position. 829 calls in total, all on the local model.

Where the lane was overruled

The lane ranks well but it over-rewards broad conscientious traits — ten of the eighteen capabilities are built on conscientiousness, so "quality", "planning" and "process" climb in almost every team. Four review corrections:

  • Marketing — the lane put planning first in both the tournament and the headline pass. Brand and creative product work is the discipline; planning runs the campaigns, it does not define the team. Product → 80%.
  • INFX Solutions — quality tied software in the tournament and won the tie-break on its pass-1 score. The headline pass, with software in the options, chose software at 91%. Software → 80%.
  • Group Executive — the lane's top six left compliance out entirely. A group executive team carries governance; compliance is in at 45%.
  • Brand Ops 2 — the lane ranked process above customer service. The team has a customer service seat and the remit names customer orders; customer service → 70%.
