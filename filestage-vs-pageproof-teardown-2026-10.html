Section 03 of 11

Filestage: project → folder → file → version → review step

The route table is the object model. Filestage's deep link is the most honest sentence in the product:

/review/:stepId/file/:fileId/:versionNumber/comment/:commentId

A decision is attached to a review step, not to a file. The file carries versions; the step carries the people who must respond. That is why one project can run several review rounds over the same assets without losing the trail.

Section 04 of 11

Filestage: the decision machine

Four decisions ship in the dictionary, and two of them are the ones I told you only PageProof had.

Approved

The plain sign-off. Present on every plan.

Requested changes

The loop back to the person who loaded the file.

Approved with changes

Sign-off plus a change list, so the next round is not a fresh approval.

Rejected

Stop the asset, not just send it back.

Two mechanisms sit above those four:

  • Custom review decisions. A team defines extra named decisions; disabling one warns that automations using it will pause. So decisions are first-class configuration, not a fixed enum.
  • Decisions can be turned off per plan — Disable review decisions invites people to comment without signing anything. Useful for a client who will never press a button.
The honest caveat. Approved with changes and rejected live behind an early-access flag and a plan feature called Extra review decisions. It ships in the code; your plan is what decides whether you see it. Check the toggle in team settings before counting on it.

What replaces a numeric threshold

Filestage does not expose "this step needs 2 of 3 approvers". It gets there by automation conditions instead: After receiving all review decisions versus After first matching decision, plus a per-group choice of whether that group must give a decision at all. The trigger vocabulary is Reviewer submits review decision — so anything downstream fires on a decision, not on a file upload.

Section 08 of 11

PageProof: the decision machine is the best-engineered one in the market

Three decisions — Approved, Changes please, Approved with changes — but the machinery around them is what Filestage does not match.

Four roles, not one

A workflow step can hold Approvers, Gatekeepers, Mandatory reviewers and Editors. A gatekeeper does not just decide; a gatekeeper decides and moves the proof on: “Approve and send on”, or “Return to-do list”, which stops the workflow dead. That distinction — sign off, versus sign off and release — is exactly what a brand sign-off chain needs and what a flat reviewer list cannot express.

A real threshold, per step

Each step carries a mandatory decision threshold: all approvers of that role, or one. The UI states it plainly: “This step waits for {n} gatekeepers to approve.” An owner can also approve manually, and the audit records it as manual.

Decisions that can be changed, and withdrawn — on the record

Every one of these is an audit event: changed decision, approval withdrawn, approved manually, approval and digital signatures withdrawn. Withdrawing an approval is a supported action, not a support ticket.

The chase is built in

Nudge and Nudge all, from the decision summary. Given that 144 of our reviews are past due and 89 are waiting on a named person, a one-click chase on a proof is worth more than a prettier dashboard.

Digital signing that means something

Approvers must reauthenticate to approve and sign; the signature is withdrawn with the approval; bulk approval is blocked on proofs that need a signature. Gated to Enterprise.

Section 09 of 11

Where I was wrong on 19 October

I told you PageProof beats Filestage because of decision thresholds and “approved with changes”. Half of that was wrong.

Filestage has approved-with-changes. It also has rejected, custom decisions, and a per-group choice of whether a decision is required at all. It is flagged early-access and plan-gated as “Extra review decisions”, which is why it never appeared in our own workspace: the flag is off.

What remains true: PageProof's per-step numeric threshold, gatekeeper-and-send-on, per-proof audit log and reauthentication-backed digital signing have no Filestage equivalent in what I could observe. Those four are the real difference — not the decision names.

Two consequences for the ClickUp recommendation: the PageProof case rests on the workflow engine, audit and signing, not on the vocabulary; and if Filestage's extra decisions are simply switched on, the cheapest version of your problem is a settings change, not a purchase.

  1. Switch the Filestage flag on and look. One setting decides whether the comparison that started this is even a comparison.
