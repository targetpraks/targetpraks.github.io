01 · ClickUp BusinessThe recommendation

What it actually fixes, in your words

“No reference to other tasks.” ClickUp Relationships let one task point at another — blocks, waits on, duplicates, or a type you define yourself, like approves or spawns brief. It is available on every plan, including free. Zoho Projects makes you build that out of tags and hope.

“Visually terrible to manage.” One task can live in several lists at once — the same deliverable shows up on the brand's board and in the designer's queue without being copied. Managers get a dashboard per brand, per person or per client instead of a list they have to read like a spreadsheet.

“Track his staff.” The Everything view filters every task in the workspace by person, and Workload shows who is carrying what against capacity. A manager opens one person and sees the queue, the deadlines and the slip.

“Get context of the work.” Comments, files, versions and the approval trail sit on the task, and Docs and Whiteboards attach to the same place. The context is not in someone's memory of a Cliq thread.

Folding Filestage in

This is the part that decides it. ClickUp Proofing annotates PNG, GIF, JPEG, WEBP, video and PDF inside the task — timestamped video comments, assignable and resolvable, with the approval recorded against the deliverable rather than in a separate portal.

Where it will hurt

  • ClickUp will let you build anything, including a mess. The tool that gives a manager a clean view also gives every staff member permission to invent a status. It needs a house standard on day one — spaces per brand, one status set, one task template — or you get a colourful Zoho.
  • It is dense. Asana is calmer and a first-time manager will feel it. ClickUp pays that back in proofing and relationships, not in elegance.
  • Proofing won't replace Filestage for client-facing review portals. Internal and manager review: yes, comfortably. Sending a branded, password-protected review link to an external franchisee or client: Filestage still does that better. If that flow matters, keep a small Filestage plan and cancel the bigger one.

The honest summary

ClickUp Business is the one platform that answers all five of your tests at once, costs roughly a third of Asana, and removes a second subscription. The risk is your own configuration discipline, not the software.

02 · Asana Advanced  ·  03 · OpenProjectThe runners-up

Asana Advanced — if clarity outranks consolidation

Asana is the best-looking manager experience in this field and the easiest to hand to a non-technical manager on a Monday. Portfolios roll projects into one status page, Workload shows who is over, and Advanced adds time tracking, custom fields and formulas on top of starter-level tasks and dependencies.

But its proofing stops at images and PDFs. Your Filestage bill stays, so the true cost is R321,527–R346,138 a year against ClickUp's R74,021–R98,631 net. If Asana's calm is what gets 50 people to actually use it, that gap is defensible — but it is a deliberate choice to run two tools, not a saving.

OpenProject Community — the open-source answer, honestly

On paper it is the closest match to what you want: work packages that carry typed relations (relates to, blocks, duplicates, precedes), a real Gantt with dependencies, wiki pages, meetings with minutes, and time tracking — all GPLv3, all free, no seat maths at 50 people. It is the structural opposite of Zoho Projects.

What you get for R0

  • Unlimited users, no licence cost
  • Typed task relations and Gantt baselines
  • Time tracking and cost reporting
  • Full data ownership, on your own server

What it costs instead

  • An owner. Backups, upgrades, SSO — half a day a month, minimum
  • No proofing. Filestage stays regardless
  • No one to call. Support starts at €5.95/user/month
  • Community tier gaps in reporting and governance — validate the feature split before committing, not after

Supported route if you ever want it: Basic €5.95/user/month, 25-user minimum — R65,980/yr ex VAT at 50 users, R75,877 incl. Premium support and remote hands cost more.

The two open-source traps

  • “Free” usually means an engineer owns it. You have a Mac mini and no sysadmin. An unfixed CVE on a self-hosted tool holding your project history is worse than a licence fee.
  • Adoption is the whole game. The best tool here is the one 50 people open on Monday. OpenProject is respected and slightly joyless; ClickUp is noisy and habit-forming. Neither is free of that trade.

If you want the open-source route anyway

Plane (open source, self-hosted, cleaner UI, built for delivery) is the modern alternative and worth an afternoon. But it is shaped for engineering teams, has no proofing, and won't give a manager the per-person staff picture. I would not put 50 non-technical staff on it as the system of record.

DecisionWhat happens next

One thing I need to pin down: which “reviews”

Your sentence carries two meanings and the answer differs, so I am not going to pretend it doesn't.

Reviews of the work

Annotations, versions, approvals on a design or video. ClickUp replaces Filestage for internal review, and keeps the client portal case as the exception.

Reviews of the person

Appraisal cycles, 1:1s, goals, ratings. None of these three does it natively. That is a separate product (Lattice, 15Five, Leapsome) — or a review ritual run monthly off the same task data.

My read is that you mean the first, because you reached for Filestage in the same breath. If it is the second, say so — it changes the stack and I would rather build it in now than discover it in six months.
