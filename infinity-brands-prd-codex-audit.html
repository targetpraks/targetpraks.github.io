REVIEW — PRD v1.2

Verdict (≤120 words: is this buildable as written? what is the single biggest defect?)

No. The biggest defect is the absence of a consistent scoring dependency contract: the mandatory five-level chain contradicts independent business metrics, while close, missing-evidence propagation and approval rules do not specify one executable path to a person's final score. Administrators must invent consequential behavior. Two workload premises are also false: the sheet contains strategic-objective statements, and V3's individual master contains 304 KPI rows, not 14. The pilot cannot reliably expose these errors because its scope, negative tests and scoring-mode transition are undefined. Correct these contracts and measurements before building the full structure.

Defects, ranked by damage

D1. The mandatory hierarchy reverses the business-score dependency [DEFECT]

  • Artefact: §4.2 — “No object may skip a level, and no object may point at two parents.”
  • Evidence: evidence/v3-Read Me.txt:14 requires business/BU results to be operational metrics, never employee averages; evidence/v3-04 Individual KPI Master.txt:4–5 links individual KPIs directly to MKT BU KPIs; evidence/v3-02 BU Outcomes.txt:14 gives ADM-01 two enterprise links. PRD §7.2 itself prohibits employee averages.
  • Why it matters: Reading A makes L4 measurements flow through individual and HOD scores into BU and enterprise totals. Reading B treats the arrows as alignment only, retaining independent enterprise measurements and BU inheritance into HOD scores. A produces circular dependencies or employee-derived BU results; B violates the no-skipping/no-multiple-parent instructions. §4.1a's row/column “totals” also cannot sum currency, margins and rates as raw numbers.
  • Fix: Replace the universal chain with separate alignment links and explicit computation dependencies, including independent business measurements and downward BU inheritance. Specify each composite's inputs and deduplication rule; never infer arithmetic from an alignment edge or Brand × BU membership.

D2. “Null, not zero” does not define the final-score gate [GAP]

  • Artefact: §7.3 — “the engine writes a null, not a zero, and leaves the weight of the missing KPI untouched.”
  • Evidence: evidence/v3-05 KPI Engine & Monthly Scoring.txt:3–7 contains evidence status, HOD override, calculated/final/weighted scores and approvals; line 7 gives a Partial-evidence demo a final score of 45. evidence/v3-05 HOD Composite Scoring.txt:2–4 combines BU, direct-outcome and leadership scores. These are dependencies the new rule must change, not merely a new display value.
  • Why it matters: A builder can either propagate any missing required input to a null person-month, or sum available weighted cells while preserving the missing weight. With weights 0.60/0.40, scores 100/null yield 60 under the latter implementation, a performance finding based on missing data. No rule specifies propagation through inherited BU results, whether Partial counts as verified, override limits, or how zero-weight mandatory controls affect eligibility. This is an incomplete new fix, not a repeat of the historical sign-off finding.
  • Fix: Require all required evidence dependencies, including inherited results and mandatory controls, to be approved before publishing any affected composite, band or HR export; otherwise publish a null composite with its missing dependencies. Explicitly replace V3's Partial discount and constrain overrides so neither can bypass that predicate.

D3. The close can remain open indefinitely, with no independent signer able to finish it [DEFECT]

  • Artefact: §8 — “The verifier is not the owner. No one signs their own month.”
  • Evidence: evidence/v3-MKT.txt:16–18 identifies Rhona as MKT's BU leader and gives her 50% BU inheritance; evidence/v3-02 BU Outcomes.txt:25–30 names Rhona/MKT among BU owners. PRD §§4.2, 5.1, 7.1 and 11.1 make the HOD both L1 owner and department-head verifier; §8 escalates on days three/five but gives no disposition deadline or final decision duty.
  • Why it matters: Sequence allowed by the stated rules: the HOD owns the BU result; the required verifier is that same HOD; self-signing is forbidden; the deputy does not acquire a defined independent approval authority; the month stays open and escalates to Ricardo and then the manager. Both can see the exception without either being obliged to resolve it. Alternatively an administrator lets the HOD self-sign. The close table's day-three scoring/export is also unconditional despite the open month, and nobody is assigned the transition from Verified to Signed off.
  • Fix: Add one close rule: the named independent close authority must, by a stated deadline, either sign verified results or sign an unscored disposition, with an accountable deputy and no self-approval through inheritance. Make score publication/export conditional on that disposition and assign the Signed off transition to that authority.

Q1 … Q8 answers

Q1 — Unsupported claims

The numeric ledger below covers the quantitative assertions by evidence class. The corrections with greatest effect on cost and design are D4–D8, D18–D22:

  • 0 objectives / 56 department labels → zero formal parent records may be true, but F contains 56 populated cells and 51 distinct statements; validate candidates, rather than authoring all strategy from zero (D4).
  • 14 individual rows / 11 roles / all Draft / none balanced → true only of the filtered RKPI subset; 304 rows / 37 BU-position groups / 26 balanced named groups in the full dump, with readiness still unproven (D5).
  • 23 INF/BND1 rows are group oversight → several explicitly concern EXP or TIE; approve a beneficiary crosswalk, not blanket ALL (D7).
  • 11 HOD roles need their weights filled → nine hold 50% personal components beside inheritance; validate composites and exceptions, not personal totals in isolation (D8).
  • 265 targets / 375 sources satisfy two gate tests → these are digit/nonblank tests; semantic pass counts remain unverified (D18).
  • 52 KPI-type spellings / 17 HOD pillars → 49 nonblank HOD types; 17 is the BU pillar count, with 157 nonblank HOD pillar strings in the dump (D19).
  • 77 ClickUp tasks / 41 CY2026 → latest annex census gives 75/39; inventory conflict unresolved, not proof that 77 never existed (D20).
  • 8 existing Brand options unchanged / 10 list BU options → annex gives 9/13; an explicit migration is required (D11/D20).
  • 0 actuals anywhere / all measurements live today → four demo actuals exist, and old tracker repair counts were not recounted; separate demo, live and dated snapshots (D22).

Q4 — The governance hole

Concrete deadlock using only stated rules: Rhona owns MKT's BU result; the verifier must be the department head; the verifier cannot be the owner. No independent L1 verifier is defined. Her deputy's existence does not establish independent approval authority. The month remains open on day two, Ricardo sees Late verification on day three, the verifier's manager receives it on day five, and no deadline or accountable final disposition follows. A Signed off state exists but has no assigned transition owner. This sequence satisfies the escalation rules while never closing the month (D3).

The single rule that closes that hole: “For each BU-period, the named independent close authority, with named independent deputy, must sign either a verified close or an unscored disposition by working day five; only that signed disposition permits final-score publication, and an unscored disposition never exports a numeric performance rating.” The exact day is a proposed decision, not a measured necessity. The rule must expressly override the incompatible department-head default for self-owned or inherited results.

A separate score-leak risk requiring an executable test: a verified role KPI depends on a BU composite with one missing required evidence item. If SUM returns a partial numeric BU result, 50% of it enters the HOD composite while that HOD's own proof is present. The PRD forbids a fabricated score in prose, but supplies no inheritance/null propagation algorithm to enforce the prohibition. With D2's transitive predicate, this is impossible; without it, retaining V3 behavior is not an acceptable implementation assumption. The supplied value dumps cannot verify formulas or establish whether a live override currently bypasses an evidence gate.

Q8 — Can S6 falsify the design?

Only weakly as written. A complete inability to capture or verify would fail S6, so the pilot is not literally unfailable. But its exit condition does not test whether the system rejects a plausible wrong result or publishes the right result for the right person. A hand-run close can satisfy the sequence while hiding lookup, weighting and null-propagation faults. Design-mode band suppression prevents observing the band path claimed in §16, and an unscored result can become the only “success” (D15).

One additional acceptance condition: before the run, the independent pilot sponsor signs a fixed known-answer test pack and the pilot passes only if every expected acceptance and rejection is reproduced by the engine, with no manual repair of outputs. Include a complete-evidence positive control and isolated cases for missing/Partial proof, inherited missing BU evidence, duplicate/wrong-person ID, conflicting target version, invalid weight set, fractional band boundary, and an attempted edit of a closed period. Compare calculated score, null propagation, permitted approval transitions and the immutable output package to that oracle. Any unexpected score, permitted bypass or changed close package fails S6. Band tests use synthetic people in a test mode, with no HR consequences.
