The claim, against the evidence

What the ad saysWhat the research says
“Brain activation exercises” rewire the pathways so a child can switch gears. Training improves the trained task and little else. A meta-analysis of 87 publications and 145 comparisons found reliable gains on working-memory measures but no convincing transfer to intelligence, reading or arithmetic — and no evidential value once treated controls were used.
“Brain-body connection” / reflex-based programs fix behaviour and focus. The best-known version, Brain Balance (~$6,000 per 12 weeks), failed a 2021 systematic review for lack of empirical support. In 2025 two papers using its clinic data were retracted — one for text overlap, one because the journal judged its conclusions unreliable.
Meltdowns stop “in about four weeks”, focus returns. One anonymous testimonial, illustrated by AI, with no control group. That is a story, not a result — the ad itself labels it “illustrative”.
Strict parenting is the wrong lever; the nervous system is the right one. Half right, and then it overreaches. Harsh discipline is linked to worse child outcomes across 111 effect sizes and 160,927 children — but the fix is not a paid exercise plan.
The honest core of it: children really do differ in how well they shift from one activity to the next, and that skill really is trainable. It is trained by repetition inside ordinary days, with an adult who stays calm — not by seven minutes of drills sold behind a quiz.

What actually moves the needle

LeverReal effect
Aerobic movement, most days The strongest children’s effect sizes I could find: across 9 randomised trials in children with ADHD, inhibitory control improved by SMD 0.83, cognitive flexibility by 0.65 and working memory by 0.48. Free, and it works on the whole child.
Sleep and predictable transitions The single biggest lever on attention and meltdowns. A warning before the screen goes off does more for “switching gears” than any exercise programme.
Reading together, daily The one activity with decades of evidence for language, attention and school readiness — and it is the opposite of a 7-minute drill: it is slow, social and repeated for years.
Treating the actual problem If meltdowns are frequent and severe, the useful next step is a paediatric assessment for ADHD, sleep problems, vision and hearing. Behavioural parent training is the first-line non-drug treatment for young children with ADHD — and it is what this ad is imitating without the trials.

So: how much does it improve kids’ brains? On the evidence, zero that you could measure. The gains that do exist are real but unglamorous — movement, sleep, reading, treated problems — and they are all free or covered by a doctor’s referral. The product is selling the wrapper around the free part.

If you want the 3-minute version, free

  • Fixed bedtime, screens out of the bedroom, no screens in the last hour.
  • Twenty to thirty minutes of hard playing or sport most days — aerobic beats stretching.
  • Announce transitions before they happen; name the feeling, hold the limit.
  • Read together every night, even when they can read alone.
  • If it is still hard at six months, get the ADHD / sleep / vision assessment instead of another programme.
