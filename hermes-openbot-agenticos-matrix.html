Engine, cockpit, building.

Hermes is the engine that thinks on your own machine. OpenBot is the cockpit where several agents are watched at once. AgenticOS is the building seventeen colleagues walk into. They are not three horses in one race — one of them can literally run another.

What it is
A runtime and a fleet. You point it at your machine, your providers, your chat apps and your schedule; it keeps thinking between sessions.
A window, not an engine. It hosts other vendors' agent CLIs side by side and gives each a folder, a thread and a queue.
A tenancy. One address, a sign-in per person, and an assistant per business unit that knows only its own documents.
Where it runs
Your machine, a VPS, Docker, an SSH box, Daytona, Modal or Vercel. Here the gateway and dashboard are already running, on v0.23.4.
Electron on macOS, Windows or Linux; an iPhone app; or a self-hosted server. Data in ~/OpenBot and one SQLite file.
Docker on the Mac mini, published through Tailscale behind Cloudflare Access. Browser only — no install, no VPN, no port on the office LAN.
Who can use it
One owner, many profiles: 35 on this Mac, each with its own config, memory, skills and credentials.
You, plus teammates you invite to a published server — a team API over WebRTC, with account data on Cloudflare.
24 seat rows: ten unit seats, brand seats, five shared desks (numbers, documents, decks, dashboards, SOP) and two admin seats. Registration is restricted to @infinitybrands.co.za.
What's underneath
A Python backend; CLI, TUI, desktop app and dashboard; ACP, REST and A2A at the edge.
Electron main plus SolidJS; a typed preload bridge; a SQLite event log; one ACP driver per provider; a sandboxed browser host.
A Dockerised web app with retrieval, run records and spend telemetry; an MCP relay out to Zoho and ClickUp; a nightly re-index at 03:05.
Brains models, memory, and how each one gets smarter
Which models
Any: Nous Portal, OpenRouter, OpenAI, or a local Ollama or LM Studio, with per-agent routing and failover.
Whatever your CLI subscriptions already buy: Codex, Claude, Grok, OpenCode, Gemini, Cursor, Cline, Kimi, Devin, Copilot, Mistral — and Hermes over ACP.
Routed through the platform gateway (glm-5.3, kimi-k3, minimax, nemotron for council and gates). Personal provider keys are a policy nobody has set.
Memory
The deepest of the three: curated memory, full-text recall, a user model and a long-term graph. It compounds — that is the product.
Deliberately thin. The provider's session, the agent's folder, skills and routines persist; understanding does not. It hands you a blank chat each time.
Not memory but retrieval: 23 bases, scoped per seat, quoted back with citations. Your thread with an assistant stays private to you.
Skills and extension
A library that writes itself: 246 SKILL.md files here, plus plugins, hooks, toolsets and 16 MCP servers with per-server filters.
A local skill library and a marketplace of skills, agents and apps; routines and webhooks; and ACP, so a new CLI is one row, not a rewrite.
64 console sections, slash commands ( /gaps, /cite, /owner), per-unit knowledge cards, and connectors that wait on OAuth.
Schedules and background
A real cron, with delivery: 16 active jobs here, running headless and reporting into channels.
Routines and webhooks that need the host awake; FIFO queues with pause, resume, cancel and crash-safe persistence.
Housekeeping, not agency: the nightly re-index and the disk guard. Workflows and triggers stay empty until Zoho consent lands.
Work who does what, and with what hands
Working with other agents
Delegation routes, Bot Mode, profiles as workers. Powerful and mostly invisible — the kanban is a CLI, not a picture.
The clearest model of teamwork: channels with one task owner, explicit delegation, reassignment, and files passed agent to agent with manifests.
One assistant per unit plus shared desks; a council of models reviews work — though that council currently fails before it reaches a model.
Files and repos
Terminal tools, native git worktrees, AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md; repository judgement left to the operator.
Each agent gets ~/OpenBot/Agents/<id> plus a shared folder, and a browser it can drive. It has no opinion about git.
Documents, not repos: retrieval over WorkDrive and the unit registers, with the employee's own account deciding what opens.
Computer use
A full computer-use toolset, with container isolation optional rather than default.
An optional driver bundled into the release, handed to every provider on all three operating systems.
None, on purpose. It reads the documents it is allowed to read and answers; a change to a live system waits for a person.
Trust the part that decides where each one may live
Permission model
Approvals per command, sandboxed backends, secret redaction, a permission system per profile. Configurable, and therefore only as good as the config.
An explicit product decision: full access, approvals never, one consent at first launch. It says so in its own README — that is not a boundary.
Default deny. A seat sees only its own collection; a personal connector answers for that person alone; company credentials are labelled and admin-only.
Licence
MIT. No paid tier, no seat count, nothing to buy. The only vendor cost is the model you point it at.
Source-available under PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0. Commercial use needs seats. Apache-2.0 only up to 0.1.11.
In-house. No licence to renew and no upstream to outrun — and exactly one operator on the hook when it breaks.
Money and momentum what it costs, and what it would take to regret
What you pay
Tokens only. Everything else is yours, including the electricity and the maintenance.
A seat per person; agents do not count, and the subscriptions already on your card carry over. Noncommercial use is free.
Internal. Month-to-date reads $0.00 across fifty runs, every run marked partial, and no spend cap set — a defect, not a bargain.
Traction
266,611 stars, pushed today. A real ecosystem around an MIT core.
11,758 stars; a release nearly every day from a small team, and a licence that tightened at 0.1.11 — momentum with single-vendor risk attached.
One deployment, sixteen ClickUp spaces, no external users yet. Its maturity is your own uptime record.
How it fails
By weight. It is the heaviest harness measured here and needs an operator who enjoys configuration.
By trust. You consent once and it has your whole disk, with no second gate between a strange page and a shell.
By telemetry and by one host. Costs and completion are not closed out, dumps sit on the same disk as the database, and a broken tool stays broken until someone looks.
01 — the engine

Hermes, for the work that must think

Scheduling, memory that accumulates, twenty channels, local models, skills that write themselves. The only one of the three that gets better at your work the longer it runs.

Keep it as the engine. Don't make it the company's front door.
02 — the cockpit

OpenBot, for watching several agents at once

A real answer to the mess of six terminal windows. It carries Hermes as one of its providers, so it can be a cockpit over the engine rather than a rival to it.

A week-long trial on your own desk. Not a migration, and not for staff.
03 — the building

AgenticOS, for seventeen colleagues

The only one whose product is the boundary: identity at the door, knowledge scoped per seat, writes held for a person. Nobody else here is built to say no to a question.

Already the right answer for staff. Its gaps are plumbing, not architecture.

The honest read

Comparing them as rivals flatters OpenBot and undersells AgenticOS. OpenBot is a shell — a very good one — that would lose every row about memory, scheduling and governance, and win the only row that matters at a desk: can I see five agents working and talk to any of them.

AgenticOS is not competing for the same job at all. It is the only one that can be handed to a finance manager without handing her the company, and that is why its weaknesses look different: telemetry that reads zero, a council that does not run, no spend cap. Those are the failures of something already in production.

The practical arrangement writes itself. Hermes stays the engine for your own work and the fleet. AgenticOS carries staff and keeps owning identity and scoping. OpenBot is a trial, and the interesting experiment is not adopting it — it is pointing it at Hermes over ACP and seeing whether one window beats twenty terminal tabs.
