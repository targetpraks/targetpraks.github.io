Why the trio, and not one big pot

Three matched pieces in one colour read as a set the moment the box is opened — that is the difference between a gift and a purchase. They also cover the whole of how a couple actually cooks: a deep pot for slow food, a small round oven for two, and a wide shallow one for anything that needs searing. Le Creuset's own Wedding Registry Guide asks for three to five investment pieces; this is exactly three, every one of them at half price or better.

And it is not a compromise on money. The steel set this replaces cost R11 799; the trio is R562 less and R9 860 off its own regular price. The prettier answer is the cheaper one.

One box, or three cart lines?

Both things are true, and they are different products. Le Creuset does sell a real 3-piece set — one SKU, one box, R3 699 today, half price. What is in it is one cast iron cooking piece and two extras: a 24 cm cast iron sauteuse, a stoneware 23 cm square serving dish, and a silicone trivet. The trio above is the opposite trade — three cooking vessels, no accessories, three separate cart lines.

Blue versus Cerise is a straight trade. Azure gets you a bigger single pot (30 cm for R5 819) on a deeper discount than the Cerise trio's largest, but only two shapes exist in blue, so there is no braiser. Cerise buys three shapes and the widest colour availability. Neither is cheaper per litre — pick the colour you would actually keep on the stove for twenty years, because that is the whole point of the object.
