The Western Cape has a real version of this market, and it is smaller and blunter than the international one. Eight of these 18 schemes publish no owner-use days at all. Of those that do, 28 days a year is the local standard — and one scheme does something better than almost anything abroad: the Colosseum lets you stay in your own suite with no accommodation charge for 28 days, split any way you like, and lets you exchange those nights at RCI resorts worldwide.

03 The levy is where this market bites

Taj Cape Town - R11 612 levy on R6m 2.3% a year
Pepperclub - R10 579 on R4.75m 2.7% a year
Harbour Bridge - R7 055 on R6.495m 1.3% a year
Romney Park - R5 600 on a R2.5m-ish unit ~2.7% a year
Colosseum - R1 188 on R1.04m 1.4% a year
One Thibault - R880 on R1.45m 0.7% a year

Two practical consequences. Cash-only: pool units in Century City are sold for cash because a bank will not bond a unit it cannot repossess cleanly - so the R1.04m Colosseum suite is not a 10%-deposit purchase. And the levy survives vacancy: a 2.7% levy on a unit earning nothing in a bad year is R125 000 a year of pure cost.

04 The ratio, applied locally

Lifestyle ROI = (services you actually use + costs you avoid) / (premium paid for the hotel + extra annual carry over a plain flat) Above 1.0 the lifestyle pays for itself; around 0.7 you are buying convenience at a fair premium; below 0.6 you are buying status.

Locally the premium is smaller than the 38% global average - a Cape Town hotel suite trades at maybe 15-30% over a comparable plain flat - but the carry is heavier, because a hotel levy funds staff and facilities a body corporate never pays for. On a R4.75m Pepperclub unit at R10 579 a month, the levy alone is R127 000 a year before rates, where the flat next door might cost R36 000. Stay 20 nights a year and that is R6 350 a night in fixed cost before the room service.

05 What this means for you

  1. The Colosseum is the best-documented owner deal in the province - 28 free nights, RCI exchange, R1 188 levy, R1.04m entry. It is also cash-only, so it is a use-and-income purchase, not a leveraged one.
  2. KLEI is the only scheme here with a multi-year guarantee (8% for three years, Totalstay), and it is bondable - but you get 14 days, and you are buying in Stellenbosch, not on the coast.
  3. The Taj and Pepperclub are the wrong end of the levy curve. R11 612 and R10 579 a month buy service, not yield.
  4. Ignore the ones with no published days. Eight schemes here will not tell you how long you may stay. That silence is the answer.

06 Method and gaps

Western Cape enforced on merge: any record without a Western Cape province field was rejected. Codex produced 14 schemes with levies; I added and verified four more (Island Club, Radisson Blu Residence, Hermanus Beach Club, Cape Royale). Each was scored by the local clef gate on a lifestyle rubric - published use rights, wellness, access, service, against levy and rental terms. 0 failed to score.

Gaps: owner-use days are published for only 7 of 18; rental splits are almost never published; several entries are agency listings rather than developer price lists; and no sale agreement or body-corporate rule was read. The Colosseum's 28 nights come from an agency listing - good evidence, but not the title deed.
