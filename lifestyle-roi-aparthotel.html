What the lifestyle is actually worth

Condo-hotels, aparthotels and branded residences judged on owner utility rather than yield — which brands deliver daily value, which amenities are levy-filling decoration, and the fine print that decides whether you own the lifestyle or merely rent it back from the operator.

The framing question changes everything. If you are buying for yield, the answer is almost always no — pooled hotel units net 4–6% before tax and cannot be bonded in most markets. If you are buying for how you live, the right question is not "what does it return", it is "what would this lifestyle cost me to buy separately, and what am I paying for the packaging?" That ratio is what this document builds.

01 Four archetypes, and who wins on lifestyle

The market has grown from 354 schemes in 2015 to 903 at the end of 2025, passes 1,000 this year, and is projected at 1,800 schemes and 300,000-plus units by 2031 (Knight Frank, September 2026). More than half of new openings are now outside major cities — coastal, island and mountain — which is exactly where lifestyle buyers go.

That premium is the price of the lifestyle, and it is the first number in the ratio below.

03 Fine print watchlist

These are the clauses that convert "I own a hotel home" into "I own a hotel room I may visit". Ask for each in writing before any deposit.

ClauseWhat to look for
Owner-use capDays per year, and whether they can be taken in peak season. Programmes commonly run 14–28 days; some limit personal nights in high season and impose minimum stays over holidays (owner-blocking rules).
Blackout dates and owner chargesThe operator may set blackout periods annually and assess an additional daily charge when you use your own unit (rental-program drafting guidance). Ask for the current calendar and the charge schedule.
Pool participation and exclusionIs participation mandatory? What is the split — 50/50, 60/40, 71/29? Can you leave the pool, and what does leaving cost (levies returning to you, furnishing reinstatement)?
Fee stackHOA levy, hotel management fee, marketing/reservation fee, FF&E reserve, insurance, utilities. Owners pay well beyond the purchase price — model the total, not the levy.
Special assessments and brand capex"Brand standard" refurbishments are called by the operator and paid by owners. Ask for the last three years of assessments and the next FF&E cycle date.
Membership termsAt Six Senses Place membership is annual, tiered, personal and non-transferable (FAQ) — so the club is a subscription, not an asset. Check what happens to your access if you sell.
Resale and re-licensingDoes a buyer have to be approved by the brand? Can the brand de-flag the building, and what happens to your services and your value if it does?
Guest accessCan your guests use the pool, gym or lounge, and at what charge? Some schemes bill private-facility use per guest per day.
Securities framingIn the US a rental programme can be treated as an investment contract — the reason Florida's documents are so heavily lawyered (Savrann). Where you buy matters to how you may be sold to.

04 The Lifestyle ROI verdict

The ratio Lifestyle ROI = (value of the services you actually use + costs you avoid) ÷ (brand premium + extra annual carry over a plain flat)

Both sides in rand per year. Above 1.0 the lifestyle pays for itself. Around 0.7 you are buying convenience at a fair premium. Below 0.6 you are buying status, which is a legitimate purchase — just not an investment.

Worked example. A full-service branded residence at R10m against an unbranded equivalent at R7.25m — a 38% premium. The R2.75m premium, invested at the 5.31% after-tax bank rate, is R146 000 a year of forgone return. Add the extra carry: R6 000 levy against R2 500 is R42 000 more a year. Total cost of the lifestyle: about R188 000 a year.

Now price what you get. Housekeeping at R6 000 a month is R72 000; concierge and security worth R2 000 a month is R24 000; a gym with classes at R1 200 a month is R14 400; a dozen spa visits R12 000; parking and valet R9 600; the convenience premium on in-residence dining perhaps R18 000. Substitutes: roughly R150 000. The ratio is 0.80 — you pay about R1.25 for each rand of service you would otherwise buy, and the difference is the price of never thinking about any of it.

Five questions that settle it

  1. Will you be there 60 nights or more? Below that, the per-night cost of the lifestyle is worse than any hotel you could book.
  2. Does the amenity replace something you already pay for? Housekeeping, gym, security and childcare do. Rooftop bars and wine rooms don't.
  3. What is the brand premium in this specific building? Price per square metre against the best unbranded stock in the same suburb. Savills puts the average at 38%; anything well above that needs a reason.
  4. Who controls your calendar? If the operator sets blackouts and charges you to stay in your own unit, you own an asset and rent a home.
  5. What does the exit look like? A branded building must be re-approved by the brand and re-sold to a buyer the brand accepts. Liquidity is a service, and it is priced.

05 What this means in South Africa

The local market is small but it is exactly this model, and the numbers are visible. Applying the same ratio to what we priced this week:

The Rockefeller, Foreshore — head-lease studio R2.0m · 5.8% net · Newmark pool, ~8% fee
The Delaporte, CBD — operator-backed 9% net for 12 months · 28 owner nights
Island Club, Century City — revenue pool ~4.5% net · 28 owner days
The Colosseum — pool unit 71% of rental to owner · cash only, no bond
Shark Bay Villas, Langebaan — leaseback R2.2m · 10.5% advertised · 28 usage days

Two South African specifics worth carrying into any decision. Pool units generally cannot be bonded — the Colosseum listing says so outright — which removes leverage and concentrates risk. And the levy is the lifestyle's real price: Century City pool units carry levies near R2 000–2 800 a month, the Taj Hotel Residences R11 612 plus rates, and a Pepperclub unit needs about R35 000 a month of revenue just to return 5% after its carry. A South African branded residence at a 30–40% premium over a plain flat in the same suburb is priced like the global norm; the services behind it are usually thinner than a Mandarin Oriental's.

06 Method and sources

Brand programmes, membership terms and pooling rules were read from the operators' own pages and from industry drafting guidance; premium data from Knight Frank's September 2026 survey and Savills' 2026 and 2026/27 editions. The ratio is a framework, not a valuation: the substitute values in the worked example are illustrative and should be replaced with your own spending for the building you are considering. Where a term is not published — most owner-day schedules are not — the honest position is that it is unknown until the sales pack arrives.
