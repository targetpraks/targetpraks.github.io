03 What the fine print actually says

ClauseWhat the catalogue shows
Owner-use daysFrom 10 network nights (BlackBrick) and 14 days (Martinhal) to 70 (Ombria) and 84 (Wyndham Grand Algarve standard apartments). Most Dubai and London schemes publish nothing at all.
Discounted stays are not possessionThe Rockefeller's owner benefit is a discounted hotel booking; BlackBrick's is nights elsewhere in the network. Read the clause as a stay allowance, not as your apartment.
Mandatory lettingViceroy Ombria requires exclusive operator letting; Martinhal sells investment apartments with a guaranteed return. There the operator controls the calendar.
Peak season and noticeOmbria's 70 days carry seasonal limits. Six Senses Courchevel asks for notification rather than imposing blackouts, which is materially better drafting.
The fee stackService charge, hotel management, marketing, FF&E reserve, insurance, utilities. Several entries publish a price and nothing else, which is the due-diligence list in order.
Membership is rentedSix Senses Place memberships are annual, tiered, personal and non-transferable. Selling the unit does not sell the club.

04 Amenities: what earns its keep

  • High value: in-residence dining, housekeeping, concierge, security, storage, valet, a gym with classes, and family facilities. That last one is childcare, and childcare prices well.
  • Medium: spa you pay per treatment, thermal suites used in winter, co-working and meeting rooms.
  • Low: rooftop bars, signature restaurants, private cinemas, wine rooms, golf and marina berths. Photographed for the brochure, used by guests, paid for by owners.

05 The ratio

Lifestyle ROI = (services you actually use + costs you avoid) / (brand premium + extra annual carry over a plain flat) Both sides in rand per year. Above 1.0 the lifestyle pays for itself; around 0.7 you are buying convenience at a fair premium; below 0.6 you are buying status - legitimate, but not an investment.

Worked example. A branded residence at R10m against an unbranded equivalent at R7.25m is a 38% premium, Savills' 2026/27 global average and about 40% in resorts. Invested at 5.31% after tax that R2.75m is R146 000 a year forgone; add R42 000 of extra levy carry and the lifestyle costs R188 000 a year. Substitutes - housekeeping R72 000, concierge and security R24 000, gym R14 400, spa R12 000, valet and storage R9 600, dining convenience R18 000 - total R150 000. Ratio 0.80.

South Africa scores badly on lifestyle for a structural reason: the local product is a hotel room with an income programme attached, not a residence with a service team. None of the three SA schemes publishes a service charge in its sales material, and one does not promise your own unit. The lifestyle case here rests on price, not service.

07 Method and gaps

Two independent legs on one written brief, blind to each other: Codex returned 24 schemes, the second leg 22; merged and de-duplicated to 39. Each was scored by the local clef gate on a four-band lifestyle rubric - wellness depth, usage rights, owner-only space, food and social, frictionless service, against fees and rental terms. 0 failed to score.

Gaps, stated: owner-use days are published for only about a third of the catalogue; service charges are missing for most Dubai and London schemes; several entries are broker listings rather than developer price lists; and no sale contract was read - this is marketing material, read carefully. A score is a judgement of published owner utility, not a legal opinion and not a valuation.
