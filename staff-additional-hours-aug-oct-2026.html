Method, sources, and what to check before Monday

Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this page is read from Zoho People → Attendance — the system the company already uses — for 1 August to 9 October 2026. Two reports were taken and cross-checked against each other:

  • Per-day detail — first clock-in, last clock-out, the person's assigned shift, and the system's own "extra time" figure for each date.
  • Per-month summary — present days, leave, weekend and holiday work for each person.

"After shift" is measured against the person's own shift, not a generic 9-to-5. The company runs several: Early Birds 07:00–16:00, Week Days 08:00–17:00, Late Bloomers 09:00–18:00, plus a Philippines shift at 08:00–17:00. Someone on Early Birds who clocks out at 18:00 has two hours recorded; the same clock-out on Week Days is one hour. That is why every detail line shows the shift.

Cross-check passed. The monthly summary and the per-day detail were compared line by line for all 39 staff across all three periods: 119 of 123 agreed to the minute. The four exceptions are named below, and all four are people on the Philippines clock.

The four exceptions, stated plainly

  • Ena Marie Olivares (Aug, Sep), Leamarie Mercado (Aug, Sep) and Sophia Tabagan (Aug) — the two reports differ by about 8 hours a month for these three. The whole difference sits on public-holiday dates: the per-day report credits the entire holiday shift; the monthly summary credits only the hours beyond the ordinary shift. Both figures are in Zoho. Page 1 uses the per-day figure. If the monthly summary is what payroll reads, these three holiday figures are roughly 8 hours lower each and the total drops by about 25 hours.
  • Ntombifuthi Nkambule (Oct) — 33 minutes per-day versus 20 minutes on the summary; a 13-minute rounding difference on one day.
  • No other person's figures are affected.

Doing the Monday conversation

  1. Lead with the top of page 1. 38 of 39 people have additional hours in the record. Naming the top few and their hours costs nothing and sets the tone.
  2. Use the two counts, not only the total. "Days" is how often they stayed; "Days over 1h" is how often it was significant. Someone with many days and a modest total habitually stays half an hour late — a different conversation from someone with a handful of very long days.
  3. Page 3 is the clearest evidence of beyond-scope work. Read those dates out by name; the person will recognise the day immediately.
  4. Do not present the totals as an entitlement. This page measures time on the clock. It says nothing about whether the time was authorised, productive or payable — those are your calls, not the report's.

The three decisions this leaves you

  • Is this the right basis to talk from? If you want it narrowed to a named team, one month, or only people above a threshold, say so and it re-cuts in the same format.
  • Whether the hours were actually worked is answered on page 5, from the file and message trail.
  • Should the long-clock-out days be confirmed first? They are listed above. If any is a clock-out error, that person's total changes.
  • Karabo Manenzhe's attendance record needs its own answer — no clock-ins for two months is an attendance-system question, not an overtime one.

First, a correction to the earlier page

The 838 hours on page 1 are the system's total, and I reported them as "additional hours". On inspection 172h 40m of that — 621 separate days — is less than 45 minutes a day: ten or twenty minutes past the shift end because a call ran over. That is normal working life, not extra work, and it should not be in a Monday conversation. The number that matters is 432h 06m across 213 days.

There is also a second number the first page never showed: 422h 32m of clock-ins before the person's own shift start. Zoho People does not count that as extra time at all, so it is not inside the 838 — but it is real time on the clock, and it changes who looks committed. Including it, the total time on the clock beyond, or outside, the person's own shift is 1261h 15m.

The test I applied. For every late day of 45 minutes or more, I looked for an actual artefact — a file modified in WorkDrive, or a message sent in Cliq — inside that exact window, from the moment their shift ended to the moment they clocked out. A file saved at 19:40 by someone who clocked out at 20:11 is work. Silence from 16:00 to 21:00 is a clock with no visible output behind it.

What I cannot see, stated plainly

  • Meetings, calls, WhatsApp, walk-ups. None of these leave a trace in Zoho. Someone can work an evening on the phone and produce no artefact at all. Where the table says "no trace", that is no trace in Zoho — not proof of no work. This is why the trace column is not a verdict column.
  • Read-only work. Opening a file, checking a report, reviewing someone else's work leaves nothing. The file trail records edits and uploads only.
  • Two separate channels of proof, both partial. A person who works entirely in Cliq shows in the message trail; a person who works entirely in documents shows in the file trail. I required either, never both, so the trace figure is a floor, not a measure of effort.
  • Whether any of it was authorised, or productive. The record shows the clock and the artefact. It says nothing about approval, quality, or value. Those are your calls.

So: working, or just clocked in?

  • The work is real, but the number was overstated. 19% of the late hours have a named artefact inside the exact window, and the closed-day work is beyond argument. But 172h 40m of the 838 was under 45 minutes a day — take that out before you use the figure.
  • Two people's totals are almost entirely closed-day work, not late nights. Olivares and Mercado are holiday shifts, not evenings. Treat them as a scheduling question, not a discipline one — and note they are on the Philippine holiday calendar.
  • The one test that settles it properly is not in this report. Ask each person for Monday's artefacts by Monday afternoon — the file, the note, the proof link. That converts "are they actually working" from an argument into a one-day measurement, and it is exactly the proof-link standard you have already asked the team for.
