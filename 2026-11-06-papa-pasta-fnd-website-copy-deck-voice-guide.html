0. How to use this deck

This deck is the authority for every word on the eight pages of the section 4.1 inventory. A developer implements copy from here; if the build needs a string that is not here, the string comes back to this deck first — it does not get invented in code.

Key convention — page.section.element page = home, create, brand, interest, franchise, gallery, seasonal, legal. section = the spec component (hero, proof, model, card, form…). element = h1, sub, cta.primary, label, error, empty, meta.title.

1. Voice guide

1.1 The register (spec §2.3, operationalised)

Context Register What it governs The test before a line ships
Hero / Hook Bold, confrontational page h1, section eyebrows Does it state a fact the reader did not expect, with no superlative in it?
Product claims Precise, confident model numbers, dimensions, process Could Ricardo defend this line in writing, with a source?
Social proof Understated, factual counters, vote totals, gallery Is it a number we can read from the database right now?
CTA language Invitation, not pressure every button, form heading Does it offer a next step rather than demand one?
Legal / disclosure Neutral, compliant /legal, consent, disclaimers, form fields Would a regulator read this as a promise of income? If yes, rewrite.

One voice at four volumes. The page never changes personality between sections: a confrontational hero is followed by a quiet, factual proof bar, not by a second shout.

1.2 Sentence mechanics

  • Person. Second person "you" everywhere in marketing copy. "We" only where the team performs an action ("we only record your interest when you choose to send it"). Never "the company", never "users".
  • Tense. Present. No "will revolutionise", no "is set to become".
  • Case. Headings in sentence case, including eyebrows ("The contrarian model", not "The Contrarian Model"). Proper brand nouns keep their capitals (§1.3).
  • Emphasis. One italic fragment per headline, maximum, marking the turn of the sentence. Bold is reserved for numbers inside data blocks.
  • Punctuation. No exclamation marks anywhere in product copy. No emoji. One em dash per paragraph at most — prefer a colon or a full stop. En dash for ranges (9–11 months, when approved). Ellipsis is the single character, never three dots.
  • Contractions. Yes. "Hasn't", "it's", "you'll". This voice is spoken, not corporate.
  • Numbers. Numerals always (2 crew, 40sqm, 3 regions, 45 territories). Comma separators above 999. "R" prefix only on approved figures. Percentages with %. No spelled-out counts.
  • Units. "sqm" lowercase, no space. "6am" lowercase. "R" before the figure with no space when approved (R450 000 or R450,000 — pick one: R450,000, comma, per §1.2 numbers rule).
  • South African English. colour, recognised, organisation, licence (noun) / license (verb), programme (plan) / program (software), metre, centre. The live prototype already follows this; keep it.
  • Trademarks. The Living Crest™, Crest Creator™, Realisation Moment™ — ™ on first use per page, dropped on later uses in the same page. "Papa Pasta" never abbreviated, never "PP".
  • Readability. Body sentences ≤ 22 words. Hero headline ≤ 9 words. Button ≤ 4 words. Consent sentence ≤ 30 words.

1.3 Lexicon

Always capitalised: Papa Pasta · Crest · The Living Crest™ · Crest Creator™ · Realisation Moment™ · Territory · Franchise Applicant.

Preferred vocabulary: operator · franchisee · Territory · crew · fresh pasta · made in-store · colourway · palette · community · your city · your colour · the model · the build-up.

Never say: opportunity of a lifetime · guaranteed · risk-free · passive income · get rich · cheap · "low-cost" (unquantified) · side hustle · business opportunity (alone, as a promise) · join our family · revolutionary · world-class · premium quality (as a bare adjective) · best · unique (unless defined in the same sentence) · limited time (without a real end date) · act now · don't miss out · spots are filling fast.

Why each is banned. Either it is an unsupported superlative, or an unquantified price claim, or manufactured urgency — and manufactured urgency plus an unquantified price is exactly the combination the Consumer Protection Act (s41, false or misleading representations) and the franchise disclosure expectations look for. The ban is not a style preference; it is the legal guard-rail around a franchise recruitment site.

1.4 Claims policy — the money gate

  1. No money number publishes without Ricardo's written approval, recorded in §2 of this deck. Investment, fee, turnover, profit, payback, ROI, margin — all of it, including "R" figures in metadata and alt text.
  2. No earnings or payback claim, express or implied. "Pays for itself", "recovers your capital", "profitable by month 11" are the same claim as a payback figure and are equally blocked.
  3. No empty gaps. Until approval, the section ships with the approved placeholder wording in §2. An empty section invites ad-hoc copy at the last minute, which is how uncounselled claims get published.
  4. Every claim carries a key (claims.c01 …) so legal review and the dev build can trace it.
  5. The spec's own hero sub-copy cannot publish as written. "2 crew. 40sqm. 11-month payback." contains one approved target (40sqm), one unverified operating claim (2 crew) and one prohibited earnings claim (11-month payback). See C-01, C-03, C-04.

1.5 Micro-copy rules (system writing)

Element Rule Example
Button Verb + object, sentence case, ≤ 4 words "Create your crest", "Express interest", "Join waitlist", "Send my details" — never "Submit", "Click here", "Continue" alone
Disabled state Say why, in a tooltip or helper "Committed — this Territory has a signed franchisee."
Error What happened + what to do + data preserved "We couldn't save that crest. Your colours are still here — try again."
Success / toast Confirm the thing, not the system "Crest saved. It travels with you to the gallery."
Empty state Name the absence, offer one action "No crests here yet. Yours would be first."
Loading Ellipsis character "Loading territories…"
Field label Sentence case, no colon; helper above, error below "Preferred Territory"
Consent Never pre-ticked; names what is sent and by whom see interest.form.consent
Alt text Describe the image's job, not the file "Ridged rigatoni tubes on a neutral ceramic plate" (live, keep)
Link text Describes the destination "See how the model works" — never "read more"

1.6 SEO and metadata (spec §8.1)

  • One H1 per page. Title ≤ 60 characters, meta description ≤ 155. Both keyed meta.title / meta.description.
  • Priority clusters: P0 franchise opportunity SA, P0 Papa Pasta brand, P1 low-cost franchise SA, P1 territory-based franchise, P2 QSR industry terms.
  • Constraint on the P1 cluster. "Fast food franchise under R500k" is a price claim. It may not appear in a title, description, heading or body until the investment band (spec §12.1 item 7) is approved — see C-07. The site can rank on that intent without printing a price ("how a small-footprint QSR investment is built up"), which is what the draft metadata below does.
  • OG image = the visitor's own Crest on packaging. OG copy stays in the same register as the page.

1.7 Accessibility (spec §10.2)

  • Every image has alt text describing its job; decorative images take empty alt.
  • Icon-only buttons carry aria-label written as a verb ("Vote for this crest").
  • Never instruct by colour alone. "Select Available" — not "select the green one". The legend in /interest uses the words, not just the swatches.
  • Legal summaries are written to a grade-9 reading level; the clause text beside them is the lawyer's, not mine.
