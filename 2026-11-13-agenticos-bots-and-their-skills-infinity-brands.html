How it was set up

  1. Installed 42 gallery skills in one batch call — the catalogue went from 17 to 62, nothing overwritten.
  2. For each bot: read its spec, add the skills capability (it was absent on all 17 business bots), set skill_ids, save the draft, validate, publish.
  3. Read every published version back — 126 bindings, 0 missing.
  4. Asked two bots, at run time, to list what they can load. They named exactly their own set.
  5. Wrote three company skills from documents already signed off — the live project portal, the document standard, the brand map — keeping the task-list ids in a reference.md beside company-brief-filing rather than buried inside its instructions.
  6. Bound each to the seats that need it: nine units get brief filing, seven brand seats get brand truth, all twenty get the document standard. Republished, then read back from the published versions.
  7. Gave Infinity Brands Company Knowledge to exactly one bot — the general assistant — and asked it a question only that base can answer.

Three things this run turned up

1. The roster was renamed while this was running. Halfway through, the bots were renamed to their BU codes — ACDC to EXP, DDS to DID, BND1 to BO1, INXL to FXL, INXS to FXS — and the knowledge bases were renamed with them. Skills hang off the agent id, so the earlier bindings came through untouched; only a script matching on the display name would have quietly missed six bots. Everything here is keyed on the id or the slug now, which the rename did not touch.

2. The company knowledge base was reachable by nobody. Thirteen documents, the company AI policy among them, sat in Infinity Brands Company Knowledge while every bot read only its own unit base. It is now bound to one bot — the general assistant — rather than copied into eighteen bases that would drift apart. The question that used to get “I don't have that” now gets a cited answer.

3. The health harness was testing the wrong bot. It looked for a bot named “Finance”, but the bots were renamed to their BU codes, so it silently fell through to the first bot on the list. Fixed, and check six now follows whichever base actually holds the evidence. The platform reads 6 of 6.

What this is, and what it is not

The forty-two gold skills are vendor-written templates — good procedure, not our procedure. None of them knows our stores, our credit policy, our franchise agreement or our Zoho forms. The three coral skills do, because each was written from a document we had already signed off. That is the part that compounds: one company skill per repeatable job, and the template it replaces retires. Next in line, by what costs us most: store opening, month-end with our own accounts, and the franchise onboarding pack.

One caveat, stated plainly: a skill is an instruction, not a guard. The bots now decline to file into a live project on their own — but that is a sentence in a skill, not a permission the platform enforces. The hard limits still live in tool approvals, and those are the only ones I would rely on.
