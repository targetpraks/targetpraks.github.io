01 Hellenic Community of Cape Town — the one that is actually yours

24 Bay Road, Mouille Point · 021 433 2374 · info@hcct.co.za · map

What it is

The Greek community's home in Cape Town — church, school, hall, restaurant and soccer club on one site at the foot of Signal Hill, with the Atlantic across the road.

This is not a club you visit. It is the institution your wife's citizenship already belongs to, and the only place in the city where your children's Greek is normal rather than a lesson.

What you actually get

  • The Greek school — Kosmas Aitolos, on the same site: nursery, beginner/intermediate, advanced, and adult classes. It is an official Ellinomatheia exam centre, only the second in South Africa, so a child can leave with a Greek-language certificate recognised worldwide. The school's own page
  • A member rate on school fees. The published table lists members at roughly half the non-member rate: nursery R950 a term against R1,900, beginners R1,200 against R2,400, advanced R1,400 against R2,800. Fee table
  • The choir — 52 years old, founded by Effie Saridakis, three-part, and explicitly open to "passionate Hellenes (Greek and xeni)" — non-Greeks welcome. Rehearsals, church services, national days, and performances as far afield as the Baxter and the old Assembly Chamber in Parliament. Choir
  • Greek dancing classes for all ages — the community's own words: "for all ages and all who have a love of dancing". Dance
  • The Hellenic soccer club — a real club with pitches and leagues, not a kickabout. hellenicsoccerpark.spawtz.com
  • The cathedral — St George's in Woodstock, plus St Raphael's; the full Orthodox year, Holy Week through Pascha. Church
  • The Greek Kitchen — the community restaurant in Mouille Point, run by Helen: spanakopita, taramosalata, moussaka, slow-roast lamb, baklava. Greek Kitchen
  • Venue hire — main hall to 350, function area to 120, bar/restaurant to 120, and the renovated church hall with stage and kitchen. Useful for King Protea events and family functions. Venues
  • A vote. The family plan carries voting rights for two adults at the AGM — you are a member of the community, not a customer of it.

What they actually put on

From the community's own news feed — this is a live calendar, not a brochure:

  • Holy Week, day by day — Bridegroom services, Holy Unction, the decorating of the Epitaphios, the Good Friday procession, the midnight resurrection service, and Red Eggs Day on Holy Thursday. The 2025 week, photographed
  • Patron saint's feast — St George, with the liturgy and the community lunch that follows.
  • 25 March — Greek Independence Day, the community's biggest national-day function.
  • Tsiknopempti — the "smoky Thursday" barbecue before Lent.
  • National Women's Day — the community's own gathering each August.
  • Diplomatic and civic evenings — the community hosts the diplomatic corps around the State of the Nation address, and its choir has sung in Parliament.
  • The baking and the bazaars — "ladies who bake", the pastry days, the Easter preparations.

Why join — in one sentence

R2,500 buys your two eldest a Greek education at half price, your wife a community that speaks her language, and your family a seat at every Greek event in Cape Town — and on the published fee table one child in nursery already saves R2,850 over three terms, more than the subscription.

The honest counter-case

  • It is a community subscription, not a facility. The money goes to the church, the clergy and the centre — no pool, no gym, no tennis.
  • The plan runs to 31 December and renews each January. Joining in November buys two months for a full year's fee — ask for pro-rata or submit in January.
  • The published school-fee table is dated 2019. Directionally right, numerically stale — my draft email asks for the current one.

02 The Portuguese Club — the one with no paperwork

10 Donegal Street, Milnerton (Rugby) · Rae, 078 422 3570 · Facebook · Sorriso on Instagram · map

What it is

A club in the old sense — a ground, a bar, a kitchen and a hall — with a Portuguese restaurant, Sorriso, running inside it. Next door to the Italian Club on the same Milnerton block.

This is the club your mother's world would recognise: a room where the food, the language and the football are all Portuguese, and nobody asks you to fill in a form.

What you actually get

  • Sorriso — authentic Portuguese food "with some twists", a bar, and outdoor seating. This is a restaurant you would go to anyway; membership makes it yours. Sorriso
  • Live music and karaoke nights — bookable Tuesday and Friday, with family-friendly afternoons and evening shows.
  • New Year's Eve — their own party, live band (Kanimambo, Latin rock) and a DJ from Smile 90.4FM. The listing
  • Big-screen matches — Springboks and Portuguese football on the big screen, with the kitchen open.
  • Function space — the club and grounds are hired out for festivals and parties; the Perception Summer Festival used it as its venue. Listing
  • The Portuguese network around it — the Portuguese Welfare Society (founded 1975, NPO 001-170) and the Academia do Bacalhau, the Madeiran dining society whose 51st world congress was held in Cape Town in October 2024. Those two are where the older Portuguese families actually are.

What they actually put on

  • Sorriso evenings — live music, karaoke, family lunches; the Friday and Saturday trade is the club's heartbeat.
  • New Year's Eve — band, DJ, dinner.
  • Speciality lunches — the Portuguese classics, leitão among them, advertised on their Instagram.
  • Match days — the big screen, the bar, the kitchen.
  • Portugal Day — the Royal Cape Yacht Club even runs a Portugal Day Race, which tells you how woven the Portuguese community is into Cape Town's older institutions.

Why join — in one sentence

Because it is the only Portuguese institution in the city with a bar, a kitchen and a football screen — the place where your children meet other Portuguese kids without it being arranged, and where you are family rather than a customer.

The honest counter-case

  • Nothing is published: no website, no form, no fee. The Facebook page has 1,400 followers and its last public post is July 2025; the restaurant's Instagram is the live channel. This cannot be done from a desk.
  • You do not know what membership includes until you ask — bar discount, event priority, voting, hall use are all assumptions until Rae says otherwise.
  • It is a long drive from Welgemoed. It will be a fortnightly place, not a weekly one.

My recommendation on this one: call, don't email, and offer to help on the events committee. In a club with no paperwork, the way in is a person who remembers your name — the script is in section 07.

03 The European circuit — four more doors, very different value

Two of these are among the cheapest memberships in the city and give real, usable things. One needs a proposer you do not have. One may have closed.

Italian Club of Cape Town — R800 a year for the family

16 Donegal Street, Milnerton · admin@icct.co.za · restaurant 021 023 3667

What you actually get

  • A restaurant and licensed bar with a pool table and a big screen for Serie A — Saturday evenings with live entertainment, reservations essential.
  • Discounted restaurant, bar and venue hire as a member — the club states this as the core of the membership.
  • Bocce on sand-and-limestone courts, with a Bocce president and regular play.
  • An archery club — 18 m indoor and targets out to 90 m; club membership is the prerequisite, so your membership is the key.
  • Two five-a-side courts and the restored full soccer field.
  • An Italian library, borrowable during club hours.
  • Function rooms from a 12-seat boardroom to a 450-seat hall, and a grassed area for 150.
  • The luncheon calendar: Easter, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Remembrance Day, Christmas, New Year's Eve, discos.

Why join

It is the cheapest family membership in this document that comes with a restaurant, a bar, a sport and a function venue — and the Italian and Portuguese communities in Cape Town overlap heavily, so this is the same evening out twice.

Why it is not first

The proposer. Their rules require one current member of two years' standing who has known you for a year. You have neither. And unlike the Hellenic community, nothing here is aimed at your children's education — it is a social and dining club.

Year one: R800 family + R250 new-member admin = R1,050. Membership runs January–December; pensioner couple R300, single R400.

Swiss Club — R700, and your family joins free

Green Point, off Bill Peters Drive · membership@swissclub.co.za

What you actually get

  • Free access to Jonkershoek and reduced camping fees there — a mountain estate an hour from home, for nothing.
  • Discounted prices at club events and facilities.
  • 2 × 32 kg baggage per person on Edelweiss, Zurich↔Cape Town — on one family trip that is worth more than the subscription.
  • Traditional Swiss events: Ostern, Samichlaus, the big-match gatherings.
  • The newsletter and the business network — preferential advertising rates for your own business in their newsletter and site.
  • Invitations to the club's functions, and the Swiss-Austrian house shared with the Austria Club.

Why join

R700 for the household, no proposer, and the only membership here whose baggage benefit alone can repay the fee in a single trip. It is the best-value line in the whole document.

Why it is not first

It is a social and travel-benefit club, not a place your children go weekly. The value is real but occasional — Jonkershoek weekends, the Christmas events, the flights.

04 The high table — where the money and the old families are

Three very different rooms. One is about being seen in the right dining room, one is about your children's weekends, one is about sailing. Read the "what you get" column, not the prestige.

The Cape Town Club — 18 Queen Victoria Street, Gardens

Founded 1858 · in its Herbert Baker building since 1898 · closed 2017, reopened 2024 · membership@thecapetownclub.co.za · 021 001 4409

The club whose rooms have hosted a century of Cape Town's commercial leadership — reopened two years ago with women members since 1979 and a deliberately broader membership.

What you actually get

  • Three venues under one roof: a café for coffee and casual food; Pelagos Gardens, modern Cape cuisine in the old ballroom with Table Mountain views, high ceilings and fireplaces; and a members-only lounge for live jazz, DJs and cocktails.
  • The Library — historic volumes and artefacts, a quiet place to read or work, with intimate talks and discussions from time to time.
  • The cigar lounge — deep Chesterfields, a stocked humidor, a barman who remembers your usual. This is the room that answers "high society" more directly than any other line in this document.
  • Two boardrooms with presentation tech and Wi-Fi, amid South African historical artefacts — where you would take a partner or a bank.
  • A private dining room for eight, with dedicated service — the room for the dinner that matters.
  • Bedrooms in the heritage house, for late nights and early meetings.
  • 26 reciprocal clubs across Africa, Europe, Asia and the Americas — the full list is below, and it is the real reason the fee is defensible.

Why join — in one sentence

It is the only club on this list where a weekday lunch, a boardroom and a dinner for eight all happen in the same building — and where the card opens the Oporto club, the Travellers in Paris and the Jockey Club in Vienna when you travel.

The honest counter-case

  • No sport, no pool, no children's programme. This is an adult, city, business club.
  • The committee decides. You have no proposer — the club says applicants without one should "confer with management", which is a conversation, not a guarantee.

Kelvin Grove Club — the family's weekend engine

144 Campground Road, Newlands · membership@kelvingrove.co.za · 021 658 4502 · centenary 2027

The largest sports club in the Western Cape, 363 days a year under 24-hour security, and the one place on this list where all five of you can spend a Saturday.

What you actually get

  • 11 tennis courts, eight floodlit, plus a practice court — social, coaching, league.
  • 3 padel courts run by Padel 365, bookable on Playtomic.
  • 8 squash courts, a resident coach, two annual tournaments, WP league sides.
  • Two croquet courts — the club calls them the best in the Western Cape — and two bowling greens.
  • A members-only gym by the pool bar: free weights, cardio, stretching area.
  • Bridge four sessions a week in the Cream Lounge, no partner needed.
  • Restaurants and coffee shop, kids' play area, and a 400-seat ballroom for functions at member rates.
  • Member benefits: 10% off beverages on a pre-funded account, a bottle of wine in your birthday month, a birthday treat from the coffee shop, preferential venue hire.
  • Golf at preferential rates at Rondebosch, 15 minutes away.

What they put on

  • New Year's and Christmas Eve dinners
  • Valentine's dinner
  • Monthly club luncheons
  • Monthly gourmet evenings
  • Monthly wine tastings
  • The annual wine festival and the annual gin festival
  • Inter-club sport: WP squash leagues, tennis leagues, bowls and croquet competitions

Reciprocity, named

Carlton Club, RAC London, MCC, Army & Navy, Travellers, Oxford & Cambridge, Sloane Club — plus the Koninklijke Industrieele Groote Club in Amsterdam, Societeit de Witte in The Hague, Saint James Club Paris and International Club Berlin. The MCC itself lists Kelvin Grove as a reciprocal club. MCC list

Why it is not first

R16,950 for the year is nearly seven Hellenic subscriptions, it needs a proposer or a GM interview, and the subscription year runs March–February — so a November start is pro-rata into a year you then renew in March. Nothing here your children could not also get from a good sports club; what you are paying for is the 40-club reciprocity and the Newlands address.

Royal Cape Yacht Club — the cheapest prestige card in the city

Table Bay harbour · membership@rcyc.co.za · 021 421 1354

What you actually get

  • Sailing without owning a boat — the club states there are always spaces on other boats; tell the sailing office you want crew duty.
  • Automatic South African Sailing membership with your RCYC card.
  • No load-shedding — the club sits in an operational port — plus free Wi-Fi, which makes it a genuine city office with meeting rooms.
  • Member discount at the restaurant and bar, and discounted venue hire for private and corporate functions.
  • Free access to club social events, weekly draws, the racing and cruising calendar.
  • A new-members meet and greet, and a members' card that signs guests in six times a year.
  • Cadet membership for under-18s at R712 a year — the cheapest route to getting Donatello on the water.
  • Marina facilities — 18-ton crane, 40-ton slipway, workshops, chandlery, laundry.

Why consider it

At R8,455 for you and Julie it buys a harbour clubhouse, a sailing life for the children at R712 each, and a load-shedding-proof office — at less than half the Cape Town Club.

Why it is not first or second

The door. A proposer and a seconder, both members for six months; a three-month scrutiny with your name and photograph on the notice board; then a committee ballot. You know nobody there. And sailing is a commitment, not a lunch.

2025/26 fees: R7,018 ordinary + R1,287 spouse + R150 card. Membership year July–June, pro-rata from August.
