The name: why the internals stay

"Quorum" was hard to say and harder to remember. Infx Quotify says what it is and carries the INFX family name. The repo stays quote-program, the hook file stays quorum.pb.js, and the API stays /api/quorum/* because renaming a deployed route and a collection prefix is a migration, not a rebrand. The PRD records that decision (D1) so nobody "fixes" the inconsistency later without a plan.

Zoho Books or Zoho Inventory?

Books. Not because Inventory is worse, but because the things this desk must prove are financial records:

What the desk needs Zoho Books Zoho Inventory Verdict
Vendor bill, input VAT, general ledger The accounting book itself Posts through the Books integration; not the ledger Books
Vendor payment and proof-of-payment attachment Vendor payments, bill attachments, payment documents Payments made exist, but the ledger and documents belong to Books Books
Purchase order Yes — lines, taxes, currency Yes — stock purchase orders One origin; Books
Goods receipt, stock valuation, warehouses Partial Yes Inventory
Reports finance signs Yes Stock reports only Books

Zoho's own integration documentation says items, contacts, purchase orders, bills and reports synchronise between Inventory and Books (accounting flow). That is exactly why the desk must pick one origin per document: creating the same PO in both systems creates two numbers and two truths.

The rule in the PRD: Infx Quotify creates the PO in Books, sends its own QX-YYYY-NNNN as reference_number, and reads back the Books-issued purchaseorder_number. Stock receipts happen in Inventory and flow back. Books owns the official number (D12); Quotify never runs a second series.

VAT is the blocker. The July 2026 tenant audit records the Books organisation as is_tax_registered: false. The PRD fails closed: no live push until finance confirms the registration, tax IDs and rates. A PO is not a tax invoice, and the old 1.15 divisor is deleted, not moved into a constant (WS-4).

Zoho Creator Procurement — what it is and what we found

Zoho Creator does ship a Procurement application: purchase requisitions, purchase orders, goods received notes, budget management, multilevel approvals, vendor management, multi-currency, a central document repository, analytics and mobile access (features).

What Infinity Brands actually has, per the July 2026 Creator audit: five applications — Cleaning Schedule, IT Asset Register, GovPortal, Site Inspector, Content Review — and no procurement app. The Zoho Procurement service is disabled on the tenant. So there is nothing to "link" today.

Decision: do not deploy it as a second procurement system. It overlaps the desk at requisition and approval, but it does not provide the three-quote proof, the VAT-exclusive like-for-like matrix, the hash-chained audit trail or the advisory evidence record. Running both would create two queues and two truths. Borrow these patterns instead:

If it is ever adopted, the only coherent split is Creator as the intake front-end, Books as the ledger, Infx Quotify as the quote and decision record. That is an explicit decision (Q17), not a silent integration.

The AI decision model: evidence, not a decision

Two layers, deliberately separated:

  1. Deterministic scorecard (code). VAT-exclusive landed total, variance to the cheapest comparable, lead time, validity remaining, payment terms, compliance state, award count, on-time rate, quality/rejection rate, and the vendor's own price history. Every number is computed in integer cents and reproducible.
  2. Local model narrative (LiteLLM, on this Mac). It reads the scorecard, the request context and the internal review notes, and returns a structured advisory: a recommendation (or an abstention), per-quote strengths and risks, line risks, questions to ask, and an evidence list.

What makes it safe in a three-quote regime:

  • Blind first. The decider records a provisional choice and reason before the model view is revealed. The model output is sealed at that moment, so it cannot be tuned to the choice. After the reveal, changing the choice requires a recorded reason.
  • Structural isolation. The award command imports no model module and cannot read qp_ai_runs. The model never writes winner, status, a price or a line label.
  • Hard stops. It abstains — and says why — on fewer than three valid quotes, a budget stop, an expired compliance document, a blacklisted or related-party-unresolved vendor, an unresolved different/unclear line, or a missing proof of payment where policy requires one.
  • Local only. No quote, review, vendor contact, bank detail or POP leaves the Mac. POP and bank data are never in the prompt.
  • Supplier text is data. Descriptions are attacker-controlled; the prompt delimits them and flags injection attempts.
  • Full audit. qp_ai_runs stores model id, prompt version, prompt hash, redacted input, output, output hash, latency and tokens. An auditor can reproduce what the approver saw after the model is upgraded.
  • Evaluation. 30 past awards, zero unsupported claims required, abstain rate and override rate monitored. A model that agrees more often but invents a delivery history has failed.

The model is a cited second opinion. It is not the decision, and it cannot satisfy the three-quote rule on the buyer's behalf.

Item-for-item: same, or not the same?

Every quote line gets one label, with evidence:

Label Meaning Counts in like-for-like total?
identical Same identity key and pack/unit Yes
equivalent Same function; pack or unit differs with a stated conversion Yes, with the conversion shown
substitute Different brand/model/spec, still meets the need No — shown separately; requester accepts if form/fit/function changes
different Not the requested item No — only a human may apply this
unclear Not enough data No — a person must resolve before award

The ladder is deterministic first: GTIN → MPN + brand → normalised identity → unit conversion → attribute comparison → fuzzy candidate finding → human. Fuzzy scores never produce identical, and an automatic label may never remove a quote from the counted set. Abstain is not different. The matcher abstains when the unit dimension differs, a pack size is missing, a numeric token differs, two candidates are within epsilon, or the text hits a character range the vendor normaliser refuses.

The journey Ricardo asked for

flowchart LR
  A[Staff request] --> B[Queue + owner]
  B --> C[Three quotes]
  C --> D[Item-for-item check]
  D --> E[Advisory model]
  E --> F[Requester confirms fit]
  F --> G[Manager approves spend]
  G --> H[PO in Zoho Books]
  H --> I[Goods received]
  I --> J[Invoice + proof of payment]
  J --> K[Four-way match]
  1. Request. A staff member submits the need: brand, department, need-by date, lines, specification, budget, attachments.
  2. Queue. The request enters a server-owned queue with a priority reason (need-by, age, value band) and an owner. Nothing sits unassigned without showing it.
  3. Work and time. A purchaser claims it; the service clock starts. A separate effort timer can be started and stopped. The clock pauses only in a named waiting-on state — requester, approval or goods — and the row shows who it waits on.
  4. Quotes ready. The requester and manager get one notification with a deep link: "three quotes ready, review and confirm".
  5. Requester confirms fit. They confirm the selected quote meets the need. This is not a spend approval.
  6. Manager approves spend. The manager approves within their band, with the evidence and the model view in front of them.
  7. Purchasing processes. The PO is created in Books, the outbox is observable, the request shows the Books PO number and state.
  8. Receipt and payment. Goods are received, the invoice is matched, the payment is recorded and the proof of payment is attached. The requester sees "ordered", "received" and "paid" without asking anyone.

Proof of payment

The desk records a payment; it never initiates one.

  • A payment record per payment, with amount, date, method, reference and last four digits only — never a full account or card number.
  • POP is a protected file with a SHA-256 hash and a malware scan; only finance, the owning purchaser and an auditor may open it; every open is logged.
  • Four-way match: PO line ↔︎ goods receipt ↔︎ supplier invoice line ↔︎ payment allocation. Tolerances are policy values; a mismatch creates an exception, not a silent pass.
  • A bank-detail change never happens through the desk. It is verified out of band by callback to a known contact, approved by two people, and recorded as a change event. This is the dominant supplier-payment fraud in South Africa.
  • POPIA: POP and bid exports carry names, amounts and bank references. They are role-scoped, never in a general export, and never sent to a model, gate or chat.
