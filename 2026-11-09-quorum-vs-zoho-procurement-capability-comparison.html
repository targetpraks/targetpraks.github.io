Quorum vs Zoho Procurement

A like-for-like read of the quotation desk we built against the source-to-pay product Zoho sells: what is actually live on each side, what is still a slide, and which one deserves the next rand of engineering time.

Quorum v2.9.0 · repo quote-program origin/main dd6fc29 · 7 Nov 2026 47 capabilities compared Prices verified 9 Nov 2026

Short answer: they are not the same animal. Quorum is a rules engine for the three-quote rule with a purchase desk attached — ZAR-native, VAT-exclusive, audit-chained, and free to licence. Zoho Procurement is a full source-to-pay suite — supplier portal, e-bidding, goods receipts, invoice matching, payments and contracts — priced per buyer. Zoho wins breadth 21 rows to our 8. We win the rows that decide whether an award is defensible. Buying Zoho today would add capability we have not asked for yet and remove the custom controls we built it for.

01

What each one actually is

Read this first: the comparison only makes sense once you separate a governance tool from an ERP module.

Ours · Quorum

A tender desk for one rule, done properly

Departments raise a requisition; purchasing captures three competing quotes with documents and VAT status; the desk normalises every bid to VAT-exclusive, shows the line-by-line matrix, and only unlocks award on the third valid bid. The manager signs with a reason; the approved award becomes a numbered PO and a Zoho-ready payload. Every step appends to an audit chain.

  • One file, PocketBase backend, Docker on Infinity's private staging host
  • ZAR and 15% VAT are the native assumptions, not a configuration exercise
  • Live functions: requests, quote capture, comparison, award, PO, budgets, spend report, vendor master, catalog, alerts
  • Built and maintained in-house; no vendor, no seat count, no exit negotiation
Theirs · Zoho Procurement

A source-to-pay suite with procurement in the middle

Employees raise requests from catalogs or Amazon Business; approvers route by role, department or amount; sourcing runs RFx events with supplier e-bidding; POs convert from awards; receipts are recorded as GRNs; invoices arrive in an inbox, are extracted by AI and matched two- or three-way; payments and vendor credits close the loop. Contracts and supplier scoring sit on the roadmap.

  • Vendor self-service portal, supplier onboarding and contextual chat
  • Approval workflow builder, real-time budgets, audit trails, custom roles
  • Analytics across spend, vendors, departments and categories
  • Sold per paid user, with 50 free requester licences per paid seat

04

Where each one genuinely wins

Stripped of marketing, the split is clean: we own the evidence of competition; Zoho owns the machinery around it.

Quorum · the governance core

Things we do that Zoho does not do out of the box

  • The three-quote gate is law. The request cannot reach approval until three distinct vendors have valid, priced, documented bids — enforced server-side, not by a reminder.
  • VAT-exclusive comparison is the default. Inclusive bids are normalised before ranking, so a vendor who quotes VAT-inclusive cannot look 15% expensive by accident.
  • Non-lowest awards need a written reason. The override memo and the sole-source waiver are part of the record, not a comment field someone can skip.
  • The audit trail is hash-chained and independently verifiable. Each event embeds the previous hash; a CLI verifies the export. That is a stronger claim than “audit logs available”.
  • It is shaped like this group. Brands, departments, ZAR, 15% VAT, tax-clearance expiry, related-party flags and a blacklist are first-class fields.
  • Zero licence, private hosting. No per-seat maths, no data leaving the estate, no vendor exit.
Zoho Procurement · the machinery

Things Zoho does that we have not built

  • Suppliers act for themselves. Onboarding, document upload, e-bidding, PO acceptance and invoice queries happen in a portal — no buyer re-keying.
  • Sourcing is a process, not a capture form. RFx events, deadlines, Q&A, bid comparison and shortlisting are native.
  • The chain continues past the PO. GRNs, partial receipts, invoice extraction, two- or three-way matching, vendor credits and payment status.
  • Approval routing is configurable. Hierarchical, parallel or ad hoc, by role, department or amount, with a workflow builder.
  • It is a maintained product. Support, uptime, security patching, mobile access, SSO — and a vendor who answers the phone.
  • It integrates with the books. Native links into Zoho Books and Inventory; custom ERP integration on Enterprise.
05

The uncomfortable truths, both ways

This is the part a vendor deck would leave out, and the part that decides the buy.

About ours

  • We have no supplier-facing surface at all. Every bid is keyed in by a purchaser. The audit chain proves the document did not change after capture — it does not prove the vendor sent it. That is a real control gap, not a technicality.
  • The chain ends at the PO. No goods receipt, no invoice match, no payment. Zoho Books still owns the money, so the “defensible file” stops one step short of the invoice.
  • Direct Zoho push is not built. The “Orders · Zoho” screen exports a paste-ready payload and records the outbox row as manual. Anything described as automated today is not.
  • A meaningful share of the newest work is switched off. Scoped bid reads, monetary capture, discounts, role authority, award execution — built and tested, but default-off behind flags. The live desk is the v2.9 behaviour, not the changelog's ceiling.
  • It is a one-machine system with a single maintainer. That is cheap until it is not: backups, restores, OAuth, migrations and uptime are ours to own.
  • The three-quote floor is configurable down to two. A setting that can weaken the group's own rule is a governance risk if nobody owns it.

About theirs

  • Five modules you may want are not shipped. Contract management, blanket POs, PO amendments, supplier scorecards and fixed-asset management are marked coming soon on Zoho's own pricing page.
  • Three-way matching is Enterprise-only. Premium stops at two-way, which is exactly the control that catches receipt-versus-invoice fraud.
  • Payments are not South African. ACH is US-only; connected banking is India-only. You would record payments, then execute them elsewhere — a gap, not a feature.
  • The South African three-quote rule is not a Zoho concept. You can approximate it with custom fields, validation and workflow, but it is configuration you own and must defend in an audit.
  • Cost scales with buyers, not with value. At $35–$75 per paid seat per year, a five-person buying team is $2,100–$4,500 a year before implementation; requesters are free, but they are also not the people doing the work.
  • It is still a suite you configure. Catalog, approval matrix, budgets, tax setup, vendor onboarding and Books integration are a project — the licence is the cheap part.

07

What it would take to close the gap

Rough sizing from the actual codebase — days and weeks of work, not a vendor quote. Ordered by what buys the most credibility per week.

  1. Direct push to Zoho Inventory 3–5 days OAuth self-client, encrypted token store, exactly-once outbox worker against the PO number. The schema and the duplicate guards are already there; the API call is the missing piece.
  2. Goods receipt (GRN) 2–3 weeks Receiver, date, accepted/rejected quantities with reason codes, delivery note, photo. Without it the “defensible file” has no proof the goods arrived.
  3. Savings ledger and quote coverage 1–2 weeks Two savings measures with a printed formula, value-weighted coverage, and desk-penetration reconciled to Zoho Books. This is the number Ricardo actually quotes to a franchisor or funder.
  4. Supplier quote-upload link 2–3 weeks A single-purpose magic link per tender: supplier uploads the PDF and keys prices; the buyer reviews. Closes the “purchaser invented the bid” gap without building a full portal.
  5. Then, only if volume demands it 4–8 weeks each RFx issuance and e-bidding, invoice inbox and matching, contract repository. These are the expensive ones, and they are exactly what Zoho already sells.
08

Recommendation

Three honest paths. The choice turns on volume and who is expected to do the work — not on which product is “better”.

Recommended now

Keep Quorum, close four gaps

Direct Zoho push, GRN, savings/coverage reporting, supplier upload link. That turns the desk from a good quotation tool into an end-to-end defensible purchase record, at no licence cost.

Choose this if: the three-quote rule and franchisor evidence are the point, the buying team is small, and R0 licence plus private hosting still matters.

Buy

Adopt Zoho Procurement Premium

Take the suite for supplier self-service, e-bidding, receipts, invoice matching and workflow routing. Budget for configuration and for Zoho Books/Inventory alignment; treat the SA three-quote rule as custom workflow you must document.

Choose this if: you are past roughly 150 invoices a month, have eight or more people touching procurement, or suppliers are demanding a portal.

Only with discipline

Hybrid: Quorum front, Zoho back

Quorum stays the tender gate and pushes approved POs into Zoho, which owns vendors, bills and payments. One vendor master, one PO entry path, no parallel ledgers.

Choose this if: you need both the SA evidence and Zoho's AP machinery — and can enforce that nobody buys outside the desk.

The one-line answer

Do not buy Zoho Procurement to replace Quorum. Buy it only when the work moves beyond quoting — when receipts, invoices and supplier self-service become the bottleneck. Until then, the gap that matters is not a missing module; it is the missing push, receipt and savings number in our own desk.
