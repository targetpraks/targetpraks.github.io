The Local Farmer · MMR mushroom room · 30 October 2026

Three audits, one answer

The CFO, the COO and Joe the farmer each took the mushroom model apart separately, from the same source files. They disagree on nothing that matters and agree on the thing that decides it.

Do not build a second tent. The room is not short of space — it is short of buyers.

The tent can make 27.6 kg a month once the physical limit and Joe's yield are both applied, not the 53 kg the sheet claimed. We sold 1.1 kg a month. Break-even is 192 bags of 100 g a month — 19.2 kg. That gap is the whole business case, and no amount of steel closes it.

The sheet is updated and live — every figure below is read back off it, not off this page.

What each seat concluded

Joe · the farmer

"The one tent can grow 53 kg a month and TLF sells 1.1 kg a month. The gap is sales, not space." A block yields 0.7–1.0 kg saleable, not 1.17 — so the room is 35–45 kg at best.

Not to add: a second tent, enoki/maitake/nameko, shiitake, an own-spawn lab.

CFO

"The costing is honest; the business case is not." Every build line ties to source at R23,515. At Joe's yield and 45 bags the room costs R396.52 a kg and only the R526 retail bag clears it.

"Do not build another tent, do not cut the price."

COO

"The model's arithmetic is clean and its physical premise is wrong. 63 bags is a multiplication, not a measurement." Seven levels in a 200 cm tent is 26 cm of clear height — that is punnets, not fruiting blocks.

Real room: 45 bags at five levels; 36 where hands fit. Trigger for tent two: 6.0 kg a week.

What to do now — in this order

  1. Weigh one full cycle. Scale, block tags, a trim-and-rejects bin, grams actually packed. Six weeks, ~45 blocks. Until then 1.4 kg, 90% and 7% are borrowed numbers. ≈ R300, and it decides everything below.
  2. Measure the shelf and one real block with a tape. Height of a 4–5 kg bag plus flush clearance settles 36, 45 or 63 bags — and settles the 45-versus-91 question at the same time. R0.
  3. Give colonisation its own dark space. About 24 bags of shelving at 7.5 a week. It is the difference between a 3-week and a 6-week turnaround on the same tent. ≈ R2,333 per seven levels.
  4. Fix the air, not the space. A real exhaust fan on the 203 mm duct, a second hygrometer on the top shelf, a fog-distribution pipe. The build's R1,150 fan only stirs the air. ≈ R1,500 with a CO2 meter.
  5. Soak for the second flush, and pack in vented bags. Joe: +0.25–0.45 kg a block on flush two, and 5–7 days of shelf life instead of two. ≈ R1,350.
  6. Sell 192 bags a month, from the room we own. That is 19.2 kg through the eleven accounts already on the book — R10,100 at R526 — and it clears break-even. This step takes the longest and it is the entire job. the only thing that turns the loss around.

The whole list is R6,583 — 28% of a tent, and none of it buys capacity. The test: get to 19.2 kg of sales a month and the room breaks even. If R6,583 cannot move 1.1 kg to 19 kg a month, the product has no market at this price and no tent fixes that.

Order the second tent at 6.0 kg a week.

Averaged over four consecutive invoiced weeks — not a feeling. That is 31 bags standing in a room that holds 45: nearly full and still selling. It has to be 6 and not 5, because the sheet's own arithmetic answers five kilos a week with 26 bags and no new tent. And it must fire early: a new tent takes six weeks before its first harvest.

Don't order if the four weeks aren't consecutive, or if more than half the kilos come from one customer — one account leaving takes a R23,515 tent back to idle. A second tent is 2.35 times Joe's entire fix-it list, and it buys capacity, not customers.

Two things only you can settle

Does the tent already exist? The sheet calls the R23,515 "1. The room — what we have", and Joe read it as the price of a second tent. If it is already standing, that money is spent and the only live number is 192 bags a month. Either way the action is the same, but the sheet should stop saying both.

Where do the colonising bags sit? If they have their own dark shelf, the room turns over 21 bags a week and the source's 91 blocks a month is right. If they sit in the same tent, 45.5 is right and half the grow room is a dark cupboard. It is one afternoon with a tape measure, and it swings wholesale from a R2,149 loss to a R3,793 profit.

What I changed in the live sheet

The workbook is updated — TLF MMR Growroom now carries the audit, not the plan. Every figure above was read back off it after the write:

  • Capacity: levels per shelf 7 → 5, so 45 bags not 63. Every downstream tab follows.
  • Yield: 1.4 kg gross → 1.05 (Joe's midpoint), saleable share 90% → 87%. Net 0.85 kg a block.
  • Block cost: R92.54 → R88.09, the oyster build rather than the 18-variety blend.
  • Packaging: added at R16.25 a kg retail, with the margin after it shown separately.
  • Tents tab: it was multiplying capacity by seven a second time and reading a blank build cost, so every payback on it said 0. Repaired — it now reads 45 bags, 6.4 kg a week, R23,515, 6.6 months.
  • Plan tab: the room limit was hard-coded at 63, so a capacity change never reached it.
  • New tab, "Audit 30 October 2026": the three verdicts in their own words, every change from → to with the reason, and what still has to be measured.

The one thing still open on your side: whether the tent is standing already, and whether colonising bags sit inside it. That is the 45-versus-91 question, and it is worth another tape measure.
