2 · What the seat asks for

The Administration blueprint in Constellation → Positions is built for a records-and-money seat, and its must-haves are hard lines rather than preferences:

  • Conscientiousness ≥ 55 — "records, money and paperwork are the function of this role".
  • DISC conscientiousness ≥ 55 — the same trait, second instrument.
  • Fairness ≥ 55 — the seat touches takings, stock and rules.
  • Heaviest weights after that: emotional stability, process discipline, honesty.

Moving up to the Chief Administration Officer seat adds what management always adds: extraversion, dominance, influence and enterprising drive move from "nice" to "important", and honesty and fairness become critical. A good administrator is not automatically a good administration manager — the manager blueprint asks for a person who fronts a room, argues a budget and holds a span.

6 · What I would actually do

  1. Do not appoint him to the seat. There is no vacancy that needs filling by an untested consultant while Anne is carrying the volume and Ntombifuthi holds the compliance register.
  2. Give him one pillar, in writing. The paperwork half he already does — buyouts, per diems, royalty checks, monthly Cin7/Xero reconciliation, statement checks — as a named queue with a due date on every item and a weekly one-page summary to Anne. That is a promotion of scope without a promotion of title.
  3. Judge him on two numbers, not on impressions. Overdue share of his own open queue, and on-time share of what he closes. Today those are 0% and 36%; a manager-grade run holds overdue near zero and lifts on-time past the team's 58% best.
  4. Stage ends when he has run that queue for a full quarter with no overdue item and has briefed the weekly summary himself, in the channel, every week. Then the assessment decides the rest.

The caveats, stated so you can discount them yourself. Task volume under-credits quiet work and over-credits ticket-generating work — a person who never raises a task looks smaller here than they are. The project returned 581 of 1,448 tasks through the API, so per-person counts are a partial pull and every one of them is understated by roughly the same share. "On time" is only computed on the tasks that carry a due date, and many of his do not. And a psychometric fit is a forecast while a task record is weather: where they disagree, believe the weather — which is exactly why §5 has to be answered before anyone is moved.
