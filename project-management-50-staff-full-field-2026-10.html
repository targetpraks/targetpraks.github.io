Why this one

  • It answers your exact sentence. A project in Asana has an owner, a status, milestones and an overview tab before anyone configures a thing. You open it and you can see whether the project is healthy — that is not true of Zoho Projects, and it is only half true of ClickUp.

The honest alternative

ClickUp Business · 50 seats

R136,305 a year including VAT — R147,550 a year less than Asana. It is not a weaker tool; it is a weaker default. Everything Asana does, ClickUp can do once someone builds it, and you have used it before so the team will not fight it. If RR147,550 a year matters more than the shape of the screen on day one, take ClickUp and appoint the owner who builds the templates. That is a real choice, not a consolation.

What I ruled out, in one line each

  • Wrike Business — R283,968 incl. VAT, same as Asana, but its capacity and budget power sits in the Pinnacle tier and it wants a paid guided deployment above 20 users.
  • monday Pro — R215,816, the best demo in the market, but portfolios are Enterprise-only and five entities in a board model gets untidy fast.
  • Smartsheet Business — R310,500, a PMO's tool dressed as a spreadsheet; portfolios are Enterprise-only and brand operations do not live in grids.

  • Basecamp — R67,925 flat, unlimited users, genuinely lovely, and it has no portfolio, no capacity and no dependencies. It is a project diary.

The one number that changes the whole conversation

Zoho Projects costs R0 more than you already pay. It is one of the apps carried by a Zoho One seat, and Zoho One is billed per employee, not per app. Whatever you choose above, the Zoho invoice does not shrink by a rand. This decision is an addition of R283,854 to R283,854 a year, never a swap — and it should be judged on whether it buys back manager time, not on whether it replaces a line item.

Why the cheap ones lose — and the one free experiment worth doing first

ProofHub — R10,223 a year, unlimited users

Flat pricing is genuinely attractive: 50 people for less than a coffee budget. But it is a task and file tool with reports bolted on. No portfolio health, no capacity, dated interface, and the reporting will not tell you which of your five ventures is in trouble. You would be paying R850 a month to keep the same problem.

Basecamp — R67,925 a year, unlimited users

The best-designed simple tool ever made, and deliberately not a management system: no dependencies, no portfolios, no workload view. It works for a studio of eight. It does not answer "how are the store openings going across three brands".

Notion — R227,174 a year

A document tool that can be made into a project tool by someone with spare weeks. You would be building the very thing you are buying. The wiki is worth having separately; it is not worth using as the plan.

Smartsheet — R310,500 a year

Excellent for a PMO running programme spreadsheets at scale, and priced like it. Your work is brand, franchise and campaign delivery — not a capital programme with baselines. Wrong shape, right price band for the wrong thing.

Jira — R96,322 a year

Engineering-shaped, and it feels like it. Marketing and store teams will treat it as a punishment.

Hive and ActiveCollab — R136,305 and R170,381

Both are decent all-rounders at ClickUp money. Hive is the better of the two for your shape (portfolios, forms, time tracking at one tier); ActiveCollab's strength is agency finance and it shows its age. Neither beats ClickUp Business on capability or Asana on clarity, so neither earns a shortlist place.

Do this before you spend anything

Five minutes of free experiment inside Zoho

Zoho Projects' portfolio dashboard and custom profiles sit in its Enterprise tier. Ask Zoho which Projects edition your Zoho One seat actually unlocks — if it is Enterprise, switch the portfolio dashboard on and look at it for a week. Two outcomes, both useful: either it shows you enough and you save R283,854 for a year, or it confirms in your own words why it fails and the business case for Asana writes itself. What I would not do is buy a new tool without having seen the incumbent at its best.

The move, and the three decisions

Total: 10–14 person-days of real effort plus roughly a month of calendar. That cost is identical for every tool on the list, which is why it should not be used to justify the cheapest licence.

Three decisions, in this order

  1. Approve the spend. Asana Advanced, 50 seats, R283,854 a year including VAT — or ClickUp Business at R136,305 if you want the bill halved and will appoint an owner to build the structure.
  2. Name the owner. One person accountable for the templates and the standard. Without a name, no tool survives month four — this is the failure mode, not the software.
  3. Set the pilot test now. One question at the end of the fortnight: can a GM open a project and tell me its health without asking anyone? Yes means buy at 50. No means we chose wrong and we find out for free.

My recommendation does not change: Asana Advanced, 50 seats, R283,854 a year including VAT, piloted with ten managers first. It is the only tool on the list whose default state is the thing you asked for.
