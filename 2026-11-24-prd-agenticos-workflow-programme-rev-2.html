PRD — AgenticOS workflow programme

What this is. The contract for the workflow layer of the Infinity Brands AI platform: which jobs become workflows, which must not, who owns each one, how they are gated, what "working" means, and what gets built next.

Why this revision exists. Rev 1 (21:30) was my own design after four drafts were built. Rev 2 puts that design in front of three independent models, keeps what survived, and reverses two calls. The review record is §10 — including the premise one reviewer got wrong, which I had supplied.

Status. PROPOSED. Four workflows exist as private drafts, every one with a succeeded test run. Nothing is published; publishing is the owner's act (§11).

1. Goal

Make the workflow layer the company's deterministic workhorse: lanes that pull company registers, compute in code, write the prose with one model step, and put the result in front of a named human who acts on it — reliably enough that a missed Monday is noticed, honestly enough that every number says how old it is, and thinly enough that no lane is a second voice for a job a bot already does.

End states this PRD may produce, in order of preference:

  1. Six to eight lanes live, each with a signed publishing contract (§5.8), a watchdog behind them and a weekly used-or-not report in front of their reader.
  2. The same lanes as drafts, proven by test runs — if the owner withholds publishing authority.
  3. A documented refusal: the layer is not worth running because the registers behind it cannot be kept clean or fresh (§9 R1/R3). Say so rather than publish reports nobody can trust.

4. The job-type test (for a non-engineer)

Two questions, in order:

  1. "Could I write every step on one card before it first runs — and would the same card be right every time?" No → judgment; go to 2. Yes → a graph, provided the card names the inputs, the order and what "finished" means.
  2. "Must it happen at a set time or on a record change, whether or not I remembered?" Yes → a routine (the unit-bot lane: judgment on a clock). No → an agent conversation.

One line: a workflow must still be right if it ran at 3 a.m. with everyone asleep.

Promotion rule (adopted from the panel). If an existing routine passes the fixed-shape test, promote it into a workflow — do not run both. Two voices saying the same thing is the failure this rule exists to prevent.

5. Design

5.1 The spine: shared graph patterns, instantiated against real registers

Decision (all three reviewers converged; wording is the synthesis). The programme is built from a small set of shared graph patterns — scheduled reconciliation, record-transition alert, approved correction, structured intake, sourced research — each instantiated against a real company register, with a named reader. Never one copy per unit: thirteen forks of a lane fork every fix thirteen times (E2 shows the estate already solved that with one routine set cloned thirteen ways).

Reviewer split and how it was settled:

  • glm-5.3-flash argued for general lanes with the unit as an input, and worried general lanes become memo wallpaper.
  • kimi-k3.1-pro argued for business lanes from shared patterns, instantiated only where a unit has a demonstrated need, and warned against centring the programme on the two easy demos (research, intake).
  • mistral-large-4 argued for unit-specific lanes with the four drafts kept as "the shared kit underneath", and added that the output is provable precisely because the counts come from our own registers.

Settled: the pattern is general, the data and the reader are specific. The two demo-easy lanes (L1 research, L2 intake) stay utilities, not the centre. A lane is only proposed when its register and its reader both exist.

5.3 The connector lane, proven

mcp.call reaches the Zoho gateway today (P1). Two facts govern its use:

  • Write gates are open at the gateway (E12). Every Zoho tool that can change something is configured approval: "writes" with named approvers — never "none", never an open group.
  • A null tool cache skips validation (E13). The connections do carry inventories (E10), so publish-time checking works; a connection whose cache is empty must be tested before a workflow names one of its tools.

Projects v3 is unavailable (P2), so L2 keeps drafting: it may propose the eight lines; a human files.

5.4 The freshness rule

No published lane reports a number without saying when the data behind it was read. Every output opens with data as of <timestamp> computed in code from the newest stamp it used, and L5 pages the owner when any feed passes its threshold (proposed 36 hours — two missed daily cycles).

5.5 The gate rule

Gate at the authority boundary — the first point where the lane stops observing and starts acting on something another person must trust: a write to a system of record, an outbound message, a filing, an obligation. Never on a read, a count, a draft or an internal exception alert.

  • Gate the exception, not the run: a judgment step pauses only below its confidence floor (L2 uses 70).
  • Never re-gate what one approval already covered.
  • A gate in front of a read, or in front of a step that already self-flags uncertainty, is waste — and repeated low-risk approvals train people to rubber-stamp, which is worse than no gate.

Applied: L1–L5, L7, L8, L10, L11, L12 have no gates. L6 has none (it only informs). L9 gates once per batch, before table updates. L11 gates only writes.

5.6 Cost and failure behaviour

One or two model calls per run is the budget; past three, the lane has rotted into a chat. on_tool_error: fail everywhere except research fetches, which complete so a missing source degrades the brief instead of killing the run. A failed step must name its node and error code; L6 turns that into a notification.

5.7 The watchdog

trigger.workflow_failed → notification.send to the owner. One node, zero model calls, built before the first publish so no schedule dies silently.

5.8 The publishing contract (adopted from the panel)

Nothing publishes without a one-page contract, signed by a named business owner. The contract carries:

  1. the business owner and the named reader;
  2. the unique job, in one sentence, and why no existing routine or workflow already does it (the §4 overlap check);
  3. the data sources, and what "fresh" means for each;
  4. the intended action the reader takes from the output, and its baseline today;
  5. the weekly outcome measure that would show it working;
  6. notification limits — how often it may speak, and to whom;
  7. the retirement condition.

A lane that cannot fill this in is not ready to publish, however green its test run. This is the programme's governance spine: without it, the failure mode is many green workflows producing polished, unowned messages (§9 R2).

7. Acceptance criteria

Runnable against the live instance from the host (ssh macserver; token from ~/agenticos/owner-credentials.txt). Each names its proving command and pass condition.

  1. The lane count is real. GET /api/v1/workflows → total matches §5.2's "built" rows, and every named lane exists. (Today: 4 + 1 probe.)
  2. Every draft validates. GET /api/v1/workflows/{id}/check → "graph_valid": true.
  3. Every lane has a succeeded test run. GET /api/v1/workflow-runs?workflow_id={id} → at least one succeeded.
  4. Every published lane has a contract. For each published workflow, a contract file exists under AgenticOS/contracts/<slug>.md carrying all seven §5.8 items. (Today: none — phase 2 work.)
  5. No published lane reports a number without an age. Its output contains the literal data as of.
  6. The freshness lane catches a stale feed. Set one feed's stamp 40 h back, run L5 → output names that feed and a notification row exists; restore the stamp.
  7. The watchdog pages. Force a failure (error.raise), run L6 → a notification naming the failed run id exists.
  8. A write path never writes without approval. Run L9 with a drawer needing correction → status awaiting_approval, drawer revision unchanged; approve → revision +1; reject → unchanged.
  9. A reconciliation with no exceptions says so. L7 or L10 on clean data → output states zero exceptions and sends no report. (An exception-free reconciliation is not a failure.)
  10. The connector lane answers. mcp.call → zoho services succeeds. (Proven: run 7e69f286.)
  11. Nothing was published by the agent. GET /api/v1/workflows → published_version null on every workflow until the owner says otherwise.
  12. Cost stays ≤ 2 model calls per run. Run detail shows at most two ai.* steps.

  3. Publish read-only lanes first; one full week against §9's measures.

9. Measures, risks, out of scope

Weekly, per published lane

  • Reliability — runs expected vs completed, failures by node, retries, duration, runs waiting on approval.
  • Coverage — records or pages examined, sources used, any source that stopped answering.
  • Quality — sampled accuracy of the arithmetic and the extraction, unsure flags fired and whether they were right, human edits, rejected approvals, confirmed false positives.
  • Action — alerts acknowledged, corrections approved, exceptions resolved, time to a recorded human action.
  • Outcome trend — the backlog the lane exists to move (baseline: 7 open findings / 4 blockers; 21/21 drawers with blanks, 11 without an owner).
  • Attention cost — messages emitted, duplicates, exception-free runs, reader feedback.
  • Resource cost — model calls and spend, and cost per actioned output, not cost per run.

Working means the named reader consumed it and acted, without redoing the arithmetic. Retire when: unread for three consecutive cycles; readers routinely redo its work; a write-path output is later found wrong (zero tolerance); it duplicates a routine or another lane; or its attention cost exceeds its measured outcome. Retire by unpublishing — the history stays.

Risks

  • R1 — Inputs with no measured freshness. The ROUTINE FEED documents arrive through one read-only credential on another Mac and nothing records their age. An unattended Monday report on a stalled feed is confidently wrong. Mitigation: L5 and §5.4 precede every publish.
  • R2 — Unowned automation sprawl. Many green workflows producing polished, duplicated messages nobody is accountable for. Mitigation: §5.8 contract, §4 overlap check, weekly used-or-not report.
  • R3 — Registers that cannot be joined. Findings and ledger may not share a reliable key; store, satellite and estate references may dangle. Mitigation: verify the key before L7/L10; if it is missing, establishing it is the work.
  • R4 — Open Zoho write gates (E12) with a permissive approval setting. Mitigation: §5.3 rule, checked at publish review.
  • R5 — Paging truncation. A one-page read on a growing table reports a false total. Mitigation: loop on has_more, or state "first 100" in the output.

Out of scope

Per-unit lane copies; a customer-facing chatbot; filing into Zoho Projects before its scope exists; anything that writes the vault; spend and legal decisions.
