1. The ask

Ricardo wants a full business model for a kid-friendly play concept in Durbanville, Cape Town, based on the places Targetpraks flagged in Gauteng. The brief: understand what those operators do, then build the Durbanville version — with real numbers, not a pitch.

2. The concept — distilled from the field

Every one of those Gauteng spots runs the same playbook, just with different emphasis:

  • Paid play is the door revenue — timed sessions or all-day passes, tiered by age and height. Drouble charges per session by height band; Kidz Fun House sells all-day play.
  • The café covers the parents — coffee, light meals and snacks while the kids are occupied. Kiddilicious sells it as "fun for the kids, coffee for the parents"; Industrial Coffee Works is a family restaurant with a play kingdom.
  • Parties are the margin — Parque sells birthday packages from R2,500 for up to 10 kids; JoyJozi and Drouble both run parties.
  • Retail is the add-on — mandatory gripper socks (Drouble: R50/pair), merch, craft kits. IPIC Play Café in Durbanville does paint-your-own ceramics.

The Durbanville version: a neighbourhood-scale indoor play café — ticketed play floor, parent-facing café, two party rooms, small retail counter. Roughly 800–1,000 m², built for Durbanville's family demographic rather than a mall catchment.

3. Why Durbanville

  • People: ~54,000 at the 2011 Census, estimated closer to ~70,000 today across 27.6 km² [Wikipedia] [city-facts].
  • Spend: affluent, family-heavy, high owner-occupation — the cohort that pays R130–250 per kid per session in Gauteng.
  • Weather hedge: Cape Town winters are wet. An all-weather indoor destination trades year-round.
  • Gap: Durbanville already has IPIC Play Café (ceramics + food), Oatlands Café (large play area) and Treat Coffee Roasters — but none is a big timed-session play centre with a party programme. That's the opening.

4. Target customer

  • Core: parents with kids aged 0–10 in Durbanville and surrounds (Bellville, Brackenfell edge) — weekday mornings for toddlers, weekends for families.
  • Secondary: birthday-party bookings, school and creche outings, adult-plus-sibling drop-ins.
  • The line to borrow from JoyJozi: "a place where kids take their adults." The adults pay because the kids won't let them leave.

5. Format & zones (~900 m²)

Toddler zone (0–3)

Soft play, ball pit, low slides — the baby-first model. Highest socks attach rate.

Junior play frame (4–10)

Climbing, tunnels, nets — the Drouble / Kidz Fun House core. Timed sessions keep the floor turning over.

Café (40–60 covers)

Coffee, toasties, milkshakes, cakes. Parents stay on-site — that is the cover charge for the play floor.

2 party rooms + counter

Weekend parties are the profit layer. Retail: R45 gripper socks.

7. Revenue streams

Play admission — ~65%

Timed sessions and all-day passes, tiered by age. Morning, midday and afternoon blocks turn the floor over.

Café — ~15%

Coffee and food for supervising adults plus kids' meals. Attach rate is the lever.

Socks & retail — ~8%

Mandatory gripper socks are near-pure margin after stock.

Parties — ~12%

Weekend party rooms at R2,500+ per package. Highest margin per hour of labour.

8. Operations

  • Staffing (9 FTE, loaded): 4 play hosts / floor supervisors, 2 café, 1 parties host, 1 manager, 1 cleaner.
  • Safety: wristband check-in per booking, floor hosts plus safety briefings on every zone, capacity caps per session, first aid on site.
  • Booking: online slots plus walk-ins; DinePlan-style table and party booking (Parque, JoyJozi and IPIC all use Dineplan in the area).
  • Cleaning: soft-play sanitisation between sessions; deep clean nightly.

11. Risks & what could kill it

  • Occupancy doesn't materialise. Durbanville is smaller than Centurion. If weekday toddler traffic is weaker than assumed, café attach and party volume carry the fix. Breakeven is ~1,040 kids/month — about 35 kids a day across three sessions.
  • Competition moves. IPIC, Oatlands or a new entrant could add a paid-play floor. Differentiate on the party programme, session structure and café quality.
  • Seasonality. December and January school holidays spike; July dips. Parties and term-time creche bookings smooth it.
  • A safety incident. One injury on the play frame is reputational. Wristband control, host-to-child ratios and visible briefings are non-negotiable.
  • The lease. Paying mall-level rent in a strip mall kills the margin. Site should be neighbourhood retail with parking, not a premium mall anchor.
