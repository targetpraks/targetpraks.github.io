The one thing standing between this framework and a working lane

The lane has no lists, and none may be created. MKT SLA - KPD is a folder with nothing inside it — read three times, three seconds apart, empty each time. Every structure described below lands in one of 19 lists that do not exist, and building them is the one thing standing instruction forbids an agent to do.

There is a second, smaller handbrake: the space's Brand field has no KPD option and Engagement Tier has no CLI, and drop-down options cannot be added through the API. Both are one manual click each, in your hands.

9.1 · The audits, and what they check

R10–R14 mirror the document's own audit machinery. The brand audit's 39 metrics cover identity; the SEO Strategy Builder's six lanes cover the search footprint — on-page (titles, meta, internal links), landing pages per service and location, off-page links, local (Google Business and location schema), content targeting long-tail terms, and technical (speed, mobile, sitemap, indexation). Each lane already names its own KPI: page score and CTR, conversion rate, domain authority, calls and map views, session time, load time and errors.

9.2 · Positive and negative keywords

The keyword library holds 400 keywords across 12 types, built from 4 services × 14 locations × 24 customer categories. That is the positive side and it is substantial. The negative side does not exist anywhere in the document — no exclusion list, no search-terms review, not one mention of a negative keyword on any of the 56 pages. R9 and R13 create that discipline from nothing, which is why they are named as new work rather than as a migration.

Two data gaps the routines will expose on day one: the SEO Importance column on the keyword table is blank for all 400 rows, and the keyword gaps recorded against the seven competitors have never been turned into keywords.

10.2 · Views and automation

Four views carry the lane: a calendar on publish date across all content, a board by status for production, a table filtered by channel, and a this month filter on every channel list so the team opens to today's work. Two automation facts must be honoured: the space rewrites due date on create to the following Monday, so tasks are created undated and dated in a second pass; and recurring routines are created as dated templates per month rather than repeating tasks, so a missed month is visible rather than silently skipped.

11 · What blocks it, and the four ways forward

BlockerWhat is true todayWhat it needs
No list to file intoMKT SLA - KPD (folder 1501000000001242) holds no lists — three reads, three seconds apart, empty each time. ClickUp files work in lists; a folder has no task collection. Building the standard set means 7 folders and 19 lists.Ricardo's word to build it, or a lane to borrow.
No KPD in the Brand picklistThe space-level Brand field offers INF, EXP, TLF, PPP, DID, FXS, FXL, NVP, ALL. KPD is not one of them, and drop-down options cannot be added through the API — the call returns 404/405.One manual click in ClickUp settings, by Ricardo.
No CLI in Engagement TierEngagement Tier offers HOF, ZOR, COR, ZEE. The tier glossary says CLI is the SLA-client tier, and this document is CLI. Every KPD task would have to carry a wrong tier or none.One manual click to add CLI, alongside the Brand option.
No home for the monthly reportsSeventeen recurring routines need a list and there is no reporting list in the standard set. Recurring is the nearest home, but a monthly report is not the same shape as a recurring production task.A decision: keep them in Recurring, or add a Reporting list to the standard set.
  1. Build the standard set in MKT SLA - KPD
    What it takes
    Your word for 7 folders and 19 lists, plus two manual clicks (Brand option, Engagement Tier option).
    What it gives
    The framework in this PRD lands exactly where section 10 says, KPD stops reporting as another brand, and the 17 routines get a home.
    What it costs
    Lifts the folder/list ban for one lane. It is your ban to lift, not an agent's.
  2. Keep filing KPD work in MKT COR - DID
    What it takes
    Nothing to build — this is today. 7 KPD tasks already sit there.
    What it gives
    No new structure. Work continues.
    What it costs
    Every KPD task carries DID's brand and tier. Reporting by brand is wrong by design, and the audit trail mixes two clients.
  3. Intake in DID, tag the brand
    What it takes
    Use DID's Briefed-In as the one door, tag every task KPD, and keep the framework doc as the plan.
    What it gives
    One intake, no new structure, a tag that can filter KPD out of DID's numbers.
    What it costs
    A tag is a report filter, not a home. The 19 lists the framework needs still do not exist, so campaigns, always-on and the routines have nowhere structured to live.
  4. Run the framework from the document alone
    What it takes
    Leave the lane empty, use the Coda document as the operating surface, and file nothing in ClickUp.
    What it gives
    No structure, no mislabelling, no new debt.
    What it costs
    No status, owner, due date, calendar view or reminder. The 17 routines would depend on someone reading a document on the right day.

My reading: option A, scoped to this one lane, because it is the only one where the 62 campaigns, the 24 calendar items and the 17 routines land somewhere a person opens on a Monday morning. Option D is the honest fallback if you would rather not widen the ClickUp structure at all — but then the framework lives in a document nobody is reminded to read.
