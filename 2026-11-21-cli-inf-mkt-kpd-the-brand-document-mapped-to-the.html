What actually stands between this framework and a working lane

The lane is built. It is wearing another client's name. MKT SLA - KPD (folder 1501000000001242) owns seven child folders and nineteen lists — every destination this framework needs already exists. All seven children and three of the lists are labelled MKT COR - DID, a copy never renamed. A plain folder read shows no lists because they sit one level down in those children; read by parent id, the tree is complete.

So nothing has to be created: zero new folders, zero new lists. What is needed is a rename of seven folders and three lists, a KPD option on the Brand field and CLI on Engagement Tier, and the seven existing tasks re-stamped — one of them currently reads INF/HOF, six carry no brand at all.

Each lane already names its own KPI: page score and CTR, conversion rate, domain authority, calls and map views, session time, load time and errors.

9.2 · Positive and negative keywords

The keyword library holds 400 keywords across 12 types. Each service gets 100: 6 general terms, 5 patterns × 14 locations, and 24 buyer terms. That is the positive side and it is substantial. The negative side is absent from the document — no exclusion list, no search-terms review, not one mention of a negative keyword on any of the 56 pages. Whether the live Google Ads account holds exclusions is a separate question the document cannot answer; R9 starts by reading them, not by assuming none exist.

Two data gaps the routines will expose on day one: the SEO Importance column on the keyword table is blank for all 400 rows, and the keyword gaps recorded against the seven competitors are not linked to any keyword row — competitor gap text and keywords overlap by name, but no relationship is recorded.

10.2 · Views and automation

Four views carry the lane: a calendar on publish date across all content, a board by status for production, a table filtered by channel, and a this month filter on every channel list so the team opens to today's work. Two working rules are proposed rather than proven. A space automation has been observed to move due dates to the following Monday on create (recorded in the team's operating notes, not verifiable through the API), so the proposed practice is to create undated and date in a second pass. And recurring routines are proposed as dated monthly instances rather than repeating tasks, so a missed month shows as a gap. Both need confirming against the live automation rules before the first import.
