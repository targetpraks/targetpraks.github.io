01 The money frame

Everything is judged against one number: 5.31% after tax, what the money earns in a five-year bond at 9.0% nominal after 41% tax. Repo is 7.25% after the MPC's 4-3 hold on 19 November, prime is 10.75%, and CPI has risen to 5.2%. A property that cannot beat 5.31% after tax and after levies is a hobby with a bond attached.

Across the 48 rows that could be priced to a yield, the average after-tax return is 1.74% and the median 1.46% - both below the hurdle. Only 2 clear it.

The rulebook

Five rules do more to the answer than any listing.

  • Personal rental profit is taxed at the investor's marginal rate, not gross rent - SARS adds rental receipts to other income and allows only permissible expenses incurred to produce that rent.
  • Deductible rental costs exclude capital improvements and private-use portions - Rates, bond interest, advertising, agency fees, qualifying homeowner insurance, garden services, repairs, secu
  • Long-term dwelling rent is VAT-exempt; do not assume the same treatment for a tourism operation - For ordinary residential letting, SARS says the supply of accommodation in a dwelling is VAT-exempt and relate
  • Section 20A rental-loss ring-fencing threshold falls to the 39% bracket from 1 March 2026 - Do not model geared losses as an automatic 41% subsidy against salary. From years commencing 1 March 2026, sec
  • Section 13sex is a five-unit new-and-unused allowance, not generic depreciation on one new flat - The base allowance is 5% of qualifying cost a year on a 20-year straight-line basis. For a unit bought as part
  • Personal investment-property CGT uses a 40% inclusion rate - On a pure investment unit, include 40% of the net capital gain in taxable income and apply the marginal rate t
  • A company defers tax at 27% only while profit stays inside; dividends to the individual normally add 20% - For a R5m-R20m portfolio, a company is strongest when rental is retained to service debt or buy more stock: re

Financing and exit

What a bank will bond decides what you can actually buy twice.

  • Bondability — plain freehold and sectional title - buy

04 The portfolio

The gate was asked how to deploy, not which single asset to buy, because the brief is diversification:

Two Parow Central units plus one Langebaan Freeport, about R2.6m committed, the balance in the bank - probability 0.64, the strongest call of the five.

The arithmetic behind it. One Parow unit at R799 000 with rent at R11 500 returns 11.55% net and 6.81% after tax - 150 basis points above the bond. A second unit doubles the income but also doubles the concentration in one building, one street and one tenant profile; the R90/month rates on the listing are almost certainly wrong and a R1 000/month figure still leaves it at 10.4% net. The Langebaan Freeport flat at R899 000 is the only coastal asset that clears the hurdle, but its income is modelled on a town-tier short-let average rather than an achieved ledger, so it belongs at small size.

What the gate rejected is as useful as what it picked: the hotel lane at 0.37 for "does not pay", the beach at 0.42 for "not yet", and every plot at 0.52 for "buy finished".

05 The cost stack that kills returns

Taj Cape Town - R11 612 levy on R6m 2.3% of price a year
Pepperclub - R10 579 on R4.75m 2.7% a year
Shark Bay houses at R7.7m under 1% after tax
Colosseum - R1 188 on R1.04m cash only, no bond
Parow Central - R1 988 on R799 000 3.0% but rent is 17% gross

Two structural traps. Mandatory rental-pool units are cash-only - a bank will not bond what it cannot repossess cleanly, which caps both the buyer pool at purchase and the buyer pool at exit. And the levy runs whether the unit earns or not: at 2.7% of price, a vacant year on a R4.75m flat is R127 000 of pure cost.

08 What is still unknown

  • 120 rows carry no yield. Many ask prices are real; the rent evidence behind them is not, and a yield built on a guess is worse than a blank.
  • Achieved short-let ledgers for Langebaan and Hermanus are not published anywhere public - the coastal numbers rest on town-tier averages.
  • Exact-unit resale prices at the hotel schemes: four properties are sold or under offer, but no launch-to-resale comparison exists for a specific unit.
  • Body corporate financials for every sectional-title scheme in the list. Nobody publishes them; they arrive at due diligence.
  • Parow's rates. The listing says R90/month. That is almost certainly wrong and the conservative case is carried alongside.
