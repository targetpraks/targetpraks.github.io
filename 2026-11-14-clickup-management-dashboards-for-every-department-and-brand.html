1. The standard — the same eight cards everywhere

  • Outstanding Open work right now — the number a manager checks first.
  • This Week Tasks completed in the last seven days.
  • Tasks by Assignee Who is carrying what, split by person.
  • Tasks Closed by Assignee Closures per person — who is actually finishing.
  • Completed Report (this week) The list of what was finished this week.
  • Workspace Points Comment, resolve, complete and worked-on points per person.
  • Worked On (this week) Everything touched this week, by person.
  • Who's Behind The contributors and what they moved.

4. Scope — each dashboard sees only its own unit

Department dashboards Sit at the top of the department space, so they roll up everything that department does — its own folders, its tier folders (HOF / ZOR / ZEE) and its brand folders.
Brand dashboards Sit inside the brand's own folder (or its client space), so they count that brand's work only — Papa Pasta's dashboard shows Papa Pasta, not all of Brand Ops 2.

5. Proof the cards compute, not just render

The workspace holds no tasks yet, so every card correctly reads zero. To prove the wiring, I put one throwaway task in a retired space (FluxFlow), marked it complete, refreshed, and read the cards: This Week went 0 → 1 and Completed Report listed the task. The task was then deleted and the cards went back to 0. Nothing was left behind.

Method: the eight cards are ClickUp's own Team Reporting dashboard template, applied identically to all fifteen dashboards. Views were created through the ClickUp API at the correct space or folder; the card set was applied in the ClickUp UI.
