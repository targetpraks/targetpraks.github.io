03 — The pick, in full

Panashe Mbiva, R10 000 a month. Her CV and the attached letter are read in full below — nothing here is inferred from a summary.

The qualification is real and it is the right one

Bachelor of Education in Foundation Phase from Rhodes University, 2022, obtained with distinction. That is the four-year, phase-specific degree for Grades R–3 — the phase the boy's reading sits in while he is placed in Grade 4. It is not a general BA plus a PGCE.

She has already taught the exact classroom this job describes

At Hillview Academy, March 2023 to September 2025, she was the support teacher inside a Grade 4 assisted class of eight learners with specific learning difficulties — ADD, ADHD, dyslexia and autism spectrum disorder — delivering small-group literacy and maths interventions and carrying one-on-one scaffolding across the whole academic day. Her CV also records that she was called on at short notice to lead classes across multiple grades when teachers were absent, and that she co-ran the aftercare programme for 25 learners.

The attached reference, from Catherine Fourie, Head of Primary at Hillview Academy, corroborates all of it independently and in writing — including that she ran the aftercare and that she substituted reliably at short notice. It ends: “I would recommend Panashe for any support position for which she applies within a school setting.” Her number is on the letter.

The two certificates map onto the two things Reddam actually asked for

Reddam's letter names a reader and a scribe, and the plan agreed with the school runs Barton as a daily scripted literacy programme delivered by the facilitator. Panashe holds a Readers and Scribers certificate from Oakley House Training (2024) and Search and Teach from NILD South Africa (2024) — the latter a paid five-day structured-literacy course that NILD SA lists at R7 850. Neither is a weekend webinar. She is the only candidate who has spent money and days on either skill.

She produces a number, not an adjective

Since February 2026 she has tutored a Grade 7 learner at Curro Uitzicht one-to-one in English and maths. Her CV's claim is specific and checkable: after the learner had failed English and repeated the grade, targeted work on reading strategies, comprehension and written expression took the result to 75%. A tutor who reports a mark has either done the work or is easy to catch out.

And she is currently only part-time

Her specialist-school post at Resilience Academy is recorded as October 2025 to June 2026; the only live engagement on the CV is the private tutoring. That reads as available from January — but it is the first thing to confirm, and the CV still calls the Resilience post “currently”, which is a stale line she should correct.

Three gaps to close before she starts
  • Police clearance. Reddam asked Prins for a name and a recent clearance. It runs about 15 working days once applied for — which is why 1 December is the appointment line in the file.
  • Driver's licence and own transport. Her CV does not state either. The agency standard for this job requires both (Little Sunshines screens for “own car, valid driver's licence”). Ask.
  • SACE registration number. A qualified teacher should hold one; Reddam may ask for it, and it costs nothing to check against the SACE register.

04 — Why not Kara-Anne

Kara-Anne Wade is not a weak candidate. She is an unverifiable one, and the gate priced that at 0.015. There is no CV in the file — only her two emails.

What is genuinely good: she is a retired South African high school specialist in English and Drama, which is precisely the subject where this boy is furthest behind and where his expressive language sits in the 5–6 range. Drama is oral language, projection, confidence — not a decorative extra for a child who will not read aloud. And she has invigilated alongside one of Reddam's own remedial teachers for five to six years, so she knows the building's support staff personally, which no other candidate does.

Her referees are real and senior. I checked the second one: Karen Kaimowitz is the principal of Herzlia Gan Aviv Pre-Primary, a post the school's own site confirms, and Kaimowitz used Kara-Anne to facilitate her own son for two years. That is a facilitator-specific reference from a school principal — the strongest single reference of the three candidates.

What is missing is everything the daily job consists of. No evidenced remedial qualification. No structured-literacy training — so Barton would be her first exposure to it. No demonstrated in-class support in a mainstream Grade 4. Five years of substitute teaching is claimed and not documented. At R18 000 a month she would sit at the very top of what the market pays a qualified remedial specialist — for a role whose core skills she has not shown she holds. She is also pressing for an answer within a week because her own contract ends on 31 December. That is her timetable, not the boy's.

The role she is actually right for

Keep her. Offer her a paid weekly English-and-Drama session alongside the facilitator — one hour, after school or on a Saturday — and use her as the second pair of eyes on the written work. It costs a fraction of R18 000, it puts the strongest English specialist in the file in front of the boy, and it keeps her Reddam relationship warm. Ask her for a CV either way: if it shows a remedial qualification she did not mention, the comparison reopens.

05 — Why not Allana

Allana Aldridge has supplied a name and a rate. That is the entire file: no CV, no qualifications, no experience, no references, no availability. Nothing can be checked, so nothing can be weighed — the gate declined to shortlist her at 0.992, which is the honest answer rather than a judgement on her.

The rate does say something on its own. R25 000 a month is R275 000 across an 11-month year and R292 000 all-in — R77 000 a year above the top of what a South African agency charges for a senior facilitator with a remedial degree, and R165 000 more than the pick. At that price she is being asked to be the single largest line in the support plan. Ask her for a CV, or close the file. Do not spend more thought on her until a document arrives.

On that scale she is a senior — qualified, experienced, degreed — asking for the junior rate, which is what an agency charges for a student who has not finished their degree. She is R5 000 to R8 000 a month under the market for her own profile. That is either a genuine bargain or the beginning of a resignation in March, and the file already names that exact failure mode: “A facilitator is an employment, not a school fee… Budget for a replacement, not the monthly rate.”

The resolution that satisfies both: appoint at R10 000 in writing for 2027, and put a documented review at R12 500 after term one in the same letter. Nothing costs more in this plan than a facilitator who leaves in March.
