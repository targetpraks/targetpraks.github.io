03 — Panashe, in full

Read from her CV and the attached letter, and now corroborated independently.

The qualification is real, it is the right one, and the university confirms it

Bachelor of Education in Foundation Phase from Rhodes University, 2022, with distinction. That is the four-year, phase-specific degree for Grades R–3 — the phase the boy's reading sits in while he is placed in Grade 4. It is not a general BA plus a PGCE. And it is no longer only her word: Rhodes' own 2022 notice lists her among the six Education students nominated by the Dean for its Top 100 Academic Achievement Award.

She has already taught the classroom this job describes

At Hillview Academy, March 2023 to September 2025, she was the support teacher inside a Grade 4 assisted class of eight learners with ADD, ADHD, dyslexia and autism spectrum disorder — small-group literacy and maths interventions, plus one-on-one scaffolding across the academic day. She substituted for absent teachers at short notice, and co-ran the aftercare for 25 learners from 07:30 to 18:00.

The reference from Catherine Fourie, Head of Primary, corroborates all of it independently and in writing, and ends: “I would recommend Panashe for any support position for which she applies within a school setting.” Her number is on the letter.

The two certificates map onto the two things Reddam asked for

Reddam's letter names a reader and a scribe; the plan runs Barton as a daily scripted programme delivered by the facilitator. She holds a Readers and Scribers certificate from Oakley House (2024) and Search and Teach structured literacy from NILD South Africa (2024) — a paid five-day course listed at R7 850. Neither is a weekend webinar, and no other candidate holds either.

She produces a number, not an adjective

Since February 2026 she has tutored a Grade 7 learner at Curro Uitzicht one-to-one in English and maths. Her claim is specific: after the learner failed English and repeated the grade, targeted work on reading strategies, comprehension and written expression took the result to 75%. A tutor who reports a mark is easy to catch out.

What she does not have
  • No one-to-one in-school facilitation. Her record is group and class support, plus seven months of private tutoring. Allana's record is the reverse, and this is the real risk in her file.
  • No police clearance — about 15 working days, which is why 1 December is the appointment line.
  • No driver's licence or transport stated on the CV, and no named referees — the letter supplies one verifier.
  • SACE number not stated. A B.Ed makes her registrable where Allana is not, so this is a strength to confirm rather than a doubt to chase.

04 — Allana, in full — the real contest

Read from her CV, supplied 12 November 2026. Her schools are real and their geography matches her dates: Herzlia Highlands Primary and Herzlia High School sit across the road from each other, exactly as her account of following one boy from one into the other requires.

She has done this job, continuously, for five years

Four one-to-one facilitation engagements since May 2021: Kiara Epstein (May 2021–Dec 2024), Maralma Da Silva (May 2021–Dec 2022), Adam Kaimowitz (Jan 2023–Dec 2024) and Cole Kovensky — Herzlia Highlands Primary through 2025, then following the same boy into Herzlia High School from January 2026. Following a child across a school transition is the behaviour you want: the family and the school kept her.

Her stated duties are precisely the interface that usually breaks: “Facilitated students' academic progress and performance, executive functioning, emotional regulation and focus. Liaised between parents, teachers and SENCO with monthly facilitation reports. Experience with students with Autism, ADHD and Dyslexia.”

Ten years of teaching children before that — including two kilometres from this house

Speech and drama teaching from 2012 to 2017 at St Paul's Primary (Grades R–7), Welgemoed Pre-Primary (2013–14), Pinelands North Primary (Grades 1–7), the French School and Dramatots. That last programme, she notes, was “developed alongside an OT and a child psychologist, with particular focus on emotional, fine and gross motor development” — she is the only candidate whose work has been shaped by allied professionals.

Her childcare record is unusually deep: long-term au pair engagements from 2008, hotel babysitting through Kidz@Heart, and full-time childcare plus home schooling for a family visiting from the UK specifically for special education and therapy. If the aftercare or au pair question is live, she has already answered it.

What she is not

She is not a qualified teacher in the school sense. Her credentials are a Cape Academy of Dramatic Art diploma (with honours, 2012–14), Trinity ATCLs in drama performance and in speech-and-drama teaching with distinction, an LTCL pending a thesis, and LAMDA Grade 8 gold. She enrolled for a B.Ed at Stellenbosch in 2005 and a social work degree at UCT in 2006 and completed neither. No remedial qualification, no reader-scribe training, no structured-literacy training, no SACE registration, no police clearance. Her expertise is experiential — which is not nothing, because it is five years of paid, referenced practice — but it is not the thing this plan is short of.

Where she is the better hire

Everything about the school interface: knowing how to sit beside a child without taking over, how to talk to a SENCO, how to write a report a parent can act on, and how to keep a boy regulated through a long day. Plus voice — she can get him to speak, to project, and to hold a feeling in words. That is the part of the plan the remedial specialist will not cover.
