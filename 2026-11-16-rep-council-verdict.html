The council's verdict

Four models were given the same brief: the two Superhuman documents, Ricardo's proposal, the measured state of REP, and ten verified ClickUp constraints. They worked blind, then cross-examined each other. Two changed their minds. All four finished in the same place.

The ruling in one line

Keep the folder, drop the subtask. One list per cadence is right. The period is a field on the report, not a parent task, and the unit is the report itself — because ClickUp hides subtasks by default in every view and every dashboard widget, and a widget that undercounts does not error. It lies.

6. What the council found that my draft had missed

1 · Nobody said who creates next week's tasks

Two seats independently landed on this, and it is the real reason today's 70 tasks are empty shells. Every proposal described migrating the existing tasks and templating new ones; none described the engine. Without a generator — thirteen tasks every Monday, twelve every month, four every quarter, pre-assigned, pre-templated, pre-dated, keyed 2026-W41 so it cannot double-fire — week two depends on someone remembering, and the system dies of the same emptiness.

2 · A date should never be hand-picked twice

Week No, Month, Quarter and Year are four editable dropdowns today, and the Week No dropdown stops at 14. Hand-picking them invites drift — Week 46 that is not November. One Period Date is the truth; the rest derive from it.

3 · One unit field cannot serve two rosters

The weekly roster is 13 operating units; the monthly roster is 12 EXCO seats. A single Space-level BUS Unit field merges them into one 25-option dropdown where a weekly head can pick "CFO". Each list needs its own unit field.

4 · The completion number needs a denominator

"9 of 13 in" only means something if the list knows the roster expects 13. The expected-unit roster per cadence is what turns a count into a compliance figure — and it is also the input the generator needs. Same object serves both.

Also carried in: concluded periods archive automatically after four weeks · the INF roll-up stays out of the unit field, because its absence is the guard against counting it as a fourteenth unit · "only Ricardo marks Reviewed" becomes an automation that logs the actor and a dashboard widget that names every Reviewed row not touched by him — soft, visible, and honest about what the plan can enforce.

7. The verdict

Ricardo's instinct, amended

Right: one list per folder. Unanimous, and it is the load-bearing half of the idea — 42 lists become 3, and each cadence finally gets the status vocabulary its document already uses.

Amended: the week or the month is not a parent task. It is a field on the report, and the report is the task. Same organisation, one fewer layer, and every number on every dashboard is countable the moment it exists rather than after someone finds a toggle.

Cost of the amendment: you open a saved view called Week 47 instead of opening a parent task called Week 47. That is the whole loss.

9. What happens to the two documents

They do not get thrown away — they get retired as systems of record and kept as the reference. The five and six section headings travel into the ClickUp templates word for word, so nothing anyone has learned to write changes. What stops is the split: two places to write, a dashboard that cannot count, and a status that lives in a table cell rather than on the work.
