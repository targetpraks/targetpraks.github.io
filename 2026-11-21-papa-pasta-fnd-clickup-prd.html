02 The brand, its clients and its network

Papa Pasta is a South African fast-casual pasta franchisor. That single sentence decides the whole content strategy, because a franchisor has two different customers and the document only describes one of them clearly.

The two client types — and the document's blind spot

Direct client = the franchisee

The person who buys a Papa Pasta store. The document describes them well: four segments in Customer Personas — owner-operators with QSR or retail experience, existing multi-unit franchise owners, hospitality professionals who want a system, and corporate career-switchers with capital. All four are B2B buyers of a R-value franchise.

End consumer = the pasta customer

The person who walks into the store. Described in Brand Personas as someone who wants “a premium pasta experience without the hassle” — fresh, refined, intentional, not heavy or generic. This is a B2C buyer of a R100-ish meal.

The blind spot. Six campaign blueprints exist (brand, always-on, and four “targeted” audiences — service/product, partner, supplier, customers) but not one of them is written for the pasta consumer. Every blueprint targets network-building audiences: franchise buyers, banks, suppliers. A QSR brand's always-on social, seasonal promos and store-level campaigns have no blueprint at all. That is the first thing the campaign architecture in §04 has to fix — and it needs a decision, not a guess.

The client list — the one register that does not exist

There is no named client list. After 56 pages the document holds segments and personas, and not a single signed franchisee, store, territory, opening date or contact. The 13 partners are named; the customers who buy franchises are not. For a franchisor this is the most important register of all — it is the proof that the marketing works. There is also no ClickUp list that holds one, so §07 and §12 treat it as a decision, not a load step.

What ClickUp can and cannot link today

It can

  • Carry Brand = PPP, Engagement Tier = ZOR, Function (Campaign Strategy / Campaign Execution / Content / Design…), Channel labels, Objective and Filestage URL on every list — verified field-by-field on eight of the twenty lists, identical option sets.
  • Show any list as a calendar or board, so events and campaigns read as a timeline without a new list.
  • Hold the campaign name in the task title, which is how a piece is traced back to its parent.

It cannot — yet

  • No Campaign field exists. I read the field schema of eight lists: the same twelve fields sit on every one (Brand, Function, Channel, Engagement Tier, Objective, Filestage URL…), and none of them is a campaign link.
  • A relationship field between a piece and its parent campaign would have to be added to the existing lists — a schema change, not a new list. That needs Ricardo's word (§12 Q3).
  • Until then the link is a naming convention: [CAMPAIGN CODE] — piece title, with the parent's task id in the description.
The rule to hold. A content piece is never filed as a campaign, and a campaign is never filed as a campaign idea. With six families and many ideas, one list is enough only if the title grammar is obeyed: FAMILY · CAMPAIGN — PIECE. Without the grammar, six months from now nobody can tell a campaign from a blog.
