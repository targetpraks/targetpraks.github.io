Building Asana for ourselves: 8–14 weeks for a usable version, and no finishing line

Measured against the code we already own, not against the pitches. The number below is the honest one for an internal web app covering the slice of Asana/ClickUp our teams actually open every day.

Verdict: don't clone it. A credible internal-only v1 is real work but not crazy — roughly 41–72 dev-days on top of Constellation. What is crazy is the next five years: you would own a second work system, permanently staffed, while the real thing costs about $5–8k a year at 60 seats.

The effort ladder estimate

Bars show the from-zero cost of each stage; stage 3 is green because we already own it — on our stack it collapses to 2–4 days. Summary: from zero 51–88 d (≈10–18 weeks); on our stack 41–72 d (≈8–14 weeks).

What the estimate hides

  • The migration. Whatever exists in Zoho Projects, boards and sheets has to move, and stay moved.
  • The behaviour change. 60 people learning a new tool costs more real hours than building it.
  • The 15%. Every year after launch, 6–11 dev-days go to fixes, integrations and the browser changing underneath us.
  • Mobile. Staff will want it on a phone. A decent responsive web is inside the range; a real app is another 20–30 days.

The one big shortcut

  • Don't rebuild the task engine — front it. Zoho Projects already holds our briefs and has an API. A thin board/dashboard layer on top of it is a 2–3 week job, not a 14-week one, and nobody has to migrate anything.
  • Do it only for the gap. If the complaint is a specific view or workflow Zoho cannot do, that view is the product. Build that.
  • Reuse the account layer. Staff log in once, through Constellation's scopes, not a fourth password.

What I would actually do

1. Keep buying the box. Zoho Projects is already in the company and already holds our briefs. Two task systems is a problem; four is a habit.

2. Build the missing view, not the product. A Constellation-authenticated board that reads and writes through Zoho's API: 2–3 weeks, no migration, no new passwords, no second source of truth.

3. Replace this estimate with a measurement. A two-day spike: one Task model, one Project model, one board view, wired into the existing scopes and audit log. If two days produces a moving board, the 8–14-week figure is believable. If it produces three days of schema wrangling, we have learned that cheaply.
