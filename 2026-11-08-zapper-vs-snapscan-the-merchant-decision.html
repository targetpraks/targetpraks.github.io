Zapper vs SnapScan

Which one to put on the counter — priced off both providers' own published rates today, with the crossover points worked out.

Verdict

SnapScan, as the default. It is cheaper at almost every turnover level, settles faster on the free plan, and has the larger customer base. Zapper earns its place as a second rail when you want loyalty, vouchers and in-app marketing — or when you push more than roughly R440 000 a month through QR and take its paid plan.

The rates, as published

SnapScan

No monthly fee. Rate falls as your monthly turnover grows.
  • 2.95% up to R10k/month
  • 2.85% R10k–R20k
  • 2.80% R20k–R30k
  • 2.75% R30k–R60k
  • 2.65% R60k–R80k
  • 2.55% above R80k
  • Rates exclude VAT · no sign-up or settlement fee

Zapper

Two plans; the paid one is per store, per month.
  • Basic — free, 2.9%, weekly settlement
  • Business — R220/store/month, 2.5%, daily settlement, loyalty cards, vouchers, in-app messaging
  • Rates exclude VAT · no fixed rate ladder, no volume discount below the paid plan
  • On-demand payout available at a small fee

How I would decide

Take SnapScan if…

  • QR is a payment rail, not a marketing channel — you want the lowest cost and the fastest money.
  • You are under ~R440k/month in QR turnover (that is most single-site operations).
  • You want the fewest moving parts: one QR, one rate that improves by itself, next-day money.

Take Zapper if…

  • You will actually use loyalty, vouchers and lapsed-customer campaigns — that is what the R220 buys.
  • You need tap on phone today and do not want another terminal (markets, pop-ups, delivery drivers).
  • You are large enough that the paid 2.5% plus R220 beats SnapScan's 2.55% floor.

Run both, if the margin is there

  • Two QRs on one stand cost nothing monthly and remove "I only have the other app" as a reason not to pay.
  • Keep SnapScan as the settlement rail and Zapper as the loyalty rail; reconcile both in the same daily cash-up.
Two things to check before signing. One: both quote rates excluding VAT, so model the 15% — the gap between them is smaller than the headline suggests and your accountant cares. Two: QR is not automatically cheaper than a card machine. If your average ticket is high and most of your volume is card-present, a terminal priced under 2.5% can beat both; QR earns its keep on small tickets, queues, tips and remote payment links.
