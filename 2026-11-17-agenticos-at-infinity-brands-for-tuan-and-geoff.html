The walls

Private by default — four locks

1 · Your chats

A conversation belongs to the person who started it

Your thread with an assistant is yours. Nobody else in the company can open it, search it or continue it. If you want to hand someone a thread you can share that one deliberately — sharing is an action you take, never something that happens to you.

2 · The room decides

In a shared channel, the channel is the audience

Ask inside a team channel and the answer belongs to that room — that is the point of a team channel. The rule is simple: private questions in your own chat, shared answers in the room.

3 · Your tools

Your accounts stay yours

A service connected as your own account — your ClickUp, for instance — can only ever answer for you. Someone else asking the same assistant silently gets no tools at all, rather than borrowing your login.

4 · Company credentials

Some things belong to the company, and say so

Where a connection is the company's own — the shared ClickUp seat, for example — it is labelled that way, it is visible only to the admins who run it, and anything it changes still waits for a person to approve.

Superman · Tuan & Geoff

The super-admin seat

The same reach, deliberately scoped to the company: one organisation credential behind it rather than one person's login, writes waiting for approval, and access limited to a named admin group — the members of which you can read in the console. Nobody else can open it.

Both are ordinary agents in the same console: same audit trail, same budgets, same approval log. The difference is only who may run them and whose accounts they act with.

The limits

What it will never do on its own

1

Pay, refund, sign or trade

Money and legal decisions are a person's. The assistant prepares, shows, and waits.

2

Delete a record

Nothing is removed on its own initiative. A deletion is a decision with a name attached to it.

3

Change a live system quietly

Writes to ClickUp, GitHub or the platform wait for an approval, and the approval log holds every one.

4

Read another person's chat

Threads do not cross. There is no admin view that opens somebody else's conversation.

5

Make up a number

It computes, cites, or says it does not know. "I could not find that" is a valid, expected answer.

Day one

Three things to try first

Try 1

Ask your own unit

Open your unit's assistant and ask something you already know the answer to. If it agrees with you, it has read the material. If it does not, you have found a document that needs fixing — that is worth more than the answer.

Try 2

Ask for the source

"Where did that come from?" should always produce a document. If it cannot, treat the answer as a draft, not a fact.

Try 3

Give it a real job

A deck from a brief, a month-end pack from a sheet, a scanned invoice read into a table. The desks are where the platform earns its keep.
