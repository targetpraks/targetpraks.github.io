02

The filament path — and the three places a break hides

spool to nozzle, in order

Each slot is its own feeder that pushes filament into one shared hub at the bottom of the box. The hub has a measuring wheel and four limit switches — one per slot — and its outlet is visible through the transparent window at the bottom of the CFS. From there a single tube runs to the buffer, then to the toolhead.

Why slot 4 is the likeliest place

Slots 1 and 4 are the outside channels and carry the longest slot tube (230 mm against 200 mm in the middle). More tube, more drag, more chance of a burr catching. Creality publishes those lengths in its <a href="https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/replace-worn-cfs-ptfe-tube-maintenance" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PTFE maintenance guide</a>. It is the first place to look, not the only one — the hub is shared, so a fragment from any slot ends up there.

03

The flush — do this, in this order

ten minutes, one hex key, no force

Three attempts, easiest first. Stop as soon as the filament moves freely — every extra attempt risks grinding the fragment into the hub gear.

1

Switch the printer off and lift out the slot-4 spool.

The state is latched and the box is retrying every four seconds; cold and powered off is the only safe way to reach in. Open the lid and take the spool out so nothing is pulling against you.

2

Look in the three places, in order.

The inlet coupler at the slot mouth, the 230 mm tube from that coupler to the hub, and the transparent window at the bottom of the hub outlet. Creality's procedure starts at that window — that is where a fragment is meant to be visible. Note which of the three holds it.

3

Release lever, then pull by hand.

Press the release lever on slot 4's feeder — it opens the gear so the filament slides freely — and pull the tail. If it comes out, the break is in the slot, not the hub: cut the damaged end off square at 45°, drop the spool back in, and go to section 06.

4

If it will not come back: flush it forward.

This is the important one. The retract path is blocked, so pulling is the wrong direction. Take a fresh length of filament, cut a clean 45° end, straighten the first 100 mm, and feed it into slot 4 until the feeder grips it. Power on and press Feed for slot 4 on the screen. The new filament pushes the fragment ahead of it, through the 230 mm tube and out of the hub. Watch the transparent window and catch it there. Keep feeding until clean filament shows.

5

Then pull it back out and re-seat.

Press Retract for slot 4, take the flushing length out, re-seat the spool and feed the real filament. The box should take up the slack and the light go blue.

Two things not to do

Do not grab the PTFE tube with pliers — a crushed tube becomes a permanent jam. And do not push a cold, blunt end in: a square-cut 45° tip is the difference between feeding and wedging. Creality's guidance is the same on both points (<a href="https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/replace-worn-cfs-ptfe-tube-maintenance" target="_blank" rel="noopener">PTFE guide</a>).

04

If the fragment is inside the hub

the three-screw job

If forward feeding stalls — the feeder motor whines and nothing moves — the fragment is past the tube, inside the hub. Creality publishes this as <a href="https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/jammed-filament-of-cfs-hub" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Troubleshooting for CFS hub filament clogged</a>, and it is a maintenance job, not a repair:

  1. Power off. Remove the three screws and the four PTFE tubes at the bottom of the CFS.
  2. Lift the hub module out — the cable stays attached, so do not pull it.
  3. Check the connector is seated and the motor drive wheel turns freely by hand.
  4. Brush debris out of the synchronous gear.
  5. Unclip the transparent cover over the four limit switches and push each one: all four must spring.
  6. Brush the switch guide rails, reassemble, and run the slot-4 feed test again.

If a switch will not spring, debris has jammed the metal dome — that alone produces the same retract error, because the printer thinks filament is still at the hub when it is not. That case is documented under <a href="https://wiki.creality.com/en/cr-series/creality-hi/FR2849-error" target="_blank" rel="noopener">FR2849 on the Creality Hi</a>.

05

The last place: the buffer

only if the hub is clean and it still will not retract

The buffer is the spring-loaded block between the hub and the toolhead. A deformed end — from a clogged nozzle or a cutter that did not cut — bulges and locks there. Creality's fix is to remove its fixing screws, pry the casing along the middle seam with a spent bank card, and cut the expanded filament out with side cutters (<a href="https://wiki.creality.com/en/cfs/buffer-jammed-filament" target="_blank" rel="noopener">buffer jam procedure</a>). Mind the springs. With a single CFS you can also park the tube in any of the buffer's other three ports and carry on.

You can tell this case apart from a hub jam without opening anything: the hub window is clear, the tube moves freely, and the error appears only when the machine tries to unload rather than load.

06

Prove it works before you trust it

one sensor, and it is at the nozzle

The 18 November audit settled an important point: of the five filament sensors the firmware reports, only one is real — filament_sensor on the nozzle MCU. The other four are mainboard pins shared with the Y and Z endstops. A green switch there means nothing about plastic.

  1. Load slot 4 from the screen and wait for the load to finish on its own.
  2. Check the real sensor: it must read filament_detected: true. I can read that for you the moment you say the load finished.
  3. Purge 100 mm by hand at temperature and look at the extrusion — steady and round, no bubbles.
  4. Only then start a plate.

The check that matters

A load that finishes without an error is not proof. An empty extruder gear train behaves exactly like a healthy one: the motor turns at the commanded rate, the position counter climbs, and no plastic appears. The only proof is extruded plastic and a true reading on that single nozzle sensor.

08

So that it stops happening

three habits, one spare part
  • PTFE is a consumable. Creality's cycle is every two months of normal use, monthly for abrasive filament. The spec is 4 mm outer, 2.5 mm inner — anything tighter causes exactly these errors.
  • Cut every end at 45°. A blunt or burred end is what jams; a cut end feeds.
  • Keep the filament dry. The box reads 44 % humidity today, which is acceptable, but a spool that has been open through a wet Cape winter snaps — and a snapped piece inside the box is precisely the fault you have now. The forum regulars put it bluntly: when it breaks like that, it needs drying.
  • Clear tubing next time. An experienced fix is swapping the slot tubes for clear PTFE, so a fragment is visible instead of guessed at — the box's own window only shows the last few centimetres before the hub.
