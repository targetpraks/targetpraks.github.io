Bottom line

What it is: a 134-year-old value-added distributor of imported, mostly exclusive, branded automotive, industrial and electronic consumables across southern Africa — effectively a portfolio of 33 decentralised niche distribution and light-manufacturing businesses with entrenched agency agreements.

Why it is interesting: it is genuinely defensive and cash-generative — FY25 operating margin 12.5%, return on equity 17.0%, R1.37bn cash from operations, and interest covered 12.8× against its own benchmark of 5×. It pays out roughly half its comparable earnings and trades at ~8.3× FY25 comparable EPS, a 6.1% yield, and a beta of 0.19 (0.20 for peer Invicta).

The 2026 twist: management discontinued the alternative-energy and Eternity battery-service operations in June 2026 — a business that thrived on load-shedding and became commoditised. This removes a drag but makes the headline numbers look alarming: for the half-year to May 2026, reported HEPS fell ~33% to 624–635c and EPS fell ~32%, while comparable EPS (continuing operations) rose 12.4% to 978c. The gap is the R106m non-cash cost of discontinuance, mostly inventory impairment.

The watch items: the rand (73% of cost of sales is imported; a firmer rand compresses selling prices), SA consumer and mining capex (28% wholesale/retail, 18% automotive, 17% mining, 17% manufacturing of sales), and capital allocation — the company bought back 1 499 892 shares (4.86%) at R189 for R283.5m in July 2026.

§1 Business model

Hudaco imports branded products — mainly on an exclusive basis — directly from leading international manufacturers, and distributes them in markets where customers will pay for the value it adds: product specification, technical advice, application and installation training, troubleshooting, and ready availability.

Distribution platform (FY25)

  • Import ~73% of cost of sales directly (USD 61%, EUR 29%, YEN 5%)
  • Over 800 international suppliers
  • Over 230 000 line items in stock
  • ~130 branches in South Africa; ~30 000 active customers
  • Inventories R2.64bn; receivables R1.45bn
  • Hedges all liabilities and 30–40% of orders

Structure & ownership

  • 33 businesses in a deliberately decentralised group; industry-specialist management teams
  • Level 3 B-BBEE contributor — customers claim 110% procurement recognition
  • Black employees own 15% of every Hudaco business (except DD Power, where Deutz AG holds 30%)
  • R13m distributed to black employees via BEE trusts in FY25
  • Acquisitions are central to the model

Alternative energy (ex-Hudaco Energy / Deltec)

  • Solar and back-up power products; thrived during load-shedding
  • Demand collapsed when load-shedding ended; market became commoditised with falling product costs
  • R125m inventory impairment (stock bought 2–3 years ago)
  • No further procurement; some value recovery attempted
  • Automotive batteries (Deltec) and UPS (Specialised Battery Systems) continue

Eternity Technologies battery bay / service

  • Traction batteries and services, primarily for forklifts
  • Labour-intensive, contract-based, low margin; above-inflation wage growth
  • Former staff with supplier support split the agency, pressuring product margins
  • Retrenching ~90% of 245 employees
  • R21m retrenchment + plant/inventory write-offs

Only cash outflow from discontinued operations: R24m from operating activities. Management states these were "hitherto loss-making operations" that will "neither be a drag on group performance any longer nor absorb important management attention."

§8 Risks

Consumer and industrial demand — 28% of sales go to wholesale/retail, 18% automotive, 17% mining, 17% manufacturing. Hudaco is a proxy for SA Inc., and the group itself notes mining and manufacturing have declined for years.
Currency — ~73% of cost of sales is imported. A stronger rand lowers selling prices (margin pressure); a weaker rand without pricing power compresses margins. Forward exchange contracts were a R18.0m liability at 31 May 2026.
Interest rates and the consumer — the SARB raised the repo rate by a cumulative 50bp between April and October 2026 to 7.25%, with headline inflation at 4.4% (Aug) but expected above 5% later in 2026. Higher rates pressure the consumer-facing half of the portfolio and Hudaco's credit book (receivables R1.4bn).
Macro-political — SA's relationship with the USA, corruption, organised crime and de-industrialisation are called out in the group's own risk register.
Earn-out and goodwill risk — goodwill of R1.18bn sits on the balance sheet (mostly Isotec and prior deals). The Isotec earn-out estimate has already been revised down and the group has twice impaired battery-related goodwill (R81m FY25 alone).
Cyber and systems — the group describes the cyber threat as unquantifiable, mitigated by a decentralised structure and regular testing. IT is a standing audit-committee agenda item.
Governance and succession — leadership succession, executive health and the expansion of the executive committee (Shaun Coetzee appointed 29 June 2026) were explicit 2025 priorities.

§9 What to watch next

  • FY2026 results (full year to November 2026). Management guides that the second half is seasonally stronger and that borrowings should fall significantly by year-end if there are no new acquisitions. The key question is whether continuing-operations comparable EPS compounds from the 978c first-half base.
  • Clean comparable base post-discontinuance. With the loss-making energy/battery operations out, FY2026 is the first year that shows the "engine" of the group without the drag — and the first year reported HEPS and comparable EPS should converge again.
  • Capital allocation. Whether the buyback continues, and at what price, versus the group's stated preference for accretive acquisitions. At ~8× earnings and 1.44× book, a buyback at R189 looks accretive on paper.
  • The rand and commodity prices. Gold above $4 100–5 100/oz and platinum at record highs support mining maintenance spend, a direct 17% of sales, while a firmer rand cuts both ways on import margins.
  • Isotec earn-out resolution (August 2028). The purchase consideration remains sensitive to three years of profit after tax.
