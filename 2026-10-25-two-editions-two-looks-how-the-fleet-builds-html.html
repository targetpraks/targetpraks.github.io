Two editions, two looks — how the fleet builds HTML

One source, two editions, two looks

Every document the fleet writes now leaves in one shape: a markdown record that lives in the vault and an html edition that a person actually reads. This page is the share look. The internal one is quieter, and on purpose.

The two looks

read share
Who reads it you, mid-task staff, clients, partners
What it is PRDs, research, status, quick reads anything leaving the household
Paper warm paper, serif headings, gold callouts dark editorial cover, display serif, pulled quotes
Length one screen of structure, one diagram, stop room to breathe, still no padding
Writing controlled, short sentences, no flourishes humanized, an opinion allowed

Neither is "nicer" in the abstract. A PRD that looks like a brochure is a tax on the reader.

The rules that do not move

  • A diagram whenever anything flows. Boxes and arrows beat three paragraphs about a process.
  • No explainer walls. If a section cannot earn its place, it goes.
  • Numbers are real. Every figure traces to a command that was actually run.
  • Short sentences, one idea each. No "delve", no "landscape", no bold-everywhere.
  • Mobile first. The reference width is 390 pixels, because that is where you read.
  • One link per document. It carries both editions; nobody hunts for a file.

What builds them

The publishing tool renders the page from the source with pandoc, then wraps it in the tier's template. The look is one file per tier, so improving it changes every future edition and none of the past ones.

  • the strict writing and the diagram rule come from karpathy-output-ladder
  • the prose pass is humanizer; the house structure and validator are vault-report-authoring
  • diagrams are architecture-diagram, diagram-design, or mermaid inside the markdown
  • the share look borrows from auteur, impeccable and popular-web-designs when it has to impress
  • the pipeline is publish-document, which files the pair and puts the link on the jump page
