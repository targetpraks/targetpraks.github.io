Can ClickUp approve artwork and video better than Filestage?

Short answer: not on its own — ClickUp's proofing is a markup layer whose "approval" is somebody changing a status. Bolted to a real proofing engine it becomes the strongest approval chain in the field, and still lands inside the ClickUp task. This page shows what each route actually does, what it costs at forty people, and the one place Filestage is still better.

The answer

ClickUp alone: no. ClickUp + PageProof: yes — and it beats Filestage on the approval itself.

Native ClickUp proofing lets a reviewer comment and draw on images, PDFs and video, with timestamps on the video. There is no approve or reject decision, no version stack, no second approver, no record of who signed off. The approval, per ClickUp's own design-review guide, is the reviewer changing the task status.

ClickUp + PageProof keeps the proof in a real approval engine and mirrors it back: the task carries a synced Proof status field — New, In proofing, To-dos requested, Awaiting new version, Has new version, Approved, Archived — plus a summary comment with the decision count, and the approved file is uploaded to the task on final approval.

01What ClickUp does on its own

ClickUp's Proofing ClickApp is real, and it is good at the part everybody complains about: getting the comment onto the right pixel or the right frame.

  • File types: images (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WEBP), PDFs, and video in MP4, WEBM and Ogg.
  • Markup: click anywhere on the image or PDF to drop a comment; on video the comment attaches to the timestamp, so "the logo is soft here" points at 00:14, not at the whole file.
  • Assignment: comments can be assigned to a person and resolved, which is a decent proxy for a to-do list.
  • Where it stops: there is no decision on the asset. ClickUp's own design-review guide describes the loop as the approver reviewing and then changing the status back — the sign-off lives in a status field, not in the proof.

The consequence for a manager

If a client asks in November who approved the pack that went to print, ClickUp native gives you a status history and a comment thread. It cannot show a decision, a version, or a name against a stamp. That is exactly the thing Filestage charges for — so native ClickUp is not better than Filestage. It is a lighter version of one part of it.

02What an approval has to prove

Six tests, taken from what actually goes wrong in a brand team. A tool either has them or it does not.

The six tests, in the order a review fails
TestWhat it means in practice
1. Version stackv1, v2, v3 of the same artwork, with comments pinned to the version they were made on, and a compare view.
2. Decision per reviewerApprove, approve with changes, or request changes — recorded against a name and a time, not inferred from a status.
3. ThresholdThe proof only advances when all approvers, or a set number of them, have decided.
4. StagesInternal review, then brand, then client — sequential or parallel, with the next person notified automatically.
5. Outside approverAn external client can review and approve from a link, without an account and without a licence.
6. Audit + handbackA record survives, and the approved file and status land back in the project task without anyone copying files around.

So the answer to "is there an approval process inside ClickUp" is: yes, if you put one inside ClickUp. Two proofing engines do that properly, and one of them is cheaper than the Filestage bill it replaces.

04ClickUp + PageProof

PageProof is an online proofing platform built for print-grade artwork as well as video. Its ClickUp integration is documented on both sides, and it moves the approval into the task rather than beside it.

That is the piece Filestage does not give you: Filestage's ClickUp route is a Zapier template that creates a ClickUp task from a new file (see Filestage's integration list). The approval record stays in Filestage, and the task learns about it second-hand. PageProof writes the state, the decision count and the approved file into the task itself.

05PageProof's approval engine

This is the part that decides whether it is better than Filestage, not just different.

  • Decision thresholds. Any workflow step can require all decisions, one decision, or a set number before the proof advances — documented in multiple approvers.
  • A real decision vocabulary. Approve, request changes, and approved with changes — a decision Filestage does not offer, and the one that matches how brand teams actually sign off. See approved with changes.
  • Decisions can be changed, and the change is recorded — changing decisions.
  • Workflow steps wait for mandatory reviewers before the proof moves on, then notify the next stage — deep dive: proof approval workflows.
  • Decision summary and progress on the proofing screen, the info pane and the tile — decision summary.
  • Artwork-grade tools. Compare mode, ColorSep colour-separation plates for packaging, barcode and link scanning, ruler and gridlines, and formats running from Adobe Creative Suite and Office to video, audio, websites, HTML emails and 3D.

The catch, said plainly

Digital signing — verified approvals — sits on the Enterprise plan, not Team. Team gets the decision record, the threshold and the audit trail; it does not get a cryptographic signature. If a client contract needs a signature, that is an Enterprise conversation. Every plan includes unlimited reviewers and unlimited proofs.

06ClickUp + Ziflow

The other credible engine, and the one most often compared with Filestage. Ziflow's own ClickUp article is blunt about the tier: the integration is Pro or Enterprise only, and it creates and updates ClickUp tasks for proofs, comments and decisions.

  • Ziflow Standard, $199/mo billed yearly — 15 users included, unlimited reviewers and proofs, 2 workflow stages, unlimited versions, pixel-level version compare, 90-day audit trail. No ClickUp integration.
  • Ziflow Pro, $329/mo billed yearly — 20 users, 3 stages, decision reasons, colour separation, minor versions, custom comment labels, 1-year audit trail, and the advanced project-management integrations including ClickUp.
  • Ziflow Enterprise — electronic signatures, SSO, white-label viewer, multi-asset campaign proofs, ReviewAI.

Read the tiers carefully: at the same $329 as Filestage Business you get 20 proofing users instead of 10, plus the ClickUp sync. At $199 you save money but you lose the sync — which is the whole point of this page.

08Where Filestage still wins

Fairness matters more than a clean table. Filestage Business does three things PageProof Team does not:

  • Review agents — automatic checks on grammar, tone, style, QR codes, barcodes and mandatory images, run on every file without a human.
  • Automations — triggers and actions that move files between reviewer groups on their own.
  • Simplicity — a reviewer needs no training at all. PageProof's depth is also its cost: thresholds, workflow steps and decision types are things somebody has to configure and then explain.

If the real problem is "our clients send messy feedback and we want it tidied", Filestage is the friendlier tool. If the real problem is "who signed this off, on which version, and why is it not in the project", PageProof is the better one — and it is the only one of the two that puts that answer inside ClickUp.

09What I would do

  1. Move to ClickUp Business for all forty seats, billed monthly. R14,388 a month. You get the inbox, the Relationships field that answers "this task depends on that task", and native proofing on artwork, PDFs and video with timestamped comments — the markup layer costs nothing extra.
  2. Add PageProof Team on the annual price, and cancel Filestage. R3,942 a month. Total R18,330 — under a thousand rand a month more than Hive with proofing, and the approval chain is genuinely stronger than the Filestage subscription it replaces.
  3. Run the kill test in week one, on your own files. A 20-page print PDF, a 60-second video, a set of pack shots. Two approvers with a threshold of two, one external client by email with no account. It passes only if: the proof returns to v2 and comments stay pinned to v1, the threshold blocks until both have decided, the client's approval shows a name and a time, the ClickUp custom field reads Approved, and the approved file is sitting on the task.
  4. Put four things to PageProof in writing before signing. All forty domain users on the Team plan at the flat $2,499 — not a per-user surprise. Whether the ClickUp custom-field sync needs a paid ClickUp plan above Business. What happens to proofs if you cancel — export and retention. And the price to add digital signing, so you know what Enterprise costs before a client asks for a signature.
  5. Annualise ClickUp in year two, not year one. Monthly costs R63,608 more a year than annual; earn that discount with a quarter of real use, not with a guess.

The one-line answer to the question

Nothing native inside ClickUp approves artwork or video better than Filestage — it annotates and then changes a status. ClickUp + PageProof does approve better than Filestage: thresholds, staged workflows, "approved with changes", version compare, and a proof status that lands on the task. For R18,330 a month at forty people.
