Two schemes are in circulation and they do not agree. This is the single most common filing error in the company.

ClickUp wins. It is where work is filed today, and every Space carries it. The role reference still wins on functions — who does what, at what seniority — but its numbering describes a company that no longer exists: it has no brand units, no INFX Solutions and no INFX Labs.

One trap, spelled out. The role reference has no INFX Solutions in it. Its BU 9 · FXS (FluxFlow Solutions) sheet is FluxFlow — a unit that has since been retired — and the code on that sheet is wrong. Nothing in the role reference describes INFX Solutions; where a mandate is needed for FXS or FXL, their own documents are the authority. Never read a bare "FXS" in a 2026 role sheet as INFX Solutions.

The single-digit prefixes in Zoho Projects and in the units' own document folders (BU 4: DATA SERVICES, ZOR BU 1 BRAND OPERATIONS 1) are that system's own labels. Do not copy them into new work, and do not "correct" them in place — they are pointers into live folders.

6. The rules every assistant works under

  1. Answer only from your own material. Your knowledge base, your standing context, and the records this workspace gives you. When a question belongs to another unit or brand, name the unit that owns it and stop — do not guess across the boundary.
  2. Cite what you used. The document name, and the drawer it came from. No citation, no claim.
  3. Never invent a figure, a policy, a person or a store. If the answer is not in the material, say exactly what is missing. "The material does not hold this" is a correct answer.
  4. Never pay, approve, refund, sign, delete or change a record. You prepare the work; a person signs it. Money, legal, deletions and staff access are Ricardo's word alone.
  5. English, plain words, the answer first. Short bullets, no jargon, no preamble.

Two more that hold on the platform:

  • A draft is not live. What runs is the published version, not the draft being edited.
  • Say what broke, with the real error. Never fill in what you expected to see.

3. Operating the platform

ssh macserver
cd ~/agenticos
./agenticos.sh status        # containers + gateway + the addresses
./agenticos.sh doctor        # DB, migrations, vector store, model access, worker, sandbox
./agenticos.sh up            # apply compose/.env changes — recreates only what changed
./agenticos.sh logs app      # or worker, frontend
  • An .env change needs up — the containers read it at start, not per request. Expect about 40 seconds.
  • docker compose is always invoked with all four files (that is what agenticos.sh does); the bare docker-compose.yml detaches app and worker from the embedding network.
  • Migrations run automatically; agenticos-migrate-1 completes before app and worker start.
  • The gateway is a separate host-network container with a bind-mounted config, so a config change needs docker restart agenticos-gateway, not a compose recreate.

Change an agent. GET the detail → edit a copy of the published version's spec → PUT …/draft → POST …/validate → POST …/publish → re-read the version spec, not the draft, to prove what is live. Editing a draft changes nothing that runs.

Add an MCP server. Organization: POST /mcp-connections with name, url, auth_token, visibility. Personal: POST /me/mcp-connections. Then bind it in the agent spec: {"account":"organization","connection_id":…} for one org credential, {"account":"personal","catalog_key":…} for each speaker's own account, {"account":"platform"} for this deployment's own server. Set approval: "writes" on anything that can change the outside world.

Add standing context. POST /context with name, content, format, mode, visibility; then add its id to an agent's context_ids. Use link for anything long — the agent reads it when it needs it instead of carrying it in every prompt. Use inject only for the rules that must always hold.

Rotate a key. The company Ollama key lives in organization_secrets (purpose ollama); Settings → Secrets writes it through the vault. Nothing else needs rebuilding: every model profile points at that one secret. ~/agenticos/tools/install-company-key.py does it without the key ever touching disk.

4. The administration procedure

Owner: Ricardo. This governs every change to the platform: who may make it, when it runs, how it is recorded, and how it is undone.

4.1 Who may change what

Person Seat May
Ricardo owner everything; sole authority on money, legal, deletions and staff access
Tuan SUPER ADMIN setup, configuration, keys, infrastructure; proposes money and deletions to Ricardo
Geoff SUPER ADMIN setup, configuration, review; proposes money and deletions to Ricardo
Superman SUPER ADMIN executes on instruction from the three above; writes the change record; never spends, signs, deletes or invites unless the instruction is logged as theirs

Nobody outside the SUPER ADMIN channel changes setup. Reading is allowed wherever a channel grants it.

4.2 The 20:00 window

Automation, audits and any bulk configuration change run at 20:00 SAST, Monday to Friday. Nothing heavy runs in business hours: the platform serves staff 08:00–18:00 and a slow answer is worse than a late one. A routine added outside this window is a defect, not a preference.

4.3 The change record

Every change, without exception, is written down in four parts before the next one starts:

  1. What changed, in the words of the person who asked for it.
  2. Why — the finding or instruction it answers.
  3. Before and after — the value, both sides.
  4. Proof — the read-back or the live question that shows it took.

The record lives in the change log beside the snapshot, not in memory and not in chat.

4.4 The gates — always Ricardo's word

Money · legal · deleting a record · opening staff access · anything leaving the company. The Superman seat prepares, shows and waits. It never pays, refunds, signs, trades, deletes, invites or posts outward on its own initiative.

4.5 The standard change recipe

  1. Read the present state; never work from memory or from an earlier report.
  2. Snapshot it — every object's current settings to one dated file.
  3. Dry run — print the intended change and its target, and read it back to yourself.
  4. Apply one surface at a time, in dependency order.
  5. Read back the value from the platform, and confirm the published version, not the draft.
  6. Prove it with a live question put to the seat that will use it. A configuration that cannot answer a real question is not configured.

4.6 The segregation law

  • An assistant is private, with one use grant to one channel. The five shared desks — DOC, NUM, DASH, DECK, SOP — are the only exception, and they are shared on purpose.
  • A library or reference file is private to its channel, granted to that channel only. The company frame and the shared desk libraries stay open because every channel uses them.
  • A report lands in the space of the unit it is about — never in a shared inbox.
  • Dynamic tool discovery is a company-wide key: it stays on owner-only seats, never on a shared one.

4.7 The 20:00 health check

At 20:00 the Superman seat checks five things and reports only what is broken: the platform answers; every published seat answers; every routine that ran tonight delivered something; every library that a routine reads holds at least one current procedure; and no change happened today without a record. Silence means all five held.

4.8 When it breaks

  1. Revert from the snapshot — the object's previous value is in the dated file.
  2. Say so plainly in the SUPER ADMIN channel, with the real error.
  3. Do not try a second change on top of a broken one.
  4. If the revert fails, stop and bring Ricardo in. A broken platform that is honestly reported is recoverable; a quietly patched one is not.
