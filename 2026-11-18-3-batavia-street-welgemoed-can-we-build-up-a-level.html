2. The rule, as it stands today

This changed on 11 September 2026. The erf is zoned Residential 1 — the City renamed Single Residential 1 to R1 in October 2025. Table 2 of the by-law gives a stand of this size one row:

The old split between wallplate height and roof height was dropped in September 2026. It is now one number: ground to the top of the building, roof and parapet included. There is no cap on storeys in this zoning, so how many levels the house has today never enters the test — only metres.

6. Why the roof has to come off

There is a roof behind the flat parapet. The 1984 drawings — Pieter J. Pelser, approved by Bellville in March 1984 — show Maitland Timber Hydro-Nail trusses at 17½° on the sheet marked NUWE DAKPLAN, and the elevation carries the annotation +55,080 at the parapet. From the street you see a flat box. Inside that box is a shallow pitched roof over a shell, not rooms.

That shell is why the attic idea was costed in revision 6 and then deleted in revision 7. It assumed a pitched roof could go back on and space could be squeezed into the truss void. Once the method is a cast slab and a full storey, there is no attic to sell. It is gone from this document on purpose, not quietly.

On that measure the order does not move: adding a level is the cheapest way to own more space on this erf, and buying modern is the most expensive. The rebuild is the cheapest way to own new space, at R29,972 a square metre, and it is the only route that comes with a new structure, a new roof and an NHBRC warranty over the whole house.

Selling is cheap in cash only because the house pays for a third of the next one. It is not cheap in value: C3 needs R12.97 m of new money to end with 608 m² instead of 780 m², on an erf that is larger but in a different suburb, and you pay R2.6 m of commission, duty and conveyancing to get there.

The honest ranking

  • Best value per m² of floor you own: the level. R14,165 against R29,972 for the rebuild. It is also the only route that keeps the 2,050 m² erf.
  • Best house per rand: the rebuild. R14.99 m for 500 m² of new structure with a warranty, against R11.05 m for 200 m² added to a 42-year-old one.
  • Least cash out of the bank: selling, at R10.89–12.97 m, because the asset pays for part of it. That is not the same as cheapest.
  • Worst of both: the level if the engineer demands a long-span slab, a new frame and full underpinning — R19.05 m in the high case, for 200 m².

11. What to do next, in this order

  1. Surveyor's levels, R8,000–20,000. It decides whether the new floor goes in at 54.7 m, and therefore whether this scheme exists at all. Nothing else is worth spending on first.
  2. The deed, about R2,000. Conditions on T15018/2024, and the base cost that sets the capital gains tax. Two answers, one errand, 48 hours.
  3. Engineer's opinion on the load path, R25,000–60,000. Can new blockwork rise on the 1984 internal walls, or does the slab have to span the perimeter? That answer is worth R780,287 on its own, and it decides whether underpinning is R900,000 or R2.5 m.
  4. A bank or estate-agent valuation, R3,000–6,000. It closes the land contradiction and tells you what the sell route is really worth.
  5. The lease, read by someone who does conveyancing. The tenant has the house until 30 June 2027, and both A and B make it uninhabitable for months. Either the buy-out is priced or the programme waits.

The first four together cost under R90,000 and remove every number in this document that is still a guess. No contractor is worth calling before the surveyor has been.

12. What is not in any of these totals

  • The tenant. R250,000–600,000 to buy the lease out early, or a delay to July 2027 with escalation on top. Both routes make the house unlivable while the roof is off.
  • Finance and escalation as separate lines: R911,511 and R504,573 on the level, R1,236,343 and R684,385 on the rebuild. R12.46 m and R16.91 m if both land.
  • Renovating the 580 m² you already have. R1.2 m as a line, and without it you finish with a brand-new level on top of a 1984 kitchen.
  • Deed conditions. A single-dwelling covenant, a height restriction, a servitude over the slope, or an architectural committee could stop the scheme before pricing matters.
  • The base cost for capital gains tax — the deed, unread.
  • Any quote. There is none. These are published 2026 rates and my own allowances, and a quantity surveyor prices drawings, not rates.
