Erf 465, Bellville · 3 Batavia Street, Welgemoed · 18 November 2026

Can we go up a level?

Revision 6. Costed, checked against the by-law as it stands today, and reviewed by a second model before publication.

Yes. You can add a level. The money is what says no.

The zoning allows 10 m from the existing ground to the top of the building on this erf. The house uses 3.1–6.6 m of that today. One more level takes it to 5.8–9.3 m — inside the limit on either reading, with 0.7 m to spare in the worst case. There is no storey limit, and a basement does not count as a storey.

The cost is the whole story. R8.97 m to add the level. R15.80 m to demolish and rebuild. Against a house worth R6.85–8.89 m.

1. The rule, as it stands today

This changed on 11 September 2026, which is why the answer moved. The erf is zoned Residential 1 — the City renamed Single Residential 1 to R1 in October 2025. Table 2 of the by-law, as substituted by the 2026 amendment, gives this stand:

The old split between wallplate height and roof height was dropped in September 2026. It is now one number: ground to the top of the building. There is also no cap on the number of storeys at all, so how many levels the house has today never enters the test — only metres. (The by-law does define a storey, and a space protruding no more than 1,5 m above ground counts as a basement rather than a storey, but that only matters for zonings that still count them. This one does not.)

The ground levels either side of the house are read off the survey contours on the plans, good to about half a metre. The two readings — downhill wall, street wall — are the two ends of the argument, and the level fits on both. 0.7 m of margin in the worst case, 4.2 m in the best. A second level would not fit from the downhill side. The design instruction that follows: set the new level back over the uphill half, where the ground is highest and the margin is largest.

3. What is still unknown

Three things, and only the first can stop the project outright.

  • The title deed (T15018/2024). A restrictive condition can bind this erf independently of the zoning. The conveyancer has been asked for a copy. Until it is read, the answer is conditional.
  • The ground-level figure the City will accept. The by-law defines existing ground level as the level on the City's Ground Level Map, a LiDAR surface that is not published. A surveyor's certificate settles it, and converts the range above into a single number. That is the first spend, and it is small.
  • The structure. The drawings do not show footings designed for a third level. An engineer has to confirm the load path; strengthening is in the budget at R550,000.

Welgemoed's established houses sell between about R5.2m and R8m. Its best new estate asks R10.995m to R21.995m — asking, not selling. To make the rebuild work you would have to own the most expensive house in the suburb by a wide margin. To make the level work you would have to add R9 million of value to a R7–9 million property.

So: build up, or knock down?

Build up, if you want the space. R8.97m against R15.80m is not a close call, and the level is legally the easier route — no demolition, no NHBRC enrolment, no reconnection, and the tenant's lease can run to its end while you draw. Don't rebuild for value. The honest reasons to demolish are a structure that has failed, a layout that cannot be fixed, or a plan to redevelop the 2,050 m² erf — and that last one is a better question than this one.

7. What to do next

  1. Read the deed. The one document that can bind the erf regardless of zoning. Chase the conveyancer.
  2. Get a surveyor's level certificate, roughly R8,000–20,000. It turns section 2 from a range into the single number the City will use.
  3. Pay an engineer R20,000–60,000 to rule on the footings and the load path. Everything else is speculation until that comes back.
  4. Then price the drawings with a quantity surveyor. That is the only step that turns R8.97m into a budget. Expect ±25% until then.

8. What is not in either total

  • Rent lost. The lease runs to 30 June 2027. Sequence the work after it and this is zero; break it early and every month costs you the rent.
  • Finance and escalation. At 11% on half the spend over 18 months, roughly R0.7m on the level and R1.3m on the rebuild. A 6% annual rise in building costs adds about R0.4m and R0.7m.
  • Asbestos. A 1984 house warrants a survey, R15,000–30,000. If it is found, removal runs R100,000–400,000 on the rebuild and it is not in the demolition rate.
  • Insurance, site security and municipal services during the build, and the fittings you choose after the handover.
