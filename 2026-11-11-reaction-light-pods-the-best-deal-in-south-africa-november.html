01 What the device actually is

Each pod is a wireless puck roughly 10 cm across, charged on a shared dock, controlled from a phone app. The app calls a drill — sprint to the lit pod, tap it, next pod lights — and logs reaction times. You touch them, step on them or hit them with a racket; they are impact-rated, not delicate. In the reel the pods are simply lying loose on the asphalt, which is normal: pod bases, cone holders and wall mounts exist for when you want them fixed in place.

Under the video, a commenter asks “Where can I find those push buttons?” — so it is not only you. The branded original is BlazePod; several lookalikes now sell on Amazon for less, and I priced those too.

03 Why the 6-pod Trainer Kit, and not the cheaper 4-pod

Two things decide it, and neither is the sticker price.

Per pod, the 6-pod kit is the cheapest genuine route

R1,225 per pod in the Trainer Kit versus R1,875 in the Starter Kit. The Starter Kit saves R1,500 today and costs R2,000+ the moment you want the two extra pods.

Adding pods one by one is the expensive path

A single pod bought on its own is R2,085 and the 6-pod case is R1,055. Mini Kit plus three pods plus a case lands at about R11,300 — worse than the Trainer Kit you could have bought first.

Six pods is also the useful number for what you saw in the reel. Five or six spread over the lawn or a court is a real drill; three is a table-top game. Four adults and kids rotating through drills want enough pods that nobody waits for a pod.

What about the R1,000 cheaper backorder?

EquipMe's own storefront lists the same Trainer Kit at R7,999 but shows “available on backorder” with no delivery date, and its storefront states a 6-month warranty; the Makro listing for the identical kit carries the BlazePod 1-year warranty and Makro's 14-day return window, with delivery quoted for 13 November. R1,000 for a dated delivery, a longer warranty and a returns counter is the better trade — the gate gave the backorder a 0.4% chance of being the right answer.

04 Importing it yourself does not pay

BlazePod's own site sells the same Trainer Kit at USD 329 — R5,398 at today's 16.41 ZAR/USD. Add roughly R1,000 of shipping, 15% VAT on the landed value and whatever duty the courier collects, and it lands between R7,300 and R8,800 with a 2–4 week wait, no local returns and a warranty claim that means shipping pods back to the United States. The official kits here are USD 249 (4 pods) and USD 169 (3 pods) for the same comparison — local pricing is within a few hundred rand of landed cost on every kit, so importing buys you nothing but delay.

06 What it needs to work

  • The app is free and ships with 100+ ready-made drills; the Pro tier is optional at USD 10.39/month and only adds programmes and long-term progress tracking.
  • Charge on the dock. Six pods charge on one base; a session is roughly an hour of play per charge.
  • Wet asphalt is fine. The reel is shot in the rain and the pods are IP-rated for outdoor use — but charge them dry.
  • Bases are sold separately if you want them fixed upright: R1,150 for a set of two.
