Infinity Brands — ClickUp implementation PRD

Version: 1.0 · Date: 29 October 2026 · Status: Ready for independent review; not approved for implementation.

Purpose: Give a reviewing agent enough information to challenge the design, and a subsequent implementation agent a precise, testable contract for a fresh ClickUp workspace.

Business sponsor: Ricardo Maio, subject to confirmation of accountable approver. Technical custodian: CTO / platform lead. Prepared by: Codex from the supplied specification, local evidence and official ClickUp documentation. This document does not adopt the unrelated Gauzy report’s attribution.

Current state: specification only. The user reports no ClickUp account, trial or workspace and a prior Composio result of connected_toolkits: []. No ClickUp connection was tested or created for this PRD. No workspace, invitation, purchase, public form, integration, Paperclip approval or Hermes card has been created. The existing nine-Space design is on paper. Local document preparation is the only implementation performed here.

Hard boundary: no jobs across. Do not import, recreate, synchronise, map or copy existing Zoho jobs, attachments, users or conversations. Do not change Zoho permissions, make Zoho read-only, announce a cut-over or retire any existing tool. Zoho remains in its existing operating state. New ClickUp work begins only on a separately approved pilot scope and start date.

1. Executive decision

Build one workspace, nine private Spaces, with functional queues and a consistent Brand field. Use existing organisational evidence to design the shape of future work. Keep each work item in one authoritative home List under its accountable delivery team. Use linked tasks for distinct cross-team deliverables and permission-aware reporting for brand visibility.

The nine Spaces are Leadership, Marketing & Creative, Finance, People, Technology, Sales & Revenue, Brand Operations, Client Delivery, and Franchise & Store Ops. Administration has an explicit proposed home in Brand Operations → Group Administration; this resolves the missing queue without silently adding a tenth Space. That choice requires sponsor confirmation before its live configuration.

External access is always to a separately permissioned container or task. A Store field, filtered view, hidden column, dashboard or tag is never an access boundary. Shared regional queues are internal only. Every store or client with an external audience receives an isolated container, even if that means more folders.

Business is the candidate plan, not an authorised purchase. Forty paid humans is a planning scenario, not an invite list. Pricing, role eligibility, plan features and actual tenant limits must be verified and approved before purchase or invitations.

5. Scope and non-goals

Must deliver in v1

  • One workspace skeleton and nine private Spaces, created only after approval.
  • Canonical hierarchy, owned queues, fields, status templates and reusable task templates.
  • Four intake forms, initially unpublished: Creative Request, Client Brief, Store Ticket, IT Support.
  • Store finance daily completeness pilot, month-end template and expiry calendar.
  • Named-user permissions, client/store separation and guest acceptance tests.
  • Four operational dashboards, with human-readable definitions of every metric.
  • Bounded automations and an optional, separately gated agent integration.
  • Configuration inventory, test evidence, operating runbook and decision log.

Explicitly excluded

Importers, legacy job recreations, user synchronisation, chat ingestion, DM reading, accounting transactions, payroll, employee file storage, credential storage, website publication, outgoing marketing, automatic external sharing, tool cancellations, CRM replacement, Zoho cut-over, public dashboards and automatic AI approvals. Do not build future client or store containers speculatively. Do not move personal/legal matters from historical project evidence into this business workspace.

Sales records in v1 are tasks for sales execution, proposal preparation and next actions. An existing CRM remains authoritative for customer/deal records unless a separate decision establishes otherwise. WorkDrive/SharePoint or the approved document repository remains authoritative for controlled files; validate which repository owns each class rather than assuming every file is in WorkDrive.

8.2 Store separation

Internal regional work has no external access. The same store-facing request must not exist as two copied tasks in internal and shared Lists. Intake starts internally. Once validated and sanitised, its canonical task may move once into that store’s Shared Requests, retaining its task ID; record status and field mapping and re-test access. Until that review occurs, the request is not shared.

If work needs confidential internal analysis, create a distinct internal work package with separate deliverable and acceptance criteria. The external task represents the customer’s request and public progress; it is not a duplicated work tracker. Link only where relationship metadata is proven not to expose restricted titles or content. Otherwise keep the connection in an internal-only register.

A region folder is not shared with external users. Creating a store Subfolder does not itself prove isolation: inspect inherited permissions and test cross-store denial. Store managers who are employees follow the same least-access design even when their account must be a paid member.

The builder maintains registers/ CSVs containing approved values and their approvers. Store register includes trading state, effective dates, region, country, legal operator, brand, business calendar, operational owner and permitted audience. 9.4 Validation contract

Required fields in ClickUp apply at task creation; they do not guarantee later transition checks. REST task creation ignores required Custom Fields by default unless check_required_custom_fields: true is supplied [V08, V09]. Every agent/API writer must validate before write and read back afterwards. Test Forms, UI, API, templates, recurrence and task moves separately.

Accepted work cannot leave Triage with missing required context, no owner or mismatched audience. Prefer native guards where proven. Otherwise use a named human acceptance check and a “Needs Correction” view; an asynchronous automation that reverts a status is not a secure approval gate. No automated publish/share/payment may depend solely on an editable status or checkbox.

12. Recurrence, service clocks and finance completeness

All timestamps display in Africa/Johannesburg unless an approved store calendar explicitly differs. Proposed support hours are Monday–Friday, 08:00–17:00, excluding approved public holidays. This is a proposal for D09, not an assertion about contracts. Store finance uses each approved store’s trading calendar, including weekends where applicable. Do not hard-code South African holidays for stores in another country.

Proposed pilot first-response targets: urgent business outage 1 business hour; high impact 4 business hours; normal request 1 business day. Urgent notifications must use the existing agreed emergency channel; a form is not an emergency response service. Resolution dates are set at acceptance by the owner and any actual contract. Acknowledgement and resolution are different measures.

Waiting may pause a resolution clock only if approved policy allows it. Keep total elapsed age visible. First-response timing does not pause simply because a task is unassigned. Working-day calculations must be proved; if native rules cannot express the calendar, calculate via the approved scheduler or record dates manually during pilot. Never silently treat “two working days” as 48 hours.

Daily finance algorithm

  1. CFO approves a schedule register: store, control type, effective dates, expected business dates, due time/offset, preparer, verifier and evidence location.
  2. Generate one new task occurrence per obligation, not a reused task that erases yesterday’s state. Key format: STORE_ID|YYYY-MM-DD|CONTROL_TYPE.
  3. Schedule independently of completion so an overdue prior occurrence cannot prevent tomorrow’s control. If native recurrence cannot produce this reliably, use an approved idempotent scheduler; do not activate a false completeness process.
  4. At the agreed check time, compare expected keys from the schedule register against actual occurrences. Detect missing, duplicate, late, unassigned and evidence-incomplete tasks.
  5. Completion requires a result and evidence; Exception requires reason, owner and next action. Not Applicable requires authorised explanation and is separately reported.
  6. On store closure or calendar change, stop future generation from the effective date. Preserve prior records; do not regenerate legacy dates before the pilot start.

Native recurrence can create new tasks and has timing options, but those options need testing for overdue and early-close cases [V10]. Recurring-task creation may trigger other rules, so it is not “free” in the overall action budget even if the schedule itself uses no automation action.

16.2 Reliability and audit

  • Maintain a local protected mapping of stable configuration key → ClickUp object ID; names alone are not safe identity.
  • Before create, discover existing matching IDs and inspect configuration. If a prior write timed out, read before retry; never blindly repeat creates or comments [V07].
  • Process signed webhook events through a durable queue. Verify X-Signature HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body, compare safely, accept only registered webhook IDs, and keep secrets outside logs [V12].
  • Acknowledge only after durable receipt, then process asynchronously. Target acknowledgement under two seconds; monitor failures and suspension [V13].
  • Dedupe by webhook/history item identity where supplied. For events without that identifier, use a documented resource/event/version key plus a read-back check. Persist keys beyond the expected redelivery window.
  • Re-fetch current task state before an action; delayed events must not overwrite newer human decisions. Record a conflict and skip if preconditions changed.
  • Suppress self-generated loops by action ledger and before/after comparison. A tag alone is insufficient loop protection.
  • Use bounded exponential backoff with jitter for safe retries and honour rate-limit headers. Permission/authentication errors halt the affected job.
  • Keep an exception queue with owner, error class, retry count and next action. Reconcile known active pilot objects on a bounded schedule to detect missed events; no continuous whole-workspace polling.
  • Record correlation ID, actor/account, approved rule, object ID, timestamp, field changes, result and approval reference. Redact secrets and personal content. This is an integration activity ledger, not a claim of complete native audit logging.

The current official MCP table gives Business 1,000 calls per rolling 24 hours without Everything AI; limits are shared by connected clients. Enterprise and Enterprise Plus differ, so do not reuse E01’s generic 25,000 Enterprise claim [V06]. Budget at most 600 for the initial agent workload, reserving 400 for human clients and diagnostics, subject to actual usage. Track reads as well as writes. Confirm REST rate limit for the selected plan and token before enabling jobs; follow returned limit headers.

Kill switch: disable worker writes and relevant automations, stop new event processing while retaining queued events, revoke the relevant grant if access is suspect, and notify the approved operator through an authorised route. Verify whether revoking one grant affects other clients. Disabling a webhook creator’s account can stop events [V11]; test identity replacement and recovery before relying on it.

20. Operations, rollback and definition of done

Daily during pilot: triage owners review new requests and missing fields; CFO delegate checks expected finance occurrences; platform owner checks failures and consumption. Weekly: owners review waiting/overdue work, unused fields and failed tests. Monthly: reconcile billing/guest allowances, access register, recurrence schedules and automation usage. Quarterly: review taxonomy, retention and template versions.

Offboarding: revoke access/grants, transfer assigned work and recurring controls, verify webhooks under the leaving user’s identity, remove external shares and test denial. Do not assume recurrence stops when its creator leaves. Archive completed containers only under the retention policy; archived recurrence must not silently stop a live obligation.

Rollback prioritises disabling new effects: pause rules/workers, unpublish affected forms, revoke the precise external shares/grants, preserve event evidence and notify authorised owners. Capture configuration before changes. Do not bulk-delete production work or destroy a workspace to undo configuration. Synthetic objects may be removed only when identified as such and within approved cleanup scope. Billing changes and any destructive recovery need their own already-existing or newly obtained explicit authorisation.

Definition of done for the live implementation: approved gates linked; all in-scope critical tests pass; owner/register decisions resolved; actual IDs and settings documented; billing checked; owners trained; manual fallbacks rehearsed; support/runbook handed over; unresolved limitations explicitly accepted.
