1. The decision this document asks for

Ricardo, three things:

  1. The department model in §5 replaces the function model entirely. Your eleven Spaces become your eight departments plus franchise services, governance and reporting. Brands stop being a dropdown and become folders where the work is genuinely per-brand.
  2. Free Forever cannot hold this design. Not "it will be tight" — it cannot: 5 Spaces against 11, 60 custom-field uses against ~960, 100 automation actions/month against an SLA system that needs thousands, and 1,000 chat messages for the entire workspace, never resetting. §17 puts the number on the fix.
  3. SLA needs Business, not Core. ClickUp has no native SLA object — no field, no report, no setting. A help-centre search for "SLA" returns six results and none of them is a feature. Breach tracking is assembled from formula fields, automations and Time in Status, and automation conditions begin at Business. §10 is the build.

Everything else in this document is how.

2. Why v4.1 was the wrong spec

Not a matter of taste. Measured against the live systems:

v4.1 claimed Reality, read 2026-11-13
Eleven Spaces, function-first Five exist. Free allows five. Six designed Spaces can never be created.
Sixteen custom fields on every list Zero custom fields exist. GET /list/{id}/field returns [] on all 23 lists.
Views that surface work ("Waiting On", "Ticket Age", "This Week") 51 views exist, all empty: 0 with filters, 0 with grouping, 0 with sorting. "This Week" filters on nothing.
Private Spaces for sensitive registers All five Spaces are public. HRM "Restricted Cases", FIN "Creditors & Approvals", Leadership "Decision Register" are readable by anyone added tomorrow.
Departments as business units Departments never appear. The spec models functions (Finance, People, Technology) and treats brands as a 14-value dropdown.
Status models per Space Every list still carries ClickUp's default pair: to do, complete. Not one of the thirteen designed status sets was applied.
A working SLA system No SLA field, no clock, no breach state anywhere in the design — "SLA" appears as prose only.
Chat replacing Cliq Seven channels exist; every description is null, so no channel states its purpose or its owner.

The deeper failure is structural, and it is mine to name: v4.1 was designed from a spreadsheet of object types, not from how this company actually works. It defined shapes (Space, Folder, List, Field) and filled them with plausible names. It never asked which desks exist, who owns them, what the six work formats are, what the brands are called in Zoho, how a brief is filed, or how a week is reported.

The evidence for how the company works was available the whole time. It just was not consulted.

3.5 SLA today is a naming convention, and it works

There is no SLA field anywhere: 118 task fields, 28 action-item fields, 46 project fields, 14 list fields were read, and none is named or displayed as SLA, escalation, turnaround or breach.

What exists is discipline:

  • 486 tasks named SLA - <store> <work> or SLA: <store> <work> — 456 of them in BU 1 Finance, 12 in ADM, 7 in HRM, 3 in MKT, 3 in BND2, 3 in SNR EXCO.
  • A prose contract in the description: WHAT, WHY, HOW, DONE WHEN, an explicit time budget in minutes, and escalated to <person> in Cliq. Definition-of-done appears on 287 BU 1 tasks and 506 in BND 2.
  • Due dates hand-set, not derived: 2,722 of 2,854 BU 1 tasks carry one; 50 of its 456 SLA tasks carry none.
  • Approval semantics carried in statuses: New Brief, In Review, Accepted, Rejected, Awaiting Approval Internal, Approved by Manager, Awaiting Feedback Internal, Awaiting Feedback External, On Hold, Reopened.
  • The only lateness measure is age_since_due, whose display name is wrongly copied as "Age (days since created)".

Your team already runs SLAs. They run them in a naming convention and a description template. The new build promotes the convention into structure without changing the discipline: same words, same escalation, now with a clock that counts and a field that flags.

Tiers exist but are barely used: service_tier Gold/Black/Silver appears on 278 of 11,717 tasks (Gold 252, Silver 19, Black 7); engagement_tier HOF/ZOR/COR/ZEE is the requester class, not a service promise; priority never once says "Urgent".

4. Five architecture laws

These decide every row in §5–§14. If a detail later contradicts a law, the law wins or the law is formally revised.

Law 1 — A Space is a desk, not a project and not a brand. You run twelve desks. Spaces mirror desks because that is where accountability, privacy and reporting boundaries already sit: a Finance person lives in Finance, and Finance's registers are not Marketing's business. Projects, campaigns, store openings and migrations are folders and lists inside a desk. Brands are not Spaces because six brands against five Free slots would consume the whole workspace and leave nowhere for Finance — and because a brand is a property of work, not a place work happens.

Law 2 — Brand becomes structure exactly where the brand is the operating unit. BND1 and BND2 exist to run brands, so inside them the brand is the Folder: ACDC Express, The Local Farmer, Papa Pasta. Everywhere else the brand is a field and a filter, used where per-brand work genuinely separates: client SLA folders in FIN, brand campaign folders in MKT, brand hiring folders in HRM. Structure follows the work, not an org chart drawn twice.

Law 3 — One list per work format, and the format is always one of six. Briefed-In (inbound work, the only list the brief form writes to), Daily (repeating control work), Recurring (weekly/monthly/quarterly obligations), Reporting (reports produced), Projects / Ad-hoc (bounded change work), Meeting Action (commitments made in a room). No seventh format. No General — that word is why BU 1 has two of them.

Law 4 — Every field that decides something is a field, not a sentence. SLA state, approval state, brand, department, store, requester, due date, response deadline. Prose stays in the description; judgement lives in fields, because a field can be filtered, counted, coloured and breached.

Law 5 — Nothing is designed that the plan cannot run. Each section carries its plan gate. A design element that needs Business is marked Business, and stays unbuilt until the plan moves — rather than built broken and blamed on the builder.

7.2 Why brands are folders in BND1/BND2 and nowhere else

Because that is where the work already sits. BU 7's 1,448 tasks are 95.2% ACDC and carry the EXP_ name prefix; BU 8's are TLF farm operations and PPP store build. Those teams do not work "in Finance" — they work on a brand, every day, and their manager is accountable to a brand P&L.

In FIN, the brand is a property of a client SLA task, not the team's identity. A Finance clerk serves every brand. Giving Finance five brand folders would create five places to lose a task and no place to see the whole creditors run.

So: brand is a Folder where a team is organised by brand; brand is a Field where a team is organised by process. BND1 and BND2 qualify. Nobody else does.

10. SLA — the working system

10.1 What ClickUp does not have

ClickUp ships no SLA object. There is no SLA field type, no SLA report, no service-level setting. The help centre search for "SLA" returns six results, none of which is a feature, and the only official material is a marketing page describing a build-it-yourself pattern. Everything below is therefore assembled from four native parts — formula fields, automations, Time in Status and dashboards — and each part's plan gate is named, because this is exactly where Free and Core stop.

10.2 The clock model

An SLA task carries four timestamps and three derived states. Nothing is typed by a human except the tier and the resolution.

Element How it works
Start Task creation time (native date_created)
First Response Due Formula: date_created + tier response minutes
Resolution Due Formula: date_created + tier resolution minutes
First Response At Automation: stamped on the first comment by a non-requester, or on the first move out of New Brief — whichever comes first
Resolved At Automation: stamped on entering any Done-group status; cleared if the task reopens
SLA State Formula over the four above: Met · On Track · At Risk (>= 75% of window consumed) · Breached · Paused (status is Waiting or Awaiting Client) · Not SLA (no tier)
Breach Reason Dropdown F17, mandatory when SLA State = Breached
Business calendar Set deliberately. Zoho's portal currently says Sunday–Thursday, 08:00–23:55 — almost certainly an untuned default. ClickUp's SLA windows must be set to the real trading week, not inherited.

10.4 The eight automations that make it real

A01 Task created in an SLA list Set SLA State = Pending, set Escalation Owner from Space owner any (100/mo ceiling)

Volume check, because Free's ceiling is 100 actions/month across five rules. A01 and A06 alone, on 486 SLA tasks/month, is 972 actions — ten times the Free allowance. This is not a tuning problem. SLA at this company's volume starts at Business.

10.5 The SLA views

Every SLA list gets four views: SLA Board (grouped by SLA State), Breach Log (filter SLA State = Breached, sorted by Resolution Due ascending), By Brand (grouped by F01), Time in Status (native, Business). The dashboard card that matters: breaches this week by brand and by class — the number that was never available in Zoho, because there was no field to count.

  1. Every channel has a description stating purpose, owner and the Space it belongs to. The seven channels in the live workspace have description: null, which makes them decorative.

13.2 Cross-space visibility — the thing you asked for

Three mechanisms, and they are not the same thing:

  1. All Tasks (formerly "Everything") — free on every plan, spans every Space, and is where a cross-space lead works from. Custom views can be saved to it: "Everything due this week, grouped by Desk", "All SLA breaches, by brand".
  2. Workspace-level views — saved views on All Tasks, visible to the people you choose. This is the honest answer to "projects across all spaces": you do not need to be in a Space to see its work.
  3. Programme Register in GOV — one list where every cross-department programme (store openings, migrations, brand launches) has a row, with a relationship field to the department tasks doing the work. This is a register, not a duplicate: the work stays in its desk.

What money buys here is exclusion, not inclusion. Members see all public Spaces by default on every plan. Folder-level permissions start at Core; Space-level permissions and private-by-default Spaces are Enterprise.

13.3 The privacy defect to fix first

All five live Spaces are private = false. HRM → Restricted Cases, FIN → Creditors & Approvals and GOV → Decision Register are readable by anyone who joins. Before a single colleague is invited, every Space gets private = true and members are added explicitly. This is a five-minute change and a permanent risk reduction.

13.4 Guests are the cheap seats — with a trap

Guests are free and unlimited on Free Forever, but every guest gets full create/edit rights that cannot be modified. Permission-controlled guest seats begin at Core (5 included, +2 per extra paid user) and Business (10, +5). Unlimited read-only guests are available on paid plans.

Two traps worth stating now: a guest can be scoped to a single Folder, Subfolder, List or task but can never be given a Space, and anyone on your own domain cannot be a guest — @infinitybrands.co.za staff become billable limited members. Franchisees on their own domains are genuinely free; your own staff never are.
