Rebuilding Gianna's iPad

Wipe the games, put back only what teaches her something — what I can do from here, what only your hands can do, and the exact list in the order to do it.

Checked on this Mac today: no Apple Configurator, no MDM enrolment, no device backups. iPadOS 26 · Family Sharing · all purchases stay in the family account.

The straight answer

Yes, it is possible — but not by me remotely, and not in one tap. iPadOS has no way to bulk-uninstall apps from another device. Deleting them has to happen on the iPad itself, or through a school-style device manager that the iPad is enrolled in — and neither iPad is enrolled in anything today.

The good news: every app you downloaded lives in the family's purchase history forever. Deleting them costs you nothing and they can come back in seconds. What you lose is the local data inside a game — a Minecraft world, a drawing, a save file — and that is worth ten minutes of protection before you start.

The gate's call on the route: on-device rebuild with a checklist, p 0.90. Apple Configurator over USB 0.02 (it can install apps and profiles on an unsupervised iPad, but bulk removal is a managed-device feature). MDM 0.07 — the right long-term answer, but it needs the iPad erased once to enrol it.

Before you delete anything

Three checks, five minutes

  • Whose Apple Account bought the apps? On her iPad: Settings → her name at the top. If it is the family account you share, reinstall is free and instant. If a game was bought on a different account — Julie's, or an old one — it will not come back without that password. Check in the App Store: tap her photo → Apps → Purchased, and see whether the games are listed.
  • Back it up first, even though you are rebuilding. Settings → her name → iCloud → iCloud Backup → Back Up Now. This does not undo the clean-out; it protects photos and app data if something goes wrong.
  • Decide about game saves. If she has a Minecraft world or a Toca town she cares about, either leave that one app in place, or use Offload App instead of Delete App — offloading removes the app but keeps its data, and it reinstalls exactly where she left off.

The 20-minute rebuild

Do it in this order and you never touch the App Store twice for the same app.

  1. Open the app list, sorted by size. Settings → General → iPad Storage. Every app is there in one scrolling list with its size — far faster than hunting icons across six home screens. Wait for it to finish calculating.
  2. Delete everything not on the keep list below. Tap an app → Delete App → confirm. It takes about four seconds each; thirty apps is roughly two minutes of tapping. Ignore the system apps — they cannot be deleted and do not need to be.
  3. Reinstall from Purchased, not from search. App Store → her photo → Apps → Purchased (or your name, if the apps were bought on your account). Everything you have ever downloaded is there, free, including the learning apps. Install in the order of the keep table so the Home Screen builds itself in the right order.
  4. Build one Home Screen and stop. One folder called School with the six learning apps, then Camera, Photos, Books. Everything else — Settings, Files, Clock — on the second page. A seven-year-old with forty icons open is a seven-year-old who does not open the learning ones.
  5. Close the door behind you. Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy → iTunes & App Store Purchases: set Installing Apps to Don't Allow and In-app Purchases to Don't Allow. Turn on Ask to Buy in Family Sharing. With those two set, no game can reappear without you.

Apple apps to leave alone

Camera, Photos, Books, Clock, Calendar, Files, Notes, Measure, Maps, Music, FaceTime and Settings. Do not delete these: several cannot be reinstalled cleanly, and Photos is where her childhood lives. Add GarageBand and iMovie only if she actually uses them — they are 1–2 GB each and she is seven.

Delete — the default list

The principle is simpler than the list: if it is a game, or it has a feed, it goes. Games with no learning content (Roblox, Subway Surfers, Among Us, Talking Tom, Toca, cooking and makeover games), anything with a feed or an algorithm (YouTube, YouTube Kids, TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat — all of which are 13+ anyway), and the free icon-farm apps that teach tapping rather than reading.

Two exceptions worth thinking about before you tap: Minecraft is genuinely creative building, and Procreate (if it is on there) is a real drawing tool. If she uses either properly, keep it — but put it on page two, not the School folder.

If you want this to be permanent

Doing it by hand works once. Doing it properly means the iPads stop being personal devices and become managed ones — which is what every school does, and what stops a game reappearing six weeks from now.

  • What it takes: a free Apple Business Manager account (or Apple School Manager, since these are school devices), both iPads erased once and supervised, and a free MDM — Mosyle or Jamf Now (three devices free). After that the app list is a policy you set once: the iPads install what is on it and nothing else, and you can change it from this Mac without touching either device.
  • What it costs you: one evening, one erase per iPad, and every app reinstalled from the family account. Her photos come back from iCloud; game saves that are not in iCloud do not.
  • What it buys: no more "we accepted a bunch of games". Ever. And a per-app time budget that enforces itself.

I can drive that set-up from this Mac once the iPads are supervised — the profiles, the app list, the restrictions — but the erase and the USB connection are yours to do, and I will not touch either device without you saying go on a specific list.

What I am not going to do

Delete anything myself. There is no remote lane, and even if there were, a bulk delete on a child's device is exactly the kind of change that waits for your explicit go on a named list. The apps are recoverable; a deleted Minecraft world is not, and she would be the one who loses it.
