Water from air: a real machine, sold with a real exaggeration

The reel is an advert for the Altitude Water Trident T-12 by its own maker’s account. The technology is genuine engineering. The number on the box is a weather report from a hot, humid day — and Cape Town does not have many of those.

Sources linked inline. All currency conversions at USD 1 = R16.96, EUR 1 = R18.75 (13 Nov 2026). Electricity priced at the City of Cape Town 2026/27 marginal home rate.

Short answer. Legitimate product, legitimate process — an atmospheric water generator (AWG) is a dehumidifier with proper filtration. Not legitimate marketing: “completely water independent” ignores that it needs a plug, and “15 gallons a day” needs 30 °C at 80% humidity.

In Cape Town, plan on roughly 40% of the rated figure. The T-12 makes about 20–28 litres a day here, not 57. That works out near R3.70 a litre in electricity — versus R0.05 a litre from the tap. It is a good emergency and off-grid tool. It is a poor way to buy water.

1 What the reel actually is

The clip is from @altdwater on Instagram — the brand’s own account, not a customer. The caption reads “If you and your family want to be completely water independent, buy this at the link in our bio. We ship worldwide.” That is an advertisement with a sales call to action, and it is the reason the comments under it read like an audit: “how much does it consume”, “what happens when the power goes out”, “15 gals per what? Day? Month? Year?” Those are the right questions, and the ad does not answer them.

The product behind it is the Trident T-12: stainless steel, 750 W, 26.5-litre tank, MERV-13 air filter, carbon block plus calcite, ozone purge, Wi-Fi, 1-year warranty, listed at $4,299.99 (≈ R72,900) and on 6–8 weeks backorder.

2 Is the process real? Yes — this is settled engineering

Nothing exotic happens inside. A compressor cools a coil below the dew point, water condenses on it, the water is collected and pushed through carbon filtration and mineralisation. It is the same physics as the puddle under a split-unit air conditioner. What makes it a product rather than a puddle is the filtration and the ozone or UV stage that stops the tank turning into a bacteria farm.

  • There is a standard for it. ASSE 1090-2020, Performance Requirements for Drinking Water Atmospheric Water Generators, is an ANSI-accredited American national standard written specifically to test these machines. ASPE records its publication.
  • The US EPA tested one for three months. In its 2019 technical brief it found no element above drinking-water limits, no Legionella, no mycobacteria, no E. coli — but high general bacterial counts, and it recommended chlorination or ozonation plus a disinfectant residual in the tank. The ozone stage on these machines exists because of exactly that finding.
  • It has been tested on South African water quality. A 2025 peer-reviewed study in Journal of Water Supply assessed a commercial AWG’s output for drinking and irrigation suitability in South Africa; the filtered water met coliform limits.
  • The honest caveat from the literature: air quality becomes water quality. A 2023 study on industrial air pollution found nickel and dichloromethane in AWG water in polluted airsheds. Filter replacement is not optional maintenance.

So: not a scam, but an American direct-to-consumer brand selling into a market it does not service. Two claims deserve a hard look. “Completely water independent” is false — the unit draws 750 W continuously and stops when the power does. “Alkaline water for health benefits” is a calcite cartridge raising pH to about 8; there is no established health benefit, and the FDA does not permit health claims of that kind. Those are marketing, not fraud.

4 The number nobody prints: output follows the weather

Air holds water in proportion to temperature. At 30 °C and 80% humidity a cubic metre of air carries 24.2 g of water. Cape Town’s July average is 12.9 °C at 79% humidity — 8.9 g per cubic metre, roughly a third as much. The machine cannot condense water that is not in the air in front of it, so the same box makes a third of the water for the same electricity.

South Africa — already here, and cheaper than importing

SkyTap imports and supports the WaterCoolers AWG range: R15,999 for the 20 L, R24,950 for the 80 L, R395,000+ for the 1,000 L. That is R312 per litre-day on the 80 L — roughly $18, against $75 for the T-12. Import duty, freight, VAT and a local service call are already in the price. For Ricardo, this is the benchmark the American reel has to beat.

The machine never wins on cost. It wins on not needing anyone: no pipe, no tanker, no delivery, no drought restriction. If that is the goal, it is worth the money. If the goal is cheaper drinking water, a filter on the tap costs a tenth as much and does more.

8 My read

Real The process is textbook thermodynamics with a filtration stack, standardised under ASSE 1090-2020 and tested by the US EPA. Anyone calling it snake oil has not read the literature.

Oversold “Completely water independent” and “15 gallons a day” are the two claims that will disappoint a Cape Town buyer. Expect 20–28 L/day from the T-12, and expect to still be on the grid.

Overpriced $4,300 (R72,900) for 57 rated litres is $75 per litre-day. A South African-supplied 80 L unit is $18, and a Chinese 30 L unit is $11–30 before freight. You are paying for a stainless cabinet and an American address.

When to buy Off-grid site, no reliable municipal water, drought backup, or a business where water delivery is the constraint. Size from the worst month, buy the largest single unit you can afford and power — output scales faster than power draw — and put it on solar if you have it.

When not to To save money on water in Cape Town. At R0.05 a litre from the tap, an AWG is a R25,000–R90,000 appliance that turns R0.05 water into R4 water.
