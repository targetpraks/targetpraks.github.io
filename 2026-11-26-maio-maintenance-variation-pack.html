Section 01 How to read this pack

Every figure here is traceable to a document. Where the evidence stops, this pack says so instead of filling the gap.

Five kinds of material sit behind it: the signed settlement agreement of 28 April 2021 (made an order of court on 4 June 2021); the bank ledger rebuilt from the monthly Bank Zero, FNB and Discovery statements; the school and medical records from Reddam House Durbanville, Discovery Health and the treating practitioners; the attorney correspondence with Merchak Inc. and with the other side's attorneys, Alice Attorneys; and the client's own file of drafts, letters and schedules.

Amounts are in South African rand. Superscript tags in gold — E1 to E14 — point to the evidence index in Section 10. Anything marked gap is something the attorney will need that this pack does not have.

Three limits stated up front

1. The ledger ends 7 July 2026. Bank transactions from August to November 2026 have not yet been captured, so the current-month picture is built from the recurring instruments (medical aid, school, maintenance) rather than from fresh bank lines.

2. The 2023 addendum has not been located. The variation agreement signed off in 2026 refers to an addendum concluded on 7 December 2023 and to paragraph 1.11.11 of it. No copy is in the client's file. The attorney must obtain it before advising, because it governs contact and may touch maintenance.

3. This is a fact pack, not advice. It sets out the position and the instruction. It does not predict an outcome, and it should not be filed or sent to the other side without an attorney's review.

The clause the consultation turns on

Paragraph 2.7 does not require proof of changed circumstances to bring a maintenance application. That matters tactically: the case can be run on the children's actual needs and the parties' actual contributions, with the change in circumstances as reinforcement rather than as the gateway. The attorney should confirm how paragraph 2.7 interacts with section 6 of the Maintenance Act 99 of 1998 in a court that already has the order.

What the 2026 draft variation would have done — and did not

The draft is a contact agreement, not a money agreement. It would have given him a fixed 10-day term-time schedule with a structured objection process, a facilitator with binding powers, and a parenting coordinator. It expressly preserved paragraph 2 of the settlement agreement, so the R5 971,50 cash maintenance and the school and medical obligations would have continued unchanged. E3

It was not signed. The other side's attorneys wrote on 1 June 2026 that he had told their client he could not pay the children's extramural activities that month and had asked her to contribute, and that he was not assisting with school clothes and glasses — and proposed that his contact be limited to alternate weekends until his finances improve. Section 06 What has changed since 2021

Six changes, each with a date. Together they are the substance of the variation: the order was made when he had one household in Gauteng and two children. He now has two households, a wife, a baby, and a dependent father-in-law — and he moved provinces to stay in his children's lives.

1 · The children moved to Cape Town, and he followed

The settlement agreement barred their relocation outside Gauteng without his written consent (clause 1.12.6). They were enrolled at Reddam House Durbanville from December 2023, and he relocated from Gauteng to the Western Cape to remain a weekly presence rather than a holiday parent. His address on the June 2026 declaration is in Welgemoed, Bellville, and the family home during 2026 was 51 Trichardt Street, Welgemoed. E1 E2 E8

Section 08 The dispute as it stands

Two positions, on the record, three months apart.

Their side, 1 June 2026: he "is unable to pay for the children's extra mural activities this month" and is "not assisting with school clothes and glasses for his son" — therefore his contact should be limited to alternate weekends "until such time as his finances improves", saving money on accommodation to be paid to their client instead.
His side, 5 August 2026: he denies acting in bad faith; any delay in maintenance "has been occasioned as a result of financial and/or other circumstances beyond his control"; he is not liable for aftercare; their client is preferring litigation over settlement.

His own letter of 23 June 2026 states the position he wants advanced: that he carries the majority of the children's costs — the court-ordered items and those outside the order — at roughly R350 000 a year, that his maintenance obligation needs to be formally varied, and that he welcomes a full investigation of both parties' finances provided it is open on both sides. E4 E14

The shape of the argument, stated plainly

The order separates two things: a cash amount to the mother (paragraph 2.2), and the direct obligations — medical aid, prescribed medical costs, school fees, clothes, stationery, books and extramurals (paragraphs 2.1.3 and 2.1.4). He has kept paying the direct obligations, and they now exceed R300 000 a year. The application would ask the court to reduce or extinguish the cash amount while the direct obligations continue — on the basis that the children's actual needs are being met directly, that his household has acquired three new dependants, and that a new educational obligation of R110 000 a year begins in January 2027.

Section 09 The instruction

Recorded as the client's instruction, not as advice:

  1. Vary the cash maintenance in paragraph 2.2 of the settlement agreement — to nil, or to a nominal amount — with effect from a date the attorney advises.
  2. Keep the direct obligations exactly as they are: the children stay on his medical aid, he keeps paying prescribed medical costs, school fees, clothes, stationery, books and agreed extramurals. He is not asking to step away from the children's costs; he is asking to stop paying a second cash amount on top of them.
  3. Bring the application under paragraph 2.7, which permits either party to apply to the Maintenance Court for a decrease without proving changed circumstances — and, in the alternative, on the basis of changed circumstances.
  4. Deal with the 2027 facilitator cost as part of the children's actual needs, and with the aftercare charge of R27 600 as a disputed item that is not his liability.
  5. Settle if it can be settled — the preference is a signed variation, not a contested application, provided the variation deals with money and not only with contact.

Section 10 Risks and gaps the attorney must test

This section exists because a pack that only argues one way is not useful to a lawyer. These are the points the other side will press.

PointWhy it matters
Income, not expenditureThe variation will be decided on means as well as needs. The bank record shows a salary from Infinity Brands in the region of R30 000–R40 000 a month through 2025–2026, plus irregular director's fees and lump-sum transfers. A sworn income-and-expenditure affidavit, with three years of assessments and the company's financials, is required. The pack does not contain it. gap
The 51 Trichardt Street purchaseHe is living in, and in the process of acquiring, a property in Welgemoed; a structural and latent-defect process is running on it. An acquisition is an asset and will be read as capacity. The offer document in the file is unsigned and no price is recorded. The attorney needs the signed offer, the bond approval and the transfer documents. gap
New household versus new dependantsA court will distinguish between costs he chose (a new household, a new car) and dependants he is legally obliged to support. The strongest items are the new child, the wife's cover and the father-in-law's maintenance; the weakest are discretionary spending. Travel of R127 636 in 2026 sits in the ledger but is not separable from business travel on the current record. partial
Her contributionNothing in this pack establishes Ms Maio's income or her direct contribution to the children. A maintenance court will want it. Her address, employment and means are not recorded. gap
The missing addendumParagraph 1.11.11 of the 2023 addendum is preserved by the 2026 draft and may qualify the maintenance clause. Advising without it is unsafe. Request it from Alice Attorneys or from the court file. gap
Arrears cut both waysUnpaid medical-aid contributions and an escalated school account are evidence of inability to pay — and also material for the other side to argue that he does not honour the order. They should be regularised before, or as part of, the application.
Contact and money are being tradedThe other side has already linked the two, proposing to cut contact to alternate weekends for financial reasons. Any negotiation should refuse that linkage explicitly; contact is the children's right, not a counter in a money dispute.
