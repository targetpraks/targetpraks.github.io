Section 09 The dispute as it stands

Two positions, on the record, three months apart.

Their side, 1 June 2026: he "is unable to pay for the children's extra mural activities this month" and is "not assisting with school clothes and glasses for his son" — therefore his contact should be limited to alternate weekends "until such time as his finances improves", saving money on accommodation to be paid to their client instead.
His side, 5 August 2026: he denies acting in bad faith; any delay in maintenance "has been occasioned as a result of financial and/or other circumstances beyond his control"; he is not liable for aftercare; their client is preferring litigation over settlement.

His own letter of 23 June 2026 states the position he wants advanced: that he carries the majority of the children's costs — the court-ordered items and those outside the order — at roughly R350 000 a year, that his maintenance obligation needs to be formally varied, and that he welcomes a full investigation of both parties' finances provided it is open on both sides. E4 E14

The shape of the argument, stated plainly

The order separates two things: a cash amount to the mother (paragraph 2.2), and the direct obligations — medical aid, prescribed medical costs, school fees, clothes, stationery, books and extramurals (paragraphs 2.1.3 and 2.1.4). He has kept paying the direct obligations, and they now exceed R300 000 a year. The application would ask the court to reduce or extinguish the cash amount while the direct obligations continue — on the basis that the children's actual needs are being met directly, that his household has acquired three new dependants, and that a new educational obligation of R110 000 a year begins in January 2027.
