The governing number

What each panel actually puts out

Prices and specs read 27 Nov 2026, 08:20–08:35 SAST

Wattage is a marketing number: a “1200 W” panel spends most of that as heat. What reaches skin is irradiance, in mW/cm², measured at a stated distance. Every bar below is the seller’s own published figure — and the distance matters, because light falls off fast with distance.

The trap this chart exists to expose: the two cheapest-looking panels are not the weak ones, and the most expensive is not the strongest. The R16,900 VanqHeal is below the R5,169 Soleira on published irradiance while costing three times as much.

Before you click buy

The three things that would change the answer

1. You want a whole torso in one session. Then the Soleira’s 38 × 22 cm is the wrong shape, and the NeuroActive 1200 W at R9,425 is the buy — 874 mm tall, full-body coverage, on sale until 23:59 tonight. Accept the thin evidence: two reviews, no published warranty, and an irradiance figure with no distance attached. The VanqHeal XPro is the better-documented alternative at nearly double the money.

2. You want a face mask, not a panel. Different class, different money: R1,380–R4,290 for LED masks at VanqHeal, R3,397 for Suēle’s Clära (currently sold out). Say so and I’ll price that lane properly — masks are a comfort product, not a dose product.

3. You want it as a bulb for a lamp fitting. A bulb lights a room; it does not deliver a therapeutic dose at a usable distance. If that’s the actual ask, the honest answer is “don’t” — the panel is the device.

What I could not verify. NeuroActive’s irradiance distance and its warranty term — neither is published anywhere on the site. VanqHeal’s warranty length — “local warranty and support” with no number. Whether Suēle’s Red Set price survives past this week — the page says “Red Week is live” with no end date. And I did not open Suēle’s individual lab PDFs; I confirmed the per-product irradiance, EMF and spectrum reports exist and are linked from their testing page.
