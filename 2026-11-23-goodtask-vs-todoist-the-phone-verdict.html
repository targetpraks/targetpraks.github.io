GoodTask vs Todoist — Todoist wins, and it isn't close for you

On paper GoodTask is the better phone app. For your setup it's the worse decision: it would create a second task database that your agents cannot write to.

T

Keep Todoist on the phone. Add GoodTask only as a viewer for the Reminders lists.

GoodTask reads and writes Apple Reminders and Calendar in iCloud; it has no path to Todoist. Running it as your main app splits capture from where the agents actually put work.

What that means for you

Why Todoist is the answer

  • One place for capture, and the same place your agents write. No bridging, no reconciliation.
  • You already chose it deliberately on 15 October, for exactly this reason: two task apps scatter the work.
  • It survives you changing phones, and it works on any non-Apple device you pick up.
  • Free tier is enough to trial; Pro only matters past 5 projects.

Where GoodTask genuinely wins

  • The one-time price. No subscription at all for personal use.
  • Calendar-first day view — better than Todoist's for time-blocking.
  • It makes Apple Reminders usable, which matters for the shared family lists.
  • Siri-free, offline, no account, no sync service in the middle.
The one honest caveat. Your grocery, kids and personal lists live in Apple Reminders, fed by the WhatsApp capture bridge. GoodTask is a much better front-end for those than the stock app — so if you want, buy it as a viewer for that shelf only, roughly $20 once, and keep personal tasks in Todoist. Do not make it the place you capture work.
