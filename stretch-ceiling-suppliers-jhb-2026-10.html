Why the list is only three

Search Johannesburg for stretch ceilings and you get a page of ceiling contractors. Almost none of them do stretch. They do rhinolite, suspended grid, bulkhead, and rigid PVC panelling — good trades, wrong product. A PVC ceiling is a stack of boards clipped into each other; a stretch ceiling is one welded membrane pulled taut into a track. They fail differently, they look different, and they cost different money.

Eight questions before anyone measures

Send these in one email to all three and you will see the market separate in a day. The ones who answer in writing are the ones who carry a warranty they intend to honour.

  • 01

    Which membrane, and what is its fire classification — in writing?

    Ask for SANS 428 or EN 13501-1 (B-s1,d0 is the usual target for a commercial interior). A supplier who cannot name the membrane cannot warrant it.

  • 02

    How many years, and what voids the warranty?

    Get the number and the exclusions. Heat, sharp objects, and work done by anyone else above the ceiling are the usual escape hatches.

  • 03

    Do your own installers hang it, or a subcontractor?

    Stretch work is a two-person craft: hot-air tensioning into a track. A fit-out company that subs it out is charging you a margin for a phone call.

  • 04

    What drop does the track need, and what happens to my cornice?

    A perimeter track costs you 30–50 mm at the wall in the standard detail. On a low mall unit that decides the design.

  • 05

    Can the downlights, air grilles and sprinklers be re-used?

    They cut and reinforce the membrane around fittings, so the answer is usually yes — but only if the positions are marked before the membrane is made to measure.

  • 06

    Can you work after trading hours and finish in one night?

    This is the whole argument for membrane in a mall. Get it in the quote, not in a phone call — including who pays for the centre's after-hours access.

  • 07

    Is it rated for steam and grease if this is a kitchen?

    The membrane is washable and holds water, but the track and the void above it are where grease goes to live. Ask what happens at the track line after two years of cooking.

  • 08

    All-in rate per m², and the call-out cost to re-tension later?

    None of the three publishes a rate card — the whole market is quote-only. Ask for the m² rate with track, cutting, fittings and labour broken out, plus the re-tension call-out.

For the 42 m² concept store

What to send with the request

  • Plan with the ceiling height and the exact light, grille and sprinkler positions.
  • Photos of the perimeter — where the track lands, and what it has to die into.
  • The centre's after-hours access rules, and who signs for them.
  • One line of intent: matt membrane, dark, no sheen, no visible joints.
  • Your trading-hours window: this is a one-night install or it is a disruption.

What good looks like back

  • A membrane named, with its fire classification attached.
  • A track detail showing the drop at the wall — 30–50 mm is normal.
  • A m² rate with fittings and labour broken out, not a lump sum.
  • A date, in writing, for a night shift.
  • A sample you can put under the actual LED before you sign.

Matt, never gloss — a gloss membrane under grazing LED mirrors the queue and every fingerprint on it. If the ceiling has to meet joinery or a bulkhead, put Sky-Gardens and Bespoked in the same round as X-Stretch: the fit-out houses price the junction, the manufacturer prices the membrane.
