Creality Hi — software audit and print-path rebuild

18 November 2026 · Dummy 13 plate 1 · independent review by Codex CLI 0.170.0

The software is not what stopped the print — but my configuration of the print start was broken, and that is fixed.

Three defects in the G-code I built for you. The slicer, the profiles and the extruder are all clean. What remains is the filament path at the machine, and no setting will fix it.

1 · What was wrong with my configuration

I replaced the machine's own start macro with a copy that removed the hub's print mode

START_PRINT begins with Rmode_exit → BOX_ERROR_CLEAR → BOX_START_PRINT. That third command is the hub's print-lifecycle entry. My MAIO_START was a hand-built copy with every box command deleted — so the hub never left idle mode and never replenished the filament while the job ran. That is a real defect, and it is mine.

I deleted the tool-select line

OrcaSlicer's machine profile emits the initial tool on the line straight after START_PRINT — T1 for white, T3 for the silk. That single line is the initial load. I stripped it to dodge a tool-change error, which removed the load entirely. Two of the three failed jobs had no filament load in them at all.

I treated a load as one step when it is three

The hub push is only the first stage. The extruder has to grab the filament, then the nozzle has to be fed. Skipping the last two leaves the extruder gears empty — and an empty extruder gear train behaves exactly like a healthy one: the motor turns at precisely the commanded rate, the position counter climbs, and no plastic appears.

Fixed. The correct files are the untouched v5 pair — START_PRINT plus T1 (white, 215 °C) and START_PRINT plus T3 (silk, 218 °C). They have been verified line by line on the machine. MAIO_START is retired and must not be used.

2 · What the independent review found

Codex was given the live Klipper configuration, the three OrcaSlicer profiles, the sliced G-code header and the box log — 21,262 characters of findings. The parts that matter:

QuestionAnswer
Is the start macro the problem?Yes — it deletes the box print-lifecycle call and the tool select. Restore the profile contract rather than cloning the macro.
Is OrcaSlicer 2.5.1 the right slicer?Yes. It emits the correct Klipper start, relative extrusion, a real prime and the firmware end macro. Creality Print is not required, and swapping slicers would not change the outcome.
Is any profile setting producing zero plastic?No. Flow 1.0, diameter 1.75, volumetric limit 10 mm³/s, layers 0.16/0.22, temps 215/202. No all-zero extrusion setting exists in any of the three files.
Is the extruder configured wrong?No. Rotation distance 6.9, 16 microsteps, pressure advance 0.04, maximum extrude-only distance 1,000 mm.
Is the toolhead switch trustworthy?As "filament is at the toolhead", yes. As "plastic is flowing", no. Sensors _2 to _5 are mainboard pins shared with the Y and Z endstops — not four more filament checks.

3 · What the machine itself did today

Same command, all four slots, nozzle at 213 °C, toolhead switch read immediately after each load:

SlotFilamentLoadReached the toolhead
1black27.3 sno
2white26.5 sno
3teal14.3 sno
4coral silk24.6 sno

Four different slots, four different durations, not one of them delivering. The hub is running and answering — it is just not putting filament where the switch can see it. That rules out one bad spool and one bad slot, and it points at the shared path between the hub and the toolhead.

The hub cannot help diagnose this: it reports filament_detected, measuring_wheel and filament as empty values, its mode reads -1, and every spool shows remain_len: 100 because they carry no RFID tags. There is no sensor on this machine that reports filament travel.

4 · The one check that settles it

Everything else is now correct, so the next thirty seconds belong to the machine, not the software:

  1. Open the CFS and look at the spool in slot 2. Is there white filament on it, and is the end fed into the feeder — not just lying against the port?
  2. Press Load for slot 2 on the touchscreen. Watch the point where the filament enters the slot: does it physically move?
  3. If it moves, follow the tube up to the toolhead. Is the tube fully seated in the coupler at the top, with the clip closed?

Whichever of those is wrong is the whole fault. Tell me which one and I will finish the job from here — the plates, the profiles and the start sequence are ready to run.
