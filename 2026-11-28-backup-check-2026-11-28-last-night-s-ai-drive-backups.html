The finding: the guard rejects a healthy dump, every time

The constellation lane (backup.sh:81–95) pipes pg_dump into gzip, then requires three things before promoting the file: a size floor, gzip -t, and a header grep. I reproduced the exact guard expression against a dump I took by hand tonight.

Guard condition Result on a good dump Verdict
1. size ≥ 2,048 B 9,139 B PASS
2. gzip -t "$f" rc=0 PASS
3. gzip -dc "$f" | head -c 400000 | grep -q "PostgreSQL database dump" rc=141 (128+13, SIGPIPE) FAIL — 40 of 40 runs
— is the header actually in the file? grep -c → 2 matches present

Under set -euo pipefail (backup.sh:8) condition 3 can never pass: grep -q exits on the first match, the pipe closes under head, gzip -dc dies of SIGPIPE, and pipefail promotes that 141 to the whole expression. Forty consecutive runs failed 40/40; the same check written with grep -c passed 40/40. It is deterministic, not a race.

The failure message is the other half of the problem: "is 9146 bytes (floor 2048) or fails gzip -t" names two conditions the file actually passed, and never names the one it failed. Two nights were spent chasing a torn pipe and a CGROUP theory that were both wrong.
