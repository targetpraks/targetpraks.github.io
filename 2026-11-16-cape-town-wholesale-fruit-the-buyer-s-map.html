The answer

Buy at the market floor, early, with a buyer card

One place beats every other door in Cape Town for wholesale fruit: the Cape Town Market in Epping. It is the only statutory, price-forming fresh produce market in the Western Cape — 5,500 producers deliver there, 8,000 buyers shop there, and the five agencies on the floor set the price the rest of the city follows. A buyer card is free, takes ten minutes and needs only your ID and a photo.

Why it wins on price, not just on choice. The market is a commission floor: you pay the producer's price plus a regulated 12.5% commission that is already in the number you see. Nothing else in the chain adds a retail margin. Today that gap is 2× to 10× on the same fruit — Woolworths charges R210/kg for cherries the floor sells at R67/kg, and R300/kg for blueberries the floor sells at R28/kg.

The plan for this week: register the card, be on the floor at 05:00, buy cherries and lychees at the subtropical agent, take oranges by the 15 kg carton, and take the ten local fruits mostly as jumble packs.

Mechanics

How to buy on the floor

The market rewards the prepared. Ten minutes of admin, then the whole city's produce is open to you at producer prices.

  1. Get the buyer card at the Buyer Information Centre on the trading floor — Mon–Fri 05:00–10:00, Sat 05:00–08:00. Bring your ID, passport or driver's licence. A photo is taken on the spot. First card is free; a replacement is R20.
  2. Choose your payment lane before you buy: cash at the market banks, a prepaid EFT account, or in-house credit through Epping Finance. Cash is simplest for a first visit.
  3. Walk the floor, then buy. Produce is displayed per agency. Salesmen answer on source, quality, price and age — ask all four. You may buy from any agency, and you should compare across them before committing.
  4. Pay, take the docket, load. The salesman books the sale on your card, you pay at the bank, you get a removal docket and a receipt, then you collect from the agency and load at the platform. Porters are independent — agree the fee first.
  5. Learn two words: single layer and jumble. Single layer and punnet lines are premium, graded and priced accordingly. Jumble packs are the same fruit with cosmetic variation — R3–R9/kg against R16–R32/kg. Buy jumble for juice, jam, drying, buffets and family eating.
  6. Grade codes matter. 1L, 1M and 1S are size grades within class 1. Ask for 1M for all-round eating, 1L for display and gifting, 1S for lunchboxes and processing.
  7. Time it. Be there at 05:00 for the best selection; the floor thins by 08:00. Late sales run 10:30–15:00 (to 14:00 on Friday) and clear leftovers cheaper — good for bulk jam fruit, risky for premium cherries.
  8. Bring the kit. Crates or boxes, a bakkie or a boot with a blanket, cash, and a cooler box for berries and cherries. Nothing on the floor is packed for a hatchback.
  9. Phone ahead for volume. For a pallet, a bin or a specific grade, call the agency the day before and ask them to hold it. The floor moves fast and the best lots are gone by 06:30.
  10. Check before you pay. Cherries: green stems, firm skin, no weeping. Lychees: pink-red shells, green stem end, no ferment smell. Mangoes: floral smell at the nose, slight give. Oranges: heavy for their size. Berries: dry, no mould at the bottom of the punnet.
The commission, in plain terms. The market is regulated at 12.5% on all produce except potatoes. That commission is charged to the producer, not added to your price — the number you negotiate is the number you pay. Cold rooms hold 800 pallets and the banana rooms ripen 55,000 cartons at a time, so you can buy green and ripen to your own schedule.
