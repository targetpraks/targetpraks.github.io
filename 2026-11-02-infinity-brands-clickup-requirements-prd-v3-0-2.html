Summary

Detail

34. Teams: sub-teams or the larger teams

You asked whether teams should follow the smaller sub-teams or the larger teams. The platform answer decides it for you before any preference does.

Business allows ten Teams. Your functional structure is already nine Spaces plus EXCO: ten. So on Business the Teams feature is exactly full, with no room for a sub-team layer, and no Team-based sharing at all — sharing items with Teams is Business Plus and above, and sharing whole Spaces with Teams is Enterprise and Max only.

Option What it means operationally Teams cost Plan needed Verdict
T1 — nine function Teams plus EXCO One Team per Space. Dashboards and workload group by Team. Permissions still granted per person. 10 of 10 used Business Recommended on Business
T2 — sub-teams of 2–4 people per function Closer to how the work actually splits: finance daily controls, creative production, store ops. Permission sharing then becomes possible. 18–24 Teams Business Plus Recommended if you want sub-teams at all
T3 — no Teams, Spaces only Simplest. Reporting groups by Space and by custom field. Loses the org chart and team activity views. 0 any paid Fallback

Ruling (proposed): the larger team is the permission boundary; the smaller sub-team is a Team object used for reporting, the org chart and workload — never for permission. That gives you both names without asking ClickUp to share by Team.

[!note] The one number to hold in your head Business = 10 Teams, and you need 10 just for the functions. Any sub-team layer is a Business Plus decision, not a naming decision.

35. Brand axis: workspaces, Spaces and folders

35.1 Why "a workspace per brand" is a money decision, not a layout decision

ClickUp bills per Workspace. Every Workspace carries its own seat count, even for the same person. The brands also do not talk to each other inside ClickUp: no cross-workspace reporting, no cross-workspace task links, and moving data between Workspaces is a ClickUp-operated migration with hours of downtime in a fixed Thursday window that permanently archives the source.

Arithmetic on the 40-human planning scenario, Business at $17 user/mo billed yearly. A seat-instance is one person holding one seat in one Workspace.

Brand layout Workspaces Seat-instances Annual, Business
One workspace, brand as a field and optional Folder 1 40 $8,160
Five workspaces, every brand-seat filled by 40 people 5 200 $40,800
Five workspaces, branded teams only (say 8 people per brand plus 15 leaders in each) 5 55–60 $11,220 – $12,240
Five workspaces on Core instead 5 200 $21,600

Add the reporting cost that no invoice shows: a company OKR dashboard cannot span five Workspaces, so your R07 company dashboard would need five separate versions stitched together by hand.

Ruling (proposed): one Workspace. A brand is a required Brand field everywhere, and a private Folder inside a Space only where the brand needs its own container — an external audience, a franchisee, a legally separate entity, or a different billing relationship. Split into separate Workspaces only for a brand that must be isolated in law or in money, and only with the arithmetic above signed off.

36. Goals and OKRs: company, quarter, team, person

ClickUp Goals are a separate object from tasks. A Goal holds measurable Targets, and a Target is one of four types: a Number, a True/False checkbox, a Currency amount, or a Task (which tracks a task, subtask or whole List). Goals exist on every plan; goal Folders — the thing that groups a year or a quarter — need Business or above. That single fact decides the shape.

The cascade.

Level ClickUp object Example Owner
Company goal, annual Goal inside Goal Folder 2027 Grow group revenue to R X Ricardo
Company key result Target of type Currency or Number Group revenue R X by 31 Dec CFO
Quarterly goal, per team Goal inside Goal Folder 2027 Q1, named <Team> — Q1 Finance closes books by day 5 Function owner
Team key result Number or Task Target 3 of 3 months closed by day 5 Function owner
Individual contribution Task Target on that person's own task, plus the Goal link Month-end close task attached to the Q1 goal The person

Why this order. ClickUp has no native "quarter" field and no native per-person goal assignment. The quarter is a Goal Folder plus a naming rule; the person is reached through a Task Target on real work, which is also the only version that cannot be gamed by ticking a box.

37. Meetings: the Monday standard and the years archive

You asked the right question — own folder or own Space — and the answer is a Space, for a reason that is about permissions rather than tidiness.

Why a Space. Meeting minutes are the most sensitive routine documents you hold. EXCO minutes, a disciplinary conversation, a client escalation: different audiences. A Space is the only real permission boundary in ClickUp; a Folder inside a shared Space inherits too much. One Space, 11 Meetings & Minutes, private, with a Folder per team and a Doc per year inside each Folder, gives you one archive that is browsable, searchable and correctly closed.

Element Standard
Space 11 Meetings & Minutes, private
Folder per team EXCO, MANCO, Finance, Marketing, Brand Ops, Tech, Sales, Client Delivery, Store Ops
Doc per team per year Monday — Finance — 2027 — one Doc, a page per week
Page name W01 · 2027-01-04 · Monday (week number, date, meeting name)
Template Agenda → decisions → actions → owners → risks → links. Actions become real tasks in the owning Space, not text in a Doc.
Capture AI Notetaker joins the calendar meeting, records, transcribes, and writes the notes Doc; the human fills decisions and actions
Findability Docs Hub search, #W07 page anchors, and a Meeting tag per Doc
Retention Years of history stay as pages in one Doc per year — no yearly migration, no folder sprawl

The mechanics that make it actually automatic. AI Notetaker works on SyncUps and on Zoom, Teams or Google Meet meetings once the Google or Outlook calendar is connected. Three notetaker types exist: Personal (only you see the notes), Shared (attendees see them), and Workspace (everyone sees them). Admins pick the default and can lock it. It writes a Doc, and a Doc can live as a view in a named hierarchy location — that is what lets each team's notes land in its own Folder without a human filing them.

Where it does not work, stated plainly. In-person meetings with no video link capture nothing. Breakout rooms are not supported. A meeting that moves after it starts yields notes for the original slot only.

The Monday format, per team. Fifteen minutes, same order every week: last week's actions, this week's numbers against target, blockers, decisions needed, then the Goal check. The Doc template enforces the order; the dashboard card (§39) supplies the numbers so nobody reads them aloud.

38. Chat: replacing Cliq

Chat is real and included: Core and above give unlimited messages and unlimited history, Channels and direct messages, Posts, SyncUps for face-to-face, task creation straight from a message, Chat automations, and AI summaries. Your team would move from Cliq without buying anything extra.

Four facts decide how far you can take it.

Fact Consequence for replacing Cliq
No Cliq importer; only Slack has one Cliq history stays in Cliq. Export what you need for legal and HR before anything is switched off.
Data retention controls are Enterprise and Max only On Business you cannot enforce a retention policy inside Chat. If retention is a compliance requirement, that is a plan decision, not a setting.
Guests can chat only inside Channels they are invited to, and cannot create Channels Client Channels are possible, but the client must be invited to each one.
Free plan caps Chat at 1,000 messages and 90 days Irrelevant here — the build is on a paid plan. Recorded so nobody tests on Free and gets a false answer.

Ruling (proposed): pilot Chat with two groups — EXCO plus one delivery team — for one operating month, in parallel with Cliq. Move a channel only when its work already lives in ClickUp, because the value is the task created from the message, not the message. Cliq goes read-only only after the export is signed off, and nobody announces a cut-over before the pilot passes.

[!note] The honest risk Chat that replaces Cliq is a behaviour change, not a feature. The plan's success measure is not "messages sent" but "tasks created from Chat messages" — that is the only number that proves work moved.

39. Dashboards: EXCO, MANCO, team, company OKR

Four dashboard tiers, because four audiences ask four different questions. Dashboards are unlimited on Business, and the cards are free — the constraint that bites is scheduled reports: 40 sends in total per Workspace on Business, and those uses never reset. So the design is "they open the dashboard", not "we email it".

Dashboard Audience Question it answers Cards
EXCO — Group Exco, 1 view each Are we winning the year? Company Goals card · Revenue vs plan · Store count and openings · SCI average · Top 10 open risks · SLA breaches this week · Brand revenue split
MANCO — Operations Manco Is delivery healthy this week? Workload by Team · Overdue by function · Recurrence health (missed obligations) · Client SLA counters · WIP over limit · Blocked items by owner
TEAM — <team> One per Team, 9 of them What do I do this week? My open work · This week's due · Blocked · Waiting on someone else · Team Goal progress
OKR — Company Everyone How is the quarter going? Goals card by brand · Quarterly targets on/off track · Department goal list · Key targets table

Design rules that keep these honest.

  • One card, one number, one owner. If nobody would act on a card, it comes off.
  • Every dashboard filters to the same fiscal calendar, defined once.
  • The team dashboards are duplicated from a master template so a new team costs minutes, not a rebuild.
  • Scheduled reports are rationed: 40 for the whole Workspace. Reserve them for EXCO only, monthly, and count them.
  • A dashboard card must be reproducible: the same card, opened by two people, shows the same number.

40. Work type: recurring, once off, requested

You asked for a folder or a Space per work type. Do not build it that way, and the reason is mechanical: a task has one home in the hierarchy. If recurring work lives in a Work-Type folder, then the Finance recurring task sits in Recurring and leaves 03 Finance, and every function dashboard and permission rule silently loses it. The work type is a property of the task, not a place.

Need you expressed Built as
See all recurring work for a team Required field Work Type = Recurring plus a Recurring — Board view inside every Space
See all once-off work Same field, Once off — List view, grouped by owner
Catch requests A real intake List per Space, 00 Requests, fed by that function's Form — this is the one place a container is correct, because a request is not yet work
Report across the company One Everything view at Workspace level filtering Work Type, which no folder structure can give you

Standard view set, identical in every Space: 00 Requests · 01 This week · Recurring — Board · Once off — List · Everything. Five views, same names, same order, everywhere. Predictability is the feature.

Field Type Values Required
Work Type Dropdown Recurring · Once off · Requested Yes, except Requested forms where it is set by the form

41. Client SLA spaces

Clients get a container of their own, and the container is a Folder inside the delivering Space — not a Space, and not a Workspace. A client Folder can be shared with a guest while the Space it sits in stays private, which is exactly the boundary you want: the client sees their work, never the company's.

Element Standard
Container Client — <CODE> Folder inside the delivering Space, privacy set to the account team
Lists Deliverables · Approvals · Issues · Reports
Fields Service Tier (Gold/Black/Silver) · Response Due · Resolution Due · First Response At · SLA Status (formula) · Client Contact
Clocks Automation stamps Response Due from Service Tier and Priority at creation; the breach is a computed field, not a human's memory
Guest access Permission-controlled guest on the client Folder only: view, comment, or edit per the contract — never the Space
Reporting One Client SLA dashboard filtered by Tier; the EXCO card counts breaches only

SLA field arithmetic. Response Due = created + tier allowance. SLA Status = a formula over First Response At and Response Due. Two limits from the platform, both verified: a formula cannot reference text fields, and a TODAY() formula cannot be sorted, filtered or grouped and cannot feed a calculated column. So the clock uses date fields, not text, and the breach state is stored, not derived from today's date at read time.

Guest arithmetic you will feel. View-only guests are free and unlimited. Permission-controlled guests cost the same as a member seat once you exceed the allowance that comes with your plan. Same-domain and SSO accounts cannot be guests at all — an Infinity Brands employee with an @infinitybrands address is a member, always. So the client guest count is a real cost line: budget it per client, not per project.

42. Replacing Coda

Coda is a document-and-database hybrid: tables that reference each other, formula columns across rows, buttons that run actions, and Packs that talk to outside services. ClickUp covers most of that list, and fails in four specific places. Map the functions, keep the honest gaps.

Coda function ClickUp equivalent Verdict
Structured table List with Custom Fields Works
Relational table (linked records) Relationship fields between Lists Works, manual
Formula column Formula Field Works, with the four gaps below
Button that runs an action Automation with a manual or button trigger Works, verify the trigger on our plan
Scheduled automation Time-based Automation Works, quota-bound
Document with embedded live table Doc with embedded List view Works
Cross-row rollup (sum of a linked column) Not supported in Formula Fields Gap
Nested formula three levels deep Nesting limited to one layer Gap
Formula inside a template Formula Fields are not compatible with templates Gap
Pack / external API call Webhook, integration, or the agent API Works, needs a builder
Public share of a page Public Doc view Works

The four gaps are real, and they change one habit. In Coda a column can sum its children. In ClickUp it cannot, so any rollup you rely on today must become either a Dashboard calculation card, an automation that writes a number field, or an agent that computes it nightly and writes back. Decide per rollup which of those three it becomes before the Coda form is retired, or the number quietly disappears.

Retirement order. Inventory every Coda doc and its functions → map each to the table above → build the mapped replacement → run both for one cycle → export the Coda doc to PDF or Markdown for the record → then freeze, and only then retire. No Coda doc is deleted, ever; freezing is enough.
