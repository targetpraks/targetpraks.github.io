One thing first. You said “Pole”, and I have read it as Paarl — the Cape Winelands town, where the R3.4m-plus estate stock lives. If you meant Parow, it is not missing: it sits in the Canal Walk chapter as the value node twelve minutes from the mall, and it happens to be the deal that wins this whole exercise. Either way you have the numbers.

Two rules I did not bend. Every price, levy and rate below comes off the listing page itself, and every rent is the lower end of what comparable units in the same suburb are actually advertised for — not what the seller hopes. And where a number does not exist, it says so rather than getting a plausible guess.

01 The hurdle has not moved

The Reserve Bank lifted the repo rate 25 basis points to 7.25% on 23 September, so prime is 10.75% and the RSA Retail Savings Bond pays 9.00% fixed for five years, government-guaranteed. After 41% tax — the bracket for taxable income of R887 001 to R1 878 600 — that is 5.31%. After Stats SA inflation of 3.6% it is 1.71% of real purchasing power.

So 5.31% is the number. And to be fairer to property than the last brief was, I have taxed the rent too: the yield column on the right of every table is what is left after SARS takes 41% of the net rent. That is the honest comparison — cash against property, both after tax. If you gear the purchase, the bond interest offsets the rental income and these after-tax figures improve; this document assumes you buy for cash.

02 Canal Walk: the mall is not where the value is

Century City has one developer with stock left — The Bridges, on the old Ratanga Junction land. Apartments are over 95% sold; townhouses start at R6.7m and penthouses at R7.2m, with Property24 listing what is left at R6.0m to R8.95m. The apartment market around it is resale, and it is priced like a CBD: R51 875/m² for a 48 m² one-bed, R79 138/m² for a 29 m² off-plan studio at Skywater. At those prices the rent does not come close to covering the cost of the money.

Walk out of the ring and the same city gets cheap fast. Parow is twelve minutes up the N1: R12 887/m², a two-bed for R799 000 with no transfer duty, and the strongest cash return in this document. Milnerton sits between the two and prices at R18 895/m².

What the developers are actually selling

  • The Bridges, Century City — three eight-storey buildings on Ratanga Park, apartments over 95% sold, townhouses from R6.7m, penthouses from R7.2m. Good land, mature node, priced for it.
  • Skywater — studios and one-beds, completion 2027, R79 138/m² on the 29 m² unit, levy a modest R711 but rates R1 365. Off-plan means you pay 2027 prices in 2026 and wait for the transfer.
  • Club Mykonos lists two-week co-ownership sequences from R25 000 beside full-title apartments from R1.4m. Two completely different products in one search result — do not let an agent blur them.

03 Paarl: the best yields in the document, and the market knows it

Paarl is where the arithmetic turns. Average municipal valuations across Paarl and Wellington rose 50–60% in five years (Drakenstein rates notice), and Paarl turned over R2.31 billion in the first half of 2026 with the average sale up 22% to just over R3.8m. Prices have run; rents have not run as fast; and a two-bed at R750 000 against R11 000 a month is the result.

That is also why two of the three Paarl units I costed went under offer while I was writing this. The market is not stupid about a 10% net yield. The available stock sits at 6.0–6.7% net, which still beats the bank after tax once you add any growth at all.

Val de Vie: a different product

Polo Pad, 80 m², R4.75m works out at R59 375/m² with a R3 932 monthly levy, and Vivante Village, 2-bed, 71 m², R4.8m at R67 606/m². There is no rental market deep enough to price these as investments; they are bought for the life, the security and the golf. Nothing wrong with that — just do not book it as a yield purchase. Retirement stock inside the estate (R1.76m–R2.2m, 60-plus only) is cheaper per metre and carries its own resale restrictions.

04 West Coast: a lovely place to own, a poor place to yield

Langebaan is the strongest West Coast market on the evidence: Cape Coastal Homes counts R574m of residential sales in the first half of 2026, sectional-title prices up 6.9% from 2024 to mid-2026, own-title up 27.5%, and an average sectional-title sale of R1 260 875. Yzerfontein carries the highest average transaction on the coast at R3.1m.

But the new stock is priced off the holiday dream, not the rent roll. A 34 m² one-bed in Middedorp at R1 699 000 is R49 971/m² — 35% above Langebaan's own average sale price per unit, for a third of the space. Against R9 000 a month long-let that is 2.5% net. Paternoster is worse on liquidity: new two-beds from R3 395 000 and not a single long-let listing in the town on the day I checked.

And the holiday-let version

Holiday letting does not rescue it. AirROI tracks every listing: the average Langebaan property billed R2 591 a night, was occupied 33% of available nights, and grossed R190 576 for the year — before platform commission, management, cleaning, linen, electricity, levies and rates. Net that properly and you land around 3%, on a property you paid a 35% premium per square metre to own.

05 East Coast: better letting, worse cost base

KwaZulu-Natal's North Coast out-earns the West Coast on holiday letting — Ballito averages R3 119 a night at 34% occupancy, Umdloti R2 617 at 40% — and the entry price per metre is lower: R21 282 at The Lofts, R25 177 at Ballito Village. The problem is running costs. Umdloti's beachfront blocks charge R5 262 levies and R3 763 rates a month on a 147 m² three-bed; R108 300 a year before a tenant arrives, against R22 000 a month rent. That is a property that costs you money to hold.

The South Coast is the other direction: a 47 m² one-bed at Shelly Beach for R550 000, and a yield that looks fine until you notice the levy is 2.5% of the purchase price every year and Margate's holiday market runs at 26% occupancy for R101 071 a year. Stock on that coast fell from 16 142 listings to 12 259 — a market that is shrinking, not maturing.

Read the low confidence on the beach question honestly: the gate is not certain, it is split. It prefers doing nothing yet at 0.55 against Langebaan at 0.29 — a margin small enough that a genuine family wish to use the place would legitimately overturn it. What it will not support is buying Paternoster (0.01) or the South Coast (0.05) as investments.

What I would actually do

  1. Take the Parow flat seriously at R799 000. It is the only deal here that beats the bank on cash alone. Before you offer: the body corporate's last three years of accounts, the levy history, and whether the R1 988 includes a special levy already running.
  2. Put the next slice into Paarl, not the coast. R1.35m for 74 m² at 6.7% net with a town that turned over R2.31bn in six months is a different risk class from a 34 m² holiday flat at R49 971/m².
  3. Leave the beach until the numbers or the use case change. If the family will genuinely spend December there, Langebaan — a house in the Country Estate or Calypso Beach, not a 34 m² new-build — is the only West Coast version that pays. Paternoster and the South Coast are emotional purchases; buy them with money you have already decided not to grow.
  4. Do not buy off-plan at R79 138/m². Skywater is a fine product at the wrong price. Off-plan earns its premium when you are first into a node, not last into a mature one.
  5. Check the levy before the view. Every bad deal in this document is a levy story: 3.0% of price at Parow, 2.5% at Shelly Beach, R5 262 a month at Umdloti. A levy above 2% of the purchase price a year needs a written explanation or a discount.
The four ways this gets you killed Co-ownership sold as ownership (two-week sequences from R25 000 at Club Mykonos); special levies in older blocks with no reserves; a resort or estate that bans short-letting after you have bought for the short-let; and any coastal purchase where the only exit is another holiday buyer.

08 Method, and what I did not find

Rent is the lower quartile of live long-let listings in the same suburb for the same bedroom count, read on 19 November 2026. Costs applied to every long-let: 5% void, managing agent at 10% plus VAT, insurance at 0.45% of value a year, maintenance at 0.75%, plus the actual levies and rates from the listing. Holiday letting uses AirROI's 12-month market average per listing, less 15% platform commission, 20% management, R450 a cleaning per turnover, R12 000 a year linen and consumables, and the same levies, rates, insurance and maintenance.

Two gaps, stated rather than filled. There is no reliable suburb-level capital-growth series for Paarl that I could verify in this pass — the growth case rests on the transaction values and municipal valuations cited above. And the Club Mykonos levy is not published on the listing; it is the first thing to ask the agent, because a resort levy can be double a normal block's.
