1. What the fleet changed

  • Competition is real. v2 said Durbanville had no large ticketed play centre. Wrong. Ipic Play — a big indoor adventure park on Aurora St — is the dominant incumbent, 4.4★ from 1,614 reviews. The white space is narrower and more specific.
  • Rent must go up. Genuine 800–1,000 m² in these nodes asks R64–R125/m². My R48,000 rent was fiction; realistic is R52,000–R73,000.
  • Equipment cost is validated. Twelve China suppliers confirmed our ~US$230/m² junior frame sits inside published bands, so the R2.56m landed figure holds.
  • Pricing was too cheap. Ipic charges R179/1 hr and R249/2 hr for juniors. Our "luxury" pricing was below the incumbent — now corrected upward.

3. The real white space

  1. A restaurant-grade café inside a play venue. Every play operator treats food as bolt‑on; every food operator treats play as bolt‑on. Nobody pairs a R95-cover café with ticketed play under one premium roof.
  2. A calm, design-led, parent-first environment. The top option is an adventure warehouse — loud and crowded at peak. No quiet or sensory space, no luxury aesthetic.
  3. Seven-day, all-weather premium play. Oatlands is outdoor and closed Mon–Tue; Kids Factory trades Fri–Sun; Bugz is outdoor. Cape winter demand goes unserved.
  4. Transparent, predictable pricing. Ipic stacks socks and harness on top of a premium ticket. Clean all-in pricing is unclaimed.
  5. A destination birthday tier at ~R380/child. Incumbents cluster at R100–R350/child or deal-site bundles. A styled, hosted, exclusive party defines a tier nobody occupies.

9. Risks & what could kill it

  • Ipic is entrenched. 4.4★ and 1,614 reviews buys trust a newcomer must earn. Price under it, but win on calm, café and parties — not on being cheaper.
  • Zoning and ceiling. The affordable sites are industrial: public play needs consent use, and a three‑level frame needs ≥5 m clear. If neither holds, rent rises to R100k+ in Durbanville retail.
  • Weekday footfall. Papachinos solves this with daily specials; Blasters copied it. Copy it or the weekdays bleed.
  • Import execution. 45–70 day lead times, 20% duty, freight and install risk. A late shipment burns working capital.
  • Premium price resistance. R290 all‑day is a real number. If Durbanville balks, revenue slides while capex stays at R3.8m.
