01What changed, and why it matters

The first edition priced everything off Rowmat's Greek retail price of R541 a square metre and used 50 bricks to the square metre for a 230 mm wall. Both were wrong for this decision. Rowmat is the reference product — the proof the material works — not the thing being sold. And 50 bricks is a single skin; a 230 mm wall is two leaves, so 100 bricks.

One percent of that is 73,000 m² a year: about R12.4m of revenue at R170, and roughly R3.7m of gross margin at landed cost. Three percent supports a small factory. Demand is not the problem; distribution and certification are.

Sanity check: 260,000 bathroom renovations is 1.5% of about 17.5 million households, and 60,000 backyard rooms a year is modest against the 919,000 households already in backyard units. Both are estimates and belong in a paid survey before any capital.

The core alone costs more than the finished import. A coating line buying local foam would need at least 40% off list before it could compete, and would still carry the certification.

Where local making does work. Thick boards, since freight climbs from R23/m² at 20 mm to R35 at 30 mm; long lengths up to 8 m; cut-to-size and next-week service. Only past roughly 50,000 m² a year, or if you extrude your own foam.

08Gates and risks

  • Fire class E closes partitions, multi-storey and most commercial compartments. Any line aimed there needs a non-combustible sister board.
  • No certificate, no external wall. European approvals do not transfer to South Africa.
  • Waterproofing lives in the joints. One leaking shower at a reference site costs more than a year of marketing.
  • The trade is loyal to brick, plaster and Gyproc. Training wins the tiler, not price.
  • Currency and lead time: dollar cost, rand revenue, six to eight weeks on the water.
  • Thin files on demand. The segment volumes are estimates; a paid survey is the cheapest de-risking step available.
