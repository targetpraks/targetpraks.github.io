Two dishes, two fires

Espetada — fast and high

  • Cubes of beef or pork, salted, sometimes bay leaf and garlic, on flat skewers.
  • Cooked high over grey embers with no flame, turned every couple of minutes — 10 to 15 minutes in total.
  • On a five-skewer frame: the top height setting. On a rack: as close to the coals as the rack allows.

Souvlaki and souvla — slower and lower

  • Bigger pieces — lamb shoulder or leg, chicken thigh, pork neck — marinated in oregano, lemon, olive oil and garlic.
  • Cooked further from the coals, for longer — 35 to 60 minutes — basted, until the fat runs clear.
  • On a five-skewer frame: the low height setting, with a smaller fire underneath.

Fuel

  • Hardwood — kameeldoring or rooikrans — gives a deep coal bed and the smoke flavour people mean by "braai".
  • Charcoal holds a steadier heat, which suits the longer souvla cook. Half and half works well for both dishes at once.
  • Wait for grey coals. Flames blacken a skewer and taste of soot.
  • Bank the coals to one side so there is a hot zone and a gentle zone on the same fire.

Before you order, measure three things

  • Width of the braai opening, between the sides — a frame wants 750 mm to 1300 mm.
  • Depth — 550 mm from the front edge back.
  • Clearance above — about 200 mm, because skewer handles and the motor sit proud of the frame.
  • If the braai is a kettle or a small portable, take the portable souvla machine (option 2) instead.
