What bolts onto the Hi

Everything here is a free model, sized and checked against this exact printer. Sliced on the same quality profile the toy plates use, so the times are real hours, not the author's guess.

Only two of these are being printed. The catalogue stays for reference, but the kit is the scraper with tool holder and the litter tray — 2 beds, 9 h, 250 g. Three of the parts below were containers doing one job and the purge chute only funnels into a bin the Hi already feeds, so the other three were cut. The layouts and the reasoning are on the print map.
Spacing, fixed and measured. The first Donnie plate welded because the generator's 14 mm gap only nudged the parts after packing them edge to edge — every plate measured 0.00 mm. The gap is now inside the footprint, verified at 13.9 mm, and every plate carries one fixed 6 mm brim. Gianna's five toys no longer fit on one bed as a result: she has two.

Keep the poop where you can see it

The printer purges a blob every time it changes colour — and on a one-colour job it still purges at the start. That blob is what ends up on the floor, on the bed and inside the machine. The Hi already ships a small purge deflector in its internal memory (the Poop Bucket's author says it works fine); what it does not ship is a bin worth the name.

Why the last plate failed. Two separate faults, both mine: the parts sat 4 mm apart while each carried a 5 mm brim, so the brims touched and the creeper welded itself to the cube. And the llama — four thin legs, a heavy body — came off the plate. The fix is spacing of at least 12 mm between brims and a brim on the tall, narrow part only.
