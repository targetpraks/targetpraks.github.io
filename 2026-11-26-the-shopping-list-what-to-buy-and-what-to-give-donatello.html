04 If you still want a bottle: the homeopathic route, honestly

This is the one shelf where a real, named, published source exists — so here it is, with exactly what it says and exactly what it doesn't.

What the British Homeopathic Association actually published

"A Child's Periodic Table" by Bob Leckridge MBChB FFHom — Glasgow Homeopathic Hospital, past President of the Faculty of Homeopathy — describes eight metal remedies and the child each one fits:

  • Alumina — the quiet, biddable child who lashes out unexpectedly; cannot be hurried; fidgety hands and feet.
  • Argentum nitricum — anticipatory anxiety; shaky hands, butterflies, loose stools before an event; craves sugar.
  • Aurum — the over-responsible, ambitious child; oversensitive to contradiction; can sink into black depression.
  • Cuprum — fiercely independent, obstinate, explosive when confronted; holds breath until blue in a tantrum.
  • Ferrum — the bossy leader with a will of iron; physically robust; anxious about family members.
  • Platina — feels special and cut off; may have been flagged autistic; insists on routine and control, often over food.
  • Plumbum — compulsive order and routine; contrary; restless with poor memory and mood swings; colicky abdominal pain relieved by firm pressure.
  • Zincum — the child whose feet never stop moving; oversensitive to noise and voices; marked mood swings.

What the article does not contain: any potency, any dose, any frequency. The words "200C", "30C", "potency" and "dose" do not appear in it once. Leckridge's own instruction is that a remedy must be "prescribed correctly by a healthcare professional who has learned homeopathy".

So if you want to act on it: read the eight pictures, notice which one sounds like Donatello and which like Gianna — and take that observation to a registered homoeopath, who will choose the remedy and the potency. That is the only version of this that is sourced rather than invented. Remedies are dispensed at Homeopathy Cape Town and stocked at health shops carrying the Pegasus range, such as The Good Stuff.

05 The short never-list

  • Chelation, IV or oral, outside a hospital — three children died of it between 2003 and 2005.
  • Any Chinese formula containing cinnabar or realgar, and any "cleansing" pill from a market or an online shop.
  • Clay, zeolite, chlorella, cilantro, binders, foot patches, "TRS", "HMD".
  • A "provoked" or challenge urine metal test — it cannot measure a body's load.
  • Any dose schedule a model wrote for you, including one of mine.
