Why the system matters more than the logo

The same child, the same year, four different systems: the curriculum decides what he is taught, the examining body decides who grants a scribe, and the qualification decides what he can do at 18. The profiles below lead with that, because a school that fits him socially but cannot run the accommodation is not a fit.

02

The education systems, explained

four systems, one child

CAPS / NSC

National curriculum, National Senior Certificate

Who uses it
Public schools, most independent schools, Curro Uitzicht, Jan Kriel, Tafelberg
What he leaves with
NSC matric, university exemption
How a scribe is granted
Yes — WCED circular 0017/2016, Grades R–12: the school's SBST applies to the district committee and the accommodation is recorded on CEMIS
The catch
The accommodation route is the most formalised of the four, and the only one with a provincial circular that names dyslexia and ADHD.

IEB

Independent Examinations Board

Who uses it
Treverton College, St Andrew's College, Elkanah House, Reddam's alternative pathway
What he leaves with
IEB National Senior Certificate, Umalusi-accredited
How a scribe is granted
Yes — applied for through the school in the FET phase (Grades 10–12), with evidence of the accommodation being used earlier
The catch
Rigorous, essay-heavy, less rote. A learner who writes slowly is disadvantaged more, not less, so the paper trail from Grade 3 matters.

Cambridge

Cambridge International (CAIE)

Who uses it
Reddam House Durbanville (Primary to Grade 6, Lower Secondary in 7–9, IGCSE in 10–12, A-Levels as a 13th year)
What he leaves with
IGCSE / AS / A-Level, accepted by SA and international universities
How a scribe is granted
Yes — Cambridge publishes access arrangements for Checkpoint and IGCSE; before that the accommodation is internal to the school
The catch
At Grade 3 he would be in Cambridge Primary, not CAPS. Cambridge Primary has no external exam; the first externally moderated checkpoint is Grade 6.

DCAPS / LSEN

Adapted curriculum for higher support needs

Who uses it
The Resilience Academy LSEN stream; special schools of skills
What he leaves with
A school-based skills certificate, not a matric
How a scribe is granted
Not the point — assessment is continuous and adapted
The catch
This is the fork in the road: it is a different destination, not a different route to the same one. Only choose it if the mainstream pathway is judged unachievable.

Two practical consequences. First, a scribe is granted by a body, not by goodwill: in CAPS it is the district assessment committee via the school's SBST and it lands on CEMIS; in the IEB and Cambridge it is the school applying to the board under that board's access-arrangement rules. Second, switching systems at Grade 3 is cheap; switching at Grade 8 is not. Cambridge Primary → CAPS Grade 4 is a normal move. CAPS Grade 7 → Cambridge Lower Secondary is also done, but the literacy expectations jump.

The one-way door

The DCAPS / LSEN route at The Resilience Academy does not lead to a matric. That is not a criticism of the school — it is what that stream is for. If there is any prospect of him writing matric, the Assisted Learning (CAPS-modified) stream is the one to ask about.

Boarding schools, profiled

only four can take him at all

Boarding at nine replaces the adult scaffolding he needs at exactly the age it does the most work. If it stays on the table, the field is four schools — and only two of them board at Grade 3. The other two start at Grade 4, so they are only relevant if he moves up rather than repeats.

06

The repeat question

a placement change is not an intervention

The evidence on holding a child back is consistent enough to state: repeating a grade is associated with weaker long-term academic and social outcomes than promoting a similarly-achieving child, and the better-designed the study, the smaller any early gain becomes (Jimerson 2001; Allen, Chen, Willson and Hughes 2009). That evidence is mostly American and mostly about older children, so it does not settle a Grade 3 dyslexia decision — but it does rule out the idea that a repeat is harmless.

The intervention matters more than the placement. A repeat year that delivers the same curriculum at the same pace, plus a scribe, buys twelve months and changes nothing. A year that delivers a smaller class, explicit literacy instruction, occupational therapy for writing, typing or speech-to-text as a daily tool, and a reader for content subjects can change his trajectory. Repeating is only worth it if it comes bundled with a different way of teaching him.

He turns ten in February 2027. Repeating makes him one of the oldest in a class of nine-year-olds — usually fine socially at this age, and a one-way door: you cannot un-repeat him.

The middle path

Repeat Grade 3 at a different school. That is what Curro Uitzicht, Dunatos and The Resilience Academy offer: the extra year and the different delivery model arrive together, rather than the repeat being a holding pattern at Reddam while the gap stays the same width.

What I could not verify

what a desk cannot tell you
  • Whether any school has a 2027 place for him. Every option needs a call; availability moves weekly in Term 4.
  • Reddam's 2027 fee, and whether the 2027 learning-support teacher will be in post, full-time, or on the external-service contract the advert describes.
  • Dunatos: grade range, class size, fees and whether seniors write the NSC. The website carries no prospectus and no fee schedule.
  • The Resilience Academy: campus address, fees and grade range. It was registered in 2025 and its first academic year is 2026, so there is no track record to check.
  • St Andrew's Prep's fee schedule and its learning-support capacity — the site blocks automated access.
  • Tafelberg's and Jan Kriel's actual availability and timeline for a Grade 3 placement in 2027; both say applications cannot be made directly, so only the district can answer.
  • Whether a reader as well as a scribe is being proposed, and for which subjects. The school's email says both; the written accommodation application has not been seen.
  • Peak-hour travel times — all distances are off-peak driving estimates.

This is desk research, not an assessment of Donatello and not a recommendation about his medical care. The clinical picture is taken as the school and the June 2026 evaluation state it. The choice of school, and whether he repeats, is yours and Julie's.
