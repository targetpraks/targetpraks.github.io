02

What makes a school a fit

three tests, not a league table

Almost every school will say it "supports learners with barriers". Three questions separate the ones that can actually deliver the package from the ones that will improvise it around one willing teacher.

1

Is a reader and scribe routine, or a favour?

Ask to see the school's assessment-accommodation policy and the name of the person who runs the SBST application. In the Western Cape a scribe is not something a parent buys on the side: the school's School-Based Support Team applies to the District Assessment Accommodations Committee, and the accommodation is recorded on CEMIS against the learner. At Grade 3 the practice is meant to start in the Foundation Phase, and it is re-evaluated at the start of every phase. A school that has never run the application is starting from zero in January.

2

Who pays for and employs the facilitator?

Every school that offers in-class facilitation in this survey expects the parent to contract and pay the facilitator — Tafelberg says so explicitly, and it is the norm elsewhere. What differs is whether the school can introduce you to people it has worked with, and whether a facilitator is welcome in the classroom or treated as an intrusion.

3

Can one teacher carry him at this gap?

He reads at roughly Grade 1 efficiency (58 words per minute against a Grade 3 norm of 138, Tina Cowley, September 2026). In a class of 25 that gap gets managed; in a class of 12 or 6 it gets taught. Class size is the single most reliable proxy for whether the support is real.

Boarding schools

only three can take a Grade 3 boarder

Boarding at nine is a different decision from boarding at thirteen: it replaces the daily adult scaffolding he currently needs, at exactly the age when that scaffolding is doing the most work. If you want it on the table anyway, the field is small — most boys' prep schools only board from Grade 4, which rules them out if he repeats Grade 3.

The only full package

Treverton Preparatory School — Mooi River, KZN Midlands

Co-educational boarding from Grade 1 to 7, and the only school in this survey that pairs junior boarding with a full on-campus team: the LEAP Centre runs an educational psychologist, speech therapist, occupational therapist, learning-support staff and in-class facilitators, all on the campus but privately contracted to parents. Grade 3 boarder R214,270 for 2026; day scholar R83,920. A four-and-a-half hour drive, or a flight to Durban and 90 minutes by road.

05

The repeat question

a placement change is not an intervention

The evidence on holding a child back a year is consistent enough to be worth stating plainly: repeating a grade is associated with weaker long-term academic and social outcomes than promoting a similarly-achieving child, and the better-designed the study, the smaller any early gain becomes (Jimerson 2001; Allen, Chen, Willson and Hughes 2009). That evidence is mostly American and mostly about older children, so it does not settle a Grade 3 dyslexia decision — but it does kill the idea that a repeat is harmless.

What the evidence does support is that the intervention matters more than the placement. A repeat year that delivers the same curriculum at the same pace, plus a scribe, buys him twelve months and changes nothing. A year that delivers a smaller class, explicit literacy instruction, occupational therapy for writing, typing or speech-to-text as a daily tool, and a reader for content subjects can change his trajectory. The repeat is only worth it if it comes bundled with a different way of teaching him.

One practical consequence: he turns ten in February 2027. Repeating makes him one of the oldest in a class of nine-year-olds. Socially that is usually fine at this age, and it is a one-way door — you cannot un-repeat him.

The middle path

Repeat Grade 3 at a different school. That is what Curro Uitzicht and The Resilience Academy are for: the extra year and the different delivery model arrive together, rather than the repeat being a holding pattern at Reddam while the gap stays the same width.

What I could not verify

what a desk cannot tell you
  • Whether any school has a 2027 place for him. Every option needs a call; availability moves weekly in Term 4.
  • Reddam's 2027 fee, and whether the 2027 learning-support teacher will be in post, full-time, or on the external-service contract the advert describes.
  • Dunatos: grades offered, class size and fees — the website carries no prospectus and no fee schedule.
  • The Resilience Academy: campus address and fees. It was registered in 2025 and its first academic year is 2026, so there is no track record to check.
  • St Andrew's Prep's fee schedule and its learning-support capacity — the site blocks automated access.
  • Tafelberg's and Jan Kriel's actual availability and timeline for a Grade 3 placement in 2027; both say applications cannot be made directly, so only the district can answer.
  • Whether a reader as well as a scribe is being proposed, and for which subjects. The school's email says both; the written accommodation application has not been seen.
  • Peak-hour travel times — all distances are off-peak estimates.
