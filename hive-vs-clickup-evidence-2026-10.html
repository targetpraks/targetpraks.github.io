03The daily driver — where comments and reviews actually land

Hive has no screen called “Inbox”. That is a fair complaint, and it is worth being precise about what exists instead, because this is the surface a manager touches twenty times a day.

 Hive: the bell and the Home pageClickUp: the Inbox
Where it lives Bell icon, top right, orange dot for unread, five tabs: Me · All · Mentions · Archived · Pinned. Hive notifications Inbox in the home sidebar, four tabs (Primary · Other · Later · Cleared) plus a fifth you can add. ClickUp Inbox
Can you filter to approvals? Yes — the bell has a filter for Approvals: requests sent to you and status changes on approvals you submitted. Also Assignments, @mentions, Comments, DMs, status changes, project updates. Approvals are tasks, so they arrive as task and comment notifications; there is no approval-specific filter.
Can you reply without leaving it? No. Clicking a notification jumps you to the action card, where you comment. Yes. The Inbox carries the thread inline, with Expand previous replies and a details sidebar. You reply and clear it in the same screen.
Can you postpone something? Pin or archive. No snooze. Snooze, plus “save for later” and a Later tab that holds what is not due yet.
How does it reach a phone? Per-category channels — Banner, Email and Mobile — with push timing set to Always, Away or offline, or Offline only, plus sound alerts. Inbox on mobile, same tabs. Guests get it too.
Where do review requests land? At the top of the Home page, as an Approvals widget, and in the bell's Approvals filter. Home is a configurable dashboard: My Actions, My Projects, People, Scratch Pad, Approvals. Hive Home · approval routing In the Inbox as notifications; the review itself happens on the task attachment.
The honest verdict: Hive has a notification centre and a configurable home dashboard, not an inbox. It is enough to catch a review request — the Approvals filter and the Home widget do that — but it costs one click per item to reply, and it cannot defer. ClickUp's Inbox is the better daily driver and it wins this row outright.

What that is worth: Hive nets R46,760 a year once Filestage is cancelled. ClickUp with Filestage kept is R171,328. The better inbox costs R124,568 a year — so this is a question about your own hands, not about features. Configure Hive before judging it: pin the Approvals widget to Home, set the bell filter to Approvals + @mentions + Assignments, set push to “Away or offline”. Then spend a day in it. If the daily loop still feels like work, that is the second thing — after the PDF proof — that can flip this decision.

04What it changes for a manager

Before

  • Status is a conversation: five tabs, a WhatsApp scroll, and asking people.
  • Review means “looks good” somewhere in an email, with no date and no name.
  • Capacity is discovered after the week overruns.
  • Hours are reconstructed before invoicing, usually badly.
  • A client with one link can wander into another brand's work.

After — Hive, at R109,044

  • One portfolio screen: every project, its fields and its open actions in a single table.
  • An approval queue that ends in a decision, stamped with a name and a date, and the card moves by itself.
  • Capacity before the commitment: estimates roll into Resourcing, so the week is checked before it is promised.
  • Timesheets reviewed before invoices leave, per person, per project.
  • A client inside one space, scoped to their projects only, never the workspace.

05What it changes for the staff

Before

  • The task lives in one tool, the file in another, the hours in a spreadsheet.
  • “Have you finished?” replaces a dependency that nobody can see.
  • Comments arrive on four different channels; two get missed.
  • Mark-up happens by screenshot, in WhatsApp, on a phone.
  • The reason for the rework is never written down.

After — Hive, at R109,044

  • One card holds the task, the timer and the file — nothing is typed twice.
  • Dependencies name the next person, so chasing stops.
  • One inbox for comments, on the iPhone, with the context attached.
  • A poster is marked up where it lives, with the version it belongs to.
  • The approval is visible: what was signed off, by whom, on what date.

07What the documentation does not prove

  • ClickUp's marketing page promises “customisable approval flows” for proofing, but its help centre documents annotation and assigned comments, and reserves the formal approval workflow for timesheets. That is a demo question, not a settled fact.
  • Hive's external-user price is not published. Premium App, quote on request. Until that number exists, the client-seat cost of the multi-brand model is unknown.
  • Hive's API tier is ambiguous between the MCP blog post and the pricing page. Get the Teams-level limit in writing.
  • A client cannot see a child project in Hive even with the parent. Multi-brand structures will hit this.
  • No vendor documents the number that matters most — how long a real client round takes in their tool. One 20-page PDF, one 60-second video, one image, one approver with no account. One working day answers it.
