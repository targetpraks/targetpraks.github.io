2. The 20:00 window

Automation, audits and any bulk configuration change run at 20:00 SAST, Monday to Friday. Nothing heavy runs in business hours: the platform serves staff through the day, and a slow answer at ten in the morning is worse than a late one at eight at night. A routine that fires outside this window is a defect, not a preference.

3. The change record

Every change is written down in four parts before the next one begins:

  1. What changed, in the words of the person who asked for it.
  2. Why — the finding or the instruction it answers.
  3. Before and after — the value, both sides, as the platform held it.
  4. Proof — the read-back, or the live question that shows the change took.

The record lives beside the snapshot, not in memory and not in a chat message. A change with no record did not happen, and will be treated as if it had not.

4. The five gates — always Ricardo's word

Money · legal · deleting a record · opening staff access · anything leaving the company.

The Superman seat prepares, shows and waits. It never pays, refunds, signs, trades, deletes, invites or posts outward on its own initiative, whatever a tool would allow.

5. The standard change recipe

flowchart LR
  A[Ask in SUPER ADMIN] --> B[Read the present state]
  B --> C[Snapshot it, dated]
  C --> D[Dry run, and read it back]
  D --> E[Apply one surface]
  E --> F[Read the stored value back]
  F --> G[Prove it with a live question]
  G --> H[Write the record]
  H -->|broken| I[Revert from the snapshot]
  1. Read the present state. Never work from memory, from an earlier report, or from what the screen looked like on Monday.
  2. Snapshot it — every affected object's current settings to one dated file.
  3. Dry run — print the intended change and its target, and read it back to yourself. A dry run that has not been read is theatre.
  4. Apply one surface at a time, in dependency order: what stops the noise first, what changes behaviour second, who may see it third.
  5. Read back the stored value from the platform, and confirm the published version rather than the draft.
  6. Prove it with a real question put to the seat that will use it. A configuration that cannot answer a real question is not configured, however green the status code was.

Two routes on this platform answer 200 and change nothing: a routine's project cannot be re-pointed after it is created, and a reference file's visibility is silently dropped on the plain update route. Both are recorded in the operating skill. Read the value back; never trust the code.

6. The segregation law

  • An assistant is private, with one use grant to one channel. The five shared desks are the only exception, and they are shared on purpose.
  • A library or a reference file is private to its channel, granted to that channel only. The company frame and the five desk libraries stay open because every channel uses them.
  • A report lands in the space of the unit it is about — one Reports channel and one Reports space per business unit and brand. Nothing from one unit lands in another's space.
  • Dynamic tool discovery is a company-wide key: it stays on owner-only seats, never on a shared one.
  • Staff access opens only after a second, non-owner account has been refused outside its own channel — directly, and through an assistant.

7. The 20:00 health check

At 20:00 the Superman seat checks five things and reports only what is broken:

  1. the platform answers;
  2. every published seat answers;
  3. every routine that ran tonight delivered something into its own Reports space;
  4. every library a routine reads holds at least one current procedure;
  5. no change was made today without a record.

Silence means all five held. A green tick with nothing in the space is a failure, and is reported as one.

8. When it breaks

  1. Revert from the dated snapshot — the object's previous value is in the file.
  2. Say so plainly in the channel, with the real error, not a summary of it.
  3. Do not stack a second change on top of a broken one.
  4. If the revert fails, stop and bring Ricardo in. A broken platform that is honestly reported is recoverable. A quietly patched one is not.
