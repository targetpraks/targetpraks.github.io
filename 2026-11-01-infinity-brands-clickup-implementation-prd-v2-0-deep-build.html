This is a contract for a future build, not a description of a system that exists. There is no tenant. 1. Executive decision

Build one workspace, nine private Spaces, with functional queues and a consistent Brand field. Use existing organisational evidence to design the shape of future work. Keep each work item in one authoritative home List under its accountable delivery team. Use linked tasks for distinct cross-team deliverables and permission-aware reporting for brand visibility.

External access is always to a separately permissioned container or task. A Store field, filtered view, hidden column, dashboard or tag is never an access boundary. Shared regional queues are internal only. Every store or client with an external audience receives an isolated container, even if that means more folders.

Business is the candidate plan, not an authorised purchase. Forty paid humans is a planning scenario, not an invite list. Pricing, role eligibility, plan features and actual tenant limits must be verified and approved before purchase or invitations.

12. Recurrence, service clocks and finance completeness

All timestamps display in Africa/Johannesburg unless an approved store calendar explicitly differs. Proposed support hours are Monday–Friday, 08:00–17:00, excluding approved public holidays. This is a proposal for D09, not an assertion about contracts. Store finance uses each approved store’s trading calendar, including weekends where applicable. Do not hard-code South African holidays for stores in another country.

Proposed pilot first-response targets: urgent business outage 1 business hour; high impact 4 business hours; normal request 1 business day. Urgent notifications must use the existing agreed emergency channel; a form is not an emergency response service. Resolution dates are set at acceptance by the owner and any actual contract. Acknowledgement and resolution are different measures.

Waiting may pause a resolution clock only if approved policy allows it. Keep total elapsed age visible. First-response timing does not pause simply because a task is unassigned. Working-day calculations must be proved; if native rules cannot express the calendar, calculate via the approved scheduler or record dates manually during pilot. Never silently treat “two working days” as 48 hours.

Daily finance algorithm

  1. CFO approves a schedule register: store, control type, effective dates, expected business dates, due time/offset, preparer, verifier and evidence location.
  2. Generate one new task occurrence per obligation, not a reused task that erases yesterday’s state. Key format: STORE_ID|YYYY-MM-DD|CONTROL_TYPE.
  3. Schedule independently of completion so an overdue prior occurrence cannot prevent tomorrow’s control. If native recurrence cannot produce this reliably, use an approved idempotent scheduler; do not activate a false completeness process.
  4. At the agreed check time, compare expected keys from the schedule register against actual occurrences. Detect missing, duplicate, late, unassigned and evidence-incomplete tasks.
  5. Completion requires a result and evidence; Exception requires reason, owner and next action. Not Applicable requires authorised explanation and is separately reported.
  6. On store closure or calendar change, stop future generation from the effective date. Preserve prior records; do not regenerate legacy dates before the pilot start.

Native recurrence can create new tasks and has timing options, but those options need testing for overdue and early-close cases [V10]. Recurring-task creation may trigger other rules, so it is not “free” in the overall action budget even if the schedule itself uses no automation action.

Purpose

This pack turns the six v1.0 status templates into an executable per-workflow status contract: exact status order, status group, allowed movers, entry and exit guards, WIP limits, escalation, re-open rules and the store-opening stage model. 1. Status groups, roles and naming convention

Status groups. ClickUp provides three default status groups — Active, Done (open but not overdue; dependencies cleared), Closed (hidden in List view by default) — plus an optional fourth, Not started, enabled through a ClickApp [V: help.clickup.com 6309452618647, “Manage task statuses”]. Custom statuses can be created in every group except Closed; Closed statuses may be renamed but not added [V: same source]. DEC-03-01 enables the Not started ClickApp workspace-wide before any status template is built.

R-03-01 Role shorthand (used in every table below): TRG = triage/List owner; ASG = accountable assignee (v1.0 §9.2: exactly one); OWN = business owner of the List per v1.0 §7; APR = named approver on the task; VER = verifier/CFO delegate (finance); AGT = approved integration identity; ADM = workspace custodian.

R-03-02 Naming convention — apply literally: - Display names in Title Case; active states are verb phrases (“In Review”, “Waiting Evidence”); end states are past participles (“Verified”, “Signed Off”, “Cancelled”). - One meaning, one name across all templates: “In Progress”, “In Review”, “Cancelled” are identical labels everywhere they appear. Never introduce a synonym (“Underway”, “WIP”). - No emoji, numbering, or workflow prefix inside a status display name; the template carries identity (templates named ST-CORE, ST-SERVICE, ST-CREATIVE, ST-FINANCE, ST-DELIVERY, ST-DECISION, versioned v1.0 in the Configuration Register). - Record every status ID and group mapping per List at build time; identical labels do not imply identical IDs (v1.0 §10, V16) [V: help.clickup.com 6309465975063, “Statuses FAQ”]. - Guests never see internal-only statuses they cannot act on renamed into euphemisms; the same status model applies inside shared containers.

R-03-03 No native transition gating. ClickUp advanced permissions control who may create/edit/delete status configurations, not who may move a task between two specific statuses [D: Business Plus/Enterprise permissions per “Manage task statuses”; per-move gating not documented]. Every “allowed mover” rule below is therefore a human guard plus audit trail (status-change history in task activity), not a technical gate [U: verify per-transition enforcement options in tenant]. QA tests T47–T54 exist precisely because moves cannot be natively blocked; an automation that reverts a status is not a secure gate (v1.0 §9.4).

R-03-04 Blocked vs Scheduled. Neither is a separate status. - Scheduled [NS] is legal only when the task has a future start date with a stated calendar basis (v1.0 §12) and awaits no predecessor. In ST-FINANCE, Scheduled additionally requires an occurrence_key matching the CFO-approved schedule register (v1.0 §12 algorithm step 2); any other use of Scheduled in FINANCE is invalid. - Blocked is modelled as Waiting [A] with Waiting Reason beginning “Blocked —”, a follow-up date, a responsible person, and either a recorded blocking dependency or a cited blocker URL. An unstarted task that merely cannot start stays in its not-started status. A task with unmet dependencies and no Waiting Reason is a validation failure → Needs Correction view (v1.0 §9.4).

2. Recurrence creation rule

R-06-01 — Register first. The expected set is computed from the register for the period, independent of which tasks exist. A task list is never evidence of what was due.

R-06-02 — Generate ahead. Horizon by cadence: per-trading-day and weekly D+14, monthly D+45, quarterly and above D+120. The generator runs at 02:00 local and writes the full horizon; it never waits for a due date.

R-06-03 — New task per occurrence. Every occurrence is a new task carrying its key. Native recursion with Create new task unselected is prohibited: it recycles one task and erases the prior occurrence’s evidence, which is exactly what v1.0 §12 step 2 forbids [V: ClickUp recurring-tasks documentation].

R-06-04 — Never retro-create. No occurrence may be created for a date before the unit’s activation date, before the approved pilot start, or before the generator’s last acknowledged successful run. Recurring tasks do not create instances in the past [V], so a back-fill attempt would either fail silently or forge history. A missed window produces one GAP record per (obligation, date range) in FIN → Close & Compliance → Generation Gaps, with the count of suppressed dates. The denominator still expects them; the gap is a defect, not an excuse.

R-06-05 — Key and idempotency. F_OCCURRENCE_KEY = UNIT_ID|YYYY-MM-DD|OBLIGATION_ID|SEQ, SEQ = 1 except where cadence exceeds one per day. This supersedes the v1.0 §12 STORE_ID|YYYY-MM-DD|CONTROL_TYPE key: CONTROL_TYPE could not distinguish 25 obligations. Before create, query by key; after a timeout, read before retry; a second matching task is quarantined in Duplicate Occurrences, never deleted, and never counted twice.

R-06-06 — Native recurrence is the exception. Only C3, fixed-day, single-obligation rows may use it. Four documented behaviours disqualify it elsewhere: the monthly schedule offers first day, last day and same day, but no last-business-day rule; “skip weekends” does not know public holidays; an instance closed early recurs immediately, which manufactures a future task before the period is checked; and a recurrence created from a template cannot trigger an automation [V]. Default route is an approved idempotent scheduler writing through REST.

R-06-07 — Owner continuity. Recurring tasks keep running after their creator or assignee leaves the workspace [V]. Schedules are owned by a role, transferred at offboarding, and verified quarterly with the Recurring Task Overview.

R-06-08 — Register change. A new register version applies forward only. Unstarted future occurrences are regenerated; started occurrences are retained and marked REGISTER_SUPERSEDED. No occurrence already in progress is cancelled to tidy a report.

R-06-09 — Silent-death guard. An obligation that generates nothing for two consecutive expected periods is auto-suspended and raised as a C1 defect to the platform owner. Archived containers do not recur [V], so archiving is treated as a generation risk, not housekeeping.

3. Store Completeness Index (SCI)

The SCI replaces “daily completeness” as the single definition; v1.0 §14’s daily metric is this formula with the period set to one business date.

Let o index register rows with F_SCORE_SCOPE Store or Store-inherited, w = weight (C1 = 5, C2 = 3, C3 = 1), E_o = expected occurrences in period P, C_o = occurrences in a completed-and-verified state.

f_o  = C_o / E_o
SCI  = 100 × Σ(w_o × f_o) / Σ(w_o)      over rows where E_o > 0

Rounding: one decimal place, half away from zero, applied once to the final ratio. f_o, weights and drags are never rounded before summation. Weighting by obligation rather than by occurrence stops a 62-occurrence refrigeration log from drowning a once-a-year fire certificate.

Excluded from both numerator and denominator: rows with E_o = 0 in the period; an authorised Not Applicable (requires reason, named approver and date); occurrences superseded by a register or calendar change; synthetic pilot units; dates outside the activation-to-closure window; Score scope = Group.

Not excluded: a missing task (E_o > 0, C_o = 0) scores zero and stays; an open exception scores zero; rejected evidence scores zero; a late completion inside grace scores one and is reported separately as on_time_rate; a duplicate counts once in C_o and is reported in duplicate_rate, never in E_o.

Drag. A failure at row o removes w_o × (1 − f_o) / Σw × 100 points, so a wholly missing C1 row costs 8.33 points in a 60-point register and a single missed day of a 31-occurrence control costs 0.16.

Gate. The number alone can flatter. GATE = FAIL when any C1 row has f_o < 1.0, or any C1 evidence is rejected. Bands: Green = SCI ≥ 97.0 and gate PASS; Amber = 90.0–96.9 with gate PASS, or any SCI with gate FAIL; Red = SCI < 90.0.

Worked example, period 2027-03 (31 days, 5 Mondays, 4 Sundays). Register weights sum to 60 across 16 expected rows, 115 expected occurrences. Entity rows included; franchise rows not applicable to either store.

Store A — clean, one authorised exclusion. OB-UTIL-01 is Not Applicable: utilities are inside a gross mall lease, approved by the CFO with the lease clause referenced. Σw = 57, every f_o = 1.0, SCI = 100.0, gate PASS, Green. It is not perfect timing: 2 of 114 occurrences were completed inside grace, on_time_rate 98.2%.

Store B — three failures.

Failure Row Weight f Points lost
Fire equipment service never generated OB-FIRE-01 (C1) 5 0.000 8.33
Pest control never generated OB-PEST-01 (C2) 3 0.000 5.00
Cash-up completed 28 of 31 trading days OB-CASH-01 (C2) 3 0.903 0.48

Σw = 60; Σ(w × f) = 60 − 5 − 3 − 3 × (3/31) = 51.7097; SCI = 86.2, gate FAIL, Red. Store B is 13.8 points below Store A, and 8.3 of that is a single absent obligation. Note what the band does not say: at 86.2 the store would be Red on volume alone, but a store missing only OB-FIRE-01 would score 91.7 and still be Amber-gated, never Green. The gate, not the number, is the compliance signal.

The monthly SCI is not the average of daily SCIs: averaging daily ratios overweights quiet days and hides a missed monthly rent.

4. Daily exception sweep

Runs 07:30, 13:00 and 17:45 local, plus 06:00 on the last business day of the month. Order is fixed; a failure at any step halts the sweep and raises a sweep defect rather than producing a partial verdict.

  1. Load the register version effective today. If it differs from the version used at the previous sweep without an approved change record, abort and alert.
  2. Resolve expected units: active stores where activation ≤ today ≤ closure_effective, with country calendar.
  3. Expand expected occurrences for [today − 30, today + 1]. The 30-day lookback finds an obligation that went stale while it could still be fixed.
  4. Materialise actual occurrences by key from Finance Lists and any linked store List.
  5. Match on key and classify: complete, complete-unverified, in progress, waiting evidence, exception open, exception accepted, not applicable, missing, duplicate, superseded.
  6. Assign state. At risk = due within 24h with no assignee or no evidence started, or past due and inside grace. Breached = past due + grace without a terminal state. Missing = no task and expected_date + grace has passed.
  7. Rank: C1 breached, C1 missing, C2 breached, duplicates, C2 missing, C3 breached/missing, then oldest first.
  8. Notify with dedupe key OCCURRENCE_KEY|STAGE so one occurrence produces at most one message per stage.
  9. Write one sweep record: SWEEP|date|time|register_version|expected|materialised|missing|duplicate|breached|at_risk, appended to registers/sweep-log.csv. The sweep writes one record, never one task per exception.

Grace: C1 zero (breach at due time), C2 one business day, C3 two business days.

Ladder L1 L2 L3 L4
L-A (C1) Store manager or franchisee principal at at-risk Finance administrator at breach + 0 CFO delegate and Express Ops lead at breach + 1 BD Sponsor at breach + 2 BD
L-B (C2) Store manager at at-risk Finance administrator at breach + 0 CFO delegate at breach + 2 BD —
L-C (C3) Store manager at at-risk Weekly review list — —

Repeat breaches are counted per (unit, obligation) over a rolling 90 days: three moves the row to Watch with weekly review; five triggers a register review, because a row that fails repeatedly is usually a wrong cadence, owner or evidence class rather than a bad store; eight requires a written root cause to the CFO. 6. Evidence and edge cases

Evidence pack. Minimum two artefacts for E1–E3: the source document and the proof of submission or payment, plus F_EVIDENCE_VERIFIED_BY and F_EVIDENCE_VERIFIED_AT. Files live in the approved repository under a folder per obligation family, named OBLIGATION_ID_YYYY-MM-DD_ARTEFACT_vN, linked through Authoritative Record URL — not attached to the task, because the repository owns its own access (v1.0 §13.2).

Every occurrence task opens with a fixed five-line audit block: what was due, when it was due, who prepared it, what proves it, who verified it. Fixed order so a script can parse it and an auditor can skim it.

Audit retrieval. An auditor holding only an occurrence key must reach the task, both artefacts, the preparer, the verifier and both timestamps in under five minutes with no human contact. If any link in that chain requires asking a person, the evidence is incomplete even when the work was done.

Store opens mid-month. F_ACTIVATION_DATE = A. Occurrences are generated from the first expected date on or after A; nothing is back-filled. Rows flagged pro-rata (rent, utilities) produce exactly one occurrence for the stub period with the pro-rata invoice as evidence. The denominator uses expected occurrences, so a store open for eleven days is not scored against a full month and is not flattered by it either.

Store closes. F_CLOSURE_EFFECTIVE_DATE = C. Generation stops at C; occurrences due after C leave the denominator from the next period; occurrences on or before C must still reach terminal. One closure checklist is generated (final stock count, final cash-up, certificate return, landlord handover, final franchise remittance) and closes once. After the last occurrence is terminal the store moves to Closed in the register and its SCI freezes at the closure period. Nothing is deleted, and native recurrences are stopped explicitly — schedules outlive their creator [V].

Holiday-shifted due date. Movable rules shift back to the previous business day; shifting forward would manufacture a breach. The generator uses the country calendar at creation time and writes F_SHIFT_REASON on the occurrence. If a holiday is gazetted after generation, the unstarted occurrence is cancelled as CALENDAR_SUPERSEDED and one replacement is issued; the superseded date is excluded from both numerator and denominator so the store is penalised once, not twice. Statutory dates are not shifted by us at all — SHIFT=AUTH rows carry the resolved date from the practitioner’s calendar.

Attribution and audit. Every agent write carries a correlation comment agent:<run-id>:<rule-id> and writes one ledger row: correlation ID, actor account, approved rule, object ID, timestamp, before/after field values, result, approval reference; secrets and personal content redacted (v1.0 §16.2) [P]. REST: 100 requests per minute per token on Business [V — developer.clickup.com/docs/rate-limits, read 2026-11-01]; honour returned limit headers. Error surfacing. Auth or permission failure halts the affected job immediately — no silent retries. Every halt creates an Integration Exceptions task (R-08-25) and notifies the platform owner over the approved internal route; acknowledgement target one business day (DEC-08-07).

Kill switch and test. Ordered: (1) set adapter write-enable flag false — stops all agent writes; (2) pause enabled native rules, recording prior states for restore; (3) stop event intake, retain the queue; (4) if identity is suspect, revoke the OAuth grant — first check whether revocation affects other clients, and record that one account’s webhook can stop when its creator is disabled [V11]; (5) notify the operator; (6) log the drill.
