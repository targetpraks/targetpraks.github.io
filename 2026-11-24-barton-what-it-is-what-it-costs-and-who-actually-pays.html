The short answer

Barton is a product you buy, not a service the school runs. It is a scripted reading-and-spelling curriculum published in California, sold one level at a time. The school has never offered it, does not own it, and its letter of 13 November promises accommodations — a reader, a scribe, extra time, a pen — not a reading programme. The one-to-one teaching was always going to be your facilitator delivering something you supply.

Nothing is bought, and I never bought anything. The contract said you would supply the materials because that is who pays for the facilitator and everything around him. You did not know that when you read it, which is the defect — not the price.

The number, if you want it: Levels 1–3 digital, R19,258 once. Level 1 alone, R6,128. There is no subscription and no annual fee.

1. What Barton actually is

Barton Reading & Spelling System, published by Bright Solutions for Dyslexia in San Jose, California. It is one of the well-known Orton-Gillingham-based systems, and its reputation rests on spelling and word structure rather than on comprehension work.

  • Ten levels, bought one at a time. Each level is a box (or a download) of scripted lesson plans and materials — the tutor reads the script, the student does the exercise. There is nothing to design and nothing to improvise.
  • The training is included. Every level ships with 3–5 hours of streaming tutor training for that level. There is no separate course to buy — this is why the contract said "the materials and the training".
  • It is one-to-one, 45 minutes, four days a week — which is exactly the slot the facilitator has inside the school day.
  • You start at Level 1. The publisher is explicit: most students start at Level 1 whatever their age, because phonemic awareness is the gate — their FAQ says so, and they exchange a too-easy level free of charge.
  • Digital delivery works here. The all-digital version is the PDFs plus the streaming training, and the letter tiles come as the Barton Tiles app on the iPad he already has — no shipping, no customs, no box.
  • It is slow and it is meant to be. The publisher's own figure is 2–3 years to work through all ten levels at twice a week; four days a week is faster. It is not a term-long fix.

The teaching itself was always the family's side of the deal.

One thing that is true in your favour: a school can own Barton — there is a site licence at $1,200 a level for schools and centres with three or more tutors. That is not a document Reddam has signed, and it is not a conversation you have had with them.
