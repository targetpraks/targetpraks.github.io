School comparison · Durbanville / northern suburbs · 13 November 2026

La Vigne vs Curro Uitzicht — and the other schools that do this job

Both are small-class CAPS schools in the northern suburbs that exist for children who need more support than a mainstream class gives. They are built differently, cost differently, and one of them has no matric record yet. Here is the whole picture, with the numbers behind it.

The 30-second answer

Curro Uitzicht is the support machine. Therapists, learning-support teachers and a counsellor are inside the school day, exam concessions are a documented service, classes are capped at 12, and the high school has three straight years of NSC results including 100% in 2025. It is cheaper than La Vigne in Grades 1–3, a wash in Grades 4–5, dearer from Grade 6–7.

La Vigne is the smaller, warmer, faith-based option. Eight learners per primary class, one support educator in the foundation and intermediate phases, homework hour built into a longer day. On its own published band it is level with Curro around Grades 4–5 and cheaper by R100–R930 a month from Grade 6–7. What it does not have: on-site therapists (you arrange and pay for those, though the school makes room for them in the day), any matric history — its Grade 10–12 phase is new and no NSC candidates were recorded in 2025 — and a grade-by-grade fee schedule.

If the child needs therapy and concessions to be part of the school day, or is heading into Grades 10–12, Curro Uitzicht is the safer bet. If the child is primary-school age, needs a tiny class and a family-shaped school, and you can carry the therapy costs yourself, La Vigne is the closer fit — and modestly cheaper from Grade 6.

01 What each one actually is

La Vigne Educational House

Inclusive CAPS · Durbanville town centre
  • Grade R–12, dual medium (English and Afrikaans), CAPS as required by WCED.
  • 8 learners per class in Grades R–7; 12 in Grades 8–12, subject-dependent.
  • “Specialised teaching to learners with various learning barriers” — inclusive rather than remedial: the child stays on the mainstream CAPS track.
  • A learner support educator in the foundation and intermediate phases, working inside school hours.
  • Christian ethos — “I am the vine; you are the branches” (John 15:5) is the founding text.
  • Small: 142 learners and 28 educators across R–12 on the 2025 school registry — about 5 learners per staff member overall.

Curro Uitzicht

Assisted learning · two campuses
  • Grade 1–12 in English or Afrikaans classes, CAPS, NSC at the end.
  • Primary (Gr 1–7): Cnr Wildebeest & Springbok Rd, Uitzicht. High (Gr 8–12): Cnr Schubert Ave & Verdi Blvd, Sonstraal Heights — 2.5 km from Durbanville centre.
  • Maximum 12 learners per class (Grade 8 allows 15).
  • Named on-site support: learning-support teachers and an occupational therapist at the primary school; a curriculum coordinator and counsellor at the high school.
  • Built-in exam concessions — a scribe or reader for prelims and finals once the education department approves it, billed at R460 per sitting in Grade 12.
  • High school: 107 learners, 19 educators (2025); classes are deliberately quiet, consistent and low-stimulus.

03 The money, honestly

Curro publishes a real 2027 fee letter per grade. La Vigne publishes a range on its home page with no year attached, which is the single biggest gap in this comparison — ask them for the 2027 schedule in writing before you plan anything.

Bars scaled to R11 410 — the highest single monthly figure in this comparison. La Vigne’s bar is its published band, not a per-grade figure.

Where the crossover sits. In Grades 1–3 Curro is the cheaper school by R310–R740 a month. Grades 4–5 is a wash — the bands cross over. From Grade 6–7 La Vigne runs R100–R930 a month under Curro. Across the high school the two bands overlap: at the low end La Vigne undercuts Curro by R690–R2 210; at the top end it is R910 dearer in Grade 8 and R610 cheaper in Grade 12. Because La Vigne publishes one high-school band instead of a per-grade schedule, you cannot actually call the high school on price until they send you grade-by-grade 2027 fees.

The hidden line item. Curro’s occupational therapist, learning-support teachers, counsellor and concession administration sit inside the fee. At La Vigne, outside therapy is explicitly “at the parents’/legal guardians’ own expense”. A weekly OT or speech session plus an educational psychologist’s review is easily R3 000–R6 000 a month — enough to erase the fee difference, or to make it much bigger, depending on what the child actually needs. Get the therapy plan priced before comparing the two.

The free (or nearly free) route most parents forget

Western Cape government schools for learners with special educational needs carry the same CAPS or DCAPS curriculum, real therapy teams and a fraction of the fee — the catch is that placement runs through a WCED assessment and the school must have space. In this area: Tafelberg School in Bothasig (Gr 1–12 remedial, dyslexia, dyscalculia, speech and reading — the value outlier of the whole list), Jan Kriel in Kuils River, Herbert Street in Bellville for autism with high support needs, and Cheré Botha for mild-to-moderate intellectual disability. One phone call to the district office tells you whether the child qualifies — worth doing before signing anything private.

05 What the two schools feel like

This is the part the brochures are honest about, if you read the language.

La Vigne reads like a family that happens to run a school. The testimonials are about fear turning into confidence, about “unique children that have special needs”, about access to teachers and management. Six-year families. A principal and two directors whose names are on the site. The class cap of eight is real and it is the whole product. The trade-off is that the school is small enough that one staffing change matters, and the high school is young — Grade 10–12 is a recent addition, which is exactly why there is no matric record to look at.

Curro Uitzicht reads like a system built for anxiety. Classrooms designed to reduce stimulus, routines held constant through the year so the child knows what comes next, assessments printed on off-yellow paper, enlarged fonts, concessions applied for as standard practice. It is the more clinical, more predictable environment, and it is the one that can point at results. The trade-off is that it is a campus of a listed group: policies, fee letters and processes come from head office, and the subject basket at NSC level is narrow — six subjects in 2025.

06 What a tour should settle

  1. Who is in the building, and for how many hours a week? Ask for the therapy roster by name and qualification — at Curro it should be on site, at La Vigne you need to know which outside therapists already come in and how the timetable bends for them.
  2. Concessions, last year, in numbers. How many learners received a reader, scribe, extra time or a separate venue in 2025? Who applies — the school or the parent? What did the department approve and what did it refuse?
  3. La Vigne specifically: the FET phase. Which subjects actually run in Grades 10–12, how many learners are in each, what happens if a subject does not make up numbers, what Tygerberg Art Centre costs on top, and when the first full matric cohort writes.
  4. Curro Uitzicht specifically: the subject basket. Six subjects at NSC level is thin. Confirm the exact line-up against what the child might need for a degree, a diploma or a trade.
  5. Teacher turnover and training. Ask how many teachers left last year, and what training in learning barriers each one has had — this matters more than any brochure claim.
  6. Cohort size per grade, and where children go afterwards. A grade of six is intimate and fragile. Ask where last year’s Grade 9s and Grade 12s went.
  7. The full invoice. Registration, books, kit, camps, aftercare, transport, therapy, exam sitting fees — ask each school to put a total Grade-1 and a total Grade-8 year on one page.
