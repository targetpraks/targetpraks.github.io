Extreme Ownership — the card that carries the whole story

Answer: all three, each doing one job. The fields hold what happened, why and the plan. The subtasks hold the actions — but only after the plan is approved. The checklist holds the close-out proof. And the approval is not a field at all: it is a status only the approver may move, with their comment naming the plan version.

Council verdict, run 661e1541436e (4 seats · 4 peer reviews · chairman deepseek-v4.1-flash):

The single most important change is making the Approver's timestamped "Approved vN" comment plus their exclusive move to in progress the only gate before fix work starts.

1. What each part is for

Piece Carries Why there and not elsewhere
What happened · Why it happened · Fix plan (long-text fields) The story, in three parts It is the only place a story reads in the list view, exports to a sheet, and can be grouped and charted across 40 cards. In a description or a checklist it is one click deep and invisible to every view.
Assignee (native) The person who owns the failure and therefore the fix He is the owner of the card from the second it is logged. One assignee — ClickUp allows a second only with the ClickApp on.
Approver (single user) The manager who signs the plan off Puts a name on the gate, so the automation can call him and the board can filter to his queue.
Status Where the card is in the gate The only thing that can make "approved before work starts" true rather than polite.
Subtasks The fix actions, one each, with their own owner and date Created after approval. A plan that may be rejected should not create six tasks first, and ClickUp cannot roll subtask fields back up to the parent anyway.
Close-out checklist Fix done · Team briefed · SOP updated · Checked again in 2 weeks Checklist items have no owner and no date, so they can never be the approvable object — but they are exactly right as the "did we actually close the loop" gate.
Comments Every version of the plan, and the rejection reason A long-text field overwrites. The comment thread does not. This is the audit trail.
Cost impact · Recurrence · Root cause type · Severity · Section · Brand What it cost, whether it has happened before, why in one word, how bad, where These are the five that make the Monday read possible without opening a card.

Why not one of the three alone: fields alone give nothing to work — you cannot assign "step 3 of a paragraph". Subtasks alone bury the story and leave a rejected plan's tasks orphaned. Checklists alone are unassignable, undated and unreportable. The mix is not a compromise; each part is doing the one thing the other two cannot.

2. The card, as it now looks

A fully filled example is on the list already: DEMO — Wrong sauce batch dispatched to 3 stores (Napoletana NAP-1121) — 18 × 5 L tubs, Cost impact 4 850, Severity High, Root cause Process, Section Central Kitchen, Approver named, and the four fix actions as subtasks. It is test data, marked safe to delete; open it once, then move it to Closed or bin it — your call, not mine.

The reading order that makes a card answer your three questions without opening anything else:

Name what went wrong, in the words of the person who was there
Severity How bad · Low / Medium / High / Critical
What happened facts only: when, where, what, who was affected. No adjectives.
Why it happened the cause, not the excuse. Ask why five times; write the answer that would have prevented it.
Root cause type one of People · Process · System / Tech · Supplier · Communication · Training
Fix plan what will change so it cannot happen again — the steps, the owner, the date. Written by the person who owns the failure.
Assignee that person. The card is his from the second it is logged.
Approver the manager who signs the plan off.
Due date when the fix lands.
Cost impact · Recurrence what it cost; first time / repeat / chronic.
Subtasks the fix actions, one each — opened only after approval.
Close-out ☐ Fix done ☐ Team briefed ☐ SOP updated ☐ Checked again in 2 weeks

3. The flow

flowchart LR
  A["Logged<br/>WHAT + SEVERITY + owner"] --> B["Owner writing plan<br/>WHY + ROOT CAUSE + FIX PLAN"]
  B --> C["Awaiting approval<br/>Approver named"]
  C -->|"Approver comments<br/>Approved vN"| D["Approved – fixing<br/>subtasks open here"]
  C -->|"Rejected vN + reason"| B
  D --> E["Verify<br/>close-out checklist"]
  E --> F["Closed"]
  E -.->|"did not hold"| B

Only two moves matter: the approver moves the card out of Awaiting approval, and nobody opens a subtask before that. Everything else is bookkeeping the automations do.

4. The three rules that make it real

  1. The approver moves it, nobody else. ClickUp will not hard-block another person from changing a status, so the gate is enforced by the trail, not by permissions: the comment Approved vN from the Approver field, then the status change, timestamped in that order on the activity feed. A card that skipped it is visible at a glance — that is the accountability.
  2. The plan is versioned in comments, not in the field. Before the owner rewrites, he posts the old text as Fix plan v1 (or the approver posts Rejected v1 — reason). The field always holds the current draft; the comments hold every version. If the version in the field has no matching comment, the plan was quietly rewritten.
  3. Nobody opens a subtask before approval. Subtasks are the execution of an approved plan. If they appear while the card still says Awaiting approval, the gate was skipped.

6. What still needs five minutes of clicks

The API cannot create a status set (it refuses open/closed bookends and lower-cases what it does create), automations, or forms. In this order:

  1. Statuses — list … menu → Statuses → Use statuses specific to this list: Logged · Owner writing plan · Awaiting approval · Approved – fixing · Verify · Closed. Names are the instruction; that is the whole point of naming them.
  2. Three automations — status → Awaiting approval: add the Approver as watcher and comment them · status → Approved – fixing: comment "Plan approved — go" and notify the assignee · task created with no assignee: notify the list owner.
  3. Form "Log an incident" — Title, What happened, Severity, Business Unit required; Brand, Cost impact, date optional; new task status = Logged. This is how staff log without duplicating a template by hand.
  4. Make Incident log the default view, and add the Status is Awaiting approval filter (plus Approver is me if you want his personal queue).
