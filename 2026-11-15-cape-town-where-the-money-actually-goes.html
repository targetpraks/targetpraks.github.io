06

The lever nobody uses: the TFSA

A tax-free savings account changes the arithmetic more than any property decision on this page.

From 1 March 2026 you may put R46 000 a year into a TFSA, up to R500 000 in your lifetime. Inside it, interest, dividends and capital gains are untaxed. A bond paying 9.00% outside a TFSA keeps 5.31% after your 41% marginal rate. A REIT ETF paying 7.09% inside one keeps 7.09% — and grows.

Why this beats the bond: the bond's whole advantage is certainty, and tax takes nearly half of it. Put the taxable money in the bond by all means, but the first R46 000 a year should be buying assets the taxman cannot touch, not income he taxes at 41%.
07

What I would actually do

Staged by dependency, not by date. Each stage opens the next one only when its exit condition is met.

  1. Fill the TFSA first. R46 000 a year into a global index feeder and a listed property ETF. Exit condition: the annual allowance is used and the position is held in your own name, not a broker's nominee account you cannot see.
  2. Keep the bond for the money you cannot lose. It is the only lane on this page that pays without a tenant, a tenant's job, or a market. Exit condition: the reserve covers the expenses you would have to pay in a bad year without selling anything.
  3. If you want bricks, buy commercial, not residential. A small tenanted unit in Paarden Eiland or Woodstock at 7.30%–8.21% net beats every flat on this page and every bond after tax. Exit condition: the lease is in place at signature, the tenant is trading, and the yield is above 5.31% after your own cost assumptions — not the agent's.
  4. Do not buy a good-area flat for yield. At 3.85% net it is a lifestyle purchase with a rental attached. Buy it if you want to live in it or hold it for a decade of growth, and say so out loud before you sign.
