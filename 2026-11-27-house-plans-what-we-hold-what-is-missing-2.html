3. Bushwillow — why our copy cannot be the house's plan

The file holds one two-page set, “Bushwillow Plans.pdf”. Page 2 carries the title block: “Addition to house Maio / Erf 1022 / Greenstone Hill Ext 13”, drawing MAIO/01 Rev 2, dated 15 September 2014, by Guy Fleuriot B.Arch (Wits). It carries a Bushwillow HOA approval stamp and a “plans received 21/10/2014” stamp; page 1 carries a City of Johannesburg Building Control “recommended for approval” stamp dated 21 January 2015. It shows a first-floor addition, a new pergola and an existing garage. No pool.

There are only two ways to read it, and both end in the same place. Either it is another stand's drawing filed under this address, or its title block was reused and is wrong. In neither case is it an approved plan for Erf 128, and neither reading gives a conveyancer what they will ask for.

The fallback, if all of the above fail. The city's own guidance for an old plan it cannot produce is to have the house measured and drawn as it stands by a SACAP-registered professional, then regularised. The tariff is R16/m² from 1 July 2026 with a minimum fee. That is a cost and a delay — not a defect in the property — and it should only be authorised after the estate and the developer have both come up empty.
