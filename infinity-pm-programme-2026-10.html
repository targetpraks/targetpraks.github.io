Page 3 of 5 · Plan

The programme, staged by dependency

No dates — each stage starts when the one above it is true. Stages 1 and 2 run in parallel across desks; 3 depends on 1, 4 on 2, 5 on 4.

1

Truth on the board

Fastest visible change. Clean the desk that is drowning, then apply one rule everywhere: every task lives in a work list with an owner and a due date.

  • Clear the 625 tasks sitting in “General” into their real work lists
  • Triage BU 8 Brand Ops 2: 597 open, 324 late, 522 of them in one General list
  • Close what is done, re-date what is alive, delete nothing
2

Rhythm

The weekly cadence is what turns a task tool into management. One short meeting per desk, exception-driven.

  • 30 minutes per desk, weekly: late work, this week’s due work, anything stuck
  • Each GM sends a one-page desk note upward
  • One monthly scorecard across the group, six numbers, no narrative
3

Intake

Work must arrive in one shape. Verbal and chat requests are the reason the board lies.

  • Every new request is filed as a brief through the intake form
  • A brief must name the work type, brand, tier, due date and requester
  • No brief, no queue position — the desk says this out loud once and it holds
4

Control

Management needs the exceptions, not the list. Today the portal offers only its eight stock views.

  • Build the desk views: mine, late, due this week, waiting on someone else
  • Ageing and workload by owner, per desk
  • A decision list: everything waiting on a manager or on you
5

Automation

Only worth doing once the data is honest. Automation on top of a lying board just spreads the lie.

  • Auto-assign owners by work type when a brief arrives
  • Escalate a late task on day 3 to the desk owner, day 7 to the GM
  • Weekly digest through Cliq; group dashboard in Zoho Analytics

Why this order

  1. Views and automation built on a board full of stale tasks simply publish the lie faster.
  2. The General-list problem is the cheapest fix with the biggest visible change — one weekend of clearing and the board reads true.
  3. The weekly rhythm is the only part that needs a human, so it starts early and never stops.

What I need from you

Ask 1

One owner for the programme?

The plan needs a single name who runs the cadence across all six companies — otherwise it becomes six hobbies.

Ask 2

Hard rule on intake?

Do you want “no brief, no work” enforced from day one, or a two-week grace while the desks learn the form?

Ask 3

Close or re-date the 473?

Anything late gets either a real new date or it closes. Nothing stays late and open.

Ask 4

Zoho Projects stays?

You already pay for it and it holds 11,847 tasks of history. I am assuming yes; say now if a move is on the table.

How a skill works — the short version

You asked how the real skill works. Four lines, no jargon.

  • It is a written procedure. A short document that tells an agent exactly how a job is done here: the steps, the sources, the traps, and how to prove it worked.
  • It loads itself when relevant. You do not call it. You ask in plain words — “file this as a brief” — and the matching skill is pulled into the agent’s working memory before it acts.
  • It carries the company’s rules, not the model’s opinions. Ours already knows your 12 desks, the 143 work types, the 8-line brief template and the exact task-list ids. That is why the numbers in this page came out consistent.
  • It is written once and used by everyone. When a rule changes — a new desk, a new due-date rule — the skill is edited, and every agent in every company follows the new version the next time it loads.

The one that runs the Infinity briefs is called infinity-briefs-dashboard. If you want a second one for the programme itself — the weekly desk review, the scorecard, the escalation rules — say go and I will write it, so the cadence runs without you remembering it.
