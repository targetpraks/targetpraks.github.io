Minutes are staggered three apart per unit so thirteen bots do not fire on the same second. Everything sits inside the 20:00 window the platform's own administration procedure mandates.

The three additions I made

  1. Stale and duplicate watch (R11) — tasks untouched for 21 days, and pairs that are the same work filed twice. The feed already carries what is needed; this is the routine that stops the board quietly rotting.
  2. Nightly feed (Courage, 19:30 Mon–Fri) — refreshes all thirteen snapshots and pushes them into the platform before any routine fires.
  3. Routine health (Courage, 21:30 Mon–Fri) — silent unless a routine failed in the last 24 hours or an active routine has not fired when it should. On Monday the window widens to 74 hours so the weekend is not read as a fault.

The Friday stand-up pack

Every unit gets a pack written for the room, at 06:30 Friday — before the meeting, after Thursday's work has closed. It reads that unit's week: what closed, what is open, what is overdue, and per person — closed, open, overdue, estimated hours still open, and the names of what that person finished.

The numbers are computed in code from ClickUp, never by the model; the model writes the sentences. A second feed run at 06:00 Friday refreshes the snapshot.

What the first pack found (ITC, 19 Nov). 40 tasks closed, 33 open, 21 overdue with one person carrying 19 of them. It also reported two real problems unprompted: time is not being tracked (0.0 estimated hours on every ITC task, 18.0 h actually logged for the unit for the week — 4 tasks workspace-wide carry logged time against 330 with estimates), and the same work appears in both the closed and the overdue lists under different ids, so "40 closed and 21 overdue" cannot be taken at face value until reconciled. The pack says both plainly rather than presenting estimates as hours worked.

Mail closes the pack as "Mail not checked — no mailbox snapshot for this unit" until the Zoho scope exists.

How the data reaches the bot

The bots have no MCP bindings and no Zoho scope. Rather than give thirteen bots a workspace-wide ClickUp credential — which the tooling PRD cannot make safe with allowlists — each unit gets a prepared slice:

ClickUp (one fleet credential, read-only)
   → 19:30 feed on the Mac mini, one brief per unit
   → AgenticOS context document "ROUTINE FEED — <unit>", mode link
   → the unit's own bot reads only its own slice

Unit isolation is real rather than promised: a bot physically cannot see another unit's tasks, because the other unit's rows were never in its context document.

The brief carries open tasks, owner, due date, status, priority, list, the space's people, and two derived lists — overdue-with-an-owner and unassigned. Every row carries its ClickUp task id.

Operating it

  • Change a routine's wording or cadence: edit routines.json, rebuild the payload, re-run the applier. It is idempotent by routine name and will not duplicate what exists.
  • Pause one unit: set that trigger inactive in the platform; the feed keeps running for it harmlessly.
  • Roll back a bot's context binding: republish an earlier version — versions are linear and every publish is recorded.
  • Every run leaves an answer in the bot's own conversation and in the run log.
