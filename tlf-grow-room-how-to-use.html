Two rooms, two sheets

Every question about a grow room — how much it makes, what it costs, and whether we need another machine or tent — is answered on one workbook per crop. Microgreens live in TLF MMG Growroom; mushrooms live in TLF MMR Growroom. These tabs explain what each one is for. If a number is not in here, it is not a number we stand behind.

Start here — three steps

1

Open the Grow Room tab

The first tab is the whole business on one page. The yellow cells are the only ones you should ever type in: how many LARGE machines, how many SMALL ones, the days in a cycle, grams a tray, the share that makes it, and the price a kilo.

Use it when: someone asks what the room can do, or what a client's order would need.

2

Type the order, read the answer

Section 2 of that tab takes a client's kilos a week and tells you whether the room covers it, in kilos and in rand, and what you would have to buy to close the gap. The block above it does the same for one machine on its own.

Use it when: a client asks for more, or a new client asks if we can supply them.

3

Only go deeper when the question is bigger

Buying a machine, pricing a franchise, or explaining a bad month — that is when you open the tabs below. Nine times out of ten you never need to.

Use it when: money is about to be spent, or someone asks why the numbers moved.

Two rooms, two sheets. Part 1 below is microgreens — MMG. Part 2 is mushrooms — MMR, built to the same shape with the same tabs, so once you know one you know both.

Part 1 · TLF MMG Growroom — microgreens

1

Grow Room

The one page. Your machine counts, the kilos and rand they give you, the what-if for a client order, the top clients, and the machine table from one to eight units.

Type in: B5 to B12, the yellow band. Read: B15 to B19.

2

Settings

The technical constants behind the calculations — cycle length, trays on a large machine, grams a tray, the share that survives, and the price. Capacity and Plan read from here.

Use it when: a cycle or a price actually changes. Change it once, here, and every tab follows.

3

Capacity

What one to twelve large machines can grow — tray spaces, trays harvestable a week, kilos a week, kilos a month, and rand a month. It is a look-up table, not a forecast.

Use it when: you want the ceiling for a given number of machines.

4

Prospects

Every sample we have dropped off, who took it, when, and whether they became a client. Twenty-six samples, three converted. A prospect never buys a machine.

Use it when: you are chasing a lead, or someone asks why we buy samples.

5

Demand

What the business actually pulls, read from the invoices: every week since April, the clients behind those weeks, and a builder at the bottom where you add a client and the room answers. Confirmed and prospect are kept apart on purpose.

Use it when: you need a real number for a client, or you want to know what another kilo a week would do.

6

Plan

The next thirteen weeks. It takes today's demand, applies the growth you type in, and shows the week the room stops coping — plus the one number that needs acting on this week: how many trays to sow.

Use it when: you are setting a production target, or a client asks for a volume we cannot grow yet.

7

Machines

The buy-or-hold decision, and nothing else. Demand against capacity, what that needs in machines, what the capital would be, and the payback in months.

Use it when: someone wants to buy a machine. Nothing on this tab is typed — it all reads from the Grow Room.

8

Notes and gaps

Where every number came from, and what is still not known — including the two things I would not sign my name to. Read this before you defend a figure in a meeting.

Use it when: someone challenges a number, or you are handing this to finance.

9

Customers

The ledger. Every client who has ever been invoiced, with kilos a week, August and September split, rand, number of orders, first order and last order.

Use it when: you need a client's history — before a price conversation, say.

10

Machine cost and ROI

What a machine actually costs — steel, trays, control box, wiring, labour — and how many months it takes to earn back. The margin you keep is a yellow cell; change it and the payback moves.

Use it when: anyone asks what a grow machine costs, or how long a machine takes to pay for itself.

11

Yield vs invoiced

What we grew against what we sold, month by month and week by week, with the gap in red where the invoice is running ahead of the harvest log. This is the tab that tells you the harvest log is incomplete.

Use it when: a month looks wrong, or stock does not add up.

12

Lists

The allowed answers for the columns that should be pick-lists — machine size, product, status. It exists so any dropdown can be wired up in four clicks.

Use it when: you are setting up dropdowns, or you want to know exactly what words to use in a Status cell.

Part 2 · TLF MMR Growroom — mushrooms

Same layout as microgreens, so you can read it without learning anything new. The difference is the unit of production: microgreens are counted in trays, mushrooms in bags. One tent fruits 63 blocks — three shelves, seven levels, three bags a level — and a block fruits for three weeks before it comes out. Colonising happens outside the tent, on the rack in the dark room. Corrected on 30 October 2026.

1

Grow Room

The one page. Tents, shelves, levels, bags, cycle length, kilos a block, the share that is saleable, the share that fails, the price, and the fixed costs. Then what it can make, what a single block costs and earns, what the tent cost and when it pays back, the what-if for a client order, and who actually buys mushrooms. Sections 7 to 10 carry the 30 October audit: what changed, the break-even, the trigger for a second tent, and what to spend this quarter.

Type in: the yellow cells, B5 to B14. Read: B17 to B20 for capacity, B30 to B32 for the margin on a block.

2

Cost and ROI

What one tent costs to build, item by item — tent, shelving, humidifier, fan, ducting, lights, controller, an electrician's worth of timers and relays, and the labour to put it up. R23,515 all in. Below that, what a block earns, the packaging cost a kilo, and the payback at retail, restaurant and mass-retail prices. At R208.35 a kilo retail, restaurant and mass retail all leave a margin — retail is still the best of the three.

Use it when: someone asks what a second tent would cost, or why mushrooms only work at retail price.

3

Demand

Every mushroom invoice we have ever issued — 23 lines, 14 July to 17 September. Date, client, product, bags, kilos, rand and rate a kilo. It is what we sold, not what the room could grow.

Use it when: you want the truth about how big mushrooms actually are right now. It is 2.35 kg, in total.

4

Capacity

Bags to kilos, for one tent up to twelve. Blocks fruiting at once — 63 to a tent — blocks a week, kilos a week, kilos a month and rand a month. A look-up table, not a forecast.

Use it when: you want the ceiling for a given number of tents.

5

Customers

Every client who has ever bought a mushroom, with kilos, rand, how many orders, kilos a month, their share of the total, and their first and last order. The last-order column is the one to watch: seven clients bought once and never came back.

Use it when: you are deciding who to call, or who has gone quiet.

6

Plan

Two halves. The top is the next thirteen weeks from 2 November — kilos wanted, blocks to harvest, blocks in the tent it needs, and how many to start each week. The bottom, from row 27, is the part the staff actually read: how many blocks a day, what the one sterilisation tank holds, how many runs a day keep the tent full, and the five things one person does every day. Three blocks a day out and three in. One run of the tank a day is enough; twice a week and the tent only fills to 57%.

Type in: the growth rate in B3, and how many blocks your tank holds in B32. Read: row 30 for the daily number, row 42 for the day's list.

7

Yield vs invoiced

Invoices on one side, an empty harvest column on the other. Nobody has ever weighed a mushroom harvest, so the sheet cannot yet prove the 0.85 kg a block it assumes, nor the 0.55 / 0.30 split across the two flushes. Weigh every block you cut, type the grams in, and within four weeks this tab either backs the assumption or kills it.

Use it when: you want to know what a block really gives. This is the most important empty column in either workbook.

8

Tents

How many tents we have, what one costs, what each additional one adds, and the decision line: tents the demand needs, tents we would have to add, and the rand. At today's sales the answer is hold — the room is 1.4% used. A second tent is triggered at 12 kg a week over four consecutive invoiced weeks, two-thirds of what one tent can make.

Use it when: someone suggests buying another tent.

9

Notes and gaps

Where every number comes from, and what we do not know. The gap that matters: no harvest has ever been weighed, and the failure rate and saleable share are industry numbers, not ours.

Use it when: someone asks where a figure came from. Read it before you defend a number in a meeting.

10

Audit 30 October 2026

The three independent reads of this workbook — the CFO, the COO and Joe the farmer — each in their own words, then every change the audit made as from → to with the reason beside it. It is the record of why the numbers moved, and what still has to be measured before anyone spends money.

Use it when: someone asks why the sheet used to say 53 kg a month, or who changed it.

11

Lists

The allowed answers for the columns that should be pick-lists — varieties, client types and the verdicts. Zoho's API cannot create dropdowns, so these are the values to point a real one at when you build it in the browser.

Use it when: you are tidying a column of free text.

The numbers to remember

6.25 kg a week

What clients actually order — August and September, read from the invoices. Not a plan, not a target.

8.72 kg a week

What the room can grow with four large machines and one small one. About 72% of it is being used.

R81,759

What one large machine costs to build — steel, trays, control box, wiring, labour. About 27 months to earn back at a 75% margin.

2.05 kg a week

What one large machine adds. Every extra machine you buy has to be fed 2 kg of client demand a week to be worth it.

1.1 kg a month

What mushrooms actually sell — 2.35 kg in total since 14 July, at R526 a kilo in 100 g retail bags. The room can make 77 kg a month with the rack keeping up. Break-even is 192 bags, about 19 kg.

R23,515

What one mushroom tent costs to build, complete. It earns R24,538 a month at full room and every gram sold — about a month to pay back — and the room is 1.4% used today. At today's sales it never pays back.

Two things to know before you use it in a meeting

  • The harvest log only covers two months out of five. Where the invoice and the harvest disagree, the invoice is the better number.
  • Electricity is not in the machine cost. Nobody has recorded what the lights and fans draw, so the payback is optimistic by whatever your power bill says.
  • On the mushroom side, no harvest has ever been weighed and every mushroom invoice is a 100 g retail bag. The R526 a kilo is a retail rate, not a wholesale one, and the 0.85 kg a block is Joe the farmer's estimate until somebody puts a scale under it.
  • The 30 October audit cut the room from 63 bags to 45 and the yield from 1.17 kg to 0.85 kg a block. Together they cut full-room earnings from R15,707 a month to R3,571. Then on 30 October you corrected the other three: seven levels fits, colonising is outside the tent, and two flushes a block. That took the room to 77 kg a month at R208 a kilo. Quote the current sheet, not any earlier note.
