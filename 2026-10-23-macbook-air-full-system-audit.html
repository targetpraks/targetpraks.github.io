Already repaired in this pass

Three of these needed no decision from Ricardo — they were unambiguous defects with reversible fixes — so I closed them while the audit was still running, and verified each one afterwards.

Quotio guard stopped

The launch agent was booted out and disabled in launchd, so it stays off across reboots. It is reversible with a single launchctl enable.

$ launchctl print-disabled gui/501 "com.maio.quotio-providers" => disabled $ launchctl list | grep quotio not listed last error line: 18:00:26 — none since

Caches reclaimed

Homebrew downloads plus four updater caches, all recreatable on demand. Nothing user-created was touched, and the Playwright and Spotify caches were deliberately left alone.

$ brew cleanup --prune=all freed approximately 4.7 GB removed updater caches 1.5 GB free before 245.5 G free after 251.4 G

Mount rail rebuilt — one human step left

The script no longer hardcodes an address: it resolves the mini live and probes each candidate until one answers the share port. Every outcome is now logged, and a real failure exits non-zero instead of pretending to succeed.

One thing I deliberately did not do: force the first mount. The Air's keychain holds a password entry only for the old address, so repointing it blindly would have thrown a password dialog onto an unattended laptop — and that is a credential act I never perform. The script now detects this, logs exactly what is missing, and opens nothing.

$ air-mount-devmini.sh 2026-10-23 18:04:55 NO KEYCHAIN ENTRY for 192.168.0.84 - not mounting (would pop a dialog). Run once by hand: air-mount-devmini.sh --connect $ /bin/zsh -n air-mount-devmini.sh syntax ok · plist lint ok · previous version kept beside it

What Ricardo does, once: run air-mount-devmini.sh --connect and confirm the share password when Finder asks. After that the rail mounts itself silently again.

The finding that reaches past the laptop

Auditing the Air exposed a problem in the fleet's shared assumption about backups. It is worth stating plainly because it changes what "backed up" means everywhere.

Two copies live inside one failure domain. The nightly job that reports "backups OK" writes to the same physical SSD that holds the live data, and that SSD is not encrypted.
$ tail -3 ~/Library/Logs/com.maio.backup.log [2026-10-23 03:31:27] backups OK (2026-10-23) $ grep -n 'HIK=' ~/.config/maio-stack/backup.sh 38:HIK="/Volumes/Hiksemi 1TB"; D="$HIK/AI-Drive/Backups"; T=$(date +%F) $ diskutil info "/Volumes/Hiksemi 1TB" | grep -i encrypted Encrypted: No $ mount | grep smb //ricky@192.168.0.174/Plex on /Volumes/Plex (smbfs, ...) ← mounted, never a backup target

What this means. The nightly backup is a real, verified dump — it ran clean at 03:31 today and every artifact passed its size and gzip check. But it protects against one thing only: a mistake inside the files. It does not protect against the SSD failing, being unplugged mid-write, stolen, or lost. Losing that drive loses the live AI-Drive and every backup of it at the same instant. The NAS is already mounted and idle, which makes the gap unusually cheap to close.

What the MoA council decided

Five seats — a first-principles analyst, a skeptic, a pragmatist, a researcher, and a chairman — reviewed the findings against six decisions. Below is each call, and what I did with it after checking it against the machine.

1. Backup architecture Adopted, sharpened

Council: copy the USB media to the mini immediately as an emergency landing, then buy a second encrypted drive for Time Machine — and do not treat the mini as a real backup, because it is not a separate failure domain.

My check: correct, and worse than the council knew. The mini's backup target is the same unencrypted SSD the live data sits on. So the mini is not a landing zone either. The NAS already mounted at /Volumes/Plex is the free separate failure domain, and nothing writes to it.

2. The mount rail Adopted

Council: do not just fix the address — rebuild it so failure cannot look like success, use the stable tailnet name, and surface a health signal where you actually look.

My check: agreed. The script's silent exit 0 on an unreachable host is what turned a one-line break into five days of nothing.

3. Quotio Adopted

Council: disable the launch agent now; do not repair a proxy that nothing uses on a machine that does its inference on the mini. Leave the app installed until Ricardo says otherwise.

My check: nothing listens on 8318, the Air has no model credentials, and the guard has never succeeded today. Disabling is reversible; uninstalling is his call.

4. Where MoA should run Adopted

Council: on the mini, over SSH — never fix this by putting keys back on a laptop that sleeps and travels.

My check: the Air is already wired that way for agents. Only its stale local preset contradicts it, and that should fail loudly instead of degrading quietly.

5. Exposure ranking Adopted, reordered

Council: the tunnel is the real problem; AnyDesk second; the Codex disk grant is an audit item; SSH multiplexers and firewall stealth are noise.

My note: I keep the tunnel first but I am not touching it — it is a working feature of an app Ricardo uses daily. It gets reported with the exact lever, and he decides.

6. The client-only thesis Adopted with one dissent

Council: the role is right, the machine has drifted, and it should be returned to a thin console that sleeps when idle.

My dissent: that is only true if the USB stick keeps holding the family archive. Once the archive lives on the NAS and the drive, the laptop can go back to being a laptop. Sequence matters more than the cleanup list.

The chairman's one-sentence close: make a verified encrypted second copy of the single-copy media before any cleanup, because every other finding here is repairable but that loss is not.

Method and limits

Seven probe passes ran over SSH from the mini to the Air, each writing raw output to a local evidence file before any interpretation. The figures quoted here come from sw_vers, diskutil, tmutil, fdesetup, csrutil, spctl, socketfilterfw, pmset, system_profiler, launchctl, lsof, du, brew, tailscale and the Air's own log files.

Not probed: sustained thermal behaviour under load, Wi-Fi signal quality over time, and whether the NAS drive itself is redundant. Those are follow-ups, not gaps in the findings above — and none of them changes the backup verdict.

One number worth explaining: an app reported as "never used" means macOS kept no launch record, not that it was never opened. It is a soft signal, and nothing was removed on the strength of it.
