What this PRD is. The build specification for the pack: what each page must contain, what governs it, what it may not assert, and how it is produced and checked.

What this PRD is not. It is not the pack. It is not a cost plan. It does not approve spend, sign anything, or commit Papa Pasta to a fit-out.

1. WHAT WE ARE BUILDING, AND THE TEST IT MUST PASS

1.1 The brief, as given (2026-10-20)

Combine the Brand Manual and the packaging designs with Ryan Jones-Hockley's lighting design (governing on how lighting should be) and Julie Chrisopoulos's 3D renders and technical drawings (governing on everything else). Produce a full Papa Pasta store design: all external and internal specification, all technical and visual design elements, material listing with explanations, material alternatives in some sections. Visually impressive. Handover grade for a shop fitter and a prospective franchisee. Final PDF.

1.2 The test

A pack is handover grade when a fitter can price it, order it, set it out, build it, connect it, prove it and hand it over without asking anyone a question — and a franchisee can decide to buy it, know what is theirs, and know what will fail an audit without a second meeting.

The mechanical test the council landed on, and which this PRD adopts as the acceptance gate:

Open the pack at any line — a lux value, a panel dimension, a fixture reference, a colour. Can a fitter build from that line without reconciling two sources? If any line requires him to choose, the pack is a synthesis document wearing a specification's clothes.

1.3 What "visually impressive" means here, precisely

It does not mean a deck of renders. It means the pack is itself built to the brand's own law: a black-substrate document with lime as the single accent, set in Montserrat and IBM Plex Mono, obeying 70/18/12 on every spread. A white corporate document about a black brand is the first thing that makes it read as a brochure. The pack's visual quality is proof of the standard, not decoration on top of it.

2.1b THE LIGHTING RULING (Ricardo, 2026-10-20 — supersedes Part 7 where they conflict)

Ryan Jones-Hockley's lighting specification governs. Where the Brand Manual and Ryan disagree on lighting, Ryan's specification is built. This includes colour temperature: 6000 K throughout, as calculated, not the manual's 4000 / 3000 / 2700 K zone law.

Consequences, applied everywhere in this PRD: - 6000 K is the built colour temperature in every area Ryan calculated. No zone he specified is re-tuned to 4000 K, 3000 K or 2700 K. The three areas he did not calculate — threshold, eat zone, exterior — are flagged where they appear, with the manual's written instruction shown alongside and the recommendation stated, so no silent temperature decision is made on site.

  • Ryan's four fixture families are specified as issued (CCT 6k), with no substitution of a warmer variant.
  • The manual's Part 7 zone CCT schedule is superseded by ruling, not deleted: it stays in the pack as a recorded variance, so the deviation is documented rather than silent.
  • The manual's CRI ≥90 requirement is no longer brand law in the pack — but Ryan does not state CRI either, so CRI becomes an open procurement requirement (gap G2), not a licence to buy 80 CRI. A 6000 K, 80 CRI source against white thermoformed acrylic and lime is the one lighting failure that looks cheap on the first day and cannot be fixed without new fittings.
  • Everything in Part 7 that is not colour temperature or light level — one-neon-per-surface, the surface-treatment rule, the 12% accent allocation, maintenance access, the sign-failure audit rule — is not lighting and remains brand law.

Where Part 7 and the manual's 4.10/4.11 disagree, Part 7 governs (stated at p.127).

The pack keeps the plates at native 624 × 420 mm. This is now a locked production rule (§8.3).

Correction 3 — there is no eat-zone lighting design, and no emergency lighting, no CRI, no IP rating, anywhere in Ryan's report. Correction 4 — the sheet set has no external drawing of any kind. All six sheets are internal: ground floor plan, reflected ceiling plan, and Sections 1–4. 3. THE FOUR RULINGS THE PACK MAKES

These are the decisions that stop the three sources fighting on the page. Ricardo can overrule any of them in §10.

Ruling 1 — Lighting is Ryan's, in full. 6000 K is built.

  • Ryan governs light levels, uniformity intent, fixture family and counts, colour temperature, wall-washing and the calculated result. His report is the issued lighting design and the pack reproduces it as evidence, not as a proposal.
  • 6000 K throughout. The manual's 4000 / 3000 / 2700 K zone law is superseded by Ricardo's ruling and recorded as a variance. No warmer variant is offered, and no zone is re-tuned.
  • Two items go back to Ryan rather than being resolved by anyone else: the BOH minimum (58.6 lx) and the absent eat zone. Both are additions to his scope, not departures from it.
  • Where Ryan is silent, the manual speaks: controls and scenes (7.15), maintenance access (7.17), the threshold's "what is here / what is not," emergency lighting and the exterior. Each such page says "Ryan silent — manual governs" so nobody mistakes a fallback for his design.
  • Julie's RGB layer is additive, never substitutive. Ryan's base layer must be able to run alone and hold 6000 K at the calculated lux. Julie's programmable RGB sits on top as a scene and accent layer only — it may not set the prevailing surface colour, may not replace the base illumination, and every RGB run needs a driver location and an access panel. Where the two compete for the same linear run, the 6000 K base wins and the RGB is a separate co-extruded or parallel channel.

Ruling 2 — The renders are geometry evidence, not finish references

Every render in the set — Julie's five and the twelve Enscape views — breaks brand law in the same five places: royal-blue/cobalt substrate where the manual demands black; magenta or blue neon where lime is the only permitted neon; a crest carrying "FRESH • SEASONAL • QUALITY" as an arc with dates reading EST 2021 and EST 2023 (frozen at EST 2025); slogans "Unusual Pasta Attitude" / "Unusual. With Attitude." standing in for "LEVEL UP YOUR HUNGER."; and RGB on every surface, which the manual's nightclub rules forbid.

Therefore: renders appear captioned as architect reference view — geometry and intent only, brand-law non-compliant, not a finish reference. No render is presented as the finished look. The finished look lives in the Finish Schedule, the Palette page and the Lighting Schedule. The pack carries a Corrective Schedule — the seven moves that convert the rendered scheme into a compliant one.

And the four colour concepts are quarantined. Concepts 02-magenta-white-box, 03-ocean-teal and 04-crimson-white-box are white-box schemes that structurally violate 70/18/12 — they cannot be built. They appear once, in the appendix, as considered and rejected, with the reason. Presented as options to a franchisee they would be chosen, and the store would fail on proportion before it opened.

Ruling 3 — The pack is a prototype kit-of-parts, not one shop

8040 × 5600 mm plus BOH is one specific shell. If the pack presents it as "the Papa Pasta store," every future site is a redesign and there is no defensible audit baseline. So the pack is tri-partitioned on every element:

Tier Meaning Examples
Immutable Brand law. Never varies by site or franchisee. Palette and 70/18/12 · typography · crest and EST 2025 · Part 7 lighting law · 1050 mm pass height · 1100 mm counters · 275 mm footrest AFFL · one-neon rule · the 14 essentials · product law
Adaptable A dimensioned module system with min/max. The franchise asset. The 2420 / 1375 / 1450 counter modules · bay widths · bulkhead run lengths · zone adjacencies
Site-specific This shell, this landlord. Re-drawn per site. This setting-out · shopfront line · extract route · landlord signage schedule

Ruling 3 is what makes the pack saleable to a franchisee. It converts a bespoke fit-out into a repeatable standard with a named adaptation protocol.

Ruling 4 — The pack states its own issue status, and names a deputy for the absent architect

Julie's sheets are stamped INFORMATION / CONSTRUCTION TENDER — two contradictory purposes in one box — and are drawn and checked by the same hand (JC/JC). She is unreachable for three months, which is the entire procurement window.

So the pack: - Reproduces the status block verbatim and states in one front-page sentence what each source may be trusted for. - Contains zero instances of "confirm with the architect." Every deferred item becomes a decision with a named owner and a default ruling, so work does not stop waiting for a signature. - Names a deputy design authority with power to approve samples, equivalents and deviations, with a stated turnaround and deemed approval on expiry. - Carries a change-control rule: any deviation is recorded on a numbered variation sheet citing the locked sheet revision, because nobody at King Protea can issue Rev B.

What is deliberately compressed (saves ~30 pages)

  • 04b Calculated illuminance (T3). The per-area table verbatim — Sales 495.62 avg / 247.7 min / 620.0 max; Retail 521.88 / 284.6 / 672.9; POS 1013 / 979.1 / 1049; Pasta work 1155 / 546.3 / 1668; Bowl dispenser 882.10 / 372.4 / 995.3; BOH 166.20 / 58.6 / 307.9 — each with a verdict column against the BM target (Correction 2), and with the calculation assumptions flagged as not stated in the source.
  • 04c Controls & scenes (T5). BM 7.15 dayparts: open–17:00 / 17:00–21:00 / 21:00–close / fixture-only; what never dims; transitions and close-down; and how the RGB accent layer is caged inside those scenes (Ruling 1). The controls hardware does not currently exist in any source — the pack specifies the requirement (protocol, zones, scene count, accessible controller and driver locations) and marks the hardware as a named gap.

  • 04d The ruling and the variance record (T6). Ricardo's ruling printed verbatim, then the variance it creates, recorded item by item: 6000 K built against Part 7's 4000 / 3000 / 2700 K zone law, with the reason, the ruling's date, and who carries it. A variance that is recorded is a decision; the same variance left unrecorded is a defect found at audit. This page is the difference. Plus the two open returns to ACDC — BOH minimum and the eat zone — and Julie's RGB shown correctly as an additive layer with its access-panel requirement.

  • 04e Verification protocol (T6). Who measures what, where, with what, at handover, and the pass/fail tolerance per zone. 06 · Joinery, fittings & equipment schedule (4 pp)

Settles: what is made, to what size, from what build-up, with what hardware and what LED integration? Carries one line per item: eating counter, payment counter, feature counter, display unit, shelving, staff swing gate (1100 mm AFFL), bowl dispensers (430 capacity), illuminated logo boxes, bulkhead with concealed track/rail, mirror assembly, glass screen. Each with carcass, dimensions, hardware, fixing, LED/driver integration, and maintenance access — an LED sealed inside a thermoformed counter with no access panel is a demolition item at year two and a direct breach of the manual's own 7.17 maintenance rule. Plus the equipment line: hero machine locked as GUSGT108 bronze-die extruder + F-21 drying rack; negative spec stated as law — dry pasta only; waffle or panini irons are an automatic reject.

07 · External envelope, glazing & signage (6 pp) — the highest-risk section

Settles: what must be fabricated for the street, at what size, fixed where, illuminated how, powered from where — and what is the minimum set that lets this store open? The source has no elevation. So this section is explicitly split: - Evidenced (publishable today): 8040 mm shell and 3600 mm datum; the 30% glazing rule and window manifestation (BM 7.6); the Z03 threshold — Ryan is silent here, so the pack shows both positions: the manual's written 3000 K / 150 lx instruction and the recommendation of 6000 K for continuity with the ruled interior scheme, with the choice flagged rather than assumed; the approval sequence — municipal approval and the landlord's signage schedule both before fabrication (4.11); exterior neon requiring UV laminate and a budgeted five-year replacement cycle (3.14); the dark-street test at 50/20/5 m; the 6.7 audit rule that a failed or flickering sign is an automatic audit failure.

  • To be established (marked, not invented): every fascia dimension, shopfront projection, sign size, fixing method and power point. Each line reads "NOT IN SOURCE — required before fabrication", with an owner. A visible gap is a professional document; a guessed dimension is a defect with Papa Pasta's name on it.
  • The 14-essentials gate (T3): BM 7.18 gives an S01–S22 schedule and the rule that fourteen essential items open a store. The source does not carry the schedule's row content. The pack therefore publishes the gate — a checklist of the fourteen mark types by zone, each with owner and sequence — and marks sizes as confirmed against shopfront survey. Nobody can mobilise on an invented elevation; everybody can mobilise on a gate.
  • Caption device, fixed on every image page: "NEVER SCALE FROM A RENDER." The renders are the only external evidence there is, and they are the most seductive thing in the pack.

08 · Services & compliance (4 pp)

Settles: what must be signed before I trade, by whom, and what is still open? Carries: - Load & distribution: 698 W lighting plus the RGB accent layer, kitchen equipment, refrigeration, extract, menu screens and POS — against available supply. A single-line diagram with a named owner. The pack does not imply electrical design is included. - Ceiling coordination (T5): lighting, extract canopy, bulkhead track, suspended pendants, sprinklers, smoke detection, AC diffusers, speakers, CCTV all competing for the same 3600 mm ceiling. Ceiling coordination is the single most common cause of QSR fit-out delay. A101 is a reflected ceiling plan, not a coordinated ceiling — the pack says so. - Extract: the canopy is specified "complete with ducting, filtration, services" and nothing else — no route, no riser, no discharge point, no make-up air, no grease access, no landlord consent. Named as a specialist design package with a hold point, and as the highest-probability show-stopper in a mall unit. - Approvals register (T3): one row per approval with status — municipal signage, landlord design review, electrical COC, fire, food-premises health, glazing and manifestation, safety glazing, light trespass and photosensitivity (7.17), accessibility, change of use. An approvals register with honest red rows is worth more than a clean one that is wrong. Two parallel approval clocks (building plan and sign by-law), not one. - The dark-street vs light-trespass conflict, named: BM 7.7 demands the brightest thing on the street at 23:00; most centre leases and municipal light-pollution clauses cap spill. The pack resolves it by design direction — façade luminance and controlled downlight rather than spill, with the wall-washers shielded and aimed — and says so, because 7.17 already names light trespass as a compliance item.

09 · Franchisee obligations & holding the standard (5 pp)

Settles: should I buy this, and can I keep it? Carries: - The Fixed / Flex / Forbidden register (T3) — element by element, the most valuable page in any franchise pack: what is brand law, what is theirs within 70/20/10 local colour, what they may not touch. - The audit translation (T6): the 6.7 audit checklist in the auditor's own language, derived from this pack, plus 7.17's automatic-failure rule on signs. - The 14-essentials opening gate, named, with consequences. - Cost of holding the standard: 698 W lighting expressed as operating cost, not spec; the five-year neon replacement budget; bespoke spares; cleaning consumables. No capex figure is published — see §7, G5. An indicative number with no stated basis is worse than no number, because a bank will lend against it. - Variation and approval rights: process, turnaround, deemed approval, escalation, and who pays when the landlord refuses the extract route or the facade. - Exit and reinstatement: what they remove, what they make good. A franchisee buying a fit-out without knowing their exit cost is being sold a half-truth. - One uncomfortable truth, deliberately included, because credibility depends on it: the night study that proves the 23:00 promise does not exist yet.

6. MATERIAL LISTING ARCHITECTURE & THE ALTERNATIVES BOUNDARY

6.1 Where alternatives are offered (and the test)

Test: if the substitution cannot be verified by a customer standing 5 m away at 23:00, it is safe to offer. Second rule, absolute: every alternative carries a "cost of substitution" line. An alternative without that line reads as "cheaper option approved" and weakens the standard through the back door.

Offer alternatives Why it is safe
Eezi Quartz tops — 2–3 named equivalents against stated criteria Julie already wrote "or approved equivalent"; the pack matches her language
Carcass substrate (18 mm MR MDF, same humidity class) Invisible; the cook area is wet and cost pressure lands here
Substrates, setting beds, levelling, tile backer Engineering-only, invisible
Drivers and controllers — hold the fitting and photometrics fixed The lux numbers depend on the fixture, not the badge
Fixings, sealants, adhesives, trims, shelf pins Genuine commodity
Dispenser material — clear acrylic or polycarbonate The source itself says either/or
Glass build-up — toughened vs laminated-toughened Subject to the safety requirement

6.2 Where alternatives are refused outright

Colour values and 70/18/12 · typefaces and the banned list (Inter, Roboto, Arial, Helvetica) · crest, wordmark, EST 2025 and the Living Crest's three permitted variables · the neon rule and the no-nightclub test · zone colour temperature and lux floors · the 14 essential signage items · product law (dry pasta only, GUSGT108) · dimensional signatures (1100 mm counters, 1100 mm eating counter, 1050 mm pass, 275 mm footrest).

Why lighting alternatives are refused above all: listing 6000 K as an "alternative" would launder the three-way conflict into a franchisee choice, the franchisee would pick the cheaper cooler brighter one, and every store would fail the same audit the same way. It is adjudicated once, in the pack, with a named owner.
