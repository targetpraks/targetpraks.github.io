01 The verdict in one page

Ricardo's instinct is right, but not for the reason it looks like.

There are 13 lists in one folder and five of them are somebody else's product — the stock ClickUp OKR template, its two example years, its planning advice, and an empty marketing field-guide. That is 75 tasks of content that will never be Infinity Brands work. They sit beside the real registers, so every staff member opening the folder sees a menu where more than a third of the entries are noise.

The other eight lists are real work, and the data underneath is in better shape than the folder suggests. What breaks staff usability is not the count of lists. It is four specific faults, each measured below.

The four faults, in one line each:

  1. Every one of the 371 commitment rows is due on 24 November 2026 — the same date, two days from now, for all four quarters. On Wednesday the whole register turns red at once.
  2. Each key result exists four times — once per quarter, identical title, identical date, quarter hidden in a dropdown. The heaviest owner carries 97 rows.
  3. The fields that make a row actionable are the empty ones. Verifier 0/371, KPI ID 0/371, Cadence 0/371, Pillar 0/371.
  4. There is no door for any role. No BU manager can answer "what am I on the hook for this quarter, what number do I report, who checks it, and when" from this folder.

The recommendation is therefore not "delete lists". It is: separate the two populations, name the eight that remain so their order tells the story, fix the four faults, and give each role one door. Mostly reversible, in three stages.

Every commitment for every quarter of FY2027 is dated 2026-11-24. This is the space's known due-date automation writing at creation time. Its consequence is total: "due this week", "overdue", the calendar, the timeline and every notification are meaningless, and on Wednesday the register turns solid red in one stroke.

Highest-value fix in this document It is also the one that needs a live probe first, because the automation may rewrite an update as well as a create. See step S2.1.

3.2 One key result, four identical rows, no visible quarter

320 key-result rows across 32 distinct (KR, Brand, Business Unit) groups. The quarter is a dropdown: not in the title, not in the date, not in any view.

Measured and reassuring — this is not a weighting error Across all 141 objective-quarter groups, the key-result weights sum to exactly 1.00 — 141 of 141. The arithmetic underneath is correct. The fault is purely that the quarter is invisible, so a correct set of four rows looks like four copies of one row. The fix is presentational, not arithmetic.

Two smaller integrity faults ride along:

  • 10 of the 32 groups are missing quarter rows — FXL-SAL has Q3 and Q4 only, EXP-DAS has Q1–Q3. Some commitments simply stop mid-year.
  • Row identifiers disagree with their rows. 20 key-result rows carry a Brand that contradicts their own KR-ID prefix (INF-BND2291 sits on PPP; five INF-BND 12xx rows sit on EXP). One KR ID contains a line break (FXS-FXS⏎ADM); one lost the space its siblings keep.

05 The target structure — v2

5.1 The constraint that decides the design

ClickUp's API has no move endpoint. A list cannot be moved between folders programmatically. The sub-folder tree proposed in PRD v1.3 §9.1 would mean recreating every list and copying 547 tasks through a throttle that has already cost this lane a half-loaded phase once.

That trade is not worth it. A staff member's experience of "too many lists" is fixed by order and name, not by nesting. So v2 keeps one folder and uses list ordering as the structure:
