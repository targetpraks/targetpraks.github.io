Full audit: what the ClickUp build actually is

A five-agent audit of the live workspace, not of the plan. Every number below was read from the ClickUp API in one pass at 11:32 and then checked independently before it was written down.

15 Spaces 123 folders 429 lists 23 field definitions 363 tasks — 1 of them real work 3 critical · 6 high · 8 medium · 5 low

The verdict in three sentences

The skeleton is sound and the vocabulary is not. Fifteen private Spaces, 429 lists, 23 field definitions and zero private field copies is a genuinely clean structural base — better than most workspaces ever get. What sits on top of it cannot yet do its job: REP cannot mark a report submitted, the SLA desks cannot calculate a breach, the unit picklists still offer retired codes and omit five live units, 56 lists carry a dead prefix, and every team mention reaches nobody.

And there is almost nothing in it yet. 413 of 429 lists are empty, and 362 of the 363 tasks are template material — three competing store-opening plans, a superseded sales revision, 30 REP templates. The only live task in the workspace is one row in FIN Store Daily Completeness. So no dashboard, no compliance card and no Monday standup can tell the truth until the templates are quarantined.

Nothing is broken beyond repair. Every fix below is a rename, a delete of an empty object, a status set or a picklist — no task is at risk in any of them, because 361 of the 362 template tasks live in lists none of these changes touch.

How this audit was made

Five auditors ran in parallel on the same evidence, each blind to the others — a mixture of agents, not one model's opinion. One audit per dimension: structure and duplication, custom fields, the reporting cadence, names and teams, operational risk. Each was told to audit only its own slice and to answer with counts, not adjectives.

The evidence was collected first, in a single read-only pass: 1,014 API calls covering every Space, folder, list, the full custom-field stack on each of the 429 lists, per-list task counts, Space status sets, the nine workspace field definitions with their options, and the team directory. It is on disk as audit-full.json (890 KB), so every claim here can be re-derived without touching the API again.

Then I checked the auditors. Every count they reported was recomputed from the same file before it reached this page. Three statements were corrected: one overstated the DAS name count (nine names, of which one was a folder), one presented four different list totals across the morning (426 → 433 → 453 → 429) as an unexplained inconsistency when the sequence is exactly the day's work — NVP +7, BU 9 +20, then the cadence consolidation −48 +24 — and one graded something I had introduced minutes earlier. The findings below are the adjudicated set.

The findings — 22 in total

3 will stop a process from working at all, 6 break a workspace rule at scale, 8 break one in a few places, 5 are polish. Nothing here is a matter of taste.

Critical — 3

critical C1 · REP cannot say “Submitted” or “Reviewed” — the reporting workflow does not exist

Where
REP - Reporting & Notes — all 51 lists inherit the Space status set.
Evidence
The Space runs Open · in progress · in review · waiting · blocked · completed. Neither Submitted nor Reviewed is available, so a report can never be marked as submitted, and Ricardo’s review step has no state to occupy. Every weekly and monthly list in the folder set is affected.
Against
§12.3 and §9: Not Started → In Progress → Submitted → Reviewed, with only Ricardo setting Reviewed. §18 test 10 fails.
Fix
Set REP’s Space workflow to exactly those four statuses, then verify the 51 lists inherit it. This is the single change that makes the reporting build functional — and it must land before the first weekly cycle.

critical C2 · The SLA clock cannot calculate a breach on any of the 131 SLA lists

Where
BU 1 FIN - Finance (42 lists), BU 7 Brand EXP (48), BU 8 Brand TLF (41) — the full 16-field stack on each.
Evidence
First Response Due is a plain date field, not a formula. Resolution Due and SLA State do not exist anywhere in the workspace. The system stores when a task was first touched, but nothing computes when the response was due, when resolution is due, or whether the SLA is breached.
Against
§10.2–10.4 and §8 F12/F13/F16: the breach calculation is the reason the SLA desks exist. §18 test 7 cannot be evaluated either way.
Fix
Two options, and Ricardo picks: (a) implement First Response Due, Resolution Due and SLA State as ClickUp formulas — needs Business Plus; or (b) keep them as dates and compute the breach in a dashboard formula. Until one is chosen the desks record SLA work but cannot report on it.

critical C3 · Two identical SLA list sets, and one of them cannot record an SLA at all

Where
HRM &rarr; HRM - Client SLA against FIN &rarr; FIN - Client SLA.
Evidence
Both folders hold lists named SLA - EXP, SLA - TLF and SLA - Breach Log. FIN’s copies carry 16 fields; HRM’s carry 9. The HRM copies have no SLA Class, no Breach Reason, no First Response Due and no Resolved At — anything filed there is invisible to every SLA measure the workspace has.
Against
§7 one home per thing; §8 places the SLA set in FIN, EXP and TLF only. HRM was never meant to hold a second copy.
Fix
Delete the HRM folder (three empty lists) or rebuild it as a pointer list. One set of SLA lists, in the desks that own the SLA, or the company will have two answers to “are we breaching?”

High — 6

high H1 · 56 lists still carry the retired BND1 / BND2 prefix

Where
BU 7 Brand EXP (28 of 48 lists) and BU 8 Brand TLF (28 of 41).
Evidence
Every tier list in the two brand desks is named BND1 ZEE EXP Recurring, BND2 ZOR PPP Briefed-In and so on — 28 names starting BND1, 28 starting BND2, 13% of the whole workspace. All of them hold zero tasks, so the rename is loss-free.
Against
§0.3: BND1 and BND2 were retired on 16 November when the Brand Ops layer went and the desks took their brand names. A retired code in 56 live names is the loudest contradiction in the build.
Fix
Mechanical rename of the leading token to the Space code — BND1 → EXP, BND2 → TLF. It also closes half of the Briefed-In naming gap in the same pass.

high H2 · Eight foreign-brand folders, 32 lists, plus a duplicate PPP folder

Where
BU 7 Brand EXP holds ZEE/ZOR folders for TLF and PPP; BU 8 Brand TLF holds them for EXP and PPP; BU 8 Brand TLF also holds a PPP ops folder that duplicates BU 9 Brand PPP.
Evidence
32 lists sit under other brands’ tier folders (16 in each of BU 7 and BU 8), plus four PPP ops lists in BU 8 that exist again in BU 9 (PPP Store Build, PPP Kitchen, PPP Menu, PPP Trading). All 36 are empty. BU 9 itself is brand-pure.
Against
§5 and §7.2: one brand per brand desk, and the spec already says these cross-brand copies come out as each brand lands in its own Space. BU 9 has landed.
Fix
Delete the eight foreign tier folders and BU 8’s PPP folder — 9 folders, 36 lists, all empty. Each brand then has exactly one home for its tier work.

high H3 · The unit picklists still offer retired codes and cannot name five live units

Where
Workspace fields Business Unit and Department, inherited by all 429 lists.
Evidence
Both fields carry 11 options and they disagree at the tail: Department ends CORP, Business Unit ends GOV. Both still offer BND1 and BND2. Neither can express EXP, TLF, PPP, DID or NVP — five of the thirteen units the workspace actually runs.
Against
§8 F01–F03 and §12.1’s 13-unit roster. Two fields with near-identical vocabularies also violate the one-home principle the field consolidation just established.
Fix
Make Business Unit exactly the 13 unit codes; delete Department or give it a different job (a sub-team inside a unit). Until then no brand task can be tagged with its own brand — the field that routes work is broken at the source.

high H4 · All 14 Teams have zero members — every @mention notifies nobody

Where
Settings → Teams: 14 teams, 14 with 0 members.
Evidence
Names and aliases are correct — FINANCE TEAM/@fin through EXECUTIVE COMMITTEE/@exco, no BRAND OPS survivor. But a team with no members is a mention that reaches an empty room. There is also no team for NVP (blocked by the plan cap of 10 additional teams, 14 in use) and none for REP.
Against
§11.2b. Memberless teams read as an API limitation; the operational consequence is larger — the workspace has no working notification layer.
Fix
Add members in the ClickUp UI (the API cannot do it on this token), then retire DIVORCED DADS TEAM and four others to free a slot for NOVA PROPERTY TEAM/@nova.

high H5 · 362 of the 363 tasks are template content — the workspace holds one task of real work

Where
15 lists inside six Templates folders; one live task in FIN.
Evidence
BU 7 carries 204 tasks including three competing store-opening plans (Legacy Store Opening Plan (Nifty import) 100, ACDC Express Project Plan 89, Store Opening Programme 15). BU 6 SAL carries 83, including a superseded Sales Activities (2025-06 revision) of 27 steps beside the current 36. REP 30, HRM 18, MKT 17, ITC 10. The only live task in the entire workspace is one row in FIN Store Daily Completeness.
Against
§12.8 accounts for 30 template tasks in REP; the other 332 exist in no section of the spec. §7 one home per thing — the Legacy plan is a retired version still living beside its replacements.
Fix
Exclude Templates folders from every view, dashboard and My Work default before real work lands, then diff the three store-opening plans into one and delete the superseded Sales revision. Until this is done every report the workspace produces describes 363 tasks that are mostly scaffolding.

high H6 · Three status sets across 15 Spaces, and none of them is the designed one

Where
11 Spaces share one set; BU 7 and BU 8 share a re-ordered copy; BU 9 and NVP run ClickUp’s default to do/complete.
Evidence
Eight distinct status names exist workspace-wide, and no Space carries the §9 spine. The two Spaces on defaults are the two newest — built by API this week — which is exactly how drift starts. With completed the only terminal token everywhere, cancelled work and finished work occupy the same state, so completion rate is not computable.
Against
§9 and §18 test 4 (“no list shows only to do / complete”) — false twice.
Fix
Give BU 9 and NVP the six-status spine first (both empty, so free), then add the §9 set to the other 13. Add Cancelled everywhere and superseded to EXCO/MANCO, or decisions and dead work stay indistinguishable.

Medium — 8

medium M1 · 16 bare ZEE / ZOR folders promise a home and hold nothing

Where
FIN, HRM, ADM, ITC, MKT, SAL, BU 7 and BU 8 — ZOR and ZEE, two in each.
Evidence
Sixteen folders named exactly ZOR and ZEE contain zero lists, sitting directly beside the lawful ZEE - EXP / ZOR - EXP folders that do hold lists. They are a misfiling trap: two places to drop a task, one of which belongs to nothing.

medium M2 · 28 of 64 Briefed-In lists ignore the intake naming law

Where
Twelve Spaces, 64 lists ending Briefed-In.
Evidence
36 match <DEPT> <TIER> <BRAND> Briefed-In. The misses: 14 are BND1/BND2 names (closed by H1), 3 BU 9 lists with no brand token, 6 HOF lists with no brand token, 4 single-unit fora (MANCO, NVP, DID, SNR EXCO), and one DAS list. After the H1 rename the score is 50 of 64.
Against
§0 naming law. For the brandless HOF/fora/BU 9 names no standard exists — the pattern has no brandless form.
Fix
Write the second accepted shape into §0 — <UNIT> <TIER> Briefed-In for single-unit work — then rename the 13 that remain off-pattern.

medium M5 · REP’s own field set cannot record what its design asks for — and the spec argues with itself

Where
REP - Reporting & Notes — 13 standup, 14 monthly and 6 meeting lists.
Evidence
Every REP list reads exactly the nine workspace fields plus Reporting Period — a clean ten-field stack. But the standup design needs Week No, Meeting Date, Recording and five narrative sections; the monthly design needs six sections plus Month/Year; the meeting lists need recording and document links. None of those fields exists. §8.0’s fixed ten-field rule and §12’s register contradict each other.
Against
§12.2, §12.4 and §12.5 against §8.0.
Fix
Reconcile §12 with §8.0 first, then add the agreed register at REP Space level (not per list) and update the expected stack count. As built, a weekly report has nowhere to put the week number.

Low — 5

low L1 · 413 of 429 lists are empty — correct for a new build, unmanaged as a rule

Where
Workspace-wide.
Evidence
96.3% of lists hold nothing; 116 of 123 folders hold nothing; 236 lists belong to the ZEE/ZOR/HOF skeleton and all 236 are empty. Eight Spaces hold zero tasks. Normal three days in — what is missing is any rule for what happens to a list still empty after a quarter.

Repair queue — in dependency order

Ordered by what unblocks the next thing, not by clock. Each stage ends on a condition that is either true or false — no dates.

Stage 1 — Vocabulary, because everything else reads through it

REP's four-status workflow (C1); the six-status spine on BU 9 and NVP (H6); Business Unit repopulated with the 13 unit codes and Department given a job or retired (H3).

Exit: zero Spaces on ClickUp defaults, REP can express Submitted and Reviewed, and both picklists name every unit the workspace runs.

Stage 2 — Names, because a rename is free while nothing has tasks

The 56 BND1/BND2 prefixes (H1), the four SNR EXCO lists (M3), the DAS decision (M4), the ten template codes (L2), and the brandless Briefed-In shape written into §0 (M2).

Exit: a scan for every retired code — BND1, BND2, SNR EXCO, FLF, TIE, DAS — returns zero hits in any name.

Stage 3 — Duplicates, because a second home is a lost task waiting to happen

The eight foreign-brand tier folders and BU 8's PPP folder (H2), the HRM Client SLA copy (C3), the 16 bare ZEE/ZOR folders (M1), the two orphan Client Deliveries lists (M8), the misfiled TLF template (M7).

Exit: no list name appears twice in the workspace, no brand desk holds another brand's tier folder, and no folder holds zero lists.

Stage 4 — Templates, because until they are out of the counts nothing reports the truth

Exclude Templates folders from every view and dashboard (H5, L5); diff the three store-opening plans into one; delete the superseded Sales revision and the Legacy import.

Exit: one store-opening plan, one sales-activities list, and a live-task count a dashboard can quote without an asterisk.

Stage 5 — Access and the SLA clock

Team membership in the UI, then retire five teams to free the slot for NOVA PROPERTY TEAM / @nova (H4); the formula decision for the SLA clock (C2).

Exit: every mention group has members, and an SLA task can state whether it breached.
