Documents, client folders, and what sits underneath

None of them replaces a document system. One of them gets close enough to be a client's front door.

Staff loading documents and a client seeing only their own folder are two different requirements: the first is storage, the second is permission scoping. Every tool on the list does the first badly or adequately; only a handful do the second properly, and which one matters depends on whether the client folder must hold files or just work.

The money point nobody says out loud

Leaving Zoho Projects does not remove WorkDrive. It is part of the Zoho One invoice you already pay, and that invoice does not shrink when Projects goes quiet. So you are not buying a document system — you already have one. The question is only which project tool can reach into it, and every tool on this list integrates with Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive, so WorkDrive can stay the archive while the project tool holds the working file.

Client foldersCan a client see only their own space?

Yes — and three of them do it at the level you described.

What you asked for has a name in each product: a scoped external seat. The client is invited to one container and cannot see the rest of the workspace, its other clients or its portfolio. Here is exactly what each does, from their own documentation.

Where this lands

ClickUp is the only tool whose scoping unit is a folder — which is literally what you asked for: a client folder, with the client inside that folder and nowhere else, holding both the work and the documents. Nifty's project-level scoping is cleaner in the interface and tells the client even less (not even the portfolio name), but the container is a project, not a folder. Asana's scoping is the tightest of all — you can share a single task — which is brilliant for approvals and awkward for a client who needs to browse their own space.

One caution from ClickUp's own community: a guest inside a shared folder sees every task in that folder, so keep a client folder narrow rather than reusing a project folder for several clients.

The awkward finding

ClickUp is the best tool for your staff and the worst for your agents. Three hundred MCP calls a day is one busy morning of my work — I would be locked out before lunch. The Everything AI add-on fixes it and costs R318,044 a year on its own, taking ClickUp to R454,349: R170,494 more than Asana, R238,533 more than monday. If agents run this work, Nifty at R90,643 flat is the cheap answer and monday Pro at R215,816 the strong one.

What I would do

  • Keep WorkDrive. It is the document system, it already has client folders and external sharing, and it is inside a bill you are paying anyway.
  • Pick for the humans first. Staff loading files and logging time each need a real seat; that is the bill, not a seat mix.
  • Prove the MCP before you commit. One afternoon, one agent, one task, on Nifty and monday — if it cannot run on the free tier, the cheap option is not cheap.
