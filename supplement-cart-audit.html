01 What is actually in the box

The brand is not the problem. Every label carries real per-ingredient milligrams — no “proprietary blend” hiding the amounts — which already puts NeuroActive above most of the South African shelf. The problem is what the milligram numbers say once you put them beside the trials.

02 Three lines are the same molecule twice

Run the cart against what you take on a normal day and the overlap is not subtle:

  • Magnesium, three times. Glycinate in your evening routine, L-threonate in the cart, L-threonate again inside the Sleep Stack. Two bottles of the same material in one order is the clearest sign nobody checked.
  • L-theanine, twice. You already take it; the Sleep Stack adds two more bottles at 100 mg, and Neuro Mind adds 200 mg on top.
  • NAC. Vita Mito carries 200 mg beside the 600 mg-plus you already take. Not harmful, just padding.

None of this is dangerous at these doses. It is money spent on the same effect twice, which is the whole difference between a stack and a shopping basket.

03 Nothing here is for the nine-year-old

The gate put the probability at 0.015 — as close to a no as it produces. The reason is not caution, it is the ingredient list:

  • Noopept, vinpocetine and huperzine A have no paediatric data at all. Noopept has no human trial outside Russian stroke and impairment reports. None of the three belongs in a child's body on the strength of a store page.
  • NMN has no paediatric trials. It is an adult longevity bet with no clinical outcome even in adults.
  • Magnesium L-threonate was tested in adults aged 18–45. Your own protocol keeps him on glycinate for exactly this reason.
  • Apigenin has never been trialled for sleep in humans at any age.

What his protocol already says, and what this catalogue does not improve on: fatty fish two to three times a week for DHA, vitamin D tested before it is supplemented, magnesium glycinate, and melatonin as the one sleep option with paediatric trials behind it — prescribed, not bought. He is on prescription medication; anything added goes past his paediatrician first, and a synthetic nootropic blend is the last thing to put in that queue.

04 The brand: keep it, drop the blends

NeuroActive is a real South African operation with transparent labels, a stated batch-testing policy (identity, purity, heavy metals) and COAs available on request. Its singles are priced well: this NMN at R399 for 30 g works out near half the per-gram price of Supplement Labs, and its threonate undercuts imported Magtein.

Three honest reservations, none of them a reason to leave: the COAs are on request rather than downloadable per lot; the imported brands name the trademarked Magtein® on the label while this one says only “magnesium L-threonate”; and the store's own buying guides read as affiliate rankings, not evidence. Switching brand would buy you paperwork, not a better outcome. Changing what is in the basket buys the outcome.

If you want to leave the site with something, the creatine 600 g at R189 is the best value on it — 120 servings at 5 g for R1.58 a serving — and it is a restock of something already in your protocol, not a new experiment.

05 What I would do

Empty the cart. If the itch to try one thing is stronger than the evidence, buy the lion's mane 1,000 mg single at R174 — it is the one ingredient here that adds a mechanism you do not already have, at a dose close to what was tested, for a third of the Brain Stack's price, and you can drop it after eight weeks without having paid for five other bottles.

The R2,090 is better spent on the thing your own protocol already flags and this store cannot sell: a sleep study. Snoring, unrefreshing sleep and daytime fatigue in a man carrying extra weight are the highest-yield unanswered question in your file, and no capsule on this page touches it.

Evidence

  • NMN safety and metabolic outcomes, systematic review and meta-analysis, Nutrients 2026 — 15 RCTs, 250–2,000 mg/day, up to 24 weeks: no effect on weight, BMI, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids or systolic BP.
  • Magnesium L-threonate (Magtein®) cognition and sleep RCT, Frontiers in Nutrition 2025 — n=100, 2 g/day, 6 weeks, overall cognition p=0.043; funded by Threotech, the ingredient's patent holder.
  • Magnesium L-threonate sleep RCT, Sleep Medicine X 2024 — n=80, 1 g/day, 21 days; funded by AIDP, the Magtein supplier.
  • Bacopa network meta-analysis 2026 — 29 RCTs, n=2,107; the working-memory benefit sits at 600 mg/day of extract and above, three to six times the Neuro Mind dose.
  • Lion's mane RCT 2026 — n=84, 1.8 g/day, 4 weeks; the blend beat lion's mane alone on anxiety, every group improved.
  • Noopept in mild cognitive impairment after stroke — one of seven PubMed records for the compound in total, none an English-language trial in healthy adults.
  • Illicit smart drugs and nootropics in Europe and Australia — where unregulated compounds like noopept turn up in the literature.
  • Apigenin and sleep: a PubMed search for apigenin with sleep and trial returns no human study; the only sedative data is a mouse study of valerian constituents (PMID 21154200).
