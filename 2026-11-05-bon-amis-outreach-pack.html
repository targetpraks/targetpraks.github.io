Follow-up rhythm

  1. Day 3 — one phone call, one voicemail. No second email.
  2. Day 7 — a two-line reply on the same thread: "Following up on the below — is Bon Amis something you can discuss, or should I speak to someone else?"
  3. Day 14 — walk in. The trail park café is open seven days; the estate gate is not a fortress.

What not to put in writing

  • Do not say the estate is desperate. Even if it reads that way, it insults the person holding the mandate.
  • Do not name a rent, a budget or a capex figure. First number spoken becomes the anchor, and it will be the wrong one.
  • Do not mention the auction history in detail. "I understand it was brought to market" is enough; a recital of failed sales makes you look like a bargain hunter.
  • Do not promise a liquor licence you do not have. Ask whether one exists.
  • Do not offer a partnership before you have seen the kitchen. Warm, not committed.
One thing to settle before you send: the signature says "we fund the fit-out ourselves" in the attachment. If that is a cash call you have not made yet, change it to "we will fund the fit-out" or take the line out. It is the one sentence a landlord will hold you to.
