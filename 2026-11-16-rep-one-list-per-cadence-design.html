One list per cadence

The weekly and monthly documents say the same thing twice, and ClickUp is bent against it: the period is already the record and the unit is already the row. Here is that shape, mapped one-to-one onto REP.

The verdict

Yes to one list per cadence — and the period becomes the task, the unit becomes the subtask. Both Superhuman docs already work this way. Today's ClickUp does the inverse: fourteen BU lists, so seeing one week means opening fourteen lists.

1. What the two documents actually do

Weekly — Weekly BU Team Meetings

One page per BU, and on each page a table with one row per week. Columns: Week No (40–52) · Meeting Date · Reporting (the written report) · Meeting Recording (WorkDrive link) · Status (Not started / In progress / Concluded) · Comment. The report itself is five fixed sections per person: Top 5 Deliverables · Achievements for the past week · Challenges & actions taken · Focus for the week ahead · Support needed.

Monthly — INF HOF Monthly Reporting

One table per month (May–December 2026), one row per EXCO seat. Columns: Report Name · Month · Year · Status (Not started / In Progress / Submitted / Reviewed) · EXCO Report · Last Updated · Buss Unit · MEMBER plus the six body columns: Executive Summary · Achievements · Challenges & Actions · KPI Overview · Focus for Next Month · Support Required. The rules are explicit: the owner sets Submitted, and only Ricardo sets Reviewed.

The pattern, stated once

cadence → period is the container → unit is the row → status is the handshake → fixed sections are the body. Weekly and monthly disagree only on vocabulary and roster, never on structure. A ClickUp list is exactly a container of rows, so the fit is direct.

3. Three things this fixes that a list-per-BU never could

  1. The Monday number. One week task with 13 subtasks answers "who is in?" at a glance — parent auto-closes when every subtask concludes. Today that answer costs fourteen lists.
  2. The handshake is native. Monthly's Submitted → Reviewed is a status transition, owned by different people. It replaces the awkward separate feedback object, and it is the same rule the doc states in words.
  3. Statuses finally fit the work. Each cadence gets its own status set, because each has its own vocabulary — weekly concludes, monthly gets submitted and reviewed. One status set for the whole workspace was the audit's critical finding; one set per cadence is the fix.

Where your instinct needs one correction

Keep BUS Unit as a field on the subtask, not just a name. Subtasks are hidden in every view by default and each dashboard widget needs "include subtasks" switched on — so the field is what keeps the BU filterable and countable. Save the views with subtasks shown, grouped by status.

And the rosters are not the same list. Weekly is the 13 operating units (FIN · HRM · ADM · ITC · MKT · SAL · EXP · TLF · PPP · DID · FXS · FXL · NVP). Monthly HOF is the 12 EXCO seats (COO · CFO · CHO · CTO · MD · CRO · CAO · CMO · Dynamics · EXP GM · TLF/PPP GM · FXS GM). A single "BU" subtask list would quietly mix them.

4. What moves, and what does not

REP holds 70 tasks across those three folders today — 14 for week 47 standups, 14 for week 47 reports, and 42 monthly (14 units × 3 months). Every one is already a unit × period row, so the move is a re-parent, not a conversion: each becomes a subtask of its period task, with BUS Unit and Period Date stamped from the list it came from. Nothing is deleted.

Then a second pass, if you want it: the ZOR monthly documents (TLF Monthly ZOR Reporting, ACDC Express Monthly ZOR Reporting) are the brand-GM tier of the same monthly rhythm — they belong in the same monthly list as a second row-set, not in their own folder.
