The sign that brought you

Of all the contacts two charts can make, there is one that astrologers have circled for centuries: one person's Sun coming to rest on the other's Moon. Yours sits at just over half a degree apart — 0.54°. His Sun at 26.9° Leo; her Moon at 26.4° Leo.

At that orb the two bodies read as a single point of light. In the old language of the craft the sentence is a short one: his sunlight falls exactly on her heart. He is the warmth; she is where the warmth lands. It is the signature of recognition — the feeling, on first meeting, of having known someone long before you met.

In the Four Pillars you are both Wood day masters — he Yi Wood, the graceful vine; she Jia Wood, the tall tree: two trees of one forest.

How this page was made

Method. Positions were cast with a deterministic ephemeris engine (Swiss-Ephemeris-grade) from the couple's birth data on file — Johannesburg for him, Rhodes for her. The natal charts use each person's recorded birth time; the wedding chart was cast for the sky over Rhodes that day at local noon, as the ceremony time was not recorded.

Aspects. The synastry contacts are the angles between the two natal charts (orb = how close the angle is, in degrees), plus the composite chart of the marriage itself. Positions are precise to roughly an arcminute in the signs and houses used here.

Scores. No compatibility score or "harmony index" appears anywhere on this page. That metric is a crude heuristic the engine itself disclaims; the aspects above are the real content, and the reading is a traditional one.

A traditional-reading keepsake, made for love and reflection — not advice, not science.
