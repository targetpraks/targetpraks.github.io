1 What iMPlayer can and cannot do in our setup

iMPlayer is a standalone player (implayer.tv, v2.20.1, App Store listing). It never links to the Snappier Server the way the Snappier IPTV app does — I tested it directly against our server: the Xtream API answers {"user_info":{"auth":0}} for any client that cannot present the server’s app key. So the server will not serve iMPlayer a playlist or a guide through its Xtream login.

That leaves exactly two correct ways to fill iMPlayer, and both are below. Everything else people do (typing the old provider host, or an old M3U URL) is what broke the iPad.

3 What is wrong right now

Fault 1 The iPad’s playlist points at a dead host.

Device “RM IPad” (iPad16,8) carries a playlist named EVESTV IPTV 4 pointing at line.quicksolve1.com, which does not answer at all. Nothing will play from it until it is replaced.

Fault 2 Two of our five device logins serve an empty TV guide.

Measured on the server just now: main, kids and ipad2 return 28.4 MB (1,918 channels, ~96,700 programmes). ipad and sports return 88 bytes — an empty guide shell. The cause is the provider’s own guide feed flapping: it delivered 6,131 channels at 21:01 yesterday and 0 channels at 10:00 today, and the server cached the empty result for those two logins. Channel-by-channel data is not the issue — all four logins publish the identical 19,236-channel lineup.

Fault 3 The iPad is unmanaged, so the dashboard cannot fix it remotely.

The device is Premium/unmanaged: its playlists can only be edited on the iPad itself. The management code nqmfz1w (“RM iPad”, usage 0/3) is what converts it to a managed device — until that is entered, Cloud Management can do nothing for it.

Fault 4 iMPlayer on the iPad costs a whole provider line.

Because iMPlayer talks straight to the provider, it occupies account 2’s single connection. Account 2 is also the kids TV’s line — so the iPad and the kids TV cannot both watch. Everything else in the house goes through the server, which is what keeps that bookkeeping sane.
