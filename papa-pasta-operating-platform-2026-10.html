Part one

The verdict, before the argument

Do not buy a new platform. You already own the best one for this job and have not switched it on.

Zoho Learn is the record. A branded front page is the face. A costed model holds the arithmetic.

Three layers, one home each. Learn holds the SOPs and the training, because it is the only one of the five that can prove a person read a specific version on a specific date — which is the entire difference between a franchise and a folder of good intentions.

The front page is the direction. A short, brand-true page that says what Papa Pasta is and refuses, written once and linked from everywhere. That is days of work, not a licence.

The formulas never go in the manual. Method in the manual, arithmetic in the model, NDA over both. A knowledge base is the wrong place to keep the thing an ex-employee could copy.

Total score out of 100, ten dimensions that matter in a pasta store: Zoho Learn 83, Build our own 64, Confluence 58, Google Sites 56, Notion 53. Scores are a reading of the evidence below, not a specification sheet.

The clock. Store one opens on 13 November — 18 days from this brief. Nothing here requires a migration to be useful this month: one manual, one mandatory read, one completion report. That is a week of writing, and it is the only part that cannot slip.

Part two

Four doors, not one

The question “which platform holds all our work” hides four different jobs with four different readers. Answering it as one job is how companies end up with a beautiful Notion workspace that nobody is accountable in.

Direction

What Papa Pasta is, what it refuses, how it makes money, and the twelve decisions nobody re-opens on a Tuesday.

Read by: Read once a quarter, and by every new manager and franchisee in week one.

The front page

Formulas

Dough hydration, sauce ratios, portion weights, yields, supplier prices, gross profit per plate — the Golden Balance numbers.

Read by: Read by two people, never by the internet, and changed only by Ricardo.

A costed model

SOPs

Open, close, prep, wok, extruder, cleaning, food safety, cash-up, complaints. The store's day, written down.

Read by: Read at 06:40 on a phone by someone who has never made pasta.

The knowledge base

Training

Crew induction, station sign-off, shift-lead and store-manager certification — with proof of completion.

Read by: Assigned, tested, scored, and reported per store. A franchisee must be able to prove it.

The training platform

The trap is that one tool can appear to hold all four. It can — the way a shared drive holds all four, which is to say the words are there and the obligations are not. Direction has one reader per quarter; SOPs have one reader per shift; formulas have two readers ever; training has a reader who must be measured. Those four cadences cannot live in one surface without one of them being badly served, and it is always the shift.

The number to watch is not the licence

If store crew have to become full Zoho users to read a page, the cost is not Learn's — it is the Zoho One all-employee seat at R518 a head a month. Ten crew is RR5 180 a month, sixty is RR31 080. Learn's external portals exist precisely so that the person who needs to read the closing procedure does not need a licence to do it. Get that boundary right once and the whole model holds.

Part six

How each one fails

Every option here is in business because it works for somebody. The useful question is the failure mode you would actually hit.

Zoho Learn — It looks like an intranet, so it gets used like one.

Left alone it becomes a cabinet: twelve tidy manuals, opened by nobody, because nothing on the floor points at it. The front page and a weekly push are not decoration — they are the difference between a knowledge base and a filing cabinet. And the design will never be the manual we want; that work sits in front of it.

Notion — It is a writing tool people love and an operating system nobody is accountable in.

No mandatory reads, no completion records, no per-store reporting. Franchise compliance becomes a WhatsApp screenshot and a promise. Cheap at three people, and the store floor costs a full member seat each because a guest cannot be made to read anything.

Confluence — Enterprise wiki gravity.

Spaces multiply until the search returns four versions of the closing procedure, and the free guests who read it are exactly the people whose training record you cannot produce. It is the strongest of the five at versioning and the weakest at the actual job, which is a nineteen-year-old opening the right page at 06:40.

Google Sites — The folder where versions go to die.

Three files called Closing, Closing-final, Closing-final-v2, and no way to say which one the store is running. Costs nothing because it promises nothing.

Build our own — 18 days is not enough, and every month after that you own plumbing instead of content.

An operating system for six stores is a real product: sign-in, mobile, permissions, backups, an editor, versioning. We would be maintaining software that Zoho already sells inside a licence we have paid for. The exception is a thin, beautiful front page over the top — that is days of work and it is worth doing, which is why it appears in the recommendation and not in this row.

The order of work, by dependency

  1. Store Open and Food Safety first — they are needed on 13 November, and they double as the template every later chapter copies: heading, steps, one photo per step, a sign-off at the end.
  2. The front page skeleton in the same week — chapter titles and chapter one only. It is the surface that makes the twelve chapters feel like a company rather than a folder, and it costs days.
  3. The costed model next — because every price on the menu is waiting on it, and because it is the one artefact that must never be public.
  4. Training, once there are three SOPs to train on — a course around two half-written procedures teaches people the wrong procedure confidently.

Everything above is one system with three surfaces, not three systems. The record is Learn. The face is a page we control. The arithmetic is a model with two readers.

What is actually left for Ricardo to decide

  1. Approve the layer split — Learn as the record, a front page as the face, the costed model for the formulas. This is the only decision that gates the rest.
  2. Name the two people who own chapters 07 and 09 — store open and food safety. Written by committee in eighteen days is written by nobody.
  3. Confirm the crew boundary — that store staff read through Learn portals rather than a full Zoho seat, which is the difference between R0 and RR5 180 a month at store one.

  • Zoho Learn pricing — G2 — Professional at US$3 per user / month.
  • Notion — plans — Free, Plus US$10, Business US$20 per member / month; granular database permissions only on Business.
  • Confluence — plans — Standard US$5.78 and Premium US$11.16 per user / month, annual billing.
  • Frankfurter — FX reference — US$1 = R16.6291 on 26 October 2026.

Assumptions, stated plainly

  • Readers, year one: 3 office users, 10 store crew, 2 franchise partners. Year three: 6 office users, 60 store crew, 6 partners with roughly 60 staff between them.
  • Zoho One is already licensed across the group, so Learn's first five users and one portal carry no incremental cost. The six-seat year-three figure counts one seat over that allowance plus one partner portal per partner at the published Professional rate, which is an estimate — Zoho quotes portal add-ons.
  • Notion is costed with every reader as a member because it has no way to oblige a guest to read anything. Confluence is costed with crew as free guests for the same reason, which is why it looks cheap.
  • Google Sites is costed at zero because the Workspace seats already exist. Its weakness is not price.
  • Build-our-own carries no rand figure — 300–500 build hours plus ongoing plumbing is a real cost that does not belong in a table next to licence fees pretending to be comparable.
  • Prices are list prices in US dollars, converted at the 26 October 2026 rate, before South African VAT.
