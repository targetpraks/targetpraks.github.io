Build vs buy · part two

Forking a repo instead of starting fresh: two to three times cheaper, and you still build the one thing you actually need

Every number here was counted in the cloned repositories today, not taken from their marketing pages. Seven candidates, four of them inspected closely.

Verdict: a fork is the right call if the goal is to replace Zoho with a real product and you accept running someone else's stack. It is the wrong call if the goal was a board with our logins — and either way, no fork on the list has an approval or sign-off state, which is the gap that already ruled out ClickUp.

The short answer

Dev-days, one strong developer with an agent. ESTIMATE — the measured part is the repository counts below. The fork route is cheaper because 150,000+ lines of board, gantt, filters and permissions arrive free; it is not free of cost, only of typing.

What a fork gives, and what it takes

You get, for free

  • ~150k–300k lines of finished behaviour: boards, gantt, filters, saved views, sharing, teams, imports, API, webhooks.
  • Migrations that have survived real users, and hundreds of tests.
  • Real OIDC in Vikunja — a session bridge to Constellation is a genuine afternoon's work, not a rewrite.
  • No licence fee. Ever.

You pay, forever

  • A second stack. Go + Vue, or TS + Django — neither is Next 15 / React 19 / Prisma. Your team's knowledge splits.
  • The rebase bill. Vikunja shipped 430 commits in 28 days; Plane 74. Merge monthly or inherit every bug they fix.
  • AGPL-3.0. Everyone who uses it over the network must be offered the source. Internal-only is survivable, client-facing is a legal question for you, not for me.
  • Their data model. Org → venture → department → staff scoping is ours, not theirs; you bend one to the other either way.
The finding that decides it: Vikunja contains zero lines matching approval or sign-off — grep -rli 'approval\|sign[- ]off' → 0 files. It has gantt, time tracking and webhooks, and no approval state. Focalboard is worse, and Plane's approval objects are project-level, not the media sign-off our teams need. So the feature we already decided we cannot live without stays a custom build on every route — and inside a fork you write it in Go and Vue, in someone else's code, and re-apply it at every merge.

How a fork actually ends

Three fates, and only one of them is cheap.

FateCostWhat it looks like
Live fork
track upstream		 2–4 d/month You merge upstream every month, re-apply your approvals patch, re-test. Honest and expensive.
Frozen fork
pin a release		 6–11 d/year Cheap until a security advisory lands in a dependency you can no longer upgrade cleanly.
Orphan
the usual ending		 sunk The person who knew the fork leaves. Nobody merges. It keeps running until it doesn't.

So: which route

If the goal is…RouteWhy
Replace Zoho with a real product we own Fork Vikunja OIDC already there, Go monolith is one binary next to a database, MIT-grade operating cost. Budget the rebase, and build approvals once in Go.
Something that looks and feels modern for the GMs Fork Plane Best UI of the list and a Next.js frontend that matches our skill set — but Django on the back, so two runtimes to operate.
Our logins, our data, no second system Fresh, thin A board inside Constellation over Zoho's API: 2–3 weeks, one stack, one source of truth. Forking cannot give you this — it is the one thing a fork structurally cannot do.
Proofing and client sign-off Neither No fork on this list does it. Buy that layer (Filestage) or build it inside whichever route you pick.

My call

Fork beats fresh on cost — 18–31 dev-days against 41–72 — and loses on fit. If you want a real ClickUp replacement rather than a view, Vikunja is the fork: AGPL, active, OIDC-ready, and it replaces Zoho instead of sitting beside it.

If you are not ready to run a second product, do not fork at all — the 2–3 week thin layer over Zoho is still the cheapest thing that ends the complaint, and it is the only route that keeps one login and one record of work.

Before either: a two-day spike. Stand Vikunja up behind Constellation's login and put one approvals screen in it. Two days tells us more than this page can, and it costs nothing we cannot throw away. Nothing gets deployed or paid for without your word.
