The shape of the visit

Fourteen nights is a long visit, and there is a newborn in the house. The plan is built on one rhythm: one big thing every second day, home in between.

That gives the couple six or seven real experiences, gives Julie and Sofia quiet mornings, and keeps you out of the car every day. Their meals split cleanly: With us day trips and lunches the baby can join, On their own the late dinners, the mountain, the city and the road trip.

Two practical rules that make the whole fortnight work:

  • No two late nights back to back. The four of you cannot carry a tasting menu at 20:00 and a 07:00 start the next morning, and Julie will not thank you for it.
  • Nobody drives a wine day. Bolt or Uber for every Winelands and Constantia lunch — a chauffeur day is cheaper than one broken bottle of something serious.

Donnie and Gianna are back at school during this visit (Western Cape term 1 starts mid-January — check the WCED calendar), so the daytime rhythm is you, Julie, Sofia and the two guests.

Three ways to shape it

The three options below share the same first week. They diverge on the one decision that matters: does the fortnight spend its big money on the table, on the West Coast, or on the bush?

With us, and on their own

Worth doing with you

  • Cape Point day — Chapman’s Peak, the Cape of Good Hope, Boulders. A pram handles all of it; leave at 08:30.
  • Constantia lunch — Groot Constantia tasting then Beyond. Twenty-five minutes from your door.
  • One Winelands lunch — Delaire Graff on Helshoogte, gardens at Babylonstoren on the way back.
  • Kirstenbosch late afternoon — the gardens take a pram, and the summer sunset concerts are the easiest family evening in the city.
  • A braai at home with the whole family. For a chef, a South African butcher and a fire is an experience too.

Better on their own

  • Table Mountain first car up — the cableway closes in wind, so take the first clear morning.
  • The city bowl — Zeitz MOCAA, the Waterfront, Company’s Garden, Bo-Kaap spice shops, Truth Coffee.
  • Saturday markets — the Neighbourhood Market at the Old Biscuit Mill and Oranjezicht City Farm Market. A chef will lose two hours happily.
  • Every dinner after 19:00 — La Colombe, FYN, Salsify, the Mount Nelson counter. Sofia does not do tasting menus.
  • The West Coast night and the safari — both are car days, and both are better as two.
  • Robben Island if they want the history — ferries book out, and it takes half a day.

If they take the West Coast

The route, in order, with the two things that must be booked:

  1. Out on the R27 at 09:00. West Coast National Park gate, then Kraalbaai on the Langebaan lagoon — white sand, shallow warm water, no surf.
  2. Die Strandloper, Langebaan, 12:30. Ten courses of fish, mussels and snoek straight off the fire on the beach. R440 a head, no walk-ins, bookings open six months ahead — so January is bookable now. 083 227 7195.
  3. Paternoster by 16:00. Whitewashed cottages, a working fishing beach, the best sunset on that coast. One night.
  4. Wolfgat is the reason a chef would want Paternoster — strandveld tasting menu, R1,750, twenty seats a sitting — and it is sold out for every lunch in your window. Ask to be waitlisted; otherwise it is a reason to come back.
  5. Cape Columbine lighthouse and the beach on the second morning, then !Khwa ttu — San culture centre, good lunch, an hour from home — and back through Darling by 18:00.

Wind is the one variable: January afternoons on that coast can blow hard. Book the Strandloper lunch for the Tuesday and keep the Wednesday flexible.
