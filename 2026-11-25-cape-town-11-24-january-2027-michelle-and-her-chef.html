The shape of the visit

Eleven full days on the ground is still a long visit, and there is a newborn in the house. The plan is built on one rhythm: one big thing every second day, home in between.

The flight buys you one free decision: arrival day is a write-off. They land on Tuesday the 12th, and nothing is booked that evening — a tasting menu on the night you clear customs is the fastest way to ruin the first three days. First proper outing is Wednesday the 13th.

That leaves nine workable days, which is five or six real experiences plus a road trip, and still gives Julie and Sofia quiet mornings. You are in on nearly all of it. The split is not us-and-them, it is day and evening: daytime outings are the four of you with Sofia in the pram, evening tasting menus are the four of you with a sitter, and the only leg that is genuinely adults-only is the bush.

The one constraint worth being honest about: option A has eight restaurant meals, five of them after dark. Five sitter nights in twelve days with a three-month-old is a lot to ask of Julie — pick three and give the other two to Michelle and Fabrizio as a couple, or move them to lunches. Every table below is bookable for four covers on the date shown, so nothing is lost by deciding late.

Two practical rules that make the whole fortnight work:

  • No two late nights back to back. The four of you cannot carry a tasting menu at 20:00 and a 07:00 start the next morning, and Julie will not thank you for it.
  • Nobody drives a wine day. Bolt or Uber for every Winelands and Constantia lunch — a chauffeur day is cheaper than one broken bottle of something serious.

Donnie and Gianna are back at school during this visit (Western Cape term 1 starts mid-January — check the WCED calendar), so the daytime rhythm is you, Julie, Sofia and the two guests.

Three ways to shape it

The three options below share the same first week. They diverge on the one decision that matters: does the fortnight spend its big money on the table, on the West Coast, or on the bush?

Together, and where you sit out

Do with them — most of it

  • Cape Point day — Chapman’s Peak, the Cape of Good Hope, Boulders. A pram handles all of it; leave at 08:30 and you are home by mid-afternoon.
  • Constantia lunch — Groot Constantia tasting then Beyond. Twenty-five minutes from your door, and the most pram-friendly estate on the peninsula.
  • Both Winelands lunches — Delaire Graff and Tokara, with Babylonstoren on the way home. Long lunches are where a three-month-old is easiest: shaded, quiet, no schedule.
  • The city day — Table Mountain first car up, Company’s Garden, Bo-Kaap spice shops, the Waterfront. First car is the trick: up at 08:30, home by lunch.
  • Saturday markets — the Neighbourhood Market at the Old Biscuit Mill and Oranjezicht City Farm Market. Fabrizio will lose two hours happily; a pram handles both.
  • Kirstenbosch late afternoon and a braai at home. For Fabrizio, a South African butcher and a fire is an experience too.

The few things to leave them

  • The bush — game lodges bar under-4s from drives, so the safari leg is theirs by rule, not by choice. One or two nights.
  • One late dinner — pick the single evening you would rather stay home, and let them take Zeitz MOCAA and dinner alone that day.
  • Robben Island if they want the history — ferries book out, it eats half a day, and it is no place for a pram.
  • The Paternoster night, optionally — they will love it as a twosome, but Sofia travels well at three months and the coast is a better family memory. Your call on the day.

If they take the West Coast

The route, in order, with the two things that must be booked:

  1. Out on the R27 at 09:00. West Coast National Park gate, then Kraalbaai on the Langebaan lagoon — white sand, shallow warm water, no surf.
  2. Die Strandloper, Langebaan, 12:30. Ten courses of fish, mussels and snoek straight off the fire on the beach. R440 a head, no walk-ins, bookings open six months ahead — so January is bookable now. 083 227 7195.
  3. Paternoster by 16:00. Whitewashed cottages, a working fishing beach, the best sunset on that coast. One night.
  4. Wolfgat is the reason Fabrizio would want Paternoster — strandveld tasting menu, R1,750, twenty seats a sitting — and it is sold out for every lunch in your window. Ask to be waitlisted; otherwise it is a reason to come back.
  5. Cape Columbine lighthouse and the beach on the second morning, then !Khwa ttu — San culture centre, good lunch, an hour from home — and back through Darling by 18:00.

Wind is the one variable: January afternoons on that coast can blow hard. And Die Strandloper only runs Wednesday to Sunday (the site says so for the current month), which is exactly why the coast leg sits on Wednesday 20 to Thursday 21 rather than earlier in the week. Book the lunch for the Wednesday and keep the Thursday loose.

Twenty rooms worth his time, what each one has actually won, and six bottles to chase while he is here. Everything below is a result from the 2026 awards cycle, not an opinion dressed up as one.

How the three sets of awards work

  • Eat Out Woolworths Restaurant Awards — South Africa’s benchmark. Seven anonymous judges eat across the country over eight months and score out of 100; stars follow the score: three stars 90+, two stars 80–89, one star 70–79. The 2026 results were announced at the Baxter Theatre in Cape Town on 23 March 2026: 66 starred restaurants nationally, only eight with three stars. the full 2026 winners
  • The World’s 50 Best Restaurants — 1,100 international voters across 28 regions. The 2026 list was announced in Lima in November 2026, with Asador Etxebarri in Spain at No. 1. Three Cape Town rooms made it: La Colombe 35th (and Best Restaurant in Africa), FYN 65th, Salsify 71st — the last two on the extended 51–100 list. theworlds50best.com
  • The World Restaurant Awards — the Paris ceremony that ran in 2019 and named Wolfgat the best restaurant in the world. A twenty-seat room in a fishing village, beating every tasting-menu palace in Europe. wolfgat.co.za

Three things to tell him at the braai

  • Wolfgat won the world. In February 2019, a twenty-seat room in a fishing village took the World Restaurant Award for best restaurant on earth. Paternoster has 2,000 people in it.
  • La Colombe’s three-year arc: 49th in the world in 2024, out of the top 50 in 2025 (55th), back to 35th and best in Africa in 2026. That is what a kitchen rebuilds for.
  • Napoleon’s last drink was South African. He refused everything else on Saint Helena and asked for Constantia. The wine is still made, and you can buy it in Constantia for about the price of a good dinner.
