Getting espetada and souvla out of an insert

Cheapest way: a rotisserie kit

The insert gives you the firebox and the grid. Add a Megamaster universal rotisserie kit — R999 and you have one powered spit shaft: a leg of lamb, a chicken, a whole fish. Espetada skewers then lie across the grid, turned by hand.

Proper way: an insert with the rotisserie built in

Jetmaster 1000 mm Entertainer Deluxe Rotisserie Hybrid — R17 499 comes as a rotisserie insert out of the box. Smaller firebox than a 1200, but the spit is engineered for the unit rather than bolted on.

The skewer-machine way

Souvla Braai Built In — R14 999 is the only unit with six skewers + flat grill + spit fork on one gearbox. But it is 850 mm wide, so your brick cheeks get built in to suit it. Best of the three for souvla, worst for fitting what you already have.

What I would not do

Do not bolt a spit onto an insert with a plastic-bodied motor facing the fire. Every option above puts the motor outside the heat path, at the front or side of the brickwork, with the gearbox shaft through the firebox wall.

The flue through the thatch — this is the part that matters

Your roof is thatch, so the chimney is not cosmetic: embers out of the top, or heat through a thin flue wall, are the two ways a braai sets a thatch roof alight. The dimensions below are the ones the Thatchers Association of South Africa quotes from SANS 10400-V. Your insurer and the City may ask for more, and their word beats this page — ask for it in writing before anyone cuts the roof.

RequirementDimension

Check before you buy

  • Does the brick breast contain an actual flue, or does it dead-end above the firebox? Run a torch up it or drop a plumb line.
  • Is that flue lined? A cracked or unlined stack leaks heat into the brick and the thatch beside it.
  • Does the insert's outlet (usually 350 mm) line up with what is in the breast? If not, you need a gather — about R2 990 for the 1200 Afflux on the Italfire range.
  • Where does the stack exit relative to the ridge, and is there a spark arrestor at least 700 mm below the top?

What to put in the written scope

  • New stainless flue from the insert outlet to 1 m above the thatch, with a gather at the braai.
  • Spark arrestor, plus a cap that keeps rain and birds out — different parts, sometimes one product.
  • 250 mm flashing at the roof penetration and a fire stop at the junction.
  • 200 mm solid brick around the flue where it passes the thatch.
  • The sweep date and findings written down, and the insurer's conditions quoted in the same document.

Measure these six things and I will pick the exact model

  1. Clear opening width — between the brick cheeks, in mm
  2. Clear opening height — hearth ledge to the underside of the lintel
  3. Cavity depth — front face of brick to the back wall
  4. Flue outlet diameter — measure the hole in the top of the firebox (300 or 350 mm typical)
  5. Whether that outlet actually connects to a flue up through the breast, or dead-ends in brick
  6. Hearth projection in front of the opening (want 400–500 mm of non-combustible)
