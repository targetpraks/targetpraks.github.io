0.1 The four shape rules that follow from the table

  1. A business unit is BU n CODE - Name. Number first, so the list sorts in Ricardo’s order without anyone dragging anything. ClickUp accepts orderindex writes and ignores them — the name is the only order that survives.
  2. A corporate service is CODE - Name. FXS - INFX Solutions and FXL - INFX Labs. COR 1 and COR 2 replace the earlier “BU 9 / BU 10” reading: the two INFX desks sit beside the departments, not inside them.

  4. A forum is its own name. EXCO, MANCO, SNR EXCO. Decisions are records, not tasks: each forum keeps its own Decisions register, Actions list, Notes and Briefed-In intake.

1. The decision this document asks for

Ricardo, three things:

  1. The department model in §5 replaces the function model entirely. Every unit becomes a Space: the eight operating units — six departments and two brand-ops divisions — plus the two INFX desks, three fora and reporting. Brands stop being a dropdown and become folders where the work is genuinely per-brand.
  2. Free Forever cannot hold this design — though it can hold half of it. Not “it will be tight”: eleven Spaces against five, 60 custom-field uses against roughly 460–960 (20 fields × the 23–48 lists §6 defines), 100 automation actions a month against an SLA system that needs thousands, and 1,000 chat messages for the entire workspace, never resetting. §15’s five-slot bootstrap does put all eight units in front of people — it cannot do SLA, chat or reporting, and §17 puts the number on the fix.
  3. SLA needs Business, not Core. ClickUp has no native SLA object — no field, no report, no setting. A help-centre search for “SLA” returns six results and none of them is a feature. Breach tracking is assembled from formula fields, automations and Time in Status, and automation conditions begin at Business. §10 is the build.

Everything else in this document is how.

4. Five architecture laws

These decide every row in §5–§14. If a detail later contradicts a law, the law wins or the law is formally revised.

Law 1 — A Space is a desk, not a project and not a brand. You run twelve desks. Spaces mirror desks because that is where accountability, privacy and reporting boundaries already sit: a Finance person lives in Finance, and Finance’s registers are not Marketing’s business. Projects, campaigns, store openings and migrations are folders and lists inside a desk. Brands are not Spaces because six brands against five Free slots would consume the whole workspace and leave nowhere for Finance — and because a brand is a property of work, not a place work happens.

Law 2 — Brand becomes structure exactly where the brand is the operating unit. BND1 and BND2 exist to run brands, so inside them the brand is the Folder: ACDC Express, The Local Farmer, Papa Pasta. Everywhere else the brand is a field and a filter, used where per-brand work genuinely separates: client SLA folders in FIN, brand campaign folders in MKT, brand hiring folders in HRM. Structure follows the work, not an org chart drawn twice.

Law 3 — One list per work format, and the format is always one of six. Briefed-In (inbound work, the only list the brief form writes to), Daily (repeating control work), Recurring (weekly/monthly/quarterly obligations), Reporting (reports produced), Projects / Ad-hoc (bounded change work), Meeting Action (commitments made in a room). No seventh format. No General — that word is why BU 1 has two of them.

Law 4 — Every field that decides something is a field, not a sentence. SLA state, approval state, brand, department, store, requester, due date, response deadline. Prose stays in the description; judgement lives in fields, because a field can be filtered, counted, coloured and breached.

Law 5 — Nothing is designed that the plan cannot run. Each section carries its plan gate. A design element that needs Business is marked Business, and stays unbuilt until the plan moves — rather than built broken and blamed on the builder.

12. Reporting & Notes — one home for the whole rhythm

The review pass, and the four things it changed. Ten reviewers read this build blind of each other — a Codex cross-reference, four seats before the build, four seats after it, and one adversarial seat. Four findings were true and are now built into the live Space:

  • Fiscal year is its own field. Year is the calendar label; Fiscal Year is a number derived from Period Date. QTR 4 straddles December and January, so a January row reads Year 2027 and Fiscal Year 2026. A single field cannot do both jobs, and every dated row was re-derived and re-read: 88 rows correct, 0 wrong. The view REP · This Financial Year filters on it.
  • Source Task is a relationship, not a sentence. Type tasks, so an action points at the standup or report row it came from as an object. Source Record remains as a human-readable note; it is not the truth.
  • The reply is a comment thread, not a text field. A text field cannot be authored by the owner, cannot notify, and holds one blob with no history. Both worked examples now carry real feedback-and-reply comments, and Feedback State moves Awaiting Reply → Replied → Resolved beside them. Owner Reply survives as an optional summary. Comments carry the words; statuses carry the proof.
  • INF has a weekly roll-up. INF Weekly Standup and INF Weekly Report join the thirteen unit lists, so the group can meet on its own record. Seven template carriers now sit in 06 Knowledge → REP Report Templates, and September and October were backfilled so QTR 3 reads as a quarter rather than a month.

Seven fields carry the period, and they are why a report can never float between quarters: Quarter (the four fiscal quarters), Year (fiscal label, 2025–2029), Period Date (the Monday, the 1st of the month, the 1st of the quarter), Month, Week No (1–52), Period Type (Weekly Standup · Weekly Report · Monthly Report · Quarterly Report · Meeting) and REP Unit (FIN … NVP, so one view can group all 65 lists by unit without caring where a row sits).

12.9 Actioning a meeting point, and the video loop

A point raised in a standup has to leave the meeting as something a named person owns and can answer. That is one working list and eleven fields:

  1. Raise — the point is written in the standup row, in whichever of the five agenda fields it belongs to.
  2. Action — a row opens in REP Meeting Actions with Action Kind, Action Owner, Action Due and Source Record naming the standup it came from, so the trail never breaks.
  3. Video — Meeting Recording holds the link; the reviewer writes the verdict in Video Feedback and signs Feedback By.
  4. Reply — the owner answers in Owner Reply, an ordinary field on a row they own, so they can edit it and everyone else can read it. A reply does not need its own object.
  5. Close — Feedback State walks Awaiting Reply → Replied → Resolved, and REP · Awaiting Feedback is the chase list.

The loop carries one live worked example — EXAMPLE — FIN standup point: supplier reconciliation signed off — which is the only row the two loop views return, and that is how they were proved to work rather than assumed.

12.11 What the API would not do

Stated plainly because one of them touches the review rule: the status spine is the single manual step in this design. The spine has to be Not started · In Progress · Submitted · Reviewed · Concluded · Cancelled, and ClickUp’s public API cannot create or rename a status — POST /space/{id}/status answers 404 and PUT /space/{id} with a statuses array answers 200 while silently ignoring it. Until those six are authored in Space settings, REP keeps the inherited Open · in progress · in review · waiting · blocked · completed, and Submitted / Reviewed ride on the Submitted By and Reviewed By fields instead.

Three smaller items are also UI-only: deleting two stray field definitions (_probe_field, and the old free-text Reporting Period), adding an INF option to REP Unit, and retiring the ten views the Space inherited from a project template.

13.2 Cross-space visibility — the thing you asked for

Three mechanisms, and they are not the same thing:

  1. All Tasks (formerly “Everything”) — free on every plan, spans every Space, and is where a cross-space lead works from. Custom views can be saved to it: “Everything due this week, grouped by Desk”, “All SLA breaches, by brand”.
  2. Workspace-level views — saved views on All Tasks, visible to the people you choose. This is the honest answer to “projects across all spaces”: you do not need to be in a Space to see its work.
  3. Programme Register in GOV — one list where every cross-department programme (store openings, migrations, brand launches) has a row, with a relationship field to the department tasks doing the work. This is a register, not a duplicate: the work stays in its desk.

What money buys here is exclusion, not inclusion. Members see all public Spaces by default on every plan. Folder-level permissions start at Core; Space-level permissions and private-by-default Spaces are Enterprise.

13.3 The privacy defect to fix first

All five live Spaces are private = false. HRM → Restricted Cases, FIN → Creditors & Approvals and GOV → Decision Register are readable by anyone who joins. Before a single colleague is invited, every Space gets private = true and members are added explicitly. This is a five-minute change and a permanent risk reduction.

13.4 Guests are the cheap seats — with a trap

Guests are free and unlimited on Free Forever, but every guest gets full create/edit rights that cannot be modified. Permission-controlled guest seats begin at Core (5 included, +2 per extra paid user) and Business (10, +5). Unlimited read-only guests are available on paid plans.

Two traps worth stating now: a guest can be scoped to a single Folder, Subfolder, List or task but can never be given a Space, and anyone on your own domain cannot be a guest — @infinitybrands.co.za staff become billable limited members. Franchisees on their own domains are genuinely free; your own staff never are.
