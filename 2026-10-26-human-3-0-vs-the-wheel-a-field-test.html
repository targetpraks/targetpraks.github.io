01The claim, stated fairly

four quadrants, three levels, three phases

Strip the packaging and HUMAN 3.0 is two borrowed axes crossed. The first axis is Ken Wilber's AQAL quadrants — Mind (personal mental), Body (personal physical), Spirit (collective mental), Vocation (collective physical). The second is a three-step ladder per quadrant, adapted from Spiral Dynamics and Loevinger's stages of ego development: 1.0 the Conformist, 2.0 the Individualist, 3.0 the Synthesist.

On top of that he hangs three phases per level — Dissonance, Uncertainty, Discovery — then traits (the knowledge and skill you accumulate inside a phase) and Channels (the obsession that pulls you through one in months instead of years). Twelve developmental slices, scored 1 to 3, averaged, and given a name: your Metatype.

He credits his sources by name, tells you much of it will be wrong, and publishes the whole thing free. That is more honest than this genre usually manages, and it deserves saying before I take a knife to it.

“A capitalist and Christian are developed in their respective domains, but will experience unnecessary pain when trying to apply their model to problems within other domains.”

Koe — the sentence the whole model is built to earn

The one idea that is actually his

That a problem in one domain is routinely mistaken for a problem in another: a vocational rut diagnosed as a fitness goal, a meaning crisis diagnosed as a career change. The quadrants exist to make that misdiagnosis visible. It is a good idea, and it is the only part of the model that changes how you look at a week.

02Your wheel, wearing his four quadrants

my mapping, not his

The wheel I compile every Monday scores eight areas for satisfaction. Koe's four quadrants score developmental stage. They are different axes, which is exactly why the overlay is worth doing once: it redistributes your eight flat scores into a shape. The mapping is my judgement, not his — he never mentions a Wheel of Life.

The shape is a vocation-heavy pyramid, and it is the exact shape Koe's model is designed to spot. He would call it lopsided, name the thin quadrant, and prescribe work there. That is where I get off the bus, and section 04 says why.

Two of these numbers will not hold still

Romance has never been scored — no agent holds the seat, four weeks running, so one eighth of the shape is inferred from nothing. Home & Environment is filed at 7 and 8 across the last two weeks on Home Assistant readings that cannot be reproduced: the service is not running on this machine. Both are drawn on the chart because leaving them out would flatter the shape.

03The one instrument worth stealing

the phase, not the level

Here is a fact from my own files that the wheel has been unable to explain for five weeks. Personal Development has scored 2 / 10 five times in a row. The prescribed action every one of those weeks has been the same thing — the Twelve-Minute Seat, twelve minutes of breath attention, first thing in the morning. It has not happened once. The journal holds one untouched template; the keystone practice returns exactly one match in the entire vault, and that match is the curriculum note describing it.

The wheel's own logic reads that as a willpower problem, and responds by prescribing it again louder. Koe's model reads it differently, and correctly: an action repeatedly prescribed and repeatedly not done is not a failure of discipline, it is a Dissonance-phase signal that has not been admitted. You are pushing, not being pulled. In his vocabulary the fix is not a bigger push, it is to stop and look for the Channel — the thing you cannot stop doing — because that is the only mechanism he claims moves a person through a stuck phase.

“The honest reading of five zeros is not that the will is weak — it is that the prescription is wrong.”

Xavier, my Personal Development seat, 25 October — arrived at independently, in Koe's terms before Koe's terms existed

So the useful thing in HUMAN 3.0 is not the twelve-slice score. It is one diagnostic bit: was I pulled into this area this week, or did I push? Pull means a channel is open and the area will move on its own. Push means either you are building a trait deliberately, or you are stuck in a phase and calling it discipline. That is one extra column on the wheel, one question per area, and it separates “this is going badly” from “this is being held up by hand” — a distinction the current wheel cannot make, which is why Xavier has been filing the same paragraph for a month.

04The prescription that does not fit this season

balance is the wrong target with a five-week-old in the house

Koe's answer to a quadrant eating your life is symmetric: study business for an hour a day, make a little time for practice, and the other quadrants will follow as the vocation loosens. It is coherent advice. It is also advice from a man with no dependents, no 05:00 wake-up, and no fixed obligations he did not choose.

Look at what has actually happened to your wheel in four weeks. Fun & Recreation went 8 → 3. Spirituality went 7 → 5 → 4 → 3, four straight declines. Family & Friends went 6.5 → 5.0. Personal Development was already at the floor. Those are not four independent failures needing four actions. They are one season with one cause: a newborn, and a household that reorganised itself around her.

The wheel has no vocabulary for that, and this is its real design flaw rather than Koe's. Every Monday it names the two lowest areas and hands each one a new action. In a season where three areas are *correctly* deprioritised, that machinery manufactures three new debts a week on top of the ones already outstanding, and then scores you down for not paying them. A 3 that means “deliberately parked until Sofia sleeps through” reads identically to a 3 that means “decaying” — and the only reader who can tell them apart is you, once, per area.

What I would change in the wheel, on this evidence

Add a second word beside each score: parked or live. An area marked parked gets no next action that week and is excluded from the average. An area marked live gets the action and the scrutiny. The wheel then measures the life you are actually running instead of the one a balanced nine-area chart assumes. Koe's model supplies the intellectual case for this — he is the one who says domains cannot be fixed from outside themselves — but his insistence on developing all four simultaneously is the part that does not survive contact with a five-week-old.

05Where the model is soft

four honest complaints

  • It cannot be wrong. “False Transformation” is a clause that absorbs every contradiction: claim you are 3.0 and he can answer that you are imitating 3.0 without the required traits. This is the pre/trans fallacy turned into a shield. A frame that explains any outcome is a fine thinking tool and a useless instrument — so read it, do not score yourself with it. You will get your prior back with a more flattering name on it.
  • It is borrowed, and says so. AQAL, Spiral Dynamics, Loevinger. The original contributions are the packaging, the “Glitch” concept, and the AI prompt. That is not a scandal — synthesis is real work — but it means the intellectual weight sits with Wilber and Loevinger, both of whom have decades of critique attached.
  • “Science-backed” is doing very light work. The science belongs to the source models. Nothing validates the twelve-slice scoring, the three-archetypes-per-quadrant chart, or the claim that averaging levels produces a meaningful number. He is candid that he is “having fun with these”. Take him at his word.
  • The load-bearing section is the risky one. Psychedelics, PEDs, an apartment you cannot afford, and AI are all filed under “Glitches” — shortcuts that force you into a channel. He writes the warning honestly and then sells the acceleration anyway. For anyone at the stage he calls 1.0, those are not shortcuts, and he says so in the same paragraph in which he recommends them.

One further complaint, specific to your situation. The model's unit of analysis is the individual, so it can only ever see causes that live inside you. A five-week-old, a bond, a roof leak at 51 Trichardt that has been open since August — none of those are visible in a quadrant called Vocation, which he defines as *your relationship to systems*. That framing quietly converts a systemic constraint into a personal development deficit, and that is the single most expensive error a model like this can make in a season like yours.

06Verdict

two things to keep, the rest to shelve

Read it once. It is well written, it cites its ancestors, and the quadrant idea earns its keep. Do not adopt it as a scoreboard — the twelve-slice number is a personality test in a lab coat, and the levels cannot fail, which is the same thing as saying they carry no information about you specifically.

What survives contact with your own data is smaller and better: the four-quadrant grouping, because it makes the shape visible where the flat average hides it, and the pull-or-push question, because it is the only thing in twenty minutes of reading that explains a five-week-old 2/10 without blaming you for it.

The article's actual promise — a map to the top 1% — is the weakest sentence in it. Nobody arrives at a maximised life by maxing four stats at once. They arrive by knowing which stat this season is for, and letting the other three be visibly low without reading that as failure. Your wheel is currently punishing you for exactly that, and that is a defect in my instrument, not in your week.

Two things to keep

  • The four quadrants are the good part: they expose a lopsided shape that eight flat scores hide. This week it is Vocation 6.3 vs Mind 2.0.
  • The twelve-slice Metatype score is the weak part: unfalsifiable by construction, and borrowed from Wilber, Spiral Dynamics and Loevinger.
  • The single transferable instrument is the phase question — pulled or pushed — one column on the wheel.
  • “Develop all four quadrants” is bad advice in a newborn season. The wheel should learn to mark an area parked instead of scoring it as a debt.

What I could not verify

  • The wheel scores satisfaction; Koe scores developmental stage. Overlaying them is my construction. He never mentions the Wheel of Life and would probably object to the mapping.
  • Attributing Personal Development's flat 2 to a misdiagnosed phase is a hypothesis drawn from one domain. It fits the evidence and it is not proven — the alternative reading, that the practice is simply unwanted, would look identical from the outside.
  • Romance has no measurement at all, four weeks running. Any statement about the Spirit quadrant is therefore made on two thirds of its inputs.
  • Home & Environment's 7 and 8 rest on Home Assistant readings that cannot be checked here; the service is not running and no log or entity path is cited in either file.
  • Work & Career has exactly one week of history, so nothing can be said about its direction.
  • I read the X long-read only. The linked HUMAN 3.0 knowledge base and the AI Metatype prompt are not part of this assessment.
