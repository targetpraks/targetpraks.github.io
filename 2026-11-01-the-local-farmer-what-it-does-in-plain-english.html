The Local Farmer — explained simply

What this business does

A simple explainer for a father-in-law. No jargon, no assumptions.

The Local Farmer is a South African urban farming business, running since 2020. Instead of one big farm far from the city, it grows fresh produce in small, clean grow rooms inside or near the communities that eat it. It is part of the Infinity Brands portfolio.

What it grows

Microgreens — tiny, nutrient-dense salad and herb leaves: 32 varieties across six signature mixes. Mushrooms — golden oyster and king oyster, launching soon as Mighty Mushrooms.

Why freshness is the whole product

A microgreen loses 30–40% of its looks within 72 hours of harvest. So "fresh" is not a bonus here — it is the product. A grow room minutes from the kitchen is a different vegetable from one that travelled three to five days from a distant farm.

The channels — where the food goes

1. Restaurants and professional kitchens

Chefs order produce that is harvested in the morning and on a chef's pass by afternoon — same-day delivery to food service.

2. Catering companies

Fresh microgreens and mushrooms go out to caterers supplying events, functions and corporate lunches.

3. Retail shops

Produce reaches retail shelves so shoppers can buy it locally.

4. Wholesalers

Bulk supply to wholesale distributors who move the produce on to shops and kitchens across the region.

5. Direct to you — at home

Everyday customers order through the online store for delivery to their own kitchens.

How it grows

It works as a micro-franchise. A local operator runs a small urban grow room. Head office provides the machines, the growing protocols, the platform, training and the brand — no farming experience is needed, and the machine does most of the work. Head office buys the harvest back, processes some of it into value-added products (powders, chips, blends), and sells it on. Revenue returns to the network and pays for the next grow room.

You can run it from home at first. Training is provided. From sign-up to first trading day can take up to three months — that is how long the machine takes to build and the training takes to complete.
