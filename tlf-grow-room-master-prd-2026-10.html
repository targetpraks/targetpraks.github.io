The room is being judged against a target it cannot reach

The 17.3 kg a week target needs all 144 spaces turned over every seven days. On a 10–14 day grow, half the room is occupied by unharvestable trays at any moment. So the September dashboard's −67.1 kg variance is not a performance story, it is a units story: capacity was computed as if a tray occupies a space for zero days.

2 · The arithmetic that has to hold

Five definitions, and every tab in the sheet is one of them arranged differently.

TermDefinition
SpaceOne tray position. A machine is 36 (6 tiers × 6 trays).
OccupancyA tray holds its space for the whole cycle. Weekly harvestable spaces = spaces × 7 ÷ cycle_days. At 14 days that is exactly half — the rule Ricardo named.
Yield per trayGrams harvested. 120 g is the planning rate, 131 g the measured average, 140 g the funding assumption.
ReliabilityThe standing rule tolerates 5% failed or contaminated trays, so capacity is stated at 95% unless the log says better.
Weekly capacityharvestable spaces × yield × reliability ÷ 1000 kg.

3 · What the sheet is

Nine tabs. One grain per tab, one owner, no tab that repeats another tab's number. Cells in bold are typed; everything else is a formula.

TabGrainWhat it holds
Start Here—What the sheet is, the five definitions, who owns it, what it deliberately excludes.
SettingsOne row per parameterCycle days (14), spaces per machine (36), machines (5), yield g/tray (120), reliability (95%), selling price R/kg, month weeks (4.33). Every other tab reads from here — one place to change a fact.
CapacityOne row per machine count 1–12Spaces, harvestable trays a week, kg/week, kg/day, kg/month, and rand at the setting price. The room's ceiling, and the cost of the next machine.
ProspectsOne row per enquiryClient, kg/day, delivery days a week, channel, price R/kg, start week → kg/week, trays/week, spaces, machines needed, can we serve it today, and the first week we can.

MachinesOne row per decision pointPeak demand, machines needed, machines held, shortfall, capital at the build cost, monthly contribution, payback months, and the verdict: ORDER / HOLD / NO.
Yield LogOne row per harvest dayDate, machine, trays sown, trays harvested, kg harvested → g/tray, variance against model, variance against plan, empty spaces, failed trays.
Invoice YieldOne row per invoiceInvoice no, date, client, grams invoiced, rand → R/kg, R per tray, and rolled up by week: invoiced grams ÷ harvested grams = sell-through, and rand ÷ harvested kg = the price we actually got.

The two formulas that matter

harvestable trays / week = spaces × 7 ÷ cycle_days
weekly capacity kg       = harvestable trays × yield_g × reliability ÷ 1000
machines needed          = ROUNDUP( (demand_kg_week × 1000 ÷ yield_g ÷ reliability) × cycle_days ÷ 7 ÷ spaces_per_machine , 0 )

4 · The machine decision, written as a rule

Order a machine when all three are true

  • Demand: confirmed weekly demand — not prospects — exceeds 90% of capacity in at least two consecutive weeks.
  • Money: the added machine's monthly contribution (10 kg × R0.4504/g = R4,503.64) covers its payback inside 24 months at the build cost.
  • Space: the room physically takes it — a machine needs its footprint and power, and that is a check, not an assumption.

Otherwise the verdict is HOLD and the sheet says how much demand is missing before it flips. A prospect alone never triggers a machine — it triggers a quote with a start date.

Two numbers in this rule are still not evidenced anywhere in the record, and the sheet marks both as typed inputs rather than guessing: machine price (build cost R13,294 against R45,000 to a franchisee — a 3.4× spread) and lead time from order to first harvest. Until someone confirms them, the payback figure is an estimate and says so on the tab.

5 · Invoiced yield, and the one thing blocking it

The invoice tab is the half of the truth the growing sheet cannot see: what was actually sold, and at what rate. Grams invoiced ÷ grams harvested is sell-through; rand ÷ harvested kg is the realised price — which on the Pico comparison sits R60–R100 above the market for the same eight products.

Books will not answer us yet

Reading invoices from Zoho Books as the fleet's API user returns 6018 — You do not have access as your account is disabled, and the Books MCP lane exposes 285 tools of which none lists or fetches an invoice. So the tab is built, formatted and formula-complete, and it takes its rows by paste or CSV until the Books seat is fixed. That is a fix for whoever owns the Books users, not a design compromise.

6 · Retiring the six, without deleting anything

Deleting a workbook destroys the audit trail that makes the numbers arguable, so nothing is deleted. The five model sheets and the August dashboard move to a folder named _Retired — superseded by TLF Grow Room Master under TLF_Reporting, with one line in the new Start Here tab saying what replaced them and when. The harvest ledger stays live: it is a log, not a model, and the new sheet reads from the same numbers.

7 · Build order, by dependency

  1. Sheet skeleton and Settings. Workbook in the TLF_Reporting team folder, nine tabs, parameters typed once. Exit: every later tab can read cycle days and yield from Settings and nothing is hard-coded.
  2. Capacity tab. The machine table, 1 to 12. Exit: 4 machines reads 8.21 kg/week and 7 machines reads 14.36, and the numbers match an independent calculation.
  3. Demand and Plan. The weekly order book and the 13-week plan that consumes it. Exit: entering a 14 kg week flags the shortfall against today's room and names the machine count that covers it.
  4. Prospects. kg/day in, machines and start week out. Exit: 2 kg a day returns 7 machines, and weekdays-only returns 5.
  5. Yield Log and Invoice Yield. Actuals against model, invoices against harvest. Exit: a month of harvest rows produces g/tray, variance and sell-through without a formula pointing at an empty cell.
  6. Retire the five and point people at the new one. Exit: the TLF GM opens one link and stops opening six.
