The no-test protocol — heavy metals

For Donatello and Gianna, with no blood test and no appointment. Homeopathy cannot do this job — there is no preparation and no trial behind it — so here is the version of a detox that has a mechanism, costs nothing, and starts tonight.

Companion to the evidence review: Heavy metal detox — the test-first protocol (26 Nov 2026). Every claim below carries its source.
Why there is no homeopathic version of this

A homeopathic "detox" remedy cannot remove a metal, because at a 30C dilution there is no molecule of anything left in the bottle — and a PubMed search for homeopathic chelation or metal detoxification returns no clinical trial at all. The literature that does exist on homeopathy and heavy metals is about the remedies containing them. So I am not going to write you a protocol that cannot work for your children. I am going to write you the one that can, and that needs no lab.

01 The three facts behind those numbers

  • Nothing is in the bottle. A 30C dilution is a 10⁻⁶⁰ dilution — past the point where a single molecule of the original substance survives in any volume you could hold. Whatever the label says, what reaches the child is water and, usually, alcohol.
  • The dilutions are mostly ethanol. A 2025 forensic analysis of 134 commonly prescribed homeopathic formulations found classical dilutions at a median of 91.02% v/v alcohol, and detected lead in 37 of 71 formulations — including highly diluted arsenic and mercury preparations (Philips et al., Medicine 2025). For a 7- and a 9-year-old, a daily "detox" drop is a daily ethanol dose.
  • Regulators have already answered the general question. Australia's NHMRC found no health condition for which there is reliable evidence that homeopathy is effective (NHMRC 2015); Europe's academies concluded there are no known diseases for which there is robust, reproducible evidence of effect beyond placebo, and flagged poor quality control in preparation (EASAC 2017).
  • And the herbal side of the same shelf has poisoned children. A 4-year-old presented with anaemia, abdominal pain and a venous lead level of 123 µg/dL — from an ayurvedic herbal medicine he had been given for asthma — and needed DMSA and CaEDTA in hospital (case report, 2025). Twenty percent of ayurvedic products sampled in the US contained lead, mercury or arsenic (Saper, JAMA 2008).

02 What a detox actually is, at home

There is one tap you can turn down and no drain you can open. Every item below acts on the tap — which is why none of it needs a blood test to be worth doing.

1
20 minutes · free · no lab

The source audit

This is the whole of a real detox, and it needs no test: if a source exists, it is in the house, and it is findable by eye. Walk the house with the twelve questions from the companion page — the year it was built, any paint dust at child height, the incoming water pipe, market pottery used for food, imported turmeric, cheap jewellery, batteries and e-waste, and anyone coming home in work clothes from paint, scrap or sandblasting. Two or more "yes" answers and we act on the specific room, pipe, pot or toy.

South Africa's 90 ppm lead-in-paint limit only took effect on 17 May 2025, so every coat before that is a fair question in an older Cape Town house (SGS).

2
5 minutes · free · every morning

Water

  • Run the cold kitchen tap for about two minutes before the first drink or the first kettle of the day — that clears what sat in the pipe overnight.
  • Cold water only for drinking and cooking; never the hot tap for food or bottles, because hot water dissolves more metal from plumbing.
  • If the house predates the 1990s, have a plumber look at the incoming pipe and any solder joints once, and keep the answer with the house file.

Baseline for what leaves the reservoir: the City's annual tap water quality report. Pipes inside the property are yours, not the City's.

3
15 minutes · free · weekly

Dust and hands

  • Wet-wipe windowsills, door frames and the floors they crawl or play on, once a week. Damp mop rather than dry-sweep, which just puts the fine dust back in the air.
  • Hands washed before every meal and after playing outside or in soil.
  • Shoes off at the door; outside clothes off before sitting on beds.

Honest calibration: the Cochrane review of 17 trials in 3,282 children found dust control made little or no measurable difference to blood lead (Cochrane 2020). It stays on the list because it is free, harmless, and takes the same dust out of the same hands that also carry every other exposure.

4
no cost · at every meal

Absorption — food, not pills

An iron-deficient or calcium-poor child absorbs more of whatever lead they swallow. The CDC's action list at every blood level includes diet focused on iron and calcium, and testing and treating iron deficiency — the one place where a test is genuinely worth having, because it also explains tired, foggy school days on its own (CDC).

  • Iron at a meal: red meat, liver, sardines, lentils, beans, spinach — with vitamin C alongside (citrus, tomato, pepper) to absorb it.
  • Calcium daily: milk, yoghurt, cheese, or calcium-set tofu and tinned sardines with bones.
  • Nothing displacing those foods: a child full of juice and refined starch eats less of what binds and blocks.
  • No iron or mineral supplement without a ferritin result — an unnecessary iron dose is its own problem.
5
the portion rule · no test needed

Fish — the mercury question, answered by size

This one needs no blood test, only a serving size. The FDA's childhood chart gives exactly their ages: a serving is about 2 oz at age 4–7 and 3 oz at age 8–10, and the advice is two servings a week from the "Best Choices" list — the small, short-lived fish (FDA fish advice). So: Gianna 2 oz twice a week, Donatello 3 oz twice a week.

Keep the big predators occasional rather than weekly — shark, swordfish, marlin, king mackerel, bigeye tuna. Tinned light tuna and salmon sit comfortably inside the weekly rule.

04 The one thing that overrides "no tests"

These are emergency-department signs, not protocol signs: confusion or unusual drowsiness, sudden weakness, repeated vomiting, severe abdominal pain with constipation, a seizure, or a clear loss of skills that were there a month ago. South Africa's Poisons Information Helpline is 0861 555 777, 24 hours (ChildSafe SA). Children with lead in the blood usually have no obvious symptoms at all, which is exactly why a symptom appearing is a reason to move, not to wait.

And if a practitioner hands you a "detox" protocol for either child, the two questions that end the conversation are the same two that would end it for me: which validated test result is this based on, and which trial shows this preparation increases metal excretion in children? There is no published answer to the second one.
