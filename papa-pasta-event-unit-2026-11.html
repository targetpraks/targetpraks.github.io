01What the unit has to do

We are not building a shop on wheels. We are building a service point: a boiler, a warmer, a fridge and a hatch. Everything that makes the store expensive stays behind at the central kitchen.

  • 140 g fresh portion, cooked 3–6 minutes to order. Nothing is fried, nothing is baked. The unit has one hot job and one cold job.
  • Sauces arrive as frozen 2 kg pouches, thaw overnight and are used within one trading day. The unit needs cold storage for pouches and gentle heat for four sauces — not a production kitchen.
  • A single 40 litre well with six baskets does 55–70 bowls an hour with one cook and one server. A 250-bowl school event needs about 83 an hour at peak, so one well is enough and a second well is a bigger trailer.
  • No extruder, no drying racks, no pasta theatre. That single decision is what keeps the unit at 3.4 m instead of 5 m — and it is the reason this unit costs a fifth of a store fit-out.

Across the width: 0.6 m back counter + 0.8 m aisle + 0.5 m serving counter = 1.9 m. That is a one-cook, one-server aisle — close, but two people pass.

Why not 2.4 m

Two of the five items go, and the one that always goes first is the handwash basin. A vendor selling pre-packed chips can work without one; a kitchen cooking pasta to order cannot, because R638 requires a handwash basin that is not used for cleaning equipment. The 2.4 m trailer is a fryer and a cooler for R38 000 — cheap, and the wrong machine for pasta.

Why not 4.5 m or more

A 4.6 m body is 4.8 m over the A-frame. It needs a wider turning circle than a school drop-off gives you, and it stops being a one-bakkie job. Everything it adds — a prep table, a second well — is work the central kitchen can do before the trailer leaves.

04Gas or electric

Our store spec is all-electric, and that is a landlord condition, not a brand law: the equipment pack says malls require electric-only cooking, which is why the store is specced that way. Outdoors, on a school field, LPG with a certificate of conformity is both legal and normal. That single distinction decides the trailer's power design.

The finding

A gas well takes the trailer from a 63 A single-phase supply — which most school halls do not have spare — down to 16 A, which almost every venue can give you from a normal socket.

It also removes the generator: a 12.5 kVA silent diesel costs around R40 000 (budget listings from R36 400), plus a bracket, diesel, and 65–70 dB at seven metres — on a school field, next to a marquee.

So the spec is: gas pasta well, electric everything else, and a 32 A single-phase inlet built in anyway. Two 9 kg bottles in a vented cage on the tow bar give roughly 7–8 hours of full-burn cooking each — a full event day per bottle. The inlet costs little at build time and means we can run all-electric where a venue insists, or where a mall landlord eventually does.

08What you cannot skip

  • Certificate of Acceptability. The City of Cape Town issues it through e-Services, standard processing is ten working days, and an Environmental Health Practitioner inspects the unit's structure, food practices, waste and pest control. A business licence is also required if we prepare meals.
  • R638 hygiene detail. A handwash basin separate from the prep sink; ventilation openings equal to at least 5% of the floor area — our hatch and roof vent count, a sealed box does not; a waste-water system and controlled refuse; and no handler works with jaundice, diarrhoea or vomiting, with a medical certificate required to return.
  • School permission. The Department of Basic Education's vendor guidelines require a permit to sell and a valid COA for anyone selling on school premises, and put the school management team and governing body on the hook for checking it. Expect to submit both before the first event.
  • Gas certificate of conformity. Bottle in a vented cage outside the working area, regulator and hose rated for the burner, extinguisher on board. Builders such as Chapret supply the CoC with the trailer — insist on it in writing.
  • Trailer registration and roadworthy. Above 750 kg GVM the trailer needs a parking brake plus an overrun or service brake, depending on the towing vehicle's tare; above the towing vehicle's tare it must have a service brake. The rules are set out in regulation 151.
  • The driver's licence. A Code B licence covers a trailer only up to 750 kg GVM. Our loaded unit will be well over that, so whoever tows it needs EB — or we hire a driver for event days.
  • Public liability cover. Schools and venues ask for it. Get the limit in writing before we quote an event; it changes what we can accept.

09Do this first

  1. Name the tow vehicle and the licence. We are not buying a trailer we cannot legally pull. Exit: one vehicle with a towbar rated above the trailer's GVM, and a named driver with EB.
  2. Rent a 3 m catering trailer for one school event. Cape Town has a hire market for exactly this — see Food Truck Finder's hire list — and one Saturday tells us more than three builder showrooms. Exit: 200+ bowls served and a written list of what broke.
  3. Put the same spec sheet to three builders. Crafted, Western Cape Trailers and the Mobile Kitchen list as the price ceiling. Exit: three like-for-like quotes, ex VAT, with lead times.
  4. Choose the fuel and write the inlet into the spec. Gas primary, 32 A single-phase secondary. Exit: one fuel decision, one electrical schedule.
  5. Start the COA application before the trailer lands. Ten working days is the City's number, and it needs the unit to inspect. Exit: COA in hand, or a booked inspection date.
  6. Then commit the build. Deposit, lead time, delivery date. Exit: signed order — signed by Ricardo, not by us.

10Ten questions for the builder

  1. With our five items against the back wall, what is the internal length, the internal width, and the aisle left for two staff?
  2. What are the tare and the GVM as built — and is it braked?
  3. What is the electrical spec: 16 A, 32 A or 63 A, single or three-phase, with an earth-leakage DB?
  4. Can it run gas and electric, and what does the changeover cost?
  5. How is steam extracted over the well — hood size, fan rating, and does the opening meet the 5% floor-area ventilation rule?
  6. Handwash basin with hot water, separate from the prep sink — included or extra? Fresh and waste tank sizes?
  7. Gas: bottle capacity, vented cage, and is the certificate of conformity included in the price?
  8. Insulation, floor material, worktop grade — and are the worktops 304 stainless?
  9. Lead time from deposit, warranty in years, and is registration and roadworthy in the price?
  10. Can we see a finished unit and speak to one customer who uses it at events?
