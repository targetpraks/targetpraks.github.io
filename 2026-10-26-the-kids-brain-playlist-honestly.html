What no playlist can do

  • Same bedtime, every night. The lever is the clock, not the content. A calm video after a 9:40pm bedtime changes nothing.
  • The five-minute warning. Transitions are hard because they are abrupt. “Screen off in five” from you is the whole intervention.
  • Your own calm. Kids borrow the adult’s nervous system. This is the part the ad actually got right, and the part it cannot sell.
  • Anything called “brain training”. Skip every channel promising right-brain activation or reflex integration — same evidence problem as the Instagram ad.

Rules that keep it working

  • 15 minutes, same slot daily. Two clips of GoNoodle is a dose; forty minutes is a different activity with a different effect.
  • Playlist only, autoplay off. The recommendation rail after a kids’ video is how a 15-minute habit becomes an hour.
  • Screen face-down for sleep audio. The blue light undoes what the meditation does.
  • Don’t quiz them on it. Movement and stories work when they are play, not homework.
The one-line answer: yes — GoNoodle for the body, Cosmic Kids for the brakes, Storyline Online for the reading, and your own routine for the sleep. That is the free version of what the ad was charging for, and it is the version that has evidence behind it.
