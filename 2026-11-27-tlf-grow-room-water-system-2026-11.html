Water system · installation drawing set · rev A

From the JoJo
to the machine

Eight grow machines, two JoJo tanks, one filter pack. Each machine tank has a pump and two float switches — the top one calls water in, the bottom one stops the pump burning out. This set draws the whole line, from the tap that fills JoJo 1 to the pipe that crosses the joint between two machines that stand back to back.

8 machines · 4 feed points 2 × JoJo 1 filter pack 2 floats + 1 pump per tank

Read this first — three rules

  1. Fail closed. Every valve that can stick is normally closed, and every tank has its own overflow to a drain. A failed solenoid must wet the floor, never the room.
  2. Protect the pump. The bottom float switch sits above the pump intake and breaks its supply. A pump that runs dry once is a pump you replace.
  3. One filter, before the header. Filtered water goes into JoJo 2 and nowhere else. Every machine downstream sees the same clean water at the same pressure.

S-01
System schematic

One line, from the mains to all eight machines

Read left to right. Water enters once, is stored, pumped, filtered, stored again, pressurised, and handed to every machine on a ring main. Where two machines stand back to back, one drop serves the pair and the pipe is carried across the joint to the second tank.

1
Mains top-up. 15 mm through a ball valve and a double-check backflow preventer into JoJo 1, controlled by a mechanical float valve.
2
JoJo 1 — reserve. Break tank and buffer. 50 mm overflow to a visible gully, 20 mm drain cock for cleaning.
3
Transfer pump P-01. 0.37 kW, suction through a strainer 100 mm off the tank floor, cut out by a low-level switch before it sucks air.
4
Filter pack. 20″ big blue: 5 µm sediment, then carbon block. Pressure gauge either side; change at a 0.5 bar drop.
5
JoJo 2 — clean header. Sealed, opaque, insect-proof. Filtered water only. Its own overflow and low-level cut-out.
6
Booster set P-02. 0.55 kW with a 24 L pressure vessel, 1.5–3 bar. Gives the first and the last machine the same fill time.
7
Ring main RM-01. 32 mm uPVC at 2.3 m, isolatable in two halves, flush valve at the far end. Route on S-03.
8
Pair drop. 20 mm tee, ball valve, ½″ strainer, 24 V normally-closed solenoid. One drop per pair — manual bypass so a failed solenoid does not stop the room.
9
Machine tank. Pump plus two float switches: the top one calls water, the bottom one protects the pump. Section on S-05; pair joint on S-04.
10
Overflow, every tank. 32 mm standpipe at the maximum water line to the floor drain. Wet floor, never wet room.
Machine count and positions taken from the room layout of 2026-11-12 (8 machines, GM01–GM08). The ring main is drawn unrolled for clarity; the route, the drops and the rear-slot entries are on S-03. Nominal bore: main 32 mm, branch 20 mm, tank fill 15 mm, pump delivery 12 mm.

S-02
Source, filtration and pressure

Where the water comes from, and why it needs two pumps

The line is broken twice on purpose. JoJo 1 breaks the mains pressure and settles the sediment; JoJo 2 is the clean buffer every machine draws from. Between them sits the only filter on the line, and on each side of the break there is a pump.

1
Mains. 15 mm, 200–400 kPa at the far side of the site. Everything downstream is designed for 15 mm.
2
Backflow. Double-check valve immediately after the isolating ball valve. Never a hose, never a temporary connection.
3
Float valve FV-01. Mechanical, no electricity, set so the tank holds about 70 % and always leaves air space above the water.
4
Overflow. 50 mm, straight through the wall to open air above a gully. If FV-01 ever sticks, the water leaves the building.
5
Suction. 25 mm, through a strainer 100 mm off the floor. Never off the very bottom — that is where the grit is.
6
P-01. 0.37 kW transfer pump. Runs on a float in JoJo 1, not on a timer: it fills, it stops, it waits.
7
Filter pack. 5 µm sediment first, carbon block second. Gauge before and after — change at a 0.5 bar difference, not on a calendar.
8
JoJo 2. Sealed and opaque so nothing grows in it. This is the tank every machine actually drinks from.
9
P-02. 0.55 kW booster with a 24 L vessel, cut in at 1.5 bar and out at 3 bar. The vessel is what stops the pump hammering every time a float opens.
Nominal sizes; the installing plumber confirms pipe bore against measured flow. Float heights to be set on site with the tanks filled. Pressures are design targets, not measured values.

S-03
Room distribution

The ring main, drawn on the room as it is

The main leaves the plant, runs a full loop around the room at 2.3 m — above the machines, clear of every working face — and comes back on itself. That is what makes the first drop and the last drop see the same pressure.

1
The loop. A closed ring, not a dead-end branch. Every drop tees off in two directions, so no machine starves when another is filling.
2
Height. 2.3 m to the underside. Machines are 1 960 mm, so the main clears every machine with 340 mm to spare.
3
Feed A — GM01 + GM03. The two machines touch along their backs at 50 mm. One drop lands in the slot and the pipe runs into each tank.
4
Feed B — GM02 + GM04. The same arrangement on the east side.
5
Feed C — GM05 + GM06 + GM07. A run of three in 50 mm slots. The drop lands on GM06 and the pipe runs west to GM05 and east to GM07.
6
Feed D — GM08. Stands alone, so it gets its own drop and its own valve.
7
Plant. JoJo 1, the filter and JoJo 2 sit outside the room. Nothing that can leak is inside the grow space.
Machine positions, sizes and clear widths are taken from the room layout of 2026-11-12 (REV E). Pipework is shown dashed because it is above the machines, not on the floor. The ring route is drawn offset outside the wall for clarity; it actually runs directly above the wall line. Bracket positions are set on site.

S-04
The pair connection

One pipe, carried across two machines that stand together

Where machines are butted, a single drop serves the whole group. The pipe lands in the slot between them, passes through both walls near the back, and fills both tanks from the same feed. One valve, one solenoid, one strainer — for two machines.

1
One drop per group. Not one per machine. Tapping the ring main eight times instead of four doubles the fittings and drops the pressure at the far end.
2
Land in the slot. The drop comes down in the 50 mm gap, not over a working face where it would be knocked.
3
Through both walls. 22 mm hole, rubber grommet each side, 15 mm pipe. The grommet is what stops the pipe sawing through the sheet every time the pump starts.
4
Above the water line. The pipe enters each tank above the maximum level, so it can never siphon from one tank into the other.
5
Same float, same pump. Each tank keeps its own two floats and its own pump. The shared pipe only carries water — it never shares control.
Detail is diagrammatic: wall thickness, grommet type and the exact penetration height are confirmed against the machine as built. The 50 mm slot is the clear width from the room layout of 2026-11-12.

S-05
Inside the tank

One pump, two floats, and the order they work in

This is the part that protects the machine. The bottom float guards the pump; the top float calls water in. They are not interchangeable and they are not both 'the level switch' — one saves the pump, the other fills the tank.

1
LS-02 — the top float. Sits at the working water line. When the level drops it opens the solenoid and the tank fills; when the level reaches it again it shuts.
2
LS-01 — the bottom float. Sits above the pump intake, below the mist maker's minimum. It breaks the pump circuit. It never opens the fill valve.
3
The pump. Submerged, on the tank floor. If it runs dry once it is scrap — hence LS-01, not a timer and not a guess.
4
The fill. Enters above the water line so it cannot back-siphon into the ring main, and so you can hear it running.
5
The overflow. Standpipe at the maximum level, taken out of the machine — never left to spill into the base.
6
The drain. A cock at the lowest point, so the tank can be emptied for cleaning without lifting the machine.
Tank dimensions are not given in the Mighty Grow drawing set and are marked to be measured. Float heights are set on site by hand. The pump is shown in its working position; its make and rating are confirmed against the unit as supplied.

S-06
The humidifier box

The box that makes the mist, drawn the same way

One box per machine, outside the grow space. Water comes in on a float valve, the mist maker sits at its set depth, and a fan carries the mist down a duct into the tent. The power stays outside the wet side.

1
Intake fan. A 12 V axial fan at the top of the box. It pushes air in, which is what moves the mist out — without it the mist just sits on the water.
2
Float valve. Mechanical, fed from the ring main or the machine tank. It opens when the level drops and shuts at the set depth — no electricity, no solenoid to fail.
3
Water depth. Set to what the mist maker's maker specifies. Too deep and it cannot lift the water; too shallow and it burns out.
4
Mist maker. Ultrasonic disc. It must have a low-water shutoff — either its own or the tank's LS-01. Running it dry is the one way to destroy it.
5
Power. Matched supply, mounted dry, above the water and outside the box. Drip loop in the cable before the plug, RCD on the circuit.
6
Mist outlet. Duct from the top of the box to the tent, sloping back so condensate runs into the box and not into the tent.
Follows the arrangement drawn for the farm: box outside the tent, duct in, power dry. Confirm the mist maker's required depth and rating, and confirm the box is under cover, before the first unit is built.

S-07
Control logic

What switches what, and in what order

Two separate systems share one pipe. The line makes water available and nothing more; every machine decides for itself whether to take it. Nothing in the line can start a machine, and no machine can drain the line.

1
Local, always. Every tank has its own two floats and its own pump. That is what lets one machine be serviced while the other seven keep running.
2
Shared, never. The line has one float valve per JoJo and one booster. If the booster stops, every machine stops at the same time — that is the trade for equal pressure.
3
Fail closed. The solenoids are normally closed. A power cut leaves every fill shut, not open.
4
Fail wet. Every tank overflows to a gully outside the room. A stuck float valve makes a mess on the floor, never a flood in the grow space.
5
The order matters. LS-01 is set below the mist maker's minimum, so the mist maker stops before the pump does. Set them the other way round and the mist maker burns out first.
Logic follows the arrangement described for the farm: bottom float protects the pump, top float calls water. Contact ratings and the 24 V supply are confirmed by the electrician before the panel is built.

Commissioning and faults

How to prove it works, and what to do when it does not

A water system is only finished when every failure has been made to happen on purpose. This sheet is the proof, in the order it has to be done, and the short list of things that go wrong afterwards.

Commissioning — in this order

#StepPass when
1Flush every new run to drain before it reaches a tank. Grit from cutting kills pumps.Water runs clear
2Pressure-test the ring main at 1.5× working pressure, capped, for one hour.No drop, no weep

Cautions

  • Never run a tank pump with the tank empty. One dry run scraps it.
  • Never run a mist maker dry. Fit the low-water shutoff before first use.
  • Never leave a hose connection in place of a fixed fill. Backflow and flooding, both.
  • Always keep the humidifier box accessible — it needs cleaning, and the mist maker needs to come out.
  • Always keep power outside the wet side, with a drip loop and RCD protection.
  • Always drain and scrub the tanks on the maintenance rota, and check both floats by hand.
Commissioning is carried out by the installing trades in the presence of the farm. This sheet is the record; it is signed, dated and kept in the grow room.
