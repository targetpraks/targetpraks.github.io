NVP — one register, three lists, one book

Four lists, one book. Audited by Codex and Claude, then corrected. Status: PROPOSED. Nothing is created in ClickUp yet.

At a glance

  • Four lists and one book — Property Register, Leases & Inspections, Maintenance Log, Utility Ledger, plus the Nova Property Book.
  • The register is the hub: six rows, twenty-four columns, every number rolled up from the three lists beside it. Each row opens onto its own document subtasks — 71 across the six houses (13, 13, 13, 11, 10 and 11) — with a file link on every one.
  • 14 of those 71 documents are actually held. That is the number the last three drafts got wrong, and it is the number that matters: the portfolio is more exposed than it looked.
  • Quotes live on the maintenance row — three quote slots, the winner, the cost, who approved it and when. The separate quotes list is gone and nothing it held is lost.
  • Leasing carries recurring reviews; the Utility Ledger carries a billed / paid / balance per month.

Six states, defined once, used everywhere:

State Means Counts as held?
Held the file is attached and the certificate number is recorded yes
Verified Jaques or Pamela has checked the address, the number and the expiry against the property yes
Requested we have asked the supplier, council or attorney; nothing has come back no
Defective a file exists but it is unsigned, expired or disputed — the Frances solar COC and the Trichardt COC are both here no
Missing nothing held, nothing asked for yet no
Not applicable the property does not have that installation or body no

The count is Held + Verified over everything except Not applicable. A defective certificate is never counted as held — that is the single failure the old document repeated: a Riley compliance certificate marked Expired, a Trichardt COC marked fake, and a Frances solar COC with no signatures, all filed as if they were in hand.

The portfolio question still answers in one click: a saved sub-task view, filtered to state ≠ Held/Verified/N-A, grouped by property. No second list.

5. What the two reviews found, and what I changed

Codex and Claude reviewed this independently, in the same repo, against the same ground truth. Both returned NOT READY, and both were right. Nine findings changed the design:

Finding What I changed
My document counts were invented — 13 for every house real sets: 13, 13, 13, 11, 10, 11 = 71; and the held number is 14, not the 19 I had drawn
The 24 new lines were described as 18 in one place and 24 in another 18 on the three sale houses + 6 on the three others = 24, stated once
5/13 counted task completion, not evidence six defined states, and only Held + Verified count — a defective certificate can never inflate it
Next action was called a formula reading the first open subtask ClickUp cannot walk an ordered child list. It is a written field, refreshed at the Monday review
Dissolving the quotes list lost the approval Approved by / Approved on are columns on the maintenance row
Arrears summed bills by paid date, so a part-paid bill vanished each row carries billed / paid / balance, and the register sums positive balances
The maintenance total did not match the register the R74,221.58 gap is Bloom's fit-out, and Bloom is a tenancy with no property row — it rolls up to the tenancy
Leases were "4 rows" here and "6" there 21 moved + Bloom = 22, with the INF Office Space row held as unresolved, not as income
Recurrence said 60 days but the dates were 90 dates corrected: 2027-05-01 and 2027-12-02 are exactly 60 days out

What both reviews asked for that I did not take. Both wanted the documents back as one flat register, because a flat list answers what is missing everywhere more cheaply and more reliably than a sub-task view. That is technically correct and it is what you told me not to do, so I kept the subtasks and covered the question with a saved view. The cost is honest: that view is built by hand in the UI, so it needs checking whenever ClickUp changes a filter, and if this ever grows past a dozen properties the flat register becomes the better shape. At six houses, density wins.

6. What this design still cannot do

  • It cannot make a certificate real. It records state and evidence; somebody still has to ring the installer.
  • It cannot auto-create an inspection when a tenant leaves. ClickUp has no event trigger for that; the monthly review is the net.
  • It cannot fix the deeds search. Samuel's title deed is not in the document and will not appear in ClickUp until the deeds office answers.
  • It cannot close the 27 cards. None of them can be closed from the document; the register tells them where the evidence is, nothing more.
