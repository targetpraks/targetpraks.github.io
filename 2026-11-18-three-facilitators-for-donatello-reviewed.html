02 — The decision, now that money is equal

This is a two-horse race and it should be described as one. Both women cost R10 000 a month and R127 000 for the year. What separates them is a single question: who teaches this boy to read?

The case for Panashe is narrower than it was last week and it is still the stronger one. The plan the school has made the place conditional on is Barton delivered daily by the facilitator, plus a reader and a scribe. She is the only candidate with a reader-and-scribe certificate, the only one trained in a structured-literacy intervention (Search and Teach, NILD SA), and the only one holding a Foundation Phase degree — the phase a boy reading at Grade R–3 level actually needs. She has also run literacy interventions inside an eight-child assisted class with this exact profile.

The case for Kara-Anne is that she is the more experienced, more available, more verifiable teacher, and she is already inside the building. Twenty-plus years, a UCT Higher Diploma in Education, currently teaching Grade 4–7 and Grade 5 English Home Language at Durbanville Primary, and reading exams for concessions at Reddam since 2021. She has her own transport and can start immediately. What she has never done is remediate reading — and Barton would be her first exposure to it.

What I would do at this price

Run a paid trial before the offer. Ninety minutes each with the boy, this week, at their day rate — roughly R500 each. Judge three things only: whether he will sit with her, whether she reads him accurately (does she notice what he cannot do), and whether she can hold his attention for ninety minutes without the lesson collapsing.

At 0.668 the lane is telling you it would probably go with Panashe and that a third of the answer is on the other side. A trial is the cheapest way to convert that doubt into evidence, and it is the only test that matters for a role whose entire success is one adult and one child in a room together. If a trial is impossible before 1 December, appoint Panashe — but then buy the Barton training before term starts, because with her it is reinforcement and with the other two it would be the first time.

04 — Panashe, in full

Read from her CV and the attached letter, and corroborated independently.

The qualification is real, it is the right one, and the university confirms it

Bachelor of Education in Foundation Phase from Rhodes University, 2022, with distinction — the four-year, phase-specific degree for Grades R–3, which is where the boy's reading sits while he is placed in Grade 4. And it is no longer only her word: Rhodes' own 2022 notice lists her among the six Education students nominated by the Dean for its Top 100 Academic Achievement Award.

She has already taught the classroom this job describes

At Hillview Academy, March 2023 to September 2025, support teacher inside a Grade 4 assisted class of eight learners with ADD, ADHD, dyslexia and autism spectrum disorder — small-group literacy and maths interventions, plus one-on-one scaffolding across the academic day. She produces a number, not an adjective

  • No one-to-one in-school facilitation, and no mainstream independent school experience. Hillview was a small independent; Resilience is a specialist school. Reddam is neither.

Why she is still second — and how close it is

None of it is teaching a child to decode. No remedial qualification, no structured-literacy training, no reader-scribe course certificate — her reading work is exam accommodation, which is a different skill from remediation — and no record of facilitating one child through a school day. At R18 000 that was disqualifying; at R10 000 it is a trade you could defensibly make, and the lane prices it at 0.332 rather than dismissing it.

If the trial favours her instead

She is appointable. If she sits down with him and it works, the gaps are closable: Barton tutor training in the December holidays, and the reader-scribe course she is already doing the practical half of at Reddam. What you would be buying is twenty years of classroom judgement, a teacher who can start on Monday, and an adult who already knows the building and the concession process. What you would be giving up is the remedial training — and that is a real trade, not an even one.
