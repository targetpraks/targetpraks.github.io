The three things that actually decide it

1. The POA you will never get

This is the whole ballgame, and the two answers are not close. Bianca's letter describes notification in South Africa as "particularly challenging" and puts the no-POA road at "two years or longer". Simone's contact describes a public-notice route that still finalises, only "slightly longer". One of those two is being optimistic — that is the single claim worth testing in writing before any money moves. Ask both for the same thing: the realistic months, without the POA, with the public-notice route named.

2. The citizenship evidence — it is not a "ground", it is a separate submission

Registering a foreign divorce in Portugal is an administrative transcription before the civil registry. Her citizenship file lives with the consulate and the central registry — a different file. So "used within the submission of our divorce" in practice means a supporting report filed alongside, not a pleading inside the transcription. That is exactly what Bianca's EST-000348 does, and it is why her firm already knows the facts of the fraudulent application. Quote A has never been asked the question. Put it to both in the same words and compare the answers — and the price.

3. What "R45,000" and "R46,530" actually cover

Bianca's single line item is with additional procuração — that is the POA variant. Ask what the fee is on the no-POA route, because the quote you have is literally priced for a scenario that will not happen. On the other side, Simone's number is the more complete one on paper but is stale and its no-POA timeline is unquantified.
