Rowmat, and what beats it in South Africa

Short answer: Rowmat is not the cheap option — it is the premium Greek one. The same cement-coated XPS board lands in Durban for R114–123 a square metre from China, against R541 for Rowmat at Greek retail and R231–481 for the European brands. South Africa has almost no XPS backer board on the shelf, so the gap is a price gap and a distribution gap at the same time.

Summary

  • Cheaper by a wide margin: China. Quoted FOB is $2.00–4.50 a board (1200 × 600 × 20 mm), which is R46–104 per m² of material. Rowmat's equivalent sells at R541 per m² retail in Greece.
  • Cheaper by about half: the European mid-market. Jackoboard (BEWI), Marmox and Orbry trade at R231–291 per m² for 12.5 mm. Only wedi (R481) sits near Rowmat's price.
  • The freight is not the problem. Board is bulky but light: a 40 ft high-cube carries about 2,870 m² of 20 mm board and adds only R17–26 per m² in ocean freight. That is why China wins on landed cost even after shipping.
  • South Africa is the real gap. Builders, CTM and Tile Africa public catalogues carry fibre cement and gypsum, not XPS backer board. The incumbent is Nutec fibre cement at about R97 per m², which is cheap but heavy, uninsulated and not a wet-area system.
  • Better than cheap: sell the system, not the board. Board plus washers, tape, adhesive, pre-formed niches and trays, with tiler training, is what makes Rowmat worth R541. Copy that, at R300–350 retail, and the margin is 55–65%.
  • Fire class E is the ceiling on every foam-core board. For commercial and multi-storey work, a non-combustible sister product is needed, and Agrément certification is the door opener.

1. Rowmat is the premium Greek option, not the cheap one

Rowmat is a cement-coated XPS board made in Larissa by Gagos Industry S.A., the company behind Hellas Stones. The board is an extruded polystyrene core, glass-fibre mesh and a polymer-modified cement face on both sides, sold as a system with Row plugs and hybrid adhesive, and backed by a training centre. It is good product. It is also priced like a premium brand: one Greek retailer lists the 32 mm flat board at €43.80 for a 2.5 × 0.6 m sheet, which is 1.5 m², so €29.20 a square metre.

That is above every European mid-market brand in this report and only wedi is in the same room. Rowmat's own published figures: face tensile strength over 400 kPa (EN 1607), fire class E (EN 13501-1), lambda 0.033 W/mK at 25 years (EN 12667), weights of 6.0 kg/m² at 12 mm rising to 9.4 kg/m² at 102 mm.

3. The China lane

Two kinds of supplier show up in the listings:

  • Trading companies and factories on Alibaba, quoting $2.00–4.50 a board for 1200 × 600 × 20 mm cement-coated XPS, with minimums from 50 to 200 pieces. Hangzhou Tyco quotes $4–25 for its range with a 2,000-piece minimum.
  • Export-facing panel suppliers such as Banarta, which publish a datasheet: XPS density 30–45 kg/m³, compression 300 kPa or better, water absorption 0.2% by volume, fire class E, thickness 6–50 mm. That is a normal, competent specification for this product family.

What China does not give you is a tested system or a certificate a South African insurer or quantity surveyor will accept. That is bought separately, with sample testing and with Agrément.

5. What South Africa already sells

The public catalogues of Builders, CTM and Tile Africa do not list an XPS backer board; what they carry in this space is fibre cement and gypsum. The established wet-area method is still cement plaster or fibre cement plus a liquid waterproofing membrane.

That is a real market: South Africa's waterproofing membrane market is put at US$354.5 million in 2025 growing to US$401.0 million by 2030. Tile backer boards are a small, premium slice of it, and the slice is currently served by importers and specialists rather than by the chains.

Two things to confirm by phone before committing money: which specialist merchants already import Jackoboard, wedi or Marmox, and what the tilers in Cape Town and Johannesburg actually pay. Absence from three retail websites is a strong signal, not proof.

6. What "better" means here

Four product families compete for the same wall. They are not interchangeable:

Family Waterproof Fire Weight Cheap The catch
Cement-coated XPS (Rowmat, wedi, Jackoboard) Yes, the board is the waterproof layer Class E 6–7 kg/m² at 20 mm Mid Combustible core limits commercial use
Fibre cement (Nutec, HardieBacker) No, needs a membrane A1 non-combustible 12–17 kg/m² Yes, and local Heavy, dusty, no insulation, needs framing
Magnesium oxide board Resists water, not a waterproof layer A1 claimed 10–14 kg/m² Yes from China Brittle, chloride and moisture history, variable quality
Calcium silicate Resists water A1 10–14 kg/m² No Expensive, harder to cut

"Better than Rowmat" for this market is therefore not a different core. It is four things:

  1. Fewer joints. A 2.4 m or 2.5 m board instead of 1.2 m halves the joint count on a shower wall.
  2. A complete system in one box — board, washers, sealing tape, adhesive, pre-formed niche, shower tray. Accessories carry the margin and make the board hard to swap out.
  3. A tested assembly with a named adhesive and waterproofing brand, so the tiler and the building inspector have a document.
  4. A non-combustible sister product for commercial work, so one sales call covers both jobs.

7. The recommendation

Import white-label from China, sell the system, and use the European mid-market as the quality reference.

  • Start with three SKUs: 10 mm for floors and underfloor heating, 20 mm for walls, 50 mm for box-outs and benches. Sizes 1200 × 600 and 2400 × 600.
  • Two suppliers, not one. Banarta and Hangzhou Tyco both publish specifications; a third quote from a Hebei or Shandong factory keeps them honest.
  • Test before the first container: density 35 kg/m³ or better, face adhesion after 24 hours of water, compression at 300 kPa, water absorption under 0.5%, and a fire test certificate that names the test house.
  • Price at R329 retail for 20 mm walls, R279 for 10 mm floors, R235 wholesale. Landed is R114–123, so the trade margin is comfortable and the retail price is still 40% under Rowmat.
  • Phase two, only above roughly 20,000 m² a year: coat locally. XPS cores are made in South Africa by Thermaboards, Isowall, Sondor and polystyrene.co.za, so a coating and lamination line removes the import lead time and the FX risk. It does not remove much cost.

The one thing not to copy is Rowmat's dependence on a single premium price point. The money in South Africa is at the R300 mark, in volume, with the accessories.

8. Risks and compliance

  • Tariff classification. Cement-faced XPS can be entered under 6810.99 (articles of cement) or 3921.90 (reinforced plastic sheet). Heading 6810 carries 0% general duty in the SACU schedule plus 15% VAT. Get a written tariff determination from SARS through the clearing agent before the first shipment; a wrong code is a refund claim and a delay.
  • Fire class E. The core burns. Check SANS 10400-T requirements for the occupancy before quoting commercial work.
  • Wet-area compliance. SANS 10400-P governs drainage and the waterproofing details around showers and wet areas. The board has to fit the detail, not replace it.
  • Certification. Agrément South Africa issues certificates for innovative building systems on a fit-for-purpose basis. That certificate is what makes a new brand specifiable.
  • Warranty and quality. A 20-year board warranty is only as good as the batch you cannot see. Pre-shipment inspection and retained samples are not optional.
  • Currency. All input cost is in dollars; all revenue in rand. A 10% rand move is 8 points of gross margin. Price in a review clause, or hedge above a threshold.
  • Trade dress. Do not copy Rowmat's board colours, plug design or literature. White-label with your own name and your own datasheet.
