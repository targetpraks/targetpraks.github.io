Two things changed since this morning's brief. The Reserve Bank held the repo rate at 7.25% at today's meeting — by a 4–3 vote, with three members wanting a hike — and October CPI came in at 5.2% against 4.5% in September, driven by fuel. The bank still pays 9.00% nominal and 5.31% after tax, but against 5.2% inflation that is a real return of roughly a tenth of one percent. Cash has stopped paying you to wait.

That does not make a bad beachfront worth buying. It makes the real comparison the only one that matters — which is what follows.

01 Where the holiday money actually is

AirROI tracks every active short-let listing in these towns. Occupancy barely moves — 29% to 37%. What separates the towns is the nightly rate, and therefore what a property can bill in a year.

Hermanus is the anomaly R379 658 a listing against a provincial average near R280 000, at the highest occupancy in the set — and only 53 active listings in a town of Hermanus's size. High rate, high occupancy, thin supply: that is what an undersupplied holiday market looks like on a spreadsheet. Plettenberg Bay earns on rate but sits at 35%. Struisbaai has thin supply (23 listings) and thin bookings (29%).

03 What each one is really buying

Hermanus Beach Club — the pick: R1.35m, 64 m², three beds

The gate did not hesitate here, and the arithmetic explains why: the highest-revenue holiday town in the Western Cape data, the lowest entry price of any beachfront property in this sweep, and a levy just over 1% of the price. At the town's average, scaled to a 64 m² flat, it models at 11.8% net and 7.0% after tax — the only properties in this document that clear the bank on letting income alone.

That result is sensitive to one thing: which Hermanus listing this unit turns out to be. AirROI publishes the spread, so here is the same unit at three points on it.

The catch is structural, not financial. This is 300 sectional-title units across seven body corporates plus 24 freehold seafront houses, under a homeowners' association that has been to the High Court. Seven schemes means seven sets of accounts, seven reserve funds and seven chances of a special levy. Read the conduct rules, the short-let policy and the last three years of financials before you offer — and ask what the association's litigation cost the owners.

The 45 m² two-bed at R1.25m is the cheaper way in and came second at 10.0% net; its smaller size is exactly where the revenue assumption bites hardest.

Keurboomstrand — R2.4m, 80 m², two beds

R900 a month levy on a R2.4m flat is 0.45% a year, which is the single best cost structure here. It sits second row at Keurboomstrand, a five-minute drive from Plett's main beach and a walk from its own. At R30 000/m² it is no bargain against the town, but the low levy means a bad December does not become a cash call.

Brenton-on-Sea — R4.4m, 195 m²

R22 538/m² is the most space per rand on the Garden Route coast, and Brenton's sea-facing position is genuine. Knysna's holiday revenue, though, trails the province at R186 629 a listing. You are buying a large house with a view in a market that pays less than average — good for family use, ordinary as an asset.

Struisbaai and Witsand — the cheap end

Struisbaai's 166 m² three-bed apartment at R2.295m is R13 825/m², and Witsand's front-row 66 m² house at R1.995m is literally on the sand. Both are cheap for the same reason: 23 and no measurable active short-let listings respectively, and a buyer pool that comes down from the Cape for December. Witsand has no AirROI series at all — which I will not paper over with a provincial average.

Bloubergstrand and Muizenberg — metro beaches

Bloubergstrand's freehold beach house at R7.995m is twenty minutes from the CBD with Table Mountain in the frame and no levy; that is a different asset class from a resort flat, and it prices like one. Muizenberg at R55 147/m² is the weakest holiday market measured (R1 808 a night, 30% occupancy) at a premium per metre — the village is lovely and the arithmetic is not.

Three things the table cannot tell you 1. The long-let fallback. Property24 throttled after about forty page loads, so the to-let comparisons for these towns were not read this session. I have not guessed them; treat the holiday column as the only modelled exit.
2. The body corporate rules. Check the short-let policy in writing where the plan is holiday letting — a ban after transfer is the expensive version of this mistake.
3. The levy you were not quoted. Two listings here (Struisbaai, Muizenberg) refused a second fetch, so their levies are marked "not read". On a R2.3m flat a R2 500 levy instead of R1 500 moves the net by about a percentage point.

04 The pick

Hermanus Beach Club, three-bed, R1.35m — investigate it, do not buy it on a brochure. The case rests on a market with 53 active listings, the highest occupancy measured and a nightly rate 24% above the provincial average; the risk rests on a seven-scheme ownership structure. Both are real, and the diligence list is short: the seven sets of financials, the short-let rule in writing, the association's litigation history, and one question to a letting agent — what did the last three Decembers actually bill?

05 Method and gaps

Short-let gross is the town's AirROI average per active listing, scaled for the unit: 0.70–0.75 for a one-bed or a small flat, 0.90–1.00 for a standard two-to-three bed, 1.10–1.15 for a large house, 1.40 for top-end beachfront. That scaling is an assumption and it is the one number in the model I have chosen rather than measured — the town averages and every cost line are sourced. Costs: 15% platform, 20% management, R450 cleaning per turnover at a 3.4-night average stay, R12 000 a year linen and consumables, actual levies and rates, insurance at 0.45% of value and maintenance at 0.75%. Net rent is then taxed at 41% so it compares with a taxed deposit. Gearing would improve these figures by sheltering rent behind bond interest.

Gaps, stated: no AirROI series exists for Witsand, Bloubergstrand, St Helena Bay or Gansbaai, so those four cannot be costed as holiday lets from this evidence. Levies for the Struisbaai apartment and the Muizenberg flat were not read. And long-let comps were blocked by the site's throttling — the fallback exit is unknown for every row.
