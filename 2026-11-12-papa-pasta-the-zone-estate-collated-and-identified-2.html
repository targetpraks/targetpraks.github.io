01What Papa Pasta is

A freshly extruded pasta quick-service franchise for South Africa, owned by Infinity Brands. Fresh pasta is made in-store by bronze-die extrusion from a proprietary "Golden Balance" flour blend — 70% Senatore Cappelli durum, 20% white sorghum, 8% chicory inulin, 2% golden flaxseed. The brand system is the "Living Crest": a fixed black-and-white crest outline that takes the colour of each territory's schools and football clubs. Tagline: Fresh. Seasonal. Quality.

Three parts make the model: a central kitchen producing dry pasta, sauces and pre-portioned ingredients; compact stores of roughly 40 m² built for takeaway and delivery; and a collectible culture of seasonal cups, limited packaging and an AR loyalty programme. The menu runs four tiers — core (8 dishes), seasonal rotation, six annual brand "take-overs" by corporate partners, and artisanal limited drops.

02The zone system — what is actually being sold

The word "zone" carries two different products in Papa Pasta's paperwork, and almost every confusion downstream comes from that.

  • A zone licence — the right to open and operate all Papa Pasta stores inside a defined territory, bought outright at a tier price from R200,000 to R3.9m. This is what the Coda document sells, and what the 135-zone template is priced for.
  • A zone-owner layer — a regional partner who buys a much cheaper territory right (R150,000–R600,000), operates a flagship store, and earns a share of royalty on the stores he recruits and supports, while Papa Pasta contracts directly with each franchisee. This is Luke's review structure, and it is the active plan.

Both sit on the same three-layer shape: Papa Pasta (franchisor of record) → zone owner (territory, recruitment, audits, support) → store operator (capital, staff, daily trade). The difference is who carries the store's capital and how the zone is paid: one big licence fee, or a small territory fee plus two points of turnover.

A five-store zone under the active structure earns its owner exactly nothing: 5 × R216,000 = R1.08m of royalty against R1.08m of zone overhead (a manager, QA, travel, training, office and zone marketing). Three stores leaves R62,280; four pays R275,040; five is the first size worth selling. A second zone manager arrives at store seven and takes the owner's cash from R418,032 back to R55,704 — which is why zones should be drawn at six or at ten, never at seven to nine.

The working paper's recommendation is a five per cent royalty split 3% zone / 2% Papa Pasta, with the zone buying 25% of each store it supports. Head office keeps every rand it keeps today because the fourth and fifth points never belonged to it; the operator's entry ticket falls by about a quarter; the zone owner earns 23.0% on R2.125m at five stores and 25.6% at ten. It also cuts regional coverage cost per store from R198,000 (employed field staff) to R144,000 (two points of turnover).

The arithmetic nobody has run yet. Zone income has never been modelled at Express volume. At R287,000 a month instead of R600,000, two points is R68,000 a store a year against R216,000 of zone overhead — the layer inverts, and a three-store Local cluster grosses R648,000 against R936,000 of cost. The active plan's fee ladder is safe only if stores actually reach the planning number.

08Legal state — nothing can be sold yet

Under South African law the zone agreements are very likely franchise agreements in their own right: they grant the right to trade under Papa Pasta's brand and system in a defined territory for a continuing relationship and a fee. That triggers the full Consumer Protection Act s7 and Regulation 2/3 apparatus for every zone sale, not just for store sales.

  • Disclosure document, 14 days before signature (Reg 3) — with per-page assumptions on any projection, a going-concern certificate from an accounting officer, the franchisee list, the organogram, and an annual update. Papa Pasta's disclosure documents are In review in the register, not approved.
  • 10-business-day cooling-off (s7(2)) — the agreement must reproduce the exact statutory text, and the 25%-on-signature territory release has to be aligned to it or the brand carries refund exposure.
  • Marketing fund in a separate bank account (Reg 2) with annual statements to franchisees. A ledger is not an account.
  • Territory schedule in the contract — the clusters are a planning atlas, not a legal territory description; encroachment and online/delivery allocation rules must be written into the agreement.
  • Commission disclosure — the R100,000 launch commission sits inside the store commitment fee and must be disclosed with clawbacks. "Not PPP earnings" does not exempt it.
  • Tax and competition — upfront territory fees are capital to the payer and income to Papa Pasta (SARS Guide G23), VAT at 15%, and the central-kitchen supply mandate needs objective justification under the Competition Act.

Current position: the disclosure document in the build estate is stamped on its first line "[TEMPLATE — NOT YET COUNSEL-APPROVED] This FDD template must be reviewed and executed by the appointed franchise attorney (Werksmans / Webber Wentzel / Adams & Adams) before use with any prospect." The Zoho register agrees: the CPA Reg 3 disclosure document sits at In review, its cooling-off record at Not started, the territory pack at Not started. No zone can be sold, no territory fee collected and no cooling-off clock started until counsel signs. Every table in this document is planning arithmetic that assumes zero sales until that gate opens.

10What has to move, in dependency order

  1. Settle the map. Luke's 38 clusters are the active plan on paper; the platform still serves papa-pasta-v7-2 and resolves the live set by newest creation date. Import the clusters as a zone set and mark v7.2 superseded, or the first prospect is quoted off the wrong map.
  2. Settle the price. R150k–R600k for a territory versus R200k–R3.9m for a zone licence is a factor of six on the same geography. Pick one product and withdraw the other rate card from circulation.
  3. Build the territory schedule. The clusters need contractual boundaries with encroachment and online/delivery rules — a planning atlas will not survive a dispute.
  4. Close the disclosure gate. CPA Reg 3 document, cooling-off text, separate marketing-fund account, commission disclosure. Nothing sells before counsel signs.
  5. Model the volume floor. Run the zone P&L at Express volume (R287k) as well as base (R600k) before any fee ladder is published — it is the difference between a paying zone and an inverted one.
  6. Decide the central kitchen's reach. If the kitchen cannot serve Limpopo, the Northern Cape or the KZN interior, a third of the active atlas is unservable and the cluster count falls.
