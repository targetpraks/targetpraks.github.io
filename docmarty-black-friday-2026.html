Does 1 g a day do anything?

The label dose is 1 g of extract a day. The human evidence sits around and above that:

  • The Mori 2009 trial in mild cognitive impairment used 3 g a day of 96% fruiting-body powder for 16 weeks and found scores rising through the trial — then falling again within 4 weeks of stopping. Small (30 people), but it is the trial everyone cites.
  • A 2025 acute study used a single 3 g dose of 10:1 fruiting-body extract in young adults and found no overall cognitive effect after 90 minutes — only one task improved. Other trials used 1.8 g and 2.4 g a day.
  • So 1 g/day is a maintenance dose, not the trial dose. Expect a modest effect if any, and continuity matters more than the brand — the gains in the Mori trial faded when people stopped.

The Prime version adds mycelium to the fruiting body at the same 500 mg per capsule and charges 23.6% more per day for it. The decision lane put that premium at 0.59 — under the bar — so pay it only if you specifically want erinacines from mycelium.
