The short version before the long one. A guarantee is not a yield; it is a promise paid for in the price. The only deal in this document that pays you 9%-plus without anyone guaranteeing anything is a R799 000 two-bed in Parow, twelve minutes from Canal Walk, and that has been true across two reports now. Everything else here is either a lifestyle purchase, a bet on a growth corridor, or a hospitality business wearing a sectional-title jacket.

01 The frame: what the money must beat

The Reserve Bank held the repo rate at 7.25% at today's meeting — a 4–3 split with three members voting to hike. October CPI printed 5.2%, up from 4.5%, driven by fuel. The RSA Retail Savings Bond still pays 9.00% fixed, which is 5.31% after 41% tax and roughly a tenth of one percent after inflation.

So the bar is low in real terms and the temptation is to accept a bad asset because cash is worse. That is exactly the trap a guaranteed-return brochure is built for. Hold the line at 5.31% after tax, after costs, and most of what follows fails — which is the finding.

02 The corridor thesis: Cape Town up the N1 to Paarl

You are right about the shape of it. Cape Town's metro bowl is built out and priced accordingly, and the release valve is the N1 north — Brackenfell, Kraaifontein, Klapmuts, then Paarl. Two things prove the direction of travel better than any agent's opinion: the state is putting a R62.8 million wastewater works upgrade into Klapmuts (Stellenbosch Municipality MTREF 2026/27), and Wesgro is backing a proposed Innovation and Educational Precinct — a "smart city" at Klapmuts in partnership with Stellenbosch University. Infrastructure money and a university precinct are the two things that turn a farm corridor into a town.

The infrastructure story is real but partial, and the two researchers checked it from the municipal side: Drakenstein's annual report records a R1.4 billion bulk sewer and treatment programme, the province has finished the R301 dualling between the N1 and Kliprug Road, and Stellenbosch's draft water-services plan names Klapmuts among its bulk-water and sewer needs. Watch the pricing games, too. Bergendal Country Villas advertises phase three from R1.19m on its own site while its agency quotes from R1.68m with inconsistent phase labels. Do not underwrite a headline "from" price without an available-unit schedule.

Paarl has the widest spread in the province: 142 houses at Calais Estate from R2.14m at one end, and Vivante Village South at Val de Vie from R4 995 000 for a 71 m² two-bed — R70 352/m² — at the other. Zanddrift Suites, the best-documented apartment scheme under R5m, asks R3 495 000 for a 75–77 m² two-bed, also about R46 600/m², inside an estate that will eventually hold 112 erven, 16 townhouses, 139 retirement homes and 50 assisted-living cottages.

The corridor caution Paarl's average sale price rose 22% to just over R3.8 million in the first half of 2026 on R2.31 billion of turnover. Growth like that is what makes a corridor thesis attractive and what makes buying at R46 000–70 000/m² dangerous: you are paying today's price for yesterday's growth. A new two-bed at Zanddrift rents for roughly R13 500 a month in Paarl — 4.6% gross, about 2.9% net. The corridor is a capital-growth bet, not a cash-flow one.

03 Beach: the West Coast and the East Coast are not the same market

West Coast (Langebaan, Yzerfontein, Paternoster) is a weekend market: 29–33% holiday occupancy, high nightly rates (Yzerfontein R4 358), and a buyer pool that comes up the R27 on Friday. East Coast (Hermanus through to Plettenberg Bay) is a holiday market: better occupancy, more stock, and one town — Hermanus — with the best revenue per listing in the entire Western Cape dataset.

Depth separates the two coasts as much as price. Yzerfontein is the only coastal town with a hard growth series anyone could source — +12.9% year on year, doubled in five years — and its stock is tightening: listings fell 19% from May to October 2026. Langebaan carries 1,444 listings (−11.8%); Knysna 1,051 (−9.9%); Plettenberg Bay 512 (−9.7%). Tightening supply is a better friend to a buyer than a brochure yield.

The buyer pools genuinely differ. West Coast visitor surveys record 84.47% domestic visitors, 78% of them from the Western Cape — a weekend drive. The Garden Route's 2025 survey records 54.76% international visitors, and George airport handled 911,354 passengers in 2025, up 10.56%. That is a touring market with a real air bridge: better for holiday letting, more exposed to a global downturn, and seasonally brutal — December's 95,858 airport passengers against February's 65,394.

Only Hermanus clears the bank, and only because one club has 53 competing listings in a town of its size. The two cheapest beachfront entries in the province — Witsand's front-row house at R1.995m and Struisbaai's 166 m² flat at R2.295m — cannot be costed as businesses at all: Witsand has no short-let data series, and Struisbaai's levy is unpublished. Cheap beachfront is cheap because the exit is another holiday buyer.

04 Aparthotels: the revenue split is the whole deal

Aparthotels are the one category where the headline yield can look like 11% and the bankable number be 4.5%. Four structures exist and they are not interchangeable: a hotel revenue pool (owner gets a share of gross, operator takes the rest), a headlease (fixed rent from the operator), a managed letting (owner keeps the income, pays a fee), and plain sectional title in a hotel building (owner does everything).

Three facts that decide this category 1. Pool units are cash-only. The Colosseum listing says it outright: banks do not finance units inside a rental pool. You are buying with cash or not at all.
2. The split is 71/29 at Century City. The Colosseum's owner receives 71% of rental and the hotel pays the levies out of it. At the Island Club, an owner reports roughly R4 500 a month net on a R1.2m unit — 4.5% — against another listing claiming 6.5% net after all costs. Demand three years of owner statements before believing either.
3. Levies are the trap. The Taj Hotel Residences charges R11 612 a month in levies plus R4 126 in rates on a 104 m² unit. At The Rockefeller the hotel pays your levies while the unit stays in the pool — leave the pool and the cost returns.

Where the price per metre is lowest per rand of income — Century City at R26 000–31 250/m², against R63 000–65 700/m² in the CBD and Sea Point — the arithmetic is closest to working. A Century City pool unit at R1.2m returning R4 500 a month nets 4.5%, pre-tax, with no bond available. The same R1.2m in Parow buys one and a half two-bed flats returning 9.6% net, bondable. That comparison is the report.

05 Guaranteed returns: what the guarantee actually guarantees

Four schemes publish usable terms. The best of them, on duration, is not the one being marketed hardest: KLEI at Devonbosch in Stellenbosch pays 8% for three years from transfer, where everyone else pays 9–10% for twelve months. A three-year guarantee is worth roughly three times a one-year guarantee of the same size — and it is the only structure here where the developer is on the hook past the first letting season.

The one with the clearest published paperwork is The Marlow on Mill, Newlands, and it is worth reading as the template for how these should be written.

Guarantee 10% p.a. of total purchase price
Base apartment + furniture pack + aircon
Term 12 months, paid monthly
Operational costs seller's account, not deducted
Rates and levies buyer's account
Net yield claimed ≈ 9.1%
Entry price R1.72m, VAT incl, no transfer duty
Finance up to 100% bond, R10 000 deposit

A 9.1% net for twelve months is better than anything else in this document — including Parow. Two things stop it being the answer.

First, it lasts twelve months. The guarantee is the seller paying you; at month 13 the apartment has to earn its keep in the Newlands short-stay market with no protection at all.

Second, you can see it in the price. The R1.72m entry buys roughly 21 m² — R81 905/m², the highest per-metre price in Newlands today, against a suburb resale median of R63 761/m² across 16 live listings. On that unit the premium over the median is about R381 000; the guarantee pays R172 000. You are paying roughly 2.2 times the guarantee in price. New build legitimately commands a premium, so some of that gap is not the guarantee — but the guarantee is not free money, and no seller can tell you which portion is which.

The other schemes found are weaker evidence: BlackBrick's Cape Town launch offered a 12-month guarantee, an anticipated 11.23% gross yield and no transfer duty, but the material dates to 2023 and should be read as precedent, not an offer. Val de Vie's Vivante Village South offers no guarantee at all — only a claim that its predecessor achieved capital growth of up to 35% a year before transfer, which is a marketing number, not a promise.

Note what the ranking does not tell you: the two at the top (9% and 10%) are twelve-month promises from a seller, the two in the middle are per-month rand amounts with unpublished durations, and the one at the bottom is not a guarantee at all — it is a revenue pool, and it is the only one of the five you can compare against three years of actual owner statements.

The test to apply to any guarantee Ask who is legally on the hook, for how many months, on what base (price, or price plus extras), and what the unit earns in month thirteen. Then price the guarantee: take the guaranteed unit's R/m² and set it against the same street's unguaranteed resale. If the gap is roughly the guarantee divided by the term, you are being paid with your own money.

And the ones above R5 million

You said the cap was mine, not yours, so here is everything above it. Only one researcher entry crossed the line — Codex self-capped at R5m because the brief told it to, Claude did not — so most of this band comes from my own sweeps of the Property24 new-development indices and the coastal listings.

Two things change when the budget moves. First, the arithmetic gets worse, not better: every property above R5m in this dataset is a position purchase — beachfront at Keurboomstrand, freehold at Bloubergstrand, farm estate in Paarl North — and not one of them clears the 5.31% hurdle on letting income. Above R5m you are buying scarcity and use, and should expect to be paid in capital growth or in family Decembers, not in rent.

Second, where the money is spent matters more than how much of it. Vivante Village South is the clearest example: the R4.995m two-bed is 71 m² at R70 352/m², while the R6.1m three-bed is 106–114 m² at about R55 455/m². For 22% more money you get 55% more space and a 21% lower price per metre, in the same village. If you are going to spend above the cap, spend it where the per-metre price falls — that is the only version of "more expensive" that is also "better value".

They were given the same brief and worked blind. Their top three picks share no property, which is itself the finding — the market is broad enough that two competent runs see different value. What follows is the disagreement, with the evidence on both sides.

On guarantees, Codex is the sceptic and Claude is the accountant. Codex refuses to treat Maritime Place as a guaranteed deal because the legal guarantor is not named, the clock started in September 2026 and a fresh term on resale is not promised — "advertisements establish advertised terms, not realised returns". Claude instead hunted for units that publish an actual rand amount and computed the net from it, which is how the Rockefeller's 5.8% emerged. Both are right about different things.

On the corridor, Claude found the cleaner entry. Twin Oaks Phase 1 in Brackenfell South asks R1.679m for 64 m² — R26 234/m², levy R1 500, transfer costs included, occupation about September 2027. Codex went to Paarl instead, to Honeydew's Oak Lodges at R2.489m. Claude's is the better risk-adjusted buy only because two or three of them fit inside the R5m budget; Codex's is the better expression of the Paarl thesis.

On the coast they simply picked different towns. Codex went north (Keurboomstrand, Britannia Bay, Paternoster, Langebaan), Claude went Overberg (Yzerfontein, Hermanus, Struisbaai, Kleinmond, Knysna). Neither found a coastal unit that clears the bank on published income — which is the same answer I reached from the AirROI data, arrived at from a different direction.

What they agree on: Hermanus Beach Club as the cheapest credible coastal entry, the Rockefeller as the only aparthotel with a defensible net figure, Val de Vie's Vivante South as the expensive end of Paarl, and Twin Oaks/Brackenfell as the corridor's live stock. Four convergences across two blind runs is the strongest evidence in this document.

Corridor capital growth, not cash flow
  1. Buy the cash flow where it exists. Parow at R799 000, 9.6% net, 5.6% after tax, bondable, no guarantee and no operator. Everything else here is a bet on something.
  2. If the corridor thesis is the real interest, buy land or a plot-and-plan in Paarl, not a R46 000/m² apartment. Azalea Acres sells 45 plots from R1.86m; Firwoods River Estate 61 plots from R2.8m. The upside of a corridor is captured by whoever owns the dirt before the town arrives.
  3. Treat the beach as a use decision. If the family will spend December there, Hermanus Beach Club is the only coastal property in two reports that clears the bank on letting alone — and the seven-body-corporate structure is the price of that.
  4. Take the Marlow guarantee to a conveyancer, not an agent. Ask for the guarantee clause, the seller's obligation, and the month-13 letting assumption. If the numbers survive that, a 9.1% net for a year with a 100% bond is not nothing.
  5. Do not buy a hotel pool unit with money you might need. No bank finance, a 71/29 split, and an exit that depends on the operator's balance sheet.

09 Method and what is missing

Three legs. Mine: Property24 listing and new-development sweeps read in the browser, AirROI town data, developer and agency pages, and the municipal budget for Klapmuts. Codex and Claude: one written brief, two separate git-isolated directories, no sight of each other's output, live sources only, a URL required on every figure, unknown values nulled, and an instruction to stop rather than invent if access failed. Codex returned 26 candidates in 9 beachside, 9 new-development, 5 aparthotel and 3 guaranteed-return; Claude returned 25, weighted to new developments. Both stamped sources 20 November 2026 (their machine clock) against my 19 November. Their candidate lists were validated on merge — a URL that does not resolve, a price with no source was dropped.
