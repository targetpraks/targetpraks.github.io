Why the first list was wrong

The first list was optimised for the wrong thing. It screened for cheap, fragmented, succession-heavy firms — and accounting fits all three, which is exactly why a dozen consolidators are already bidding for them.

But a roll-up's return depends on the revenue still being there at exit. Accounting's fee base is compliance filing, bookkeeping, payroll calculation and tax prep — the processing of information. That is the one category of work a language model does at near-zero marginal cost, at a quality that gets better every quarter.

The specific error: I priced the multiple arbitrage and ignored the terminal value. A merged group of ten accounting firms trading at 7× is only worth 7× if a buyer in 2036 still believes those fees exist. On current trajectory, that is the most contestable assumption in the whole thesis.

So the screen inverts. Instead of asking is this cheap and fragmented, it asks:

What does a machine have to physically show up to do? Embodied labour at a location. A statutory mandate with a signature attached. A licence that rations who may perform the work. An installed base that took decades to build.

EmbodiedA certified human with tools must be at a lift shaft, a roof, a care home, a hullThe output is a document, a calculation, a design or a line of code

The honest counter-argument

AI does not delete these businesses. It compresses their headcount and their admin cost — which is an argument for owning them at scale rather than against. A lift contract still exists in 2036; what changes is that the small independent cannot afford the dispatch software, the compliance reporting and the technician wages. That is the condition that makes a roll-up work, and it is why this list is a better fit for the thesis than the accounting one was.

The genuine long-term risks here are different, and each is a diligence question:

  • Regulatory. A mandate removed or relaxed kills the recurring revenue. Check the statute's age and political exposure.
  • Labour supply. Technician shortages bid up wages faster than contracts reprice. Check contract indexation.
  • Licence value. Concessions get re-tendered. Check the term and the renewal record.
  • Owner dependence. A licensed operator whose founder holds the registration is worth less than the contract book suggests.

How to price these, and what to ask for

Same ladder as before — Spain 3.0–4.5× below €300k EBITDA rising to 6.0–8.5× above €1.5M, Portugal 4.0–7.0× for services and 5.0–8.0× for clinics — but with one difference that matters: these firms deserve a premium to accounting, not a discount. A maintenance contract book with statutory renewal is a better asset than a book of tax filings, and the market has not yet repriced that.

The data request, in order: contract book by renewal date; client concentration; technician count and licence registrations; average contract value and indexation clause; the regulatory citation that makes the work mandatory. That last one is what you are actually buying — and it is the one thing AI cannot write for you.
