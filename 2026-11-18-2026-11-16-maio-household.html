The four days, in words

  • Beds stripped and changed first — all four.
  • Bathrooms: dry, no marks, towels out.

Tuesday · deep day
  • Kitchen deep clean, inside and under.
  • Living & dining: surfaces, glass, floor.
  • The week's ironing, then uniforms in.
Thursday · rooms day
  • The children's rooms, top to bottom.
  • Bathrooms again — mid-week reset.
  • Study and passages, then floors.
Saturday · reset day
  • Whole-house reset before the weekend.
  • Deep jobs 1–4 off the standing list.
  • Courtyard and bins, uniforms washed.

The deep jobs 1–4 — Saturday

The four rotating jobs behind "deep jobs 1–4". One a week, in rotation, so nothing waits more than a month.

  • 1 · Windows and glass — inside, and the outside of the ground-floor panes.
  • 2 · Cupboards and shelves — one block of the house: kitchen cupboards or the bedroom built-ins.
  • 3 · Appliances — oven, fridge shelves, washing-machine filter, kettle and toaster.
  • 4 · Soft furnishings — curtains, cushions, rugs beaten and aired, mattress turned.

The four jobs are the house's own rotation. Agree the order with Lily on the Friday walk.

The one rule that overrides the list

Nothing of Ricardo's is moved to another room without Lily knowing where it went. The list can slip a day; a found-again laptop cannot.

03 The gardener

Tuesday and Friday. Front first, then back. The lawn once a week, the solar panels once a month, and nothing else — the pool stays with Ricardo. Lily runs his days and the standard.

What is agreed

  • Tuesday — front garden, then back. Lawn mown and edged.
  • Friday — back garden, then front. Beds, weeds, hedges, whatever the week left.

The Friday walk — four steps, then hand it over

  1. Walk the house against the standard at 16:00 — every room, not a glance.
  2. Write the list: what did not get done, what ran out.
  3. Hand it to the domestic before she leaves on Saturday.
  4. Nothing gets bought twice; nothing runs out on a Sunday night.

Thursday — the check-and-plan list

The week's plan, written down before the weekend: what is short, what is finished, what the domestic and the gardener owe. Julie does hers on Wednesday.

10 Open questions — settle these

Everything on this page is built from documents that disagree in a few places, and from assumptions nobody has confirmed. These are the ones that matter. Tick them off here and the page gets corrected once, for everyone.

Where the documents contradict each other

  1. The nights. Split 22:00–02:00 / 02:00–06:00, or Julie takes the whole 22:00–06:00?
  2. The gardener's days. Tuesday and Friday, or Tuesday and Saturday?
  3. The bins. Tuesday evening, or Sunday night with the house walk-round?
  4. Homework. Donnie Mon/Wed and Gianna Tue/Thu, or every school day rotating Ricardo → Julie → Lily?
  5. Lily's cooking. Tuesday and Thursday, or Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday?

Assumptions still standing

  • Lily's 12.8 hours a week — the number most likely to be wrong.
  • The pool has no service contract — if it does, three tasks and about 1.5 hours disappear.
  • Bed linen — nobody has said whether it is the domestic's or Lily's.
  • The 07:30 drop-off — not on the children's switch, so it stays even in a week without them.
  • No away week has ever been run. The surge configuration exists on paper (Julie 38.7 hours) but has never been used.
  • Green waste — leaves with the household refuse, or its own day?
  • The four deep jobs — the rotation order is proposed, not agreed.
