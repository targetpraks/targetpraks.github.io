01 · The Average Brand

SDR and Deal Maker cost per brand — pipeline vs SLA

Two kinds of brand, and the difference between them is the whole story. A pipeline brand is hunted: cold calls, emails, LinkedIn, discovery. An SLA brand is sold to franchisees already inside the network — nobody to cold-call, so it is account management, not hunting.

Average hours per brand, per month. The SDR side of a pipeline brand is 53.2 h; the Deal Maker side averages 60.7 h. An SLA brand costs the SDR only 18.5 h — a little over a third of a pipeline brand — but still costs the Deal Maker 42.7 h.

Why the SLA split matters

On the SDR side an SLA costs 18.5 h against 53.2 h — 2.9× cheaper, because there is no hunting. On the Deal Maker side the gap narrows to 42.7 h against 60.7 h — only 1.4× cheaper, because the meetings, proposals and follow-ups still all happen.

That asymmetry is exactly why the SLA brands belong on the SDR side as account management, not on the Deal Maker's book.

02 · What If 400 Leads Come In

INFX Solutions, 400 leads in one month

Priced straight off the tracker's own standards: 5 minutes a call, 4 minutes a templated email, 3 minutes a LinkedIn touch, 30 minutes a booked meeting and 30 minutes of discovery, with Apollo at 10 minutes an account. The sheet's own funnel converts 160 calls into 20 qualified meetings — 12.5%.

400 leads in, 400 outbound touches, and at the sheet's own conversion rate, 50 qualified meetings — which is 50 bookings plus 50 discovery calls to sit through.

The verdict — 400 leads cannot be worked in one month

196.7 hours of work against 168 hours in a month. One person cannot do it, and it is more than the entire team's spare SDR capacity of 44.5 h by a factor of 4.4.

At 19.5 minutes a lead even without the research, 400 leads fill 77% of a whole SDR month on their own — which would mean doing nothing else for INFX. Nothing else gets sold.

Four ways to make 400 leads work

#OptionSDR hours% of a monthVerdict
1 Pre-qualify before they reach the SDR. Marketing or Apollo filters 400 down to 150 real accounts of the right profile. 48.829% Fits in normal capacity
2 Pre-qualify harder — to 100 accounts. Same filter, tighter profile criteria. 32.519% Comfortable
3 Spread the 400 over three months. A steady drip instead of a wall. 43.326% Fits in team spare capacity
4 Work all 400 in one month without a filter. 196.7117% Impossible — needs a second SDR
The cheapest answer

Pre-qualification is the whole game. Cutting 400 raw leads to 150 real accounts before they reach the SDR turns 196.7 hours into 48.8 — and it costs nothing but a filter. An unfiltered list is not a workload problem, it is a targeting problem being paid for in SDR hours.

At 150 leads the work lands at 48.8 h, which sits inside the 44.5 h of team spare capacity once shared across the two SDRs — and INFX still gets 19 qualified meetings out of it.

Another 80 hours lands on the Deal Maker closing INFX — on top of the 61.7 h the book already carries. That is 141.7 h against 168 = 84% of a month from this campaign alone, for one book. Filtering to 150 leads cuts the DM side too: roughly 30 h, taking INFX to 92 h = 55%.

The rule this establishes

A lead list is not free because the leads were free. Every unfiltered lead costs the SDR 12 minutes minimum and about 29.5 minutes all-in — and a 12.5% conversion means the Deal Maker absorbs the other half of the cost. Filter before the list is built, not after the hours are spent.
