4. The findings

Twelve, hardest first. Every one carries its receipt in the evidence files beside this record.

1

Two seats are master keys to every brand's library CRITICAL

AgenticOS Bot and Architect Bot each name all 23 knowledge bases and search any of them. Both answered a Papa Pasta register question with exact figures — eleven drawers, 252 files, 101 folders — drawn from a base neither is bound to. Their published versions bind only the company base, so this is their knowledge tool reaching the whole company, not a product-wide leak: the same tool on a unit seat stays inside its own base, as FIN and SAL proved by refusing.

What closes it: Either they become private seats inside a platform channel of your own, or you accept that anyone with an account can ask them for any unit's finance, HR or legal material. The test: ask either one for the Papa Pasta register and require a plain refusal.

2

The fourteen audits fire Monday with nothing to audit CRITICAL

Each unit library holds one document — the 2026 role reference — and the SOP library holds none. The first run of the watchdogs can only report that no procedures were found, fourteen times, and each report will be delivered nowhere.

What closes it: Load one real procedure per unit, or pause the routines. The exit condition is a unit library that holds at least one procedure document, checked before the schedule fires.

3

Nobody is told a report exists HIGH

All fourteen routines have delivery mode, notification channels and destination conversation empty, and usage notices are off. Work happens on a schedule nobody hears.

What closes it: Name the route — a chat message, mail, or the platform inbox — and set it on all fourteen. Exit condition: one delivered report in the named place, from a real run.

4

The libraries are company-wide even where the seats are private HIGH

All 23 bases and all 17 reference files are visible to the whole company. The assistants and projects are properly locked — private plus one grant — but the material behind them is not, so a unit's grant is documentation rather than a lock.

What closes it: Decide whether a brand may read another brand's library. If not, the bases and reference files take the same private-plus-grant shape as the assistants. Exit condition: a second account cannot see a base it has no grant for.

5

No guardrails anywhere HIGH

The product can redact secrets and personal data and can stop a run. It is switched on nowhere, in an estate that will soon hold identity numbers, salaries and bank detail.

What closes it: Turn it on for the fourteen unit seats, the fourteen watchers and the two platform seats, then prove one redaction per seat. Exit condition: a seeded identity number comes back masked.

The standards and coverage seat

Took the product's 37 options and the configuration surfaces one by one, and asked which are truly set, which are dormant, and which are set badly.
Ruling

Not covered — the roster, the private seats and projects, the five desks, one library per unit, the models, the embeddings and the sandbox all exist, but essential boundaries, content, delivery and controls are absent or wrong. Switching on all 37 options is not the objective.

Findings, hardest first

The first three changes
  • Done when every unit, brand and forum library and reference file is private with only its intended grant, deliberately shared resources stay shared, and cross-channel read tests fail.
  • Done when approved read-only access to the company drive has indexed real procedures, policies and brand manuals per unit, each forum's default seat reaches its own library, and live answers cite those sources.

What this seat refuses

Live writes into company systems through the platform, or proactive workflows, until the exact connections and permissions are approved and the segregation and guardrail gates pass. A built-in “writes” approval setting is not a business mandate.

The dissent — the seat that argued against the council

Took the comfortable story apart on purpose: what the tests never covered, and what the audit cannot see at all.
Ruling

Do not open staff access on this snapshot: it was an owner-only estate with two demonstrated cross-unit disclosures and twenty-eight assistants never tested across a boundary.

Where the evidence did not support the comfortable story
  • “Sealed” was a smaller claim than it sounded. Seven assistants were ever asked a cross-boundary question; five held and two disclosed. The other twenty-eight — including all fourteen watchdogs — were never tested. “Thirty-five answered” proves availability, not isolation.
  • “Two master keys” was too kind. Seven assistants were organisation-wide: the two platform seats and the five desks. The desks returned nothing even for material of their own, which is an empty search, not a lock — an ambiguity the council should not have read as safety.
  • Guardrails were not the whole safety gap. The organisation also let a chat waive its own approval, while the organisation-wide general seat held twenty-eight functions and the whole hundred-dollar ceiling. With no live company data connected, the write exposure was nil — and that did nothing about the read exposure that was actually demonstrated.
What this audit cannot see at all

One member, and he is the owner. No test as an ordinary member, no group-membership enforcement, no staff member opening a library or a register, no malicious prompt or hostile document, no attempt to talk a seat past an approval. A dishonest result would count a refusal, or an empty search, as proof of a lock. An unlucky one would ask a single foreign question that retrieval happens to miss and call it sealed — which is exactly what the two desks showed.

The failure that would actually hurt

On the first staff day: a salesperson asks the sales seat for finance material, is refused, and then asks one of the organisation-wide seats — or simply opens the library — and gets it. The proven pattern was Papa Pasta's 11 drawers, 252 files and 101 folders returned by a seat that was bound to a different library. With HR, finance and the three forum libraries open to every member, that is a confidentiality breach and a fast loss of staff trust.

The one thing not to do this week

Do not open staff access yet. Open it only when a second, non-owner account is denied — both directly and through an assistant — outside its own channel, and the organisation-wide seats have each been narrowed or knowingly accepted.

After this seat sat, the chair changed the platform. The two platform seats are now private to the owner; seventeen libraries and fifteen reference files are private to their own channel; guardrails are on all thirty-five seats; and a chat can no longer waive its own approval. Two of this seat's three arguments therefore no longer describe the estate. The third does, and it is the one that matters: twenty-eight assistants were never given a cross-boundary test, and there is still one member, so nothing here has been proved against a second person. The recommendation stands unchanged — no staff access until a non-owner account is denied outside its own channel.

What actually happens the week staff get accounts.
Ruling

Nothing breaks. That is the problem: a silent system teaches people to ignore it.

Monday morning, when someone logs in

Assume two staff accounts appear this week and a store manager opens the Papa Pasta channel. What happens, in order:

The ruling

Both questions get a qualified no, and the qualification is the useful part. It is not completely built: no guardrails on any of 35 seats, no route from a scheduled report to a human, no assistant bound to the four company registers, no way for any seat to read live company data.

The method seat — what an audit can and cannot prove

Structure is fully evidenced; behaviour is proved in seven seats of thirty-five.
Ruling

The structural record is sound. The behavioural record is a sample, and it must be repeated before anyone writes policy on it.

What this audit actually proves

Every figure here was read from the running platform, not from the deployment notes: 35 assistants with their published versions, 22 projects, 23 libraries with their document counts and visibility, 17 reference files, 65 skills, 4 tables with 58 rows, 14 routines with their schedules and delivery fields, 4 model profiles. Structure is fully evidenced. Behaviour is evidenced only where a seat was asked, and that is seven seats of thirty-five.

Three things I want on the record as limits
  • One member. A boundary that has never met a second person is a design, not a proof. Nothing in this record can tell you what a store manager sees on their first day.
  • Empty libraries cannot be tested. Five of 23 libraries hold nothing. A seat bound to an empty library looks sealed because there is nothing to find — which is exactly why I will not call the five desk seats safe.
  • Cost is unreadable here. Every run reports zero because the provider catalogue has no price rows. Spend has to be watched at the model provider, not in this platform.

What would make me doubt the whole record: a second test run that flips a result — a sealed seat that answers, or a leaking seat that refuses. Behavioural tests on a probabilistic system need repeating before anyone signs a policy on them.
