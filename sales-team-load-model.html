1Corrections applied in this version

Five changes from v3

1. INF Consultancy now carries travel — 50% off-site, 5.0 h a month. It was wrongly costed at zero.

2. FluxFlow is removed. The team no longer sells it. Five pipeline books remain, not six.

3. Apollo AI is loaded. The team uses Apollo to build lead lists, so account research drops from 20 to 10 minutes per account — a 2.0 h saving per book.

4. SLA outbound is pooled, not repeated. The SDR runs one campaign across all SLA brands at once, not four separate ones. This is the single biggest saving on the sheet.

5. Travel is per book — not a flat 30 hours. FND meetings are held at our office; TLF product goes to site.

Net effect: the Deal Maker line falls from 216% to 141%, and after pooling the SLA work the whole picture closes with no new hire.

9Are the targets realistic?

Per brand — yes, comfortably

One SDR book = 53.2 h of 168 = 32% of a month.
One Deal Maker book = 56.7 h of 168 = 34%, or 40% with travel.

The per-brand targets are sound and always have been.

Per portfolio — was no, now fixed

Nine rows on one SDR and eight on one Deal Maker was the whole problem — not the size of any single brand's target.

The balanced plan above puts every book inside a month without adding a person.

Rule to adopt: cap at 2–3 pipeline brands per person. SLA brands carried separately as account management, outbound pooled.

12What to fix first

  1. Pool the SLA outbound. One campaign for four brands — saves 62 hours a month, costs nothing.
  2. Centralise the hunting. Run calls, LinkedIn and research once, not per book — saves 69 hours.
  3. Move the SLA brands to account management on the SDR side — saves 97 hours of Deal Maker time.
  4. Rebalance the books as laid out at §8. Everyone lands inside a month.
  5. Attach the proof. 28 unverified SDR rows is the cheapest fix on this list.
  6. Start signing off. Not one row was signed off last month.
  7. Cap at 2–3 pipeline brands per person. The standing rule that stops this recurring.
The one thing to take away

The team is capable, the targets are achievable per brand, and the load was simply spread wrong. Pooling the SLA outbound, centralising the hunt and treating the SLAs as account management brings every person inside a month — with no new hire. The only thing the plan asks for is a decision about which books sit on which desk.
