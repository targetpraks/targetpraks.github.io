The answer in one line. Thirty-eight territories now hold 3,182 hexagons covering 2,496.3 km² — about 0.2% of South Africa, because a territory is a set of store catchments, not a piece of the map. The fee ladder restates at R12,595,000 against R12,900,000 today, and the 25% per-store rule carries R89,023,750 of network equity.

The gate that still stands. The Franchise Disclosure Document is stamped [TEMPLATE — NOT YET COUNSEL-APPROVED]. No territory listed here may be offered, no fee collected and no cooling-off clock started until the appointed franchise attorney reviews and executes it. This page is a register, not an offer.

How the ground was drawn

  1. Nodes first. Every territory is anchored on its named retail nodes — the malls, main roads and suburbs a customer would recognise. 149 nodes across the 38 territories.
  2. A catchment per node. Every resolution-8 hexagon whose centre falls within 2.5 km of a node belongs to that store's protected draw. That is about 27 hexagons a store.
  3. One hexagon, one owner. Where two nodes claim the same hexagon the nearer node wins. Only 35 of 3,182 hexagons were contested between different territories — the catchments are almost perfectly disjoint, which is the test passing on its own.
  4. The grid records, it does not choose. The map-maker is still the shop, the road and the people. The hexagon net only makes the answer countable and checkable in court.

Checked, not assumed. An independent QA pass re-derived all 3,182 hexagons from the geometry and the register: zero hexagons with two owners, zero missing, every ring closed, every declared area within 0.11% of the computed area. All checks passed (see qa-report.md). A separate validation pass recomputed every rand and every hexagon from the CSVs: 73 flags, 20 of them high severity, and the flag that matters is the one above — the ground is thinner than the store count.

The finding that matters: the drawn ground is thinner than the store count

A store needs about 27 hexagons of protected draw. On that measure 35 of the 38 territories are drawn under weight — the ground allocated cannot hold the stores the fee schedule sells. Sandton, at 14 stores, holds 4.3 hexagons per store; Pretoria East holds 3.8.

This is not a mapping error, it is a pricing question made visible. Either the territory carries a reserve ring beyond the 2.5 km catchment (ground reserved for stores four to fourteen, owned by the same owner but with no protected store on it yet), or the store counts come down to what the ground can hold. The register cannot answer that — it can only prove the two numbers are different.

What is still open

  • Reserve rings — how much ground beyond the 2.5 km catchment a fee buys. Ricardo's call.
  • Three Local territories below the floor — Richards Bay, Thohoyandou and Kimberley carry 3 stores each, under the 6-store threshold the model needs.
  • Fourteen nodes are approximate — suburbs the national gazetteer does not carry (Melrose, Parkhurst, Greenside, Lonehill, Rynfield, Glen Marais, Southgate, Meyersdal, Jabulani, Waterfall Mall, Riverside, Bendor, Fauna Park, Waterfall City). Survey each before any schedule is signed.
  • Two nodes were merged before publication — Cashan sat 315 m from Waterfall Mall and Ferndale 891 m from Randburg, so each pair counted one catchment twice. Merged into the surviving node; the register, the map and the QA pass all carry the merge.
  • Klipspruit could not be placed, so Soweto and Lenasia runs on three nodes not four.
  • The FDD gate — counsel first, sales second.
