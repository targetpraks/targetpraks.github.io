Beach is a tax, not a bonus

Every property within walking distance of sand ranks at or near the bottom of its group: the Olhão fixer by the markets at 2.5%, Cabanas at 2.7%, the Praia da Luz villa at 3.3%. You pay for the view in the purchase price and you cannot charge guests enough to recover it.

New builds are the worst of the three

Not one of the five clears 5%. You pay the developer's margin, then 7.5% transfer tax on the margin, then run a 90 m² flat that earns what an 85 m² flat earns. The €320,000 Praia da Luz unit is the best of them and it is the smallest and cheapest of the set.

The structure questionIndividual or company

A Portuguese company pays transfer tax on the resident scale — 1% to 8% stepped — while a non-resident individual pays a flat 7.5%. On the €495,000 Lagos flat that is €37,125 against €27,149, a real saving. But the company then owes 20% corporation tax on profit, and 28% on the dividend when the money comes out. The individual pays 8.75% of gross rent and 28% on the gain at sale, and nothing else.

Green rows are the best structure at that price. Selling the shares is the company's escape hatch: the buyer takes the company, the property never changes hands, no transfer tax is paid again, and you pay 28% on the share gain instead of 20% corporation tax plus 28% dividend tax.

The answer: under about €300,000, buy as an individual — the company's tax saving is a few thousand euro and the exit cost eats it. Above €300,000 a Portuguese company starts to win, but only if you leave profits in and eventually sell the shares. If you intend to take the rent out every year to spend, the company is the worst of the three at every price.

MethodWhat is measured and what is assumed

Measured: prices, floor areas, bedrooms and locations come from the live adverts linked above, each opened and checked on 25 October 2026. Auction base values, opening bids, minimum accepted bids, current bids and closing dates come from the auction register itself.

Assumed: three auction lots do not state a floor area — Quarteira modelled at 100 m², São Clemente at 120 m², Castro Marim at two 70 m² units; treat those three with caution. Revenues for Quarteira, Loulé, Olhão, Silves, Cabanas, Praia da Luz, Castro Marim and Alcoutim are estimated from the four towns with reported short-term-rental medians, adjusted for size and beach access — call them ±15%. Works costs are contractor estimates, not quotes. Every auction lot is assumed to be a sound shell, which is generous.

Fixed in every model: 12% co-host management plus VAT, 3% booking commission, cleaning, utilities, 4% maintenance, condominium, insurance, IMI at 0.35% of 85% of rateable value, accounting, 75% occupancy ramp in year one, 3% revenue growth from year three, 4% annual capital appreciation, 6% selling cost, and full exit tax in year ten. Tax: 7.5% flat transfer tax and 8.75% effective income tax for a non-resident individual; resident scale, 20% corporation tax and 28% dividend tax for a company.

Not modelled: mortgage finance (these yields do not survive 4.3% non-resident debt), personal use (two months of owner use costs roughly a quarter of the yield), rand–euro movement, and the risk that the Alojamento Local licence is refused or capped inside a containment zone. All of those move the answer, and none of them move it in your favour.
