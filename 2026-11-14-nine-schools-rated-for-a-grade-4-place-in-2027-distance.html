Twelve schools, rated for him — by distance, price and what they actually staff

An exercise, as asked: every school within reach of Welgemoed that could host a boy entering Grade 4 in 2027 who reads and writes below grade level and needs a reader, a scribe and an in-class facilitator. Scored out of 5 on the same six criteria, weighted the way the priorities were given — support first, then distance, then price.

Scope of this exercise, and the one distinction that matters most. He is a bright mainstream-curriculum boy with a specific reading and writing difficulty — not autistic, no intellectual disability, not a special-school learner. That single fact rules out three schools that score well on staffing and one that is five minutes away: special schools are built for a different population, and a place there would be a placement of last resort, not a fit. What he needs is structure and expertise about reading, delivered inside a normal school day.

The answer in one line

Curro Uitzicht wins on the weighted score (4.60 of 5) and the decision gate agrees — 0.83 against Tafelberg in a head-to-head. Nothing else in reach combines a 12-per-class cap, therapists inside the school day, a confirmed 2027 fee of R101 520 and 13 minutes from the front door. Tafelberg is the wildcard: better on price by a mile and 15 minutes away, but the family cannot give itself the place — the district does.

And the council disagrees, with reason. Told that he is a mainstream child whose problem is reading — not autistic, not a special-school learner — seven models put Tafelberg first, 5 votes to 2. The remedial school is the right instrument; the only thing holding it back is that the district gives the place, not the family. Section 08.

And on the readings’ own lens — emotional, academic, physical, spiritual — the order changes again: La Vigne first (4.00), Curro second (3.90), Generation third (3.90). La Vigne is the only school on the list that answers Kim’s “something larger than himself” inside the building; it is also the weakest of the top group on explicit literacy. Section 09 sets the two lenses side by side rather than pretending they agree.

The full ranking, the weighting and the two gate runs are below. Prices are the schools' own published figures for the year shown; where a school publishes none, that is said instead of guessed.

01 What he needs, in the schools' own terms

Reading and writing below grade level in English; support in place at school since August 2024 and continuing outside it; the support professionals at both the current school and Curro Uitzicht have put him at a Grade 3 level for English. What that translates into, school by school, is a package: a smaller class, a learning-support teacher with hours in the timetable, and a reader, scribe and extra time granted for assessments. A school that cannot staff the first two cannot deliver the third properly.

So the scoring below rewards, in order: a model actually built for this (30%), staffed support on site (20%), class size (10%), distance from Welgemoed (15%), published 2027 price (15%), and what he leaves with at 18 (10%).

04 Standing — and there are two currencies

Prestige does not sit on one ladder here, and the answer changes depending on who is doing the judging.

Social standing · the school-gate currency

Reddam House Durbanville wins this outright, and nothing on this list is close. It is an Inspired group school with IEB and Cambridge, an international exchange network and published results — 100% pass at Cambridge AS in 2025, 149 subject distinctions, 29 A-aggregates, a 73% overall aggregate — and the second-highest fee on the list. That is a brand built to be aspirational.

Curro has the widest name recognition of any private network in the country, but it is a volume brand: Curro Uitzicht is its assisted-learning campus, and assisted learning is not sold as prestige. Oakley House and Tafelberg carry almost no social cachet — nobody is impressed at a braai.

Professional standing · the currency that matters here

Oakley House wins this one. Umalusi-accredited, built specifically for reading difficulty, speech and occupational therapy on site during the school day, Grades 1–12, R175,920 a year plus a R32,400 placement fee. Among educational psychologists and remedial teachers in Cape Town it is the reference school — the place they name when a bright child cannot read.

Tafelberg comes second on this ladder for the same reason: a state remedial school with a therapy department and district-gatekept admissions is credible precisely because it cannot be bought into.

What the gate said when asked directly. “Which carries the most standing?” — Oakley House 45.9%, Reddam 23.3%, Tafelberg 13.8%, Curro Uitzicht 8.9%, the small independents 8.1% (confidence 0.12, which is the split between the two currencies showing up in the numbers). Put more bluntly: the most prestigious school on this list is the one that has already failed him, and the most respected school on this list is the one 31 km away.

Where the council and the weights disagree

Oakley House: council 3rd, weights 9th. The widest gap on the board, and it is honest in both directions. The models ranked it third because a specialist reading school with speech and occupational therapy on site is precisely the instrument for this difficulty. My weights dropped it to ninth because it is 28 minutes and R176 000 a year before the R32 400 placement fee. Both are true; the disagreement is about how much a daily hour of driving and a five-figure fee are worth against the best reading remediation in the province. That is a family decision, not a scoring decision.

Tafelberg: council 1st, weights 2nd. The models put the state remedial school top because support and expertise are their first criterion; my weights hold it at second only because the placement is not the family’s to give — the district decides, and intake runs early. If a place exists, this is the best school on the board on every view.

Reddam: named the most overrated school by five of the seven. None of them were moved by Cambridge and IEB while the reading has not shifted in two years. It came 10th of 12 on the council’s average rank, against 11th on my weights — rare agreement on a school the family is currently paying for.

Underrated, according to the council: Tafelberg (3 votes), Boston Private (2) — “closest and cheapest, with a remedial add-on nobody mentions” — and Dunatos once.

Where the two lenses collide — and this is the honest part. Kim’s direction is a traditional school with a brotherhood and daily sport, and eventually boarding. Nothing within half an hour of Welgemoed offers that and teaches a dyslexic boy to read; the schools with the brotherhood and the sport (Reddam) are the ones where he is currently drowning, and the schools that teach reading (Tafelberg, Oakley, Curro’s assisted stream) have no brotherhood at all. The resolution is not to pick one lens. It is to buy the literacy deliberately and build the brotherhood somewhere else — a club, a team, a dojo, a church, a boarding decision at 12 — which is exactly what Kim says when she calls sport his religion rather than his hobby.

La Vigne now tops this lens, and it is worth understanding why. Eight to a class, an explicit Christian code, a school that calls itself a family and behaves like one — that is the only place on the list that answers the “larger than himself” requirement in the building. It is also the weakest on explicit literacy of the top group, and it has no matric history. If the family's real risk is identity rather than decoding, La Vigne is the live option. If the risk is decoding, it is not.

Generation Schools moves up more than anything else. A daily football programme under Real Madrid coaching, self-directed learning and a purpose frame is the closest physical and spiritual match on the list — and it drops on exactly the axis the readings care about least in a classroom: it has no learning-support team at all.
