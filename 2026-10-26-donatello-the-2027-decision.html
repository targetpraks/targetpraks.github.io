What this report does — and does not — decide

It scores the four routes on the things that matter for him, prices each one from published fees, and puts the shortlist to the local decision model. It does not assess Donatello, does not second-guess the school's clinical picture, and does not sign or pay for anything. The placement is yours.

The repeat question, in one paragraph

The retention evidence is consistent: repeating a grade is associated with weaker long-term academic and social outcomes than promoting a similarly achieving child, and the better designed the study, the smaller the early gain (Jimerson 2001; Allen et al. 2009). The practical finding is that the intervention matters more than the placement. A repeat that delivers the same curriculum at the same pace buys twelve months; one that adds a smaller class, explicit literacy instruction and daily writing support can change the trajectory. That is why A scores 2 on the criterion that matters most.

B — conditional on the written package

The weighted matrix puts C first (3.95 vs 3.65) because it is cheaper and structurally better at the reading gap. The gate puts B first because the priority order I took from your email weights his confidence and his peer group above those advantages, and because the pairwise and its mirror agree at high probability. The two are not in conflict: the matrix says what is structurally best, the gate says what is best under your values. The decision is B — but only if the school turns the package into staff and paper.

The condition is not a formality. B's support score is 4, not 5, precisely because the school has named the package without committing it. A written commitment lifts B to 3.80 — still just behind C. B only overtakes C when the package is also working: if the reading score rises to 5 as well, B reaches 4.05 and leads the matrix. That is why the review gate below measures reading, not attendance. If the school will not commit, C is the answer at 0.98.

What would change the answer

Three levers, in order of size: (1) make the Reddam package real and effective — B moves from 3.65 to 4.05 (a written commitment alone takes it to 3.80); (2) weight peer continuity at 20% and cost at 0 — B moves to 4.05 and C falls to 3.75, so B leads; (3) weight cost at 20% and peer continuity at 0 — C widens to 4.15 against B's 3.25. The first is the one you can actually influence on 5 November.

What I could not verify

desk research, not a visit
  • Whether Reddam will commit the reader, scribe and facilitator in writing. The 5 November meeting is the only way to know; the school's email names the package but does not promise it.
  • Reddam's 2027 Grade 4 fee, the 2027 class size, and whether the new Learning Support Teacher will cover Senior Prep or only the Junior Prep phase.
  • Whether Curro Uitzicht, Dunatos or The Resilience Academy has a place for him in January 2027. Availability moves weekly in Term 4 and none of them publishes it.
  • The quality of the facilitator market at the senior end: the agency rates are published, but no quote has been obtained for Donatello's specific needs.
  • Dunatos's fees, class size and grades; The Resilience Academy's campus address and fees; St Andrew's Prep's learning-support capacity.
  • Peak-hour travel times. All distances are free-flow estimates, not a 07:30 school run.
  • Any independent assessment of Donatello. This report takes the school's and the evaluation's functional description as given; it does not test him.

This is a decision aid, not a placement. The fees are published figures with their years; the scores are judgements and are editable; the gate's probabilities are a local model's read on the shortlist, not a measurement of your son.

The review gate

what would send him to the specialist route

If you choose B, the decision is not finished on 5 November. It is a one-term trial with a written exit. Put these three checkpoints in the acceptance letter, and review them at the end of Term 1 2027 — not at the end of the year, when the window for a Grade 5 place has closed.

CheckpointQuestionTrigger for moving to C
ReadingHas his reading age moved against the September 2026 baseline, measured by the reading programme, not by class marks?No measurable movement by the end of Term 1.
Written outputCan he produce a paragraph with the reader and scribe support in the time the class is given, or is the facilitator carrying him?He is still dependent on the adult for the thinking, not just the handwriting.
Teacher judgementIs he accessing the Grade 4 curriculum, or is he being managed through it?The teacher would not recommend Grade 5 on the current trajectory.

Two out of three triggers means apply to C for Grade 5 in the 2028 admissions window, which opens in 2027 — not a mid-year move. If you choose C, the mirror review is at the end of Grade 4: is the gap closing enough to return to mainstream for Grade 6? Curro Uitzicht runs to Grade 7, so there is time to answer that properly.

One thing not to do

Do not let the repeat decision and the support decision drift apart. A repeat at Reddam without the package is the weakest route on the matrix (2.50); a repeat at a specialist school is a different proposition, because the year and the teaching change together. If the school's answer on 5 November is 'repeat, and we'll support him', ask what that support is, who delivers it, and how many hours a week — then score it again.
