Why the SLA pooling matters most

An SLA is sold to franchisees who are already customers. So the SDR runs one outbound campaign that serves all four SLA brands simultaneously — not four. That single change frees 62 hours a month, and it costs nothing to adopt.

What is working

Marketing is fully captured, fully verified and far above target. That side of the business is being run properly and the sheet proves it.

What is not

Not one row is signed off anywhere — 0 / 9, 0 / 7 on every line. Month after month the sign-off step is skipped, so no month can be formally closed.

  • SDR captures but does not evidence. Twenty-eight rows sit Unverified — the work is very likely happening, the proof link simply is not attached. That is a discipline fix, not a capacity fix.
  • Deal Maker at 0%. Proposal-to-close is captured at zero and most rows carry no verified number, so the score collapses.
  • The trap to watch. A high verified score sitting next to a high unverified count means a person is only evidencing the KPIs they happen to be hitting. That pattern is worth a conversation.
  • Company trend. The verified score has fallen from 412% in June to 65% in October — while captured rows have dropped from 240 to 73. Less is being entered, and less of it is provable.

The uncomfortable truth

If the pipelines, templates and push automation are not loaded, the Deal Maker cannot hit the number — and that is a management accountability, not a salesperson one. The manager owns the tooling. Blaming the closer for a missing automation is blaming the driver for a missing road.

09 · Daily Discipline

The rule that holds it all together
  • Same day — every call, email, meeting and LinkedIn touch logged against the right CRM record.
  • Same day — every stage change made when it happens. A deal that moved must show it moved.
  • Every week — numbers entered on the Monthly Capture tab, with a proof link beside every number.
No proof, no score

A number without a dated evidence link is marked Unverified and excluded from the score entirely. It does not score zero — it does not count. That single rule is what makes the whole scorecard worth reading.

10 · What To Do First

Seven actions, in order
  1. Pool the SLA outbound. One campaign for four brands. Saves 62 hours a month, costs nothing.
  2. Centralise the hunting. Run calls, LinkedIn and research once, not per book. Saves 69 hours.
  3. Move the SLA brands to account management. Off the Deal Maker, onto the SDR side. Saves 97 hours of closing time.
  4. Rebalance the books as laid out above. Every person lands inside a month.
  5. Attach the proof. Twenty-eight unverified SDR rows is the cheapest fix on this list.
  6. Start signing off. Not one row was signed off last month.
  7. Adopt the cap. Two to three pipeline brands per person, SLA accounts carried separately. The rule that stops this recurring.
One decision, two-thirds of it free.

Pool the repetitive work, centralise the hunt, treat the SLAs as the accounts they are — and move two books off two overloaded desks. That brings every person inside a working month without adding a single head. All that is left is deciding which book sits where.
