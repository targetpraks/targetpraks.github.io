Papa Pasta — Franchise Introduction Film

PRD, brand pack and the standing brief for three independent builds.

What this is. The brief and the brand pack for a short film that introduces Papa Pasta to a prospective franchisee, and the standing instruction for the three agents building it. It is not the creative. It is the spine, the facts, the colours, the claims law and the tests — the parts that must not vary across builds.

What it is not. Not a finished script, not a storyboard, not a deck. Every agent writes its own film inside these walls: same facts, same brand, same acceptance tests, three different hands.

1. The job

Audience. A South African operator with capital, local presence and a site in mind — not a chef, not a restaurant group. Someone who already runs something and is looking at a food format that will not eat their life. They have been pitched franchises before. They are bored of the brochure.

The shift the film must make. From “another food franchise” to “this one has a mechanic I understand, a box I can afford, and a brand my street would recognise.”

What it must not do. Sell returns. A film that promises money to a franchisee in South Africa is a consumer-protection problem before it is a marketing one (see §4).

Where it plays. The franchise page of the website, a WhatsApp attachment, an email link, and a loop on a screen at a pitch. Therefore: sound off must still work — every line carried by burned-in captions.

2.5 What is not established — say it plainly

  • No store has traded. There is no audited unit P&L, no proven payback, no measured conversion.
  • The R600k/month mature-turnover basis and the 22% EBITDA line are modelling assumptions, and they are contested internally: the investor review runs a R287k Express volume against the same format.
  • The zone/master layer (regional owner between franchisor and store) is a proposal, not a signed term.
  • Investment band is not locked. Finance has not signed a figure.

A film that hides this reads like every other franchise brochure. A film that handles it — “we model it with you, in writing” — reads like an operator who has done this before. That is the tone.

3. The brand pack

Source of truth: Papa Pasta Brand Manual.pdf (30 pages, Canva, dated 2026-07-29), at /Volumes/Hiksemi 1TB/AI-Drive/Work/Papa Pasta/Brand Assets/. Where the manual and an older brand note disagree, the manual wins.

3.1 The crest — fixed elements

The identity is a fixed crest. Five elements may never be altered, redrawn, re-lettered or dropped:

  1. The shield/crest shape
  2. The PAPA PASTA wordmark
  3. EST 2025
  4. Crossed forks + pasta knot
  5. The tagline FRESH • SEASONAL • QUALITY

Clear space: keep a margin equal to the width of the fork tines on all sides. Never place the crest on a busy photograph without a scrim. Never stretch, never outline, never recolour the wordmark itself.

The crest ships in two lockups: pop-black (white linework, neon doodles, for dark ground) and pop-white (dark linework, for light ground). Use the one that belongs to the ground you are on.

3.2 Colour

Role Name Hex Use

Accent 2 Lime #C9F034 Signal, numbers, “the answer”

The law of the manual: “Use neon as spice, not the meal.” The page is monochrome; one accent family per beat; never all three at once at full strength. The manual’s own avoids: over-neon, illegibility, Italian clichés, generic minimalism, corporate coldness.

The franchise colourway (this is the film’s money shot). In the franchise system the crest carries three independently colourable regions:

Region Default example
Background (field) #C9F034
Outline (linework) #FF3255
Inner fill #A766E6

A territory chooses its own three-part colourway. This is the one visible thing a franchisee gets that a McDonald’s operator never will — and it is a brand-system claim, not a financial one, so it is safe to say.

Seasonal palettes also exist and may be used for the menu beats: Summer Blaze · Autumn Harvest · Winter Frost · Spring Bloom. Website colourway names: Midnight Gold · Sage Cream · Rosso Gilt · Harbour Copper.

Do not substitute a rounded or “friendly” face. The brand is a coach, not a barista. Fonts ship in fonts/ beside this brief; both are open-licence and safe to embed.

3.4 Motion law

  • May move: crest colour regions (a territory filling in), neon doodle strokes drawing on, type rising on a hard cut, numbers counting.
  • May not move: the wordmark (no wobble, no warp, no letter-by-letter reveal that deforms it), the crest outline shape, the tagline.
  • Cuts are hard. Fades are short (0.3–0.5 s). No slow cross-dissolves over faces or food.
  • One accent gesture per beat. The film should feel like a scoreboard, not a lava lamp.

3.5 Voice — the Tactical Performance Coach

Direct, kinetic, unsentimental, a little cocky, never cruel. Short clauses. Numerals over words. It speaks to an operator as a peer who has done the arithmetic.

Say: fresh · seasonal · quality · the layered individual · made in store · the system · territory · colourway · the box.

Banned — the manual is absolute on these: Nonna · authentic · homemade · rustic · traditional · family · “Mamma’s” · checkered tablecloths · fake Italian accents · foodie · artisanal · hand-crafted · street food.

This is a testable rule, not a style note: the finished script is grepped for the banned list (§6, A6).

3.6 Photography and art direction

  • Real food, hard light, dark ground, steam. Appetite first.
  • Store and food renders are acceptable only with the concept-phase disclosure on screen (§4).
  • No stock-photo handshakes, no smiling models with forks, no red-checkered anything, no map of Italy.
  • Screenshot-style UI, glass counters, neon signage and the extruder are all on-brand — the machine is a hero prop, not a kitchen appliance.

4. The claims gate — money and legal

Standing rule in this house: Ricardo signs money and legal, nobody else. A franchise film is the single most litigated piece of marketing a food business owns. So the film is built in three tiers.

Tier A — green, publishable now

Brand, product, format, footprint, system components, the colourway mechanic, territory counts, the concept-phase disclosure. Nothing in §2.1–2.4 marked DATA/DESIGN/POLICY that is not money.

Tier B — needs Ricardo’s written sign-off, per exact string

Any figure or band a franchisee could read as a promise:

String Why it is locked
Investment / all-in cost band Finance has not signed a band
R600k/month mature turnover A modelling assumption; contested against R287k Express volume
22% EBITDA, any margin Modelled, never traded
Payback period, any month count Not evidenced; the internal review calls the 4–7 month claim impossible
Royalty %, marketing levy %, tech fee Contractual but unannounced; must come from the signed agreement, in writing
Store count targets (e.g. 273) A plan, not a commitment

A build may include Tier B only as an on-screen placeholder reading [PER TERRITORY · IN WRITING], never as a number. The moment a number appears, the build is out of spec.

Tier C — never

Projected earnings for a named territory · “guaranteed” · “risk-free” · “passive income” · any comparison to a named competitor’s unit economics · any testimonial from a store that does not exist.

Mandatory disclosure

On any frame showing a store or food render, and again on the end card, in ≥14 px equivalent legible type:

Papa Pasta is in concept phase. Store visuals are renders, not photographs.

End card additionally:

Nothing in this film is an offer, or a promise of earnings.

5. The spine — nine beats

Each agent may cut, merge or re-order within this spine. The beats are the story; the words are theirs. Suggested duration in brackets.

# Beat Must say Must show
1 Cold open (10–14 s) It did not start in a kitchen. It started at 2 a.m. Crest on dark; a time stamp; nothing else
2 The customer (14–18 s) The Gastronomy Gamer — striker, mage, connoisseur, same night. An audience nobody serves properly Three facets of one person, not three people
3 The product (14–18 s) Fresh pasta, made in store, 4 shapes, 9 signatures, seasonal across a 3-year calendar, dough at 06:00 Real pasta; numbers as numerals
4 Pasta Theatre (14–18 s) The machine is the shopfront. Order, watch, collect. 3–4 min. ~120 dishes/hour at peak The extruder, glass, queue
5 The Crest (18–24 s) Every franchise enforces one colour. We invert it: three regions, your colourway, your street The three colourable regions filling in
6 The box (14–18 s) ~40 m² target, compact roster, tight nine-dish core, local site hunt Footprint against a known reference
7 The system (14–20 s) Central kitchen, site spec, plans, equipment, training, opening, marketing. The system is the product One supply chain, drawn simply
8 The money, in the open (14–18 s) These are the six cost lines. No figures here; we model it with you, in writing, before you commit Six named cost lines, no numbers
9 The territory (18–22 s) 48 territories, 9 provinces, 21 metros, one operator each. Pick the colour, claim the city The map logic; crest in a territory colour; disclosure

What a franchisee should be able to repeat back after one viewing: fresh pasta made in store · a small box · a central kitchen behind it · my own crest colour · 48 territories · they will model my numbers with me.

7. Three builds, three lanes

Ricardo wants three films to compare, so the lanes are deliberately pulled apart. Copying each other’s treatment is the failure mode.

Codex — the system film. Data-led, kinetic type, near-motion-graphics. The franchise as a machine: inputs, throughput, supply chain, territory grid. Type is the star; photography supports. Cold, exact, confident. Think an operations deck that learned to cut on the beat.

Claude — the character film. Warmer and more documentary. The people inside the format: the 2 a.m. customer, the operator behind the counter, the kitchen at 06:00. Handheld energy, real grain, voice-led. The system is shown by following one day in one store rather than diagramming it.

Batman — the mechanism film. Editorial neon. The Crest is the protagonist: one identical brand on every high street, then the inversion — every territory its own colourway. Hard cuts, big numerals, cinematic stills, restraint on the neon. The film argues one idea: the brand is the product, and the brand is local.

All three: same facts, same nine beats, same tests.

8. How Ricardo judges them

Weight Criterion Question
30% Would a prospect watch to the end? Does it hold attention without a presenter?
25% Does it explain the model? Could he repeat the box, the kitchen, the territory back?
20% Brand authenticity Would this pass as Papa Pasta’s own work, not a generic franchise video?
15% Legal safety Zero Tier B/C claims, disclosure present
10% Reusability Can it be re-cut for the website, a pitch loop and social without a rebuild?
