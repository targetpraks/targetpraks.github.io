The verdict, before the arithmetic

Buy one tool, and buy it for the managers only. Not for everyone.

Recommended

Asana — Advanced, 25 seats

R123,415 a year (R10,285/month). It is the only one of the five where a manager's view of a project is the product rather than a thing you configure: portfolio roll-up, project status, goals, workload and approvals are native at this tier.

Value alternative

ClickUp — Business, 25 seats

R59,263 a year (R4,939/month) — less than half of Asana, and you already know it. It can do everything Asana does, but you have to build the clarity yourself. That build is the reason ClickUp works brilliantly for one owner and turns to soup for a group.

Three things that decide this

  • Your Zoho One seat already includes Projects. Moving project management out of Zoho saves nothing — the subscription does not shrink. Every number on page 2 is new money on top of R621,600 a year you already pay.
  • Seats, not tools, are the cost. Price everybody and you pay three to four times over. Twenty-five paid manager seats with staff as free guests buys the same visibility.
  • Wrike is the third option, at Asana's price. R123,464 a year, and Wrike insists on a guided deployment above 20 users — which is a consult you pay for. Right answer if client and project profitability becomes the requirement; overkill today.
The trap to avoid

Do not move everybody in one go

Moving 100 people means 100 seats, 100 logins and a training programme across five ventures. Moving 25 project owners and letting the rest view as guests costs a fifth as much and produces the visibility you are actually asking for. If it works, expand next quarter — the tool will still be there.

The Zoho side of the ledger

Zoho One runs at roughly R518 per user per month on the all-employee model — call it R51,800 a month across the group, R621,600 a year. It is billed per employee, not per app, so removing Projects from your working life changes that invoice by nothing. Books, People, Cliq, WorkDrive and Desk are in that same seat and they stay.

Read the costing as: "what does a real project tool add on top", not "what do we save by leaving Zoho". The saving is zero. The gain is the thing you asked for.

The honest read

  • Zoho Projects is not bad at tracking work — it is bad at showing a manager a project. Detail lives in tabs, and the one view built for you (the portfolio dashboard) sits behind the Enterprise tier of Projects, not the working tier most people meet.
  • Asana wins on the exact sentence you used. A project has an owner, a status, milestones and an overview before anybody configures anything. For a group running store openings, campaigns and staff work across five ventures, that default shape is worth the premium.
  • ClickUp wins on value and loses on discipline. Everything is possible, which means the structure is only as good as whoever builds it. It is the right buy if you want one person to own a tight standard and you do not want to pay double.
  • Wrike is built for agencies billing time against projects. Its real power (budgeting, billable rates, resource planning) is one tier above what you would buy today.

What it costs to move — the number nobody quotes

The licence is the small number. The expensive number is the fortnight where the team lives in two systems and the person who owns the migration is also doing their day job.

StepEffortWhoWhy it cannot be skipped
Decide the shape: what a "project" is in this group2–3 daysYou + me + one GMWithout this you migrate the same mess into a prettier tool
Build 3–4 templates — store opening, campaign, briefed-in work, reporting3–4 daysOps ownerTemplates are the actual product; they decide what a manager sees
Migrate live projects and briefs2–4 daysOps ownerOnly what is live; history stays in Zoho as the archive
Write the rules — who creates, who closes, what a status means1 dayYouThe single thing that stops a reversion in three months
Train the 25 managers2 sessionsMeOne session for the tool, one for the standard
Parallel run — both systems live2 weeksEveryoneCheap insurance; nobody trusts a cutover they cannot check

Total: roughly 10–14 person-days, then two weeks of parallel running

That is the real switching cost, and it is the same for Asana, ClickUp or Wrike. It does not scale with the licence price — which is exactly why the licence price should not be the deciding number. It also means a pilot costs almost nothing: build the templates, run ten managers on a trial, and judge it on the Friday of week two.

Failure mode to name now

No owner, no tool

Every failed project-tool migration I have seen failed the same way: it was nobody's job. Before the first licence is bought, one named person owns the standard, the templates and the tidy-up. If that name is not decided, the rand cost is irrelevant.

What stays, what goes, what is left to decide

Zoho keeps its job

  • Books and Invoice — the money. Untouched.
  • People and HRM — attendance, leave, payroll feed. Untouched.
  • Cliq — chat. Stays, with project notifications pushed into it from whichever tool you pick.
  • WorkDrive — files and the archive. Decide one home for project files; running two file stores is how documents go missing.
  • Desk — service tickets. Untouched.
  • Projects — the only module on the table. It can stay as the read-only archive of everything that already happened.

Two integrations to verify before signing anything

  1. Cliq notifications. Both Asana and ClickUp post to chat through their own connectors; the bridge into Cliq needs Zoho Flow or an incoming webhook. Confirm it works in the trial, not after.
  2. Files. If project attachments move into the new tool, WorkDrive stops being the single source. Pick one — tool for live project files, WorkDrive for the archive — and write it down.

Three decisions, in this order

  1. Seats: 25 manager seats, or 50 for all office staff? (25 is my recommendation; 50 doubles every number on page 2.)
  2. Tool: Asana Advanced at R123,415/yr for structural clarity, or ClickUp Business at R59,263/yr for a lower bill on a tool you already know. Both trials are free — a ten-manager pilot in the same fortnight settles it on feel, not on a spreadsheet.
  3. Owner: the one person accountable for the standard and the templates. Name them before the licence is bought.

My recommendation stands: Asana Advanced, 25 seats, R123,415 a year, piloted with ten managers first. If the pilot feels like administration rather than clarity, take ClickUp Business at R59,263 and pocket the difference — that is a real, defensible fallback, not a consolation prize.
