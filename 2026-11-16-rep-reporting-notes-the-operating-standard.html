These are the company's own headings and their mandatory line format. They exist in ClickUp as five text fields on the standup and weekly-report rows, so each point is filterable and exportable rather than trapped in one long note.

The rules that travel with them

  • Written before the Monday meeting, not after it.
  • Nobody edits another person's section.
  • No screenshots — a link instead.
  • After the meeting every commitment is scored delivered / partly delivered / not delivered — that is the Commitment Score field.
  • KPI scorecards belong in the weekly 1:1, not here.

5 · Filtering by quarter

The fiscal quarters are not calendar quarters. The company's document is explicit:

QTR 1 — March, April, May · QTR 2 — June, July, August · QTR 3 — September, October, November · QTR 4 — December, January, February

So a report filed in January belongs to QTR 4 of the previous fiscal year. Two consequences the build enforces: Year is the fiscal year label, not the calendar year of the row; and Period Date plus Quarter are written together so a report can never float between quarters when someone edits a date.

6 · Actioning a meeting point, and the video loop

A point raised in a standup has to become something a named person owns, can see, and can answer. That is four fields and one working list, and it is already carrying a worked example.

  1. Raise — the point is written in the standup row, in whichever of the five agenda fields it belongs (for a blocker that is Challenges & actions taken).
  2. Action — a row is opened in REP Meeting Actions with Action Kind, Action Owner, Action Due, and Source Record naming the standup it came from, so the trail never breaks.
  3. Video — Meeting Recording holds the link. The reviewer writes the verdict in Video Feedback and signs it in Feedback By.
  4. Reply — the owner answers in Owner Reply, which is an ordinary field on the row they own, so it is editable by them and visible to everyone else.
  5. Close — Feedback State walks Awaiting Reply → Replied → Resolved, and the view REP · Awaiting Feedback is the chase list.

7 · The four things the ClickUp API will not do

Stated plainly because two of them touch the review rule and one is cosmetic. Each was attempted, and each has the exact error recorded.

WhatEvidenceWhat has to happen instead
The status spine — Not started · In Progress · Submitted · Reviewed · Concluded · Cancelled POST /space/{id}/status → 404; PUT /space/{id} with a statuses array → 200 and silently ignored Five clicks per status in Space settings → Statuses. Until then REP keeps the inherited Open · in progress · in review · waiting · blocked · completed, and Submitted/Reviewed ride on the Submitted By and Reviewed By fields instead.
Deleting a field — the stray _probe_field, and the old free-text Reporting Period DELETE /space/{id}/field/{id} → 404 Delete both in the UI. Nothing reads them; they are the only two fields no view or report uses.
An INF option on REP Unitdropdown options cannot be edited by API Add INF to the REP Unit options, or leave the two INF roll-up rows unit-less — they are whole-company rows, and their list already says so.
Ten inherited template viewsthe Space arrived from a project template: Overview, Work, This Week, Everything, Calendar, Waiting On, Ticket Age, Board, Due date, Blocked & waiting Ricardo's yes/no. They are harmless but they are noise in a reporting Space, and five of them filter on statuses that the new spine retires.

9 · How this was checked

Three checks ran over the same facts, deliberately blind to each other:

  1. A Codex cross-reference — an independent agent read the three source files and wrote its own build spec before seeing what had been built. Its verdict, divergences and three highest-risk implementation mistakes are folded into §10 where they changed the build.
  2. A four-seat Mixture-of-Agents review — one seat per question: can every department actually run the cadence; is the fiscal-quarter model right; does the action and video loop close; and an adversarial seat comparing this build against the Codex spec item by item. Findings land in §10.
