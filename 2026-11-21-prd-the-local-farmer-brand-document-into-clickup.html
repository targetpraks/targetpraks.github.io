01 The brand

Everything below is quoted or counted from the Company Snapshot and the Brand Persona page. The snapshot holds 13 fields in a single row; one of them (Legal Name) still reads “Company Name”, which is a template placeholder, not the brand’s name.

What it is

The Local Farmer · Urban agriculture / Agri-tech Industry
Head office: 21 Eastwood Office Park, 11B Riley Rd, Bedfordview, Germiston, 2007
Established: 2020-07-15 · Stack: Zoho

Founded in 2020, The Local Farmer brings urban farming directly to Bedfordview homes and kitchens, offering fresh, flavourful microgreens and framed by a vision of greener, self-sustaining futures. The brand empowers individuals by supplying both finished produce and the tools to grow their own micro-greens at home.

Mission and vision

Mission. To bring sustainable urban farming solutions to individuals and communities by making fresh, nutritious microgreens accessible while promoting environmentally-friendly practices.

Vision. To be the leading urban microgreen farming brand in South Africa, transforming how communities access fresh produce through innovative growing methods

The unique selling proposition, in the document’s words

The Local Farmer uses precision hydroponic growing and ultra-local harvesting to deliver exceptionally fresh, nutrient-dense microgreens at peak flavor — eliminating the delays and quality loss of traditional supply chains. By combining sustainability, rare varietals, and chef-focused innovation, TLF transforms microgreens from garnish to core ingredient while reducing waste, water usage, and environmental impact.

Primary target market

The Local Farmer primarily targets restaurants operating in higher-income suburbs within and surrounding Bedfordview . These establishments typically prioritize quality ingredients, consistency, and presentation , and are willing to pay a premium for fresh, locally grown produce that enhances their dishes and brand positioning. Restaurants in these areas often compete not only on price but on food quality, uniqueness of ingredients, and dining experience . As a result, chefs and restaurant owners are actively seeking reliable suppliers who can deliver fresh, high-quality microgreens and specialty produce that elevate their menus. TLF’s focus on hyper-local production, freshness, and consistent supply makes it particularly appealing to restaurants that position themselves as premium dining experiences or modern casual eateries . The initial market focus is concentrated within Bedfordview and surrounding affluent suburbs , including: Bedfordview Linksfield Houghton Parkhurst Parktown North Rosebank Sandton Melrose Greenside Bryanston These areas have a high concentration of restaurants, boutique cafes, and upscale dining establishments , many of which cater to professionals, corporate clientele, and high-income residents.

The four brand answers

These four questions sit on the Brand Personas page and are the brand’s own definition of why it exists — the top of the ladder every campaign should trace back to.

How a piece stays attached to its campaign — proven, not proposed

A Campaign field already exists in this space: one short_text field, id 1c50012c-4f40-4ba4-bd53-a1a25ade6fbb, present on every list in the lane (and on the ZOR/FND and FXE lists too). It was added on 2026-11-21, independent of this PRD, and it is empty.

  1. Each piece carries its campaign in that Campaign field — written, verified and reportable by group.
  2. The explicit join is ClickUp’s native Task Link (POST /api/v2/task/{task_id}/link/{links_to}, the Links panel). A link is a relationship, not a field.
  3. No new field is required. Objective stays the objective, not a campaign pointer.

12 The recurring cadence

Two rhythms, both already present in the document as tables: a monthly reporting cycle with 12 monthly slots, and a periodic audit implied by the brand’s own risk list. Both file to Recurring 1501000000002315.

The document’s Marketing Reporting table has one row per month, January to December 2026, and 10 measures — the table has 11 columns and Month is the slot, not a measure. Each measure is either gathered by its own task or ticked off on the single monthly pack.

Why four tasks and ten measures. The 10 measures are the report’s columns. Four of them need evidence pulled from a different system each month, so those four are tasks with an owner and an evidence link. The other 6 are assembled from them at month end — that assembly is the monthly pack, one task that carries the rest as checklist lines. A measure is never both.

The four statistics tasks, each monthly:

  1. Social media performance — reach, engagement and follower movement per platform across the 8 channels in the access register.
  2. SEO and keyword performance — ranking movement on the tracked set, plus what entered and left the top ten.
  3. Paid media and AdWords — spend against the campaign budget, cost per result, and which campaign it belonged to.
  4. Website traffic — sessions, landing-page conversion, bounce and load time, matching the six page types in Keyword <> Landing Page.

Periodic: the full audit that forces optimisation

This is the part the document does not yet have as tasks — it exists only as a 39-metric asset audit. The audit cycle below makes it operational.

AuditCadenceRoutes toWhat it must produce
Website auditQuarterlyOptimization 1501000000001599Every page type checked for title, meta, speed, broken links and conversion path; each finding becomes an optimisation task.

The negative keyword list does not exist. All 12 keyword types in this document are positive: base, best, top, near-me, location and customer formulas. There is no exclusion set anywhere in the 56 pages. So that audit task does not “update” a list — it creates the first one, and its first output is a decision about which intents to refuse.

And a hard limit. ClickUp’s API cannot set the recurrence itself: POST /list/{id}/task accepts a recurrence object and returns null. Each of these tasks can be created with its cadence in the name, the description and Work Format = Recurring, but one person has to set the repeat schedule in the UI, once per task. That is a two-minute manual step and it cannot be automated.

What “creates nothing” means, precisely

It means no folders, no lists, no fields. It does not mean no records: the plan creates approved tasks, checklist lines and task links, which is the point of it.

  • 0 folders, 0 lists, 0 new fields. All 19 lists, the 12 lane fields and the existing space-wide Campaign field already cover every phase; the options the load needs (Brand = TLF, Engagement Tier = COR) exist. The one field that exists beyond the original 12 is the duplicate drop-down described in §11, which needs a UI delete.
  • No moves planned — and moves are possible. ClickUp’s v2 task update silently ignores a new list id, but v3 exposes a real move: PUT /api/v3/workspaces/{ws}/tasks/{task}/home_list/{list}. A mis-filed item can therefore be moved instead of re-created, which removes the old argument for naming every list id up front. (The house skill still says no move endpoint exists; that note is about v2 and is now corrected.)
  • No deletions. The 25 KPD keyword rows are retired inside the document; they were never in a list.
  • Links, not fields. Campaign→content uses the native Task Link; a Campaign field would be a creation and is your decision.
  • No due dates on create. A space automation rewrites due_date to the following Monday; dates are written in a second pass and undated items left undated.
  • The lane already has occupants: 16 legacy tasks plus the two proof items — Social 8, Projects 5, Blog 1, Email 1, Briefed-In 1. Campaigns and Seasonal Promotions are empty. ClickUp’s list metadata reported 5 for Social; the task read returned 8, so counts come from task reads.
