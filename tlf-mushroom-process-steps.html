One load, in days

A bag starts every day, so the room always holds three weeks of colonising and three weeks of fruiting at the same time.

The magic mix — exact numbers

One bag is 6 kg: 2.7 kg dry, 3.3 kg water

The blend is hardwood pellets and soybean hulls, with millet. Six bags to a cook, because there is one pressure cooker. Mix dry, weigh into the bag, then weigh the water in.

Where 2.7 kg comes from: the farm's hydration sheet gives wet weight ÷ 2.2 = dry weight for pellets, so a 6 kg bag is 6 ÷ 2.2 = 2.7 kg dry. The other 3.3 kg is water. Dry is 1.2 kg pellets + 1.2 kg hulls + 0.3 kg millet — the millet is ◆ one tenth of the dry weight until the room's own ratio is written down here. Weigh the dry part, then weigh the water: never estimate it.

01

The magic mix

Day 0 · one 6 kg bag at a time

  1. 01

    Blend the dry

    1.2 kg hardwood pellets, 1.2 kg soybean hulls, 0.3 kg millet. One bag's dry part, 2.7 kg.

    Watch · Millet feeds the spawn. Too much and the bag sours before it is white.

  2. 02

    Fill to weight

    2.7 kg of dry mix into the bag, no more. Leave 15 cm of film empty at the top.

    Watch · An overfilled bag splits when it takes water.

  3. 03

    Add the water

    Add 3.3 kg of water, then gather the neck, fold it over twice and clip it. Turn the bag over twice so every pocket is wet.

    Watch · Clip, never heat-seal. A hot sealer fuses the film and blocks the filter patch.

2.7 kg dry, 3.3 kg water. That is a 6 kg bag, and the bag is the measure.

02

Cook the bag

Day 1 · six bags, one cooker

  1. 04

    Load six bags

    Six bags and one pressure cooker. Load in the morning and cook through the day — there is no second batch.

    Watch · Six is the cooker's load, not a target. A seventh and the water never comes back to a simmer.

  2. 05

    Cook, 2 h 30

    Bags in, water over them. Start the clock when it comes back to a simmer: 2 hours 30 minutes.

    Watch · Every bag under the water. Hold them down with the rack.

  3. 06

    Leave it overnight

    Then leave it. Do not open the cooker, do not lift the bags. They cool in there overnight and come out the next morning.

    Watch · Eight hours of slow cooling. Opening early fogs the film and invites the wrong things in.

One cooker, six bags, one day: cook in the day, take them out in the morning.

03

Inject

Day 2 · the lab, one bag at a time

  1. 07

    Wipe everything down

    Benches, walls, gloves, hands: 70% alcohol on all of it, then wait 10 minutes. Door shut, no fans, no sweeping.

    Watch · One person in the room. Moving air carries spores.

  2. 08

    Inject the spores

    10 ml of liquid oyster spores per 6 kg bag, through the port, flat against the film, then tape the hole.

    Watch · Needle into the bag, not into the mix.

  3. 09

    Seal the port

    Tape the injection hole. Then gather the neck, fold it over twice and clip it. No heat sealer.

    Watch · Folded and clipped, not fused. The filter patch has to breathe all 21 days.

  4. 10

    Tag the bag

    Date, batch, grower on the tag. Straight to the dark room.

    Watch · An untagged bag is anonymous for 21 days.

The lab is the only step that can lose the whole bag.

No liquid spores that week? Break one fully colonised block into five new bags — crumble it, mix it through the top 5 cm, seal. Same wiped bench, same gloves, one block to five. Never more than five, and never a block with any green on it.
04

Colonise

Day 2–23 · 21 days, dark, 20 °C

  1. 11

    Wrap and stack

    Wrap each bag tight, filter patch up. Stack two high, never three. Bags may touch. 20 °C, dark, still.

    Watch · Three high and the middle bag cooks itself.

  2. 12

    Day 7 mould check

    One week in, look at every bag in daylight. Green, black or pink patches, or a sour smell: out of the room that day, sealed and logged.

    Watch · Mould at day 7 never recovers, and it reaches the bags beside it by day 10.

  3. 13

    Day 21 check

    Ready on day 21: white right through, firm to the press, smells of mushroom.

    Watch · White on day 14 is not ready. Day 21 is the day.

Twenty-one days. Not two weeks, not three weeks — 21 days from the tag.

05

Fruit

Day 23 on · three weeks in the tent

  1. 14

    Cut the X

    One X, 50 mm across, centred on the face that looks into the tent. Blade 10 mm deep, through the film only. Then stop.

    Watch · Leave it shut. An X pulled open dries out and pins nothing.

  2. 15

    Into the tent

    3 shelves × 7 levels × 3 blocks = 63 places. 35 cm clear above each block. The X faces the aisle.

    Watch · The top level runs hottest and driest.

  3. 16

    Daily check

    Five numbers a day: temperature, humidity, fresh air, water, and anything growing that should not.

    Watch · Oyster wants air before warmth.

The mushrooms come out of the X on the side and grow upwards. Never off the top.

06

Two flushes

Day 23–35 · cut, soak, cut again

  1. 17

    Cut flush one

    Day 4 to 6 after the X: twist or cut at the base, no stubs. 0.43 kg a block. Straight into the cold.

    Watch · A day late halves the shelf life.

  2. 18

    Soak the block

    Cold water in the tub, block under the water for 12 hours, drain 30 minutes, back in the tent.

    Watch · The soak is what buys the second flush.

  3. 19

    Cut flush two

    Six days later, same cut. 0.42 kg a block. Then the block is finished.

    Watch · No soak, no second flush. Do not skip it.

0.43 kg, soak, 0.42 kg. Weigh every block and write it down.

07

Clear and send

Day 37 · out and away

  1. 20

    Clear the block

    Spent block into the crate and out the same day. Wipe the shelf, spray it, log the weight.

    Watch · Spent blocks in the room are spores and flies.

  2. 21

    Pack and dispatch

    Into the punnet, batch code on the label, cold, delivered same day.

    Watch · Condensation in a sealed punnet turns it slimy by morning.

  3. 22

    Full clean of the tent

    One day, once the last block is out. Empty the tent completely, then work top to bottom.

    Watch · A wiped shelf with blocks still on it below is not a clean.

Out the same day, every day. Nothing spent sleeps in the room.

The deep clean — one day, once a cycle

Tent and racks, stripped and washed

  1. Strip it. Blocks out, shelves out, floor clear. Nothing left inside the tent.
  2. Wash the racks. Every shelf and upright washed, then wiped with 70% alcohol. Stand them out to dry in the sun.
  3. Wipe the tent. Inside walls, roof, floor and door, top to bottom. Alcohol on the film, then the frame and the zips.
  4. The dry run. Door open, fans off, one full hour to dry. Water left in a corner is where the next mould starts.
  5. Log it. Date, who cleaned, and what was replaced on the sheet. One day, once a cycle.
