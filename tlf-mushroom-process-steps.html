The mushroom process, step by step

Thirteen steps, one drawing each: from a heap of substrate to a labelled punnet. Read it in order once, then keep it on the wall and work the day off it.

13 steps in five stages · 63 fruiting places · two flushes a block. Every number here comes from the room’s own growth model, not from a book.

63fruiting places — 3 shelves × 7 levels × 3
21blocks a week in and out, 3 a day
0.85 kga block over both flushes: 0.43 + 0.42
3 + 3weeks colonising, then weeks in the tent

Stage one

Make the block

Day 0 to day 2 · one cook run is six blocks

1

Weigh the mix

  • Weigh every ingredient to the recipe. By eye is how a batch goes wrong quietly.
  • One cook run fills six blocks — the tank is the measure, not the order book.
  • Mix until the heap is evenly damp: squeeze a handful and two or three drops fall, no more.

Watch for: A dry pocket in the middle of the heap is a block that stalls three weeks from now.

2

Cook the load

  • Load the tank, cover the substrate with water and put the lid on.
  • Hold it at 65–70 °C for one to two hours◆.
  • The load is done when the straw is soft and smells nutty. Sour means it has gone too far.

Watch for: Read the temperature in the middle of the load, not at the tank wall.

3

Cool and drain

  • Drain over the trolley, then spread the load out to cool.
  • Cool to room temperature — hand-warm at most◆.
  • Bag it hot and you cook the spawn you paid for.

Watch for: The outside of a heap cools an hour before the middle does. Take the reading from the middle.

4

Spawn and bag

  • Mix 10–15% spawn by weight through the cooled substrate◆.
  • Fill each bag loosely to the fill line, then fit the filter patch.
  • Seal it, weigh the block, and write the weight on the tag.

Watch for: A bag packed tight has no air in it, and the mycelium needs air more than it needs space.

5

Tag the block

  • One batch code per cook run: date, room, grower, hour.
  • The tag travels with the block — colonising, fruiting, packing.
  • A problem on a shelf then traces to one mix, not the whole room.

Watch for: An untagged block cannot be traced in either direction.

Stage two

Colonise

Weeks 1 to 3 · on the rack, in the dark, outside the tent

6

Colonise in the dark

  • Dark, still, 20–24 °C◆, for two to three weeks.
  • Ready when the block is white through, firm under the hand and smells of mushroom.
  • Keep the bag closed. Every time it is opened, the room gets in.

Watch for: Green, black, or a sour smell — into a bag, sealed, out of the room. Do not open it to look.

Stage three

Fruit

Week 3 onward · three weeks in the tent for both flushes

7

Carry it into the tent

  • Three shelves, seven levels, three blocks to a place — 63 fruiting places.
  • 35 cm clear above the block, so the flush grows out and not into the shelf.
  • Three blocks in and three out every day, so the room turns over every three weeks.

Watch for: The top shelf runs hottest and driest. It fruits first and it dries out first.

8

Open the block

  • Cut the slots with a clean blade. Do not tear the plastic.
  • Open it when the block is white through, not before.
  • A torn bag leaks moisture and the block dries at the pin.

Watch for: Cut on the face the air reaches, not the face pressed against the shelf.

9

The daily check

  • Every morning, five things, written down: temperature, humidity, fresh air, water, and anything growing that is not a mushroom.
  • Pinheads about a week after the block is opened◆. Nothing by ten days means the room is too still or too dry.
  • One skipped day costs three blocks for three weeks running.

Watch for: A still room. Oyster mushrooms want air before they want warmth.

Stage four

Cut and clear

Every day, two flushes running · three first-flush and three second-flush a day

10

Harvest flush 1

  • Twist the cluster off at the base or cut it clean. No stubs left on the block.
  • Weigh every block and write it down. Flush 1 in this room is 0.43 kg a block.
  • Straight into the cold, never into a warm tray.

Watch for: Harvest before the cap margins turn up and the gills darken. A day late halves the shelf life.

11

Rest for flush 2

  • Rest the block, re-soak it, let it pin again — about three weeks after the first cut.
  • Flush 2 pays 0.42 kg a block when the block was soaked; 0.25–0.45 kg when it was not.
  • Same weighing, same tag, a second line on the log.

Watch for: The block nobody soaked. It comes back thin and still holds a shelf place for three weeks.

12

Clear and clean

  • Spent block straight into the crate and out of the room — not on the floor beside the tent.
  • Wipe the level, spray the shelf, log the block out.
  • Nothing sits clear for a day.

Watch for: Spent blocks in the room are a spore load and a fly farm. The flies come from the block you left.

Stage five

Send it

Same day · cold from the room to the door

13

Pack and dispatch

  • Weigh into the punnet and label it with the batch code that was on the block.
  • Keep it cold from the room to the door.
  • Deliver the same day. A mushroom has about three days in a fridge and one in a warm car.

Watch for: Condensation in a sealed punnet turns a good mushroom slimy by morning.

The day, in five jobs

One person, every day, five things. One load moves one step further down the line each day, so the same hands cook, bag, rack, move and cut without ever doubling back.
