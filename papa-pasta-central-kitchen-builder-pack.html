01 — THE PLANThe sheet the builder prices from

Everything below is drawn on one A0 sheet at 1:25. The image here is a close-up of that sheet for reading on a phone; the PDF is the one to print and scale from.

How to read it

Red dimensions are the clearances that must be kept. Green is the clean flow, the canopy and the extract route. Purple marks the extract duct runs to the solid façade. Circled numbers are Venturas equipment tags — the schedule in section 6 gives each one.

What moved from Venturas' base plan

The room does not change. Five things do, and each one is there to shorten a service run or clear a walkway:

  • Canopy 5.6 m → 4.2 m, over the cooking units only.
  • Cooking line flipped: tilt pans 4 and 5 and combi 7 moved to the east end, onto the wash-up wall.
  • Hand basin 11 moved to the NW corner by the west door.
  • Store back-of-house mirrored: double sink east, storage and fridges west.
  • Mobile GN racks 13 and 14 appear once, in prep — the duplicate set in the passage is gone.

02 — WORKFLOWOne direction, no crossing

Clean food moves east to west, from prep to the west door. Dirty returns go back east to wash-up. The two routes never meet, and nothing doubles back through the cooking aisle.

03 — SERVICESOne wall for water, one hole for air

The layout is organised around two short runs. Every wet item sits on the east partition, which already carries the wash-up sinks and the grease trap. Both hoods duct to the same solid section of the north wall, which has no glazing.

Plumbing and drainage

Extraction and make-up air

Why it is arranged this way

  • Combi and both tilt pans sit at the east end, on the same wall as the wash-up sinks and grease trap: one drain run, one water run, one floor gully.
  • The canopy shrinks to 4.2 m, so it extracts roughly 25% less air than the 5.6 m drawn by Venturas.
  • Both hoods duct north to the same solid section: one penetration, not two, and no duct crossing a window.
  • Gas goes to the same east end, beside the water and drain, instead of running the length of the line.

What the trades must confirm

  • Drain stack position and slab build-up for the spine and the floor channel.
  • Gas route and meter capacity at the east end.
  • Extract and make-up-air volumes, and the landlord's consent for the façade cut.
  • Ceiling height for the duct route and for the lighting calculation.

07 — BEFORE ORDERINGSeven things to confirm on site

Every dimension on this sheet was scaled from the drawings, not measured in the room. These are the checks that change numbers, and two of them are known trade-offs rather than open questions.

  1. Drain stack position and slab build-upConfirm the route for the wet spine and the 2.9 m floor channel to the stack before any slab work is priced.
  2. Façade penetrationThe duct runs need a cut in the solid section of the north wall. Landlord or body-corporate approval is required.
  3. Gas route and capacityRoute and meter capacity for tilt pans 4 and 5 and combi 7 at the east end. Size the extract and make-up air in the same visit.
  4. Fire rating and escapeFire rating of the store's north wall, and the escape route through the west door and the passage.
  5. The blast chiller stands in front of a windowA 0.9 m west window sits behind unit 12. Either accept it or blank off that pane — a decision for the owner, not the builder.
  6. Ceiling heightNeeded for the duct route and for ACDC's lighting calculation.
  7. Every dimensionSite-measure before anything is ordered or fabricated.
The one real compromise in this layout: the blast chiller stays about 4 m from the combi because there is no room for it opposite the cooking line. It costs a few steps per batch; nothing else in the flow crosses because of it.
