Where the Johannesburg money could go

We are selling the Johannesburg places. This is what the Western Cape offers for that money, what it actually returns after costs and tax, and what I think we should do about it.

5.31% what the same money earns in the bank, after tax

01 The short version

Above one million rand, no property we looked at pays for itself better than leaving the money in the bank. Rent, after levies, rates, management, voids and tax, comes to less than a five-year bond pays. That is not a view — it is what the numbers said across 46 properties we could price properly.

There is one exception, and it is not really a rental at all: the Colosseum in Century City. A suite costs R1 040 000, must be bought for cash, and comes with 28 nights a year for us to use, free, and those nights can be swapped for other resorts worldwide. The rent alone returns 3.8% after tax. But the nights are worth about R35 840 a year at the hotel's own rate, which lifts the real return to 7.2%.

What I would do Buy the Colosseum for cash, keep the rest in the bank for now, and decide on a beach flat only if we actually want one as a family rather than as an investment.

03 The Colosseum, worked through

This is the only property where the sums beat the bank, and only because of the nights — here is the listing:

The arithmetic R1 040 000 purchase · about R15 600 transfer costs · R1 188 a month levy · R970 a month rates
Rent after all costs and tax: 3.78% on the cash — below the bank.
28 nights at the hotel's own published R1 280 a night: R35 840 a year, which is 3.40% of the price, and we would otherwise spend it.
Together: 7.2%, above the bank at 5.31%.

The catch, and it is a real one: the banks will not lend against these suites, so it must be cash, and when we sell, our buyer must also pay cash. That makes it slower to sell than an ordinary flat. It suits money we would not need in a hurry.

04 What we are not doing, and why

Building on a plot

We priced nineteen plots against the finished houses standing next to them. Building cost more in every single case — in one example R16–21 million to build against R6.5 million for a larger finished house nearby. Building here is a lifestyle project, not an investment.

Buying a beach house as an investment

The expensive coastal houses return under 1% after tax. A R7.7 million house in Langebaan earns less than R80 000 a year net. If we want a beach place, we should buy it because we want it, and accept that it costs us money to own.

Anything with a rental guarantee

Most guarantees run for twelve months from transfer. A year of guaranteed rent is a marketing cost the developer pays to sell the unit, not income we can rely on.

Anything under a million rand

We ruled those out. Worth saying plainly: the two properties that beat the bank were both under R1 million. The rule is what removes them, not the market.

05 The risks I want us to talk about

  • The levy never stops. On the pricier city apartments it is R10 000–R11 600 a month. A bad year with no tenant is R130 000 gone before anything else.
  • Cash-only suites are slow to sell. The Colosseum will take longer to turn back into money than a normal flat, and the price may have to move to do it.
  • Body corporate health. If the building has no reserve fund, or owners are in arrears, a bank will refuse the next buyer's bond — which freezes our exit, not just their entry.
  • Short-letting rules. Cape Town is moving towards registering short-term rentals, and a building can ban them outright. A flat bought to let nightly could be worth less later.
  • Rates and levies rise. Everything here is a snapshot; the levies will not stay still.

06 What I need from you

Three questions, in order of importance:

  • Do we want the Colosseum at all? R1 040 000 in cash for a suite we use 28 nights a year and let out the rest of the time. If we would not use those nights, it is just a below-bank rental and the answer is no.
  • Do we want a beach flat as a family thing? Hermanus at R1 350 000 is the best of them — it returns about what the bank does, and we would get the use of it. That is a fair trade, not a clever one.
  • Is the R1 million floor still right? If we dropped it, two cheap flats in Parow and Langebaan returned 6.95% and 5.68% after tax — genuinely better than the bank. The floor is a choice about the kind of property we want to own, and it costs us about 1.5% a year to keep.
One thing to be clear about None of this makes us money quickly. The best case here matches or modestly beats a bond, with the use of a property thrown in. If the goal is a strong return, the honest answer is that the bank is competitive and property is not the bargain it is often described as.
