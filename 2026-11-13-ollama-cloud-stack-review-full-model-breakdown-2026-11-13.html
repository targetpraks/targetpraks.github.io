Ollama Cloud: the full model breakdown, and what to change

A read-only audit of every Ollama Cloud model against our live routes, our own 30-day usage, and four independent benchmark boards pulled this morning. Nothing has been changed.

The answer in five lines
  1. I got the vision call wrong, and this is the correction. Inkling 2 Small beats GLM-5.3-Flash on every vision row of the published cross-model table — charts, documents, OCR, screenshots, visual reasoning — including the one we care about most, OmniDocBench: 94.6 vs 92.3. I argued the vision question from the LMArena board (which has no row for a model that reached the cloud ten hours ago) and from τ-bench, a tool-policy benchmark that cannot see an image. Both were the wrong instruments. The vision lane should move to Inkling 2 Small. What still stands is narrower: it should not become the main model.
  2. There is one free win on the board: kimi-k3 → kimi-k3.1-pro. Cheaper on both input ($3.00 → $2.00) and output ($15.00 → $10.00), and a different league on quality (AA 17.4 → 46.5). It is already on Ollama Cloud — our own October matrix says it is not.
  3. Our primary is still the right primary. No Ollama Cloud model at or near its price beats deepseek-v4.1-flash on coding, long-agentic work or cost. It is 79% of our token spend and worth it.
  4. The cheapest model in the catalogue is also the best tool-caller (mimo-v2.6-flash, τ 0.779 at $0.14/$0.28) and we barely use it — 8 calls in 30 days. That is the biggest unexploited lane.
  5. The single largest lever is not a model at all: it is the clock. Off-peak halves the deepseek price; our 30-day main-lane spend is $128.80 at peak rates, $64.40 if the heavy runs move outside the peak windows.

04 The vision question, corrected

My first pass got this wrong and it is worth being precise about why. I answered from the LMArena vision board and from τ-bench. Neither instrument can settle a vision question: the board has no row for a model that reached Ollama Cloud ten hours ago, and τ-bench measures multi-turn tool policy on an airline simulation — it never sees an image. An absence on a lagging leaderboard is not a measurement, and I treated it as one.

What actually speaks to vision is the published cross-model table from the model card, where Inkling 2 Small and GLM-5.3-Flash are scored on identical rows alongside MiMo, MiniMax, DeepSeek, Muse Spark, Mistral, Kimi K3, GPT-6 Luna, Gemini 3.8 Flash and Claude Haiku 5.5.

Corrected verdict: move the vision lane to Inkling 2 Small.

It wins eight of nine vision rows against the model we run today, by the widest margins on exactly the work this house does — charts (+20.4), document reasoning (+9.8), perception (+15.1) — and it is second-best in the entire 13-model table on documents and on MMMU Pro. Everything that beats it in a row is either not on Ollama Cloud (Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Haiku 5.5) or costs ten times as much (Kimi K3, $3.00/$15.00). On our own catalogue it is simply the best-documented vision model we can reach.

And the price is not a real objection. Our vision lane spent $1.15 in 30 days at GLM rates. The same workload at Inkling rates is $1.90. The entire question is worth 75 cents a month.

What "better than everyone else" is and is not. It is better than every model we can reach at Ollama Cloud, on evidence. It is not the best model in the world: Gemini 3.8 Flash leads five of these rows and Claude Haiku 5.5 leads two, and both are unreachable from here. The claim to keep honest is the narrow one — best on Ollama Cloud — and on this table that holds.

What still stands: not the main model

Two things argue against putting it in the driver's seat, and both survive this correction. Its τ-bench score is 0.633 — last in the cheap tier, measured independently — and Thinking Machines' own limitations section says it "doesn't always choose the best tools for the job" and is "less optimized for the chat experience". Against that, the vendor reports 85.4 on MCP Atlas. Two tool-use benchmarks disagreeing is not something I can resolve from a table — which is precisely why agentic use still wants a bake-off, and single-shot vision does not.

Method note: a vendor card is the first evidence to read for a model released in the last fortnight, and the independent boards are the check that follows. I ran them in the wrong order.
