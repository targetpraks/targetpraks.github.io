Individual or company — the finding

  • As an individual you win every one of the fifteen. A company pays €3,000 of social security and €2,000 of accounting a year before it earns a euro, then 22% corporate tax, then 5% on the dividend. On €13,000–€28,000 of revenue that overhead alone costs more than the tax difference.
  • The crossover is revenue, not price. A company starts to beat the individual at roughly €26,000–€41,000 of annual revenue, depending on the property's value: above €36,400 on a €150,000 property (profits retained) or €40,800 distributed; above €32,200 / €37,900 on €250,000; above €25,900 / €30,500 on €400,000, because depreciation shielding grows with value. That is two flats, or one large beachfront villa — not one €64,000 apartment.
  • 1 July 2027 changes the maths, but only at size. If the announced non-EU transfer tax lands at 15.45% (from 3.09%), a company saves 12.36% of the price on everything except a new build. On the €389,000 Archangelos house that is €48,080 — enough to flip the ten-year return in the company's favour on the four largest resale, auction and fixer-upper deals (A5, F1, F2, F3).
  • Order of operations. Complete at 3.09% as an individual before 1 July 2027 if you can. If you cannot, and the ticket is above roughly €150,000, take the company. If you are buying one €64,000 flat either way, stay an individual.
  • Greek law forces the question anyway. A natural person letting three or more properties must register as a business and charge VAT. Below three, the individual route is a choice — at these values, the better one.

What the two stated constraints cost

Ricardo asked for one beachfront or near-beach property in each section, and one in the most popular place. Both are available, and both are paid for in yield:

  • The beachfront premium. F3, one of four unfinished villas on a 5,000 m² waterfront plot at Tholaros, returns 3.8% net against the page's best at 7.1% — about 3.3 points of yield for the water. Not automatically wrong (beachfront land cannot be replicated and the exit multiple is a different bet), but wrong if you told yourself you were buying income.
  • The popularity premium. Lindos (F1) returns 3.6%, Rhodes Town's new builds (N1, N2) 2.5–2.6%, and the Old Town listed townhouse (F2) 1.6% before restoration. The most popular place on the island is the most expensive place to earn a living from.

What can go wrong, in order of cost

  • Auction mechanics. 30% of the hammer price is due immediately, the balance within a short window, and you generally cannot look inside first. Lots can be withdrawn or adjourned. Four of the five lots bid on 25–27 November 2026 — four weeks, not four months, to raise a deposit, run a title search and decide.
  • The unfinished flats. A2 is 65% built under a 2006 permit. Permits lapse, and an unfinished property cannot get the AMA short-let registration until it has power, water and a completion certificate. Budget the re-licensing, not just the plasterboard.
  • Short-letting in a block. An Athens court held in July 2026 that a short-let in a residential building needs the co-owners' consent, and a bill is being drafted to codify a 51% approval threshold. Buy a maisonette, a detached house, or a building whose rules already permit short-lets.
  • Two dated risks. 1 July 2027 — non-EU transfer tax from 3.09% to 15.45%, €48,080 on the €389,000 house. 31 December 2026 — the new-build VAT suspension expires; if it lapses, 24% lands on the price, €93,600 on the €390,000 Rhodes Town flat.
  • Downside test. Rates down 25%, occupancy down 20%, works up 30%: A4 still returns 3.3% net, the Rhodes Town new builds fall to 1.4%, and the beachfront villa lands at 2.0% — it survives, but it stops being an income asset and becomes a land holding. Fully managed instead of light-touch (20% to a manager plus a 15.5% platform fee) takes A4 from 7.1% to 3.4%.

Method, and what could not be verified

Purchase costs are 3.09% transfer tax, 1.2% notary, 1.5% legal, 0.5% registry and 0.5% incidental — 6.79% of price; new builds carry no transfer tax while the VAT suspension runs, and 3.7% of fees. Works are €100/m² for a cosmetic refresh, €350–450/m² for a dated flat and €700–1,100/m² for a full restoration or a shell, each including a 10% contingency, with €12,000 of furnishing or nothing where the developer delivers equipped. Revenue is area-based nightly rates and annual occupancy: Rhodes Town and Old Town €125 at 45%, Lindos €140 at 42%, Ixia–Ialysos €95 at 45%, Archangelos €78–82 at 42–44%, the south coast €110 at 40%, Tholaros beachfront €180 at 42%, Embonas €70 at 35%. Those are conservative readings of a market where AirROI and Airbtics put materially different revenue on the same island, so the model leans on the rate and occupancy range that could be corroborated rather than either provider's headline.

The page's own honest gaps: the Ialyssos auction lot (A1) publishes no bedroom count — 82 m² on a ground floor, treated as two bedrooms; two lots found during the research were withdrawn and are excluded; and the AMA and co-ownership position of each specific building is not verified for any listing. That last one is a lawyer's job before deposit, not a spreadsheet's.
