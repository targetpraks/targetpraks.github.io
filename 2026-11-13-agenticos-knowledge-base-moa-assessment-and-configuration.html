1. Verdict

The section is structurally sound and under-supplied. Eighteen bases exist and every bot is bound to exactly one of them, which is the right shape. What was wrong was inside the bases: role documents were chunked at three to five times the size their headings imply, titles carried retired codes that then appeared in citations, one base held another unit's workbook, and the whole-company base carried nine duplicate copies of unit content. All of that is fixed or queued. What remains is not configuration — it is corpus: most bases hold one document, because WorkDrive read access is still refused, so the SOPs, brand manuals and policies that would make these bots useful cannot be loaded yet.

5. The constraint that limits everything else

The real corpus — SOPs, brand manuals, finance policies, supplier contracts — still cannot be read: WorkDrive returns 405 F6004 at GET /workdrive/api/v1/teamfolders, which is a missing OAuth scope, not a platform fault. Until that opens, every base past its role workbook stays thin by definition. That is the INF-217 lane, and it is the highest-value thing anyone can do to this section.

6. Rules now in force

  1. One base per authorised unit, named on the authority label, holding that unit's material only.
  2. The company base holds cross-unit material only — never a copy of a unit slice.
  3. Ingestion config is forward-only: a chunking change is not live until its documents are re-ingested.
  4. Markdown for narrative documents, recursive for tables and registers. A departure gets a written reason on the base.
  5. No document title may carry a retired form; fix the title at the source before upload.
  6. Re-ingest on a source change, a naming change or a chunking change — never to "refresh".
  7. New base: authority name → standard profile → one unit bot → knowledge-reach proof.
  8. Never upload secrets, personal files, drafts or a pointer-only note as a base's sole content.
