02Ranked, and why

1

OpenProject — openproject.org

GPL-3.0 · Community edition free, unlimited users · Enterprise edition adds support and add-ons

Why first: it is the only one that carries the whole shape of a multi-brand group — portfolios, programs, projects, sub-projects, budgets, cost reports, meetings with minutes, and work packages that block, precede or relate to each other. That is the "one task can reference another" complaint fixed, properly.

What it will not do: look at a poster. There is no proofing viewer and no approval stamp. The Enterprise edition adds Gantt baselines, custom fields and SSO — not creative review.

Read: feature list · Enterprise edition · source

2

Plane — plane.so

AGPL-3.0 · Community edition self-hosted free · Pro $6/seat a month billed yearly ($8 monthly), Business $13 — on their cloud, 12 users free

Why second, and why it matters to you: Intake is the closest open-source answer to the inbox you asked about — feedback, requests and comments arrive in one queue and you decide which become work items. Cycles and modules then give a manager the "what is actually happening this fortnight" view without a spreadsheet.

What it will not do: speak marketing. It assumes a product team: cycles, modules, states, no client accounts, no proofing, and time tracking only on the paid tiers.

Read: self-hosting · pricing · source

3

Huly — huly.io

EPL-2.0 · self-hosted free · their cloud is priced by resources, not seats: unlimited users, $0 for 10 GB, then $19.99 / $99.99 / $399.99 a month

Why third: on paper it is the dream — projects, documents, chat, inbox, planner and video calls in one place, all open source. It is the only project here whose inbox is a first-class feature rather than a bell icon.

Why not first: self-hosting needs a real stack (MongoDB, Elasticsearch and friends) and real RAM; it is a young company with a young codebase, and a five-brand group would be an early adopter, not a customer.

Read: huly.io · source

4

Kitsu — kitsu.cg-wire.com

AGPL-3.0 · self-hosted free · the review engine, not the project tool

What it is: the open-source equivalent of Filestage for video and images, built by CGWire for animation studios. Draw on the frame, type notes, tag the frame so the timestamp appears in your comment, review 3D files, request review by moving a task to "waiting for approval", and let a client review from a browser with no account.

What it is not: a company project platform, and it does not touch PDFs. If the review has to stay open source, this is the piece that makes it possible — bolted onto OpenProject or Plane, not instead of them.

Read: review docs · review engine · source

5

Leantime — leantime.io

AGPL-3.0 · Community edition free · Pro $10/seat monthly, $8 yearly

Why it is here: the calmest interface of the eight, built deliberately around goals and attention. If your team finds project tools hostile, this is the antidote.

The catch: the free edition is the shell; the features that make it useful are paid plugins, so the licence is open and the bill is not zero.

6–8

Taiga · Vikunja · Kanboard — good tools, wrong size

Taiga (taiga.io, MPL-2.0): clean scrum for one team; no portfolio, no client space, quiet self-hosted community.

Vikunja (vikunja.io, AGPL-3.0): excellent personal-to-small-team task manager with list, kanban, gantt and table views; not a company platform.

Kanboard (kanboard.org, MIT): minimal, dependable, runs anywhere — and does almost nothing you need beyond columns.

Also considered: Redmine (redmine.org) — time tracking and a plugin for everything, but it looks and feels like 2011, which fails your "visually hostile" test outright.

03The review gap, stated plainly

There is no maintained open-source tool that gives you a stamped PDF approval. Not OpenProject, not Plane, not Huly, not Leantime, not Taiga, not Vikunja, not Kanboard. The closest is Kitsu, and it covers video and images, not documents.

So the open-source route splits in two, and only one of them is honest about Filestage:

Route A — structure only: OpenProject or Plane replaces Zoho Projects, and Filestage stays for review. Cost: R0 licence plus hosting and admin, plus Filestage's R37,673–R62,284.

Route B — structure plus review, all open source: OpenProject or Plane plus Kitsu, and Filestage goes — but only if your reviews are video and images. One PDF with a client signature requirement puts Filestage back on the bill, because Kitsu cannot open it.
